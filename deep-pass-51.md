# Cubic non-generation and actual quartic corrections

Within the actual degree-two divisor setup, a reduced second-image
obstruction cannot be resolved by any generating set of intrinsic
degree at most three. The algebra generated by the entire cubic
filtration has a two-dimensional closed fiber, whereas the actual
algebra has a one-dimensional fiber.

An ordinary triple point of the full primitive plinth forces such
an obstruction to be reduced. Actual flatness supplies two quartic
corrections, and their fourth derivatives leave precisely two
possibilities for the fourth-image ideal. These conclusions neither
exclude the obstruction nor prove generation in degree four.

The broader working question is
\[
A[s_*]\simeq\mathbb C^{[4]}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad A\simeq\mathbb C^{[3]}.
\tag{1}
\]
This is the unconfirmed complex-threefold interpretation of the supplied
conjecture. The pasted conclusion has a dimension mismatch for larger
dimension. Equation (1) remains unresolved here. In particular, the
special coordinate frame used below is an additional hypothesis, not
a consequence of an arbitrary polynomial cylinder.

## 1. Actual local setting

Let \(D\ne0\) be the primitive intrinsic locally nilpotent derivation
supplied by the established independent degree-two frame. Write
\[
C=\ker D\simeq\mathbb C^{[2]},\qquad
R=C_{\mathfrak m},\quad\mathfrak n=\mathfrak mR,\quad
\mathcal A=A\otimes_C R,\quad L=\operatorname{Frac}R.
\tag{2}
\]
The actual inclusion \(C\subset A\) is faithfully flat and its closed
fibers are nonempty curves. The polynomial-cylinder hypotheses give a
smooth contractible threefold, and restriction of its smooth projection
from affine four-space to a suitable affine three-plane is dominant.
Kaliman-Saveliev's curve-times-plane domination result identifies the
intrinsic quotient; the regular-base dimension equality gives flatness.[^1]
This does not assume that the unknown threefold is affine three-space.

Put \(F_i=\ker D^{i+1}\) and \(I_i=D^i(F_i)\subset R\).
At a reduced second-image obstruction the inherited actual module data are
\[
\mathfrak n=(a,b),\quad
F_2=R\oplus Rz\oplus Rw,\quad F_3=F_2\oplus Rt,\quad
r=az+bw,\quad I_2=p\mathfrak n.
\tag{3}
\]
Here \(a,b\) are regular parameters and \(p=Dr\ne0\).
For invariant coefficients and a unit \(\epsilon\in R^*\), write
\[
B_1=ac+bd,\quad B_2=ae+bf,\quad Q=aq+bh,
\]
\[
Dz=\epsilon br+B_1,\quad Dw=-\epsilon ar+B_2,\quad
p=aB_1+bB_2,\quad
\epsilon r^2-2B_2z+2B_1w=Q.
\tag{4}
\]
The actual cubic lift satisfies
\[
bt=\epsilon rz-2ez+2cw-q,\qquad
-at=\epsilon rw-2fz+2dw-h.
\tag{5}
\]
The first-jet argument has already shown \(cf-de\in\mathfrak n\)
and \(D(F_3)\subset\mathfrak nF_3\). Thus the residue coefficient
matrix has rank zero or one. The full divisor presentations, not a
general conic approximation, identify the algebra at every height-one
base prime. These are the inputs of the
[complete cubic note](research/cubic-generation-obstruction-pass51.md).

## 2. Presentation of the entire cubic algebra

Set \(S=R[Z,W,T]\), \(r_S=aZ+bW\), and
\[
F=bT-\epsilon r_SZ+2eZ-2cW+q,\qquad
G=aT+\epsilon r_SW-2fZ+2dW-h.
\tag{6}
\]
There is an actual surjection
\(S/(F,G)\to K:=R[F_3]=R[z,w,t]\).
For every height-one prime \((\pi)\subset R\), one of \(a,b\)
is a unit. Eliminating \(T\) leaves exactly the full local conic, so
\[
(S/(F,G))_{(\pi)}=K_{(\pi)}=\mathcal A_{(\pi)},\qquad
(S/(F,G))_L=L[r].
\tag{7}
\]

The polynomials \(F,G\) are coprime in the UFD \(S\). A common
prime factor becomes a unit generically and hence is associated to
a base prime \(\pi\). But the nonzero prime ideal \((F,G)_{(\pi)}\)
avoids \(\pi\) by (7). It cannot be contained in \(\pi S_{(\pi)}\):
primeness would allow cancellation of every power of \(\pi\) from
a nonzero member, contradicting that containment.

A presentation test will remove the remaining possible torsion.
A finite \(S\)-module \(N\) with a free resolution of length two has
\[
\operatorname{depth}N_{\mathfrak p}
\ge\operatorname{depth}S_{\mathfrak p}-2
=\operatorname{ht}\mathfrak p-2.
\tag{8}
\]
Thus all its associated primes have height at most two.[^2]
If \(N\) has the generic and divisor localizations (7), no associated
prime contracts to a height-one base prime, since those localizations
are domains. If all primes over the closed base point containing its
defining ideal have height at least three, (8) excludes them too.
The only associated prime then lies over the generic point. Every
nonzero base element is a nonzerodivisor, so \(N\hookrightarrow N_L\).
A generically injective map \(N\to\mathcal A\) is therefore injective.
This tests the presentation; it does not prove \(R\)-flatness.

### Rank one

The residual equations are
\[
\bar F=2\bar eZ-2\bar cW+\bar q,\qquad
\bar G=-2\bar fZ+2\bar dW-\bar h.
\tag{9}
\]
If their linear coefficient matrix has rank one, actual fiber
nonemptiness forces consistency. Their residual ideal is one
nonconstant affine-linear equation, so its quotient is
\(\mathbb C[V,T]\). Every prime above the closed base point has
height at least three in \(S\).

Coprimality of \(F,G\) gives the length-two Koszul resolution.
The presentation test proves
\[
K=S/(F,G),\qquad K/\mathfrak nK\simeq\mathbb C[V,T].
\tag{10}
\]
This is a plane fiber, not the actual curve fiber.

### Rank zero

Now \(c,d,e,f\in\mathfrak n\), and nonemptiness also forces
\(q,h\in\mathfrak n\). Write each of these six coefficients as
\(v=av_a+bv_b\). Then
\[
F=aA_{11}+bA_{12},\qquad G=aA_{21}+bA_{22},
\]
\[
\begin{aligned}
A_{11}&=-\epsilon Z^2+2e_aZ-2c_aW+q_a,\\
A_{12}&=T-\epsilon ZW+2e_bZ-2c_bW+q_b,\\
A_{21}&=T+\epsilon ZW-2f_aZ+2d_aW-h_a,\\
A_{22}&=\epsilon W^2-2f_bZ+2d_bW-h_b.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]
For \(\Delta=A_{11}A_{22}-A_{12}A_{21}\), direct expansion gives
\[
a\Delta=A_{22}F-A_{12}G,\qquad
b\Delta=-A_{21}F+A_{11}G,
\quad
\Delta=-T^2+\Lambda(Z,W)T+\Omega(Z,W),
\tag{12}
\]
where \(\deg\Lambda\le1\), \(\deg\Omega\le3\).
The quartic terms cancel. In particular \(\bar\Delta\ne0\),
and cancellation of \(a\ne0\) in the actual domain makes
\(\Delta\) vanish in \(\mathcal A\).

The matrix
\[
M=\begin{pmatrix}
A_{22}&-A_{21}\\
-A_{12}&A_{11}\\
-a&-b
\end{pmatrix}
\tag{13}
\]
has signed maximal minors \(\Delta,-G,F\), and
\((F,G,\Delta)M=0\).
Its two columns are independent over the fraction field of \(S\).
Every row syzygy is their unique rational linear combination.
At every height-one prime of \(S\), one of \(F,G\) is a unit,
so the appropriate invertible minor makes both coefficients regular
there. Intersecting those localizations in the UFD makes them global.
This proves the exact resolution
\[
0\longrightarrow S^2\xrightarrow{M}S^3
\xrightarrow{(F,G,\Delta)}S
\longrightarrow S/(F,G,\Delta)\longrightarrow0.
\tag{14}
\]
No blanket assertion that arbitrary minors give a presentation is used.

Equation (12) puts \(\Delta\) in \((F,G)\) over every height-one
base localization. The nonzero residual determinant makes closed-base
primes have height at least three. Applying the presentation test yields
\[
K=S/(F,G,\Delta),\qquad
K/\mathfrak nK=\mathbb C[Z,W,T]/(\bar\Delta).
\tag{15}
\]
The residual algebra is finite free of rank two over
\(\mathbb C[Z,W]\), with basis \(1,T\), and has dimension two.
Nilpotents are retained. The equality of presentations also proves
that \((F,G,\Delta)\) is the generic-component saturation of \((F,G)\).

Both rank cases give the conditional conclusion
\[
\boxed{\,J R=\mathfrak n\ \Longrightarrow\
R[F_3]\ne\mathcal A,\quad
\dim(K/\mathfrak nK)=2.\,}
\tag{16}
\]
The map from that surface algebra to the actual curve fiber has
nonzero kernel. Higher-degree actual elements must account for a
strict contraction
\(\mathfrak n\mathcal A\cap K\supsetneq\mathfrak nK\).
This excludes every cubic generating set, not merely a list arising
from one projected slice.

## 3. Ordinary full-plinth triples force a reduced obstruction

For this section start with a possibly nonreduced proper obstruction
\(JR=(a,b)\), an \(\mathfrak n\)-primary complete intersection.
The actual branchwise trace theorem gives positive order for
\(B_1/b\) wherever the denominator is nonzero on the branch, and the
companion statement for \(B_2/a\). At a point with two distinct
tangent directions it gives \(B_1,B_2\in\mathfrak n^2\) and
\(p\in\mathfrak n^2J\). These are inherited actual normalized-cover
results, not assertions about arbitrary conics.[^3]

Suppose the full element \(p\) has a squarefree cubic initial form.
Then \(J\) contains an element \(a\) of order one. Choose a complementary
parameter \(b\). The image of \(J\) in the DVR \(R/(a)\) is
\((b^n)\), so an exact change of minimal generators gives
\(J=(a,b^n)\). This happens in \(R\), not only after completion.

The conic is preserved by invariant basis changes:
for \(x=(z,w)^t,\ell=(a,b)\), replace
\(x,\ell,B,\epsilon,Q\) by
\(Mx,\ell M^{-1},MB,\epsilon\det M,Q\det M\).
The identity \(MSM^t=(\det M)S\) for the two-dimensional skew
matrix proves covariance and preserves \(B_i\in\mathfrak n^2\).
Thus the exact generator change is allowed in the actual equations.

If \(n\ge2\), the degree-three initial form satisfies
\[
p_3=a_1(B_1)_2.
\tag{17}
\]
Its three distinct factors give a smooth normalization branch tangent
to \(a=0\). There \(b\) has order one, and the other two tangent
factors give \(\operatorname{ord}(B_1)=2\).
The trace inequality instead requires
\(2\ge\operatorname{ord}(b^n)+1=n+1\), a contradiction.
This works also when several branches belong to one irreducible
component. Consequently
\[
\boxed{\,p\text{ ordinary full triple},\ JR\ne R
\ \Longrightarrow\ JR=\mathfrak n.\,}
\tag{18}
\]
The conclusion reduces the obstruction scheme; it does not exclude it.

## 4. Actual quartic corrections and the fourth-image alternative

Assume \(J=\mathfrak n=(a,b)\) and \(B_1,B_2\in\mathfrak n^2\).
The conic and faithful-flat contraction give \(Q\in\mathfrak n^2\).
Choose
\(B_i=aC_i+b^2\beta_i,\ Q=aQ_1+b^2q\), and put
\[
\begin{aligned}
G_a&=\epsilon az^2+2\epsilon bzw-2C_2z+2C_1w-Q_1,\\
H_a&=\epsilon w^2-2\beta_2z+2\beta_1w-q.
\end{aligned}
\tag{19}
\]
The conic is exactly \(aG_a+b^2H_a=0\).
The regular sequence \((a,b^2)\) remains regular in the actual
flat algebra, so \(H_a=aT_a\) and \(G_a=-b^2T_a\) for
an actual \(T_a\in\mathcal A\). No fraction-field-only division
has been substituted for this step.

Since \(D^2z=\epsilon bp,\ D^2w=-\epsilon ap\), and their
third derivatives vanish, differentiation gives
\[
T_a\in F_4,\qquad D^4T_a=6\epsilon^3ap^2,\qquad D^5T_a=0.
\tag{20}
\]
The symmetric decomposition
\(B_i=a^2\gamma_i+bE_i,\ Q=a^2q'+bQ_2\) gives
\[
H_b=\epsilon z^2-2\gamma_2z+2\gamma_1w-q'=bT_b,
\qquad D^4T_b=6\epsilon^3bp^2.
\tag{21}
\]
Both elements have degree exactly four.

At every height-one base prime the full quadratic model has degree-four
basis \(1,T,Y,TY,Y^2\), with \(DT=p,\ DY=2T\) and
\(pY=T^2-\eta\). The last fourth derivative is \(24p^2\);
the others vanish. Where \(p\) is a unit the polynomial-line model
gives the same ideal. Therefore \(I_4R_{(\pi)}=p^2R_{(\pi)}\)
at every height-one prime. UFD contraction and (20)-(21) imply
\[
p^2\mathfrak n\subset I_4\subset p^2R,\qquad
\boxed{\,I_4=p^2\mathfrak n\ \text{or}\ I_4=p^2R.\,}
\tag{22}
\]
The quotient ideal \(p^{-2}I_4\) contains the maximal ideal,
which is why these are the only two possibilities.

The weaker triple conic has
\(B_1=-a^2,\ B_2=b^2,\ Q=-ab+a^3,\ \epsilon=1\).
Its proposed corrections \((w^2-2z)/a\) and
\((z^2-2w-a)/b\) do not lie in that ring: modulo the respective
parameter the nonzero numerator is killed by the square of the
other parameter. This is exactly the regular-sequence failure.
The [full quartic note](research/triple-point-quartic-corrections-pass51.md)
keeps this nonflat control separate from the actual construction.

No conclusion here chooses the ideal in (22), proves quartic generation,
or proves the two constructed quartics lie outside \(R[F_3]\).
Their degree four proves only nonmembership in the module \(F_3\).

## 5. Whole-fiber velocity invariance

At an actual second-image point the original frame has
\(f=g=h_1=h_2=0\). In the original sliced cylinder
\(\mathcal A[s_*]\), put \(P_i=Ex_i\) for the two good
coordinate velocities and \(p_i=\bar P_i\in\bar A\), where
\(\bar A=\mathcal A/\mathfrak n\mathcal A\).
The actual slice identity gives
\[
(p_1,p_2)\bar A=\bar A,\qquad
I_1',I_{12}',I_2'\in F_2,\qquad
\bar I_1'=p_1^2,\quad\bar I_{12}'=p_1p_2,\quad\bar I_2'=p_2^2.
\tag{23}
\]
Primes distinguish these three invariants from the image ideals \(I_i\).

The induced derivation \(\bar D\) kills their residues because
\(D(F_2)\subset\mathfrak nF_2\).
On \(\bar A[p_1^{-1}]\), differentiating \(p_1^2\) and
\(p_1p_2\) gives \(\bar Dp_1=\bar Dp_2=0\).
The other open gives the same conclusion, and the unit ideal
makes the two opens cover. Thus
\[
\boxed{\bar Dp_1=\bar Dp_2=0\text{ in the whole }\bar A.}
\tag{24}
\]
This remains valid with zero divisors and nilpotents.

At a reduced rank-zero point, the initial-conic theorem gives the actual
image \(S_0=\mathbb C[\bar z,\bar w]\subset\bar A\) with
\(1\le\dim_{\mathbb C}S_0\le3\). Adjoin the two velocities:
\(T_0=S_0[p_1,p_2]\).
The squares and mixed product in (23) lie in \(S_0\), so \(T_0\)
is spanned over \(S_0\) by \(1,p_1,p_2\).

On every Artinian local factor of \(S_0\), one square is a unit.
Otherwise both squares would be nilpotent there. That factor's
nonzero idempotent stays nonzero under the actual inclusion into
\(\bar A\), where two nilpotents cannot generate the unit ideal.
This contracts the unit ideal without assuming a flat inclusion.

On a factor where \(p_1^2\) is a unit,
\(p_2=(p_1p_2)(p_1^2)^{-1}p_1\), so two module generators suffice;
the other case is symmetric. Hence
\[
\boxed{\dim_{\mathbb C}T_0\le2\dim_{\mathbb C}S_0\le6,\qquad
\bar D|_{T_0}=0.}
\tag{25}
\]
This bounds a particular finite-dimensional subalgebra with its
nilpotents retained. It does not bound the whole curve fiber,
its number of components, or the derivative of every higher-degree
element. Details are in the
[whole-fiber proof](research/fiber-velocity-invariance-pass51.md).

## 6. Verification scope and remaining problem

The two new standalone suites contain five methods. The cubic suite
checks the exact syzygies, determinant cancellations and saturation
in specified formal coefficient models. The quartic suite checks both
conic divisions as polynomial identities, fourth derivatives, basis
covariance, divisor computations and the failed divisions in the weak
conic. These finite controls do not prove the universal geometric
premises, the associated-prime argument, or actual flatness.

Independent AI reconstructions and observed reruns are recorded in
the [working audit](research/proof-audits-pass51-working.md).
They are not human expert review or proof-assistant verification.

The [captured expanded regression](verification-pass-51.json) passed
989 methods in 120 suites, including the five new methods. The recorded
run spans 20:25:02-20:26:35 UTC on 13 September 2026. It verifies the
recorded finite controls, not the full cancellation conjecture.

Reproduction commands are:

~~~sh
/opt/anaconda3/bin/python research/run_regression_pass51.py
/opt/anaconda3/bin/python research/cubic_generation_obstruction_checks_pass51.py
/opt/anaconda3/bin/python research/triple_point_quartic_checks_pass51.py
/opt/anaconda3/bin/python research/consistency_checks_pass51.py
~~~

The next local question is how the actual higher-degree algebra changes
the two-dimensional cubic fiber. The fourth-image alternative is a
concrete intermediate question, not the original cancellation problem.
Singular and repeated plinth geometry, global polynomial recognition
and existence or reduction of a universal actual frame remain separate
obligations. No historical novelty, publication readiness or general
solution follows from the results above.
