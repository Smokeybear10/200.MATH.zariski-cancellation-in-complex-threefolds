# Quadratic double components cannot intersect

In the actual nonconical quadratic frame, a closed fiber with
two components of multiplicity two has disjoint reduced
components. Consequently the power-column alternative in the
collapsed double-component classification cannot occur when the
first normal action is nonzero.

The proof excludes both possible quotient ranks at an
intersection. Rank zero gives a quadratic pencil whose initial
Jacobian field is a semisimple linear field multiplied by an
invariant. Rank one gives the analogous obstruction on a smooth
surface. In each case this contradicts the local nilpotence
inherited by the polynomial associated-graded derivation.
No local nilpotence of a localization or completion is assumed.

## 1. Actual hypotheses and saturation at degree two

Retain the actual nonconical setup of
[global Jacobian91](nonconical-global-jacobian-field-pass91.md),
[total multiplicity92](nonconical-total-fiber-multiplicity-pass92.md)
and [weighted frontier94](nonconical-weighted-frontier-pass94.md).
In particular A is a smooth affine complex threefold,
\(S=\mathbb C[s_1,s_2]\subset A\) is faithfully flat,
\(N\in\operatorname{LND}(A)\) is the canonical Jacobian field
with kernel S, and \(A=S[A_1,A_2,c]\). The generic relative
degrees of \(A_1,A_2,c\) are bounded by 2,2,4.

Fix a closed base point m whose whole fiber has exactly two
components, with generic multiplicities two each:
\[
C=A/mA,\qquad \operatorname{Min}(mA)=\{\mathfrak q_1,\mathfrak q_2\},
\qquad \mu_1=\mu_2=2,\qquad
N^3A_1=N^3A_2=N^5c=0.
\tag{1}
\]
Both components are fixed by N. Indeed a rank-two quotient
differential at a generic component would make its transverse
fiber have length one. Its rank is one by the quadratic
generic-rank exclusion92, so the Jacobian field vanishes
generically and hence on the entire reduced component.

The proof of [saturated base change94](nonconical-saturated-base-change-pass94.md)
applies with six replaced by four: its only numerical use of six
was equality of total multiplicity with the generic degree bound
\(2d\). For clarity, choose any complex linear combination U of
\(A_1,A_2,c\) nonconstant on both components. The total inequality
gives \(4\le2f_1+2f_2\le\deg_vU\le4\). Hence both residue
degrees are one and the generic degree is four.

The flat-rank saturation theorem93 then gives a finite free
rank-four semilocal normalization. Its intermediate actual base
change is already equal to it, because an integral localization
of a domain cannot invert a new element. Applying this for
every admissible U, the rational-function pole argument94
identifies both reduced components with affine lines in the
relative three-space. The integral-generator and CM arguments
of the same proof give
\[
\begin{gathered}
A/\mathfrak q_i=\mathbb C[t_i],\qquad
\bar A_{1,i},\bar A_{2,i},\bar c_i
\in\mathbb C+\mathbb Ct_i,\\
C\simeq\mathbb C[U]^4\text{ as a module for every admissible }U.
\end{gathered}
\tag{2}
\]
Distinct components give distinct lines because these three
functions generate C. This does not yet assert disjointness,
and the module isomorphism does not identify the algebra.

## 2. Suppose the two relative lines meet

Suppose their common point is p. Two distinct affine lines
have at most one common point, and their two tangent directions
at p are linearly independent. Translate the relative functions
and the base functions so that their values at p and m are zero.
Write \(f=s_1-s_1(m)\), \(g=s_2-s_2(m)\), allowing a constant
invertible base change when needed.

The actual fiber is a closed subscheme of the relative
three-space, since C is generated by the three relative
functions. Thus its two component tangent directions remain
independent in the tangent space of A. At a fixed point the
quotient rank is either zero or one; rank two would make the
Jacobian field nonzero.

We use the following elementary initial-derivation observation.
Let actual elements \(z_1,\ldots,z_e\) give regular parameters
at a fixed point, either in A or in an N-invariant quotient.
Suppose N raises maximal-ideal order by at least r>0 and has
nonzero homogeneous leading operator D of degree r. The
associated graded ring is a polynomial ring in the initial
parameters. If each actual \(z_i\) has finite N-degree, then
\[
D^{k_i}\operatorname{in}(z_i)=0
\quad\text{for suitable }k_i,\qquad
D\text{ is locally nilpotent on the polynomial graded ring}.
\tag{3}
\]
Successive leading operators compose, so the first statement
follows from the actual vanishing \(N^{k_i}z_i=0\).
A derivation nilpotent on each of finitely many algebra
generators is locally nilpotent on their polynomial ring,
by the iterated product rule. This proves the second statement.
No claim about inverses or infinite series being N-nilpotent
enters the argument.

Regular completions below use the given complex coefficient
field and actual regular parameters, as in Stacks 0C0S(2).[^complete]
The derivations extend continuously because their order shift
is positive.

## 3. Rank zero gives coprime quadratic initials

Assume both base differentials vanish at p. Since A is
generated by the base and the three relative functions,
the differentials of \(A_1,A_2,c\) form a basis at p.
Their centered versions are therefore a regular parameter
system for \(A_p\).

Choose an admissible constant linear projection U with U(p)=0.
It is a regular parameter at p. By (2), C/(U) has complex
dimension four. On each of the two reduced lines U has exactly
one zero, namely p, so this entire finite fiber is supported
at p. Thus, in the regular local surface \(R=A_p/(U)\),
\[
f,g\in\mathfrak n_R^2,\qquad
\operatorname{length}R/(f,g)=4.
\tag{4}
\]
Completion preserves this finite length. In a two-variable
regular power-series ring, (4) forces the two degree-two
initial forms to be linearly independent and coprime.
Here is a direct proof.

The quotient by the maximal-ideal square already has length
three. Its square therefore has length one in \(R/(f,g)\),
and its cube is zero by Nakayama. The image of (f,g) modulo
the cube has dimension two inside the three-dimensional
space of quadrics. Hence f and g have independent quadratic
initials. If those initials shared a linear factor, their
linear multiples would span at most three dimensions of the
four-dimensional cubic space. An element af+bg of order at
least three must have both a,b in the maximal ideal, since
the quadratic initials are independent. No other cubic
initial can therefore fill the missing dimension. This
contradicts inclusion of the whole cube in (f,g).

Let F,G be the full three-variable quadratic initials of f,g
in the relative regular parameters. Their restrictions to
U=0 are the coprime quadrics just proved. Consequently F,G
are nonzero, independent and have no common factor: a
nonconstant common homogeneous factor would either restrict
to such a factor or make both restrictions zero.

## 4. The fixed lines determine the quadratic pencil

Use constant linear relative coordinates P,Q,Z such that the
two component lines are the Q-axis and the Z-axis. Because
these are the actual relative lines, their ideals in the
completed regular local ring are (P,Z) and (P,Q), respectively.
One can check the ideal equality by quotienting by either
pair of independent parameters: the smooth line quotient
has dimension one and admits its remaining parameter.

Both f and g vanish on both lines. Their quadratic initials
therefore have the form
\[
\binom FG
=aP^2+bPQ+cPZ+dQZ,\qquad
a,b,c,d\in\mathbb C^2,\qquad \gcd(F,G)=1.
\tag{5}
\]
The original canonical Jacobian formula in these local
coordinates is \(N=\epsilon\operatorname{Jac}(f,g,-)\)
with \(\epsilon\) a unit. Its leading operator is
\(D=\epsilon(p)\operatorname{Jac}(F,G,-)\).
It has degree shift one and is nonzero. By (1) and (3), it
is an LND of \(\mathbb C[P,Q,Z]\).

Moreover D vanishes on both axes. N vanishes on each actual
fixed component, so its values on the three parameters lie
in both homogeneous line ideals in the completion. Every
homogeneous component, including the quadratic initial one,
lies in these ideals too. This avoids assuming that generic
multiplicities automatically pass to the tangent cone.

Evaluating the Jacobian on the two axes gives
\(\det(b,d)=\det(c,d)=0\). If d=0, both quadrics have the
common factor P, impossible. Thus a constant base change
makes one member of the pencil a nonzero scalar multiple
of P squared. After scaling, the other is
\(QZ+P(\alpha P+\beta Q+\gamma Z)\).
The constant linear substitutions
\(Q'=Q+\gamma P,\ Z'=Z+\beta P\), followed by subtraction
of a multiple of \(P^2\), put the pencil into
\[
(P^2,Q'Z'),\qquad
D=\lambda P(Q'\partial_{Q'}-Z'\partial_{Z'}),
\qquad \lambda\in\mathbb C^*.
\tag{6}
\]
But \(D^kQ'=\lambda^kP^kQ'\ne0\) for every k. This contradicts
(3), excluding rank zero at the supposed intersection.

## 5. Rank one gives the same obstruction on a surface

Suppose instead that df(p) is nonzero after a constant
base change. The invariant quotient \(R=A_p/(f)\) is a
regular local surface. Both component curves lie in it and
their tangent directions are independent.

Choose constant linear combinations y,z of the relative
functions so that y vanishes on the first line and z on
the second, while each is nonconstant on the other. Their
differentials form a parameter basis in R. In its completion
\(\mathbb C[[y,z]]\), the two component ideals are exactly
(y) and (z). The divisor of the second base parameter is
therefore
\[
\bar g=e(y,z)y^2z^2,\quad e\in\mathbb C[[y,z]]^*,\qquad
D_0=\lambda yz(y\partial_y-z\partial_z),\quad\lambda\ne0.
\tag{7}
\]
Here is a local length argument for the unit factor, without
assuming anything about unspecified formal branches. The two
smooth component primes in R are (y) and (z), and their DVR
orders of g are the given multiplicities two. The powers of
these principal parameter primes are primary, so contraction
from their DVR localizations gives \(g=y^2z^2h\) in R.
For example primarity of \((y)^j\) follows by taking the
least y-order and using the domain \(R/(y)\).

Choose a common admissible linear projection U through p.
It is transverse to both lines, so R/(U) is a DVR in which
y and z each have order one. Equation (2) gives
\(\operatorname{length}R/(g,U)=4\), concentrated at p.
If h were a nonunit, its image in this DVR would have positive
order or be zero, making that length greater than four or
infinite. Thus h is a unit already in R. Its completed image
is the e appearing in (7).

The canonical Jacobian formula in the coordinates f,y,z
descends modulo f to a unit times the Hamiltonian derivation
of \(\bar g\). Its leading operator is the displayed D0,
of degree shift two. The actual y,z are constant combinations
of \(A_1,A_2,c\), so they retain finite N-degree before
localization and after quotienting. Equation (3) therefore
makes D0 locally nilpotent on \(\mathbb C[y,z]\).

This is impossible: \(D_0(yz)=0\) and
\(D_0^ky=\lambda^k(yz)^ky\ne0\) for every k. Rank one is
excluded as well. The two reduced components in (1) are disjoint.

## 6. Consequence for the collapsed double component

The [polynomial column classification94](nonconical-conormal-column-classification-pass94.md)
shows that a collapsed quadratic double component with
nonzero first normal action has column span either
\(\{1,c\}\) or \(\{t,t^k\}\), k at least two. The latter
has a quotient-rank-zero point and forces a (2,2) fiber
with a fixed companion meeting it.

The preceding contradiction rules out that alternative. Thus
\[
\boxed{\quad
\mu=2,\ \text{collapsed},\
\text{nonzero first normal action}
\ \Longrightarrow\
\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb C}\{\ell_1,\ell_2\}
=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb C}\{1,c\}.
\quad}
\tag{8}
\]
In this sector the quotient differential has rank one at
every point of the component.

### The remaining weighted coefficients are constant

Normalize the surviving column by a constant base change to
\((\ell_1,\ell_2)=(1,c)\), retaining the good normal centers
F,G of the column proof. Put x equal to the first centered
base parameter and y=G. The weighted construction94 has
\(\alpha=c\), \(\epsilon_0=\nu\in\mathbb C^*\), and
\(k_1=\epsilon_0\alpha'/a\in\mathbb C^*\). Therefore
a is already a nonzero complex constant; no new global
reducedness argument is needed.

The first base relation is \(x\equiv F\pmod{\mathfrak q^2}\).
For weights (2,1) on x,y, the actual F has weight-two part
\(F_*=x+b(c)y^2\), with b rational in c; all remaining terms
have strictly greater weight. Let
\(\delta=-x\partial_y+2ay\partial_c\), so \(D_*=\nu\delta\).
The actual identity \(N^3F=0\) implies
\[
\begin{gathered}
\delta^3\bigl(b(c)y^2\bigr)
=24ab'x^2y-36a^2b''xy^3+8a^3b'''y^5=0,\\
\boxed{\quad a\in\mathbb C^*,\quad b\in\mathbb C,\quad
F_*=x+by^2,\quad G_*=y,\quad
D_*=\nu(-x\partial_y+2ay\partial_c).\quad}
\end{gathered}
\tag{9}
\]
The coefficient of \(x^2y\) forces \(b'=0\). For direct
verification, the second derivative is
\(2bx^2-10ab'xy^2+4a^2b''y^4\), whose derivative gives
the first line. This is a constant-coefficient weighted
model, not a change of coordinates on the full A. In
particular \(N^2G=0\) has not been proved.

## 7. Remaining scope and finite controls

The conclusion does not exclude the
component itself, the first-normal-action-zero sector,
higher multiplicity components, or disjoint double fibers.
It supplies no reduction from an arbitrary sliced action
to the actual frame.

The written arguments above prove the parameter, length,
initial-operator and pencil gates. Finite symbolic controls
in the [standalone checker](double_component_initial_checks_pass95.py)
check the two displayed nonnilpotent operators and (9), but
cannot substitute for those geometric gates. Historical
novelty, human expert review and formal verification remain
outstanding. Pass94 and earlier files are unchanged.
