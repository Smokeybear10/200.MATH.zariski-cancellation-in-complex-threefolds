# Finite all-LND Lie algebras: the transitive recognition criterion

Date: 2026-09-13.

The strengthened hypothesis is sufficient: if a finite-dimensional
Lie algebra of locally nilpotent derivations of C^[4] contains a
sliced derivation delta and has only constant common rational
invariants, then ker delta is C^[3]. The structural steps are already
published. This note checks their exact hypotheses, supplies a finite
integration reconstruction, and keeps the missing existence statement
explicit.

Without the rational-transitivity condition, the one-dimensional
Lie algebra C delta satisfies every remaining hypothesis for every
cylinder A[s]=C^[4]. It adds no information to cancellation. With
transitivity included, existence of such a Lie algebra is equivalent
to polynomiality of A, not a property proved here for every unknown A.
No novelty, expert-review, or Lean-verification claim is made.

## 1. The precise conditional theorem

Let

\[
B=A[s]\simeq\mathbb C^{[4]},\qquad
\delta(s)=1,\quad \delta|_A=0,
\tag{1}
\]

and let g be a finite-dimensional complex Lie subalgebra of Der(B).
Assume

\[
\delta\in\mathfrak g,\qquad
\mathfrak g\subseteq\operatorname{LND}(B),\qquad
\operatorname{Frac}(B)^{\mathfrak g}=\mathbb C.
\tag{2}
\]

The middle condition concerns every linear combination and bracket
in g, not merely a displayed list of generators. Then

\[
A=\ker\delta\simeq\mathbb C^{[3]}.
\tag{3}
\]

In particular there exists an ambient LND V with V(s)=0 and a
polynomial slice in A. The proof does not require V to belong to g,
or require the subgroup generated by delta to be normal in the group
integrating g.

An equivalent geometric replacement for the last condition in (2)
is generic tangent rank four of g. Once integration is established,
either condition says that its group has a dense orbit.

## 2. Integration is a genuine algebraic action

Kraft and Zaidenberg's Theorem 3.1.1(1) states that a
finite-dimensional Lie algebra of vector fields generated by locally
nilpotent fields on an affine variety is the Lie algebra of a
connected algebraic automorphism group. Apply it to any basis of g.
The stronger all-members condition in (2) will make this group
unipotent, not just algebraic.[^kz]

Here is a finite reconstruction of the needed assertion, to make
clear that formal exponentials are not being substituted for an
algebraic action. Choose a basis D_1,...,D_m of g. For b in B,
the PBW theorem spans the module U(g)b by the ordered words

\[
D_1^{e_1}D_2^{e_2}\cdots D_m^{e_m}b,
\qquad e_i\geq0.
\tag{4}
\]

There are only finitely many nonzero values of these words. Start
at the right: powers of D_m on b give a finite set. Powers of
D_(m-1) on that finite set again give a finite set, and repeat.
Local nilpotence is used on a finite collection at each stage,
so no uniform nilpotence bound on all of B is assumed. The resulting
span is stable under g because it is U(g)b.

Take the sum of these modules for 1 and finitely many algebra
generators of B. This yields a finite-dimensional stable subspace W
generating B as an algebra. The representation

\[
\mathfrak g\longrightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(W)
\tag{5}
\]

is faithful. Every member is nilpotent on W by (2). Engel's theorem
therefore supplies a basis of W in which all matrices are strictly
upper triangular. In particular g is nilpotent. This agrees with
the more general published nilpotence statement of Petravchuk and
Sysak, Theorem 2.5, whose hypotheses allow any integral domain.[^ps]

On strictly upper triangular matrices, exp and log are inverse
polynomial maps and the Baker--Campbell--Hausdorff expression is
finite. Thus exp(g) is a closed unipotent algebraic subgroup of
the upper unitriangular group, with Lie algebra g. These are the
characteristic-zero exponential/BCH facts in Milne, Proposition
14.32 and Theorem 14.37.[^milne]

For D in g the matrix exp(D) is the restriction to W of the actual
polynomial automorphism exp(D) of B. The finite matrix formula on
W shows that these automorphisms depend algebraically on D;
because W generates B, it gives a regular action on Spec(B).
Faithfulness on W identifies its image with the unipotent group

\[
G=\exp(\mathfrak g)\subseteq\operatorname{Aut}(\operatorname{Spec}B).
\tag{6}
\]

This proves the integration needed here without relying on an
unbounded formal series or a general assertion about solvable
finitely generated Lie algebras.

### 2.1. An equivalent finite solvability formulation

Instead of checking that every member of a finite-dimensional g is
an LND, one can assume that g is solvable and is generated as a Lie
algebra by finitely many LNDs. Kraft and Zaidenberg's Theorem
3.1.1(1) first integrates g to an algebraic group. Its action on B
is locally finite: each polynomial lies in a finite-dimensional
stable vector space. Their Corollary 3.1.4 then applies to give the
all-members-LND condition. Its statement and the relevant first
part of the preceding proof were read directly.[^kz]

This implication also has a short matrix reconstruction. Use local
finiteness to place algebra generators of B in one finite-dimensional
stable space. Lie's theorem triangularizes the solvable matrix Lie
algebra there. Its LND generators have zero diagonal, so the Lie
algebra they generate consists of strictly upper triangular matrices.
An element nilpotent on this stable space is locally nilpotent on
the algebra it generates, by the Leibniz rule. Conversely the finite
all-LND condition implies nilpotence, hence solvability, by Section 2.
Thus the two formulations are equivalent here.

Finite dimension of a Lie algebra and local finiteness of its
action are distinct hypotheses. For example, the one-dimensional
Lie algebra spanned by x^2 partial_x is finite-dimensional but does
not act locally finitely on C[x]. In the alternative formulation,
local finiteness follows from the integration theorem and the LND
generators, not from finite dimension alone. Nor does this argument
say that an arbitrary finitely generated solvable Lie algebra is
finite-dimensional. The finite-dimensional closure remains an
explicit requirement.

## 3. Common rational invariants give a transitive group action

A rational function is killed by an LND if and only if its
corresponding additive one-parameter group fixes it. Consequently

\[
\operatorname{Frac}(B)^G
=\operatorname{Frac}(B)^{\mathfrak g}
=\mathbb C.
\tag{7}
\]

Rosenlicht's orbit-dimension theorem now gives a dense G-orbit.
The exact version used identifies the transcendence degree of the
rational invariant field with the minimum codimension of an orbit;
it is stated for an arbitrary algebraic group and irreducible
variety in Brundan's Lemma 1.1, not only for reductive groups.[^ros]

Unipotent-group orbits on an affine variety are closed by the
Kostant--Rosenlicht theorem. Thus the dense orbit is all of
Spec(B).[^milne] Equivalently, if the generic tangent rank of g
is four, an orbit already has dimension four and the same conclusion
follows. No assertion about an individual additive subgroup's
normality follows from this transitivity.

## 4. Triangularization is an exact published theorem

Kraft and Zaidenberg, Theorem 5.2.2, proves that a nested unipotent
subgroup of Aut(A^n) with a dense orbit is transitive and conjugate
into the unipotent de Jonquieres subgroup. An algebraic unipotent
group is a special case of a nested one. Applying that theorem to
(6)--(7) gives actual polynomial coordinates x_1,...,x_4 in which
every member of g is strictly triangular:

\[
D(x_i)\in\mathbb C[x_1,\ldots,x_{i-1}]
\quad(D\in\mathfrak g),
\tag{8}
\]

where D(x_1) is a constant. The theorem's proof was read, including
its stabilizer-normalizer step and affine additive-torsor
trivialization.[^kz] It is a polynomial-coordinate conclusion, not
just triangular matrices on the larger auxiliary space W of Section 2.

To delimit the subgroup issue in that proof, if H is the stabilizer
of a point in a unipotent homogeneous space G/H, one enlarges H
inside its normalizer. For an enlargement H normal in K with
K/H isomorphic to Ga, right multiplication by K/H on G/H is well
defined and commutes with left G. The map G/H -> G/K is its torsor.
Successive affine-base torsor trivializations provide the coordinate
tower used in (8). This uses normality of the successive stabilizers
in their enlargements. It does not replace the prescribed subgroup
exp(C delta) by a normal subgroup or identify its quotient with G/H.

The finite-dimensional integration and this dense-orbit
triangularization are therefore inherited facts with independent
premise checks, not new general Lie-theoretic lemmas.

## 5. The given slice supplies properness, then recognition

The action generated by delta is already translation on the s factor
of Spec(A) times A1. If q denotes projection to Spec(A), its action
map identifies Ga times Spec(B) with

\[
\operatorname{Spec}(B)\times_{\operatorname{Spec}(A)}
\operatorname{Spec}(B).
\tag{9}
\]

The latter is closed in Spec(B) times Spec(B) because Spec(A)
is separated. Thus the action map is a closed immersion and the
action is proper. This argument uses the actual slice (1), not
only absence of fixed points.

By (8), this same action is triangular in actual coordinates on A4.
Dubouloz, Finston and Jaradat's final corollary on printed p. 1963
states that every proper triangular additive action on affine
four-space over a characteristic-zero field is a translation.[^dfj]
All of its hypotheses are now met, proving (3).

If delta(x_1) is a nonzero constant in (8), one can instead conclude
directly: the triangular slice projection using x_1/delta(x_1)
gives invariant coordinates by a triangular polynomial change.
If delta(x_1)=0, the action fixes an ambient coordinate and is
precisely the triangular relative three-space case underlying the
cited corollary. Neither case invokes a Jacobian conjecture.

Finally, write A=C[q_1,q_2,q_3]. Equation (1) then gives actual
coordinates q_1,q_2,q_3,s of B. The derivation partial_(q_1) in
these coordinates is tangent to s=0, commutes with delta and has
slice q_1. This is an existence consequence of recognition. It is
not a claim that a prescribed central element or highest bracket
of g already has these properties.

## 6. What this criterion does and does not add

For a fixed cylinder (1), the following statements are equivalent:

1. A is a three-variable polynomial ring.
2. There exists a finite-dimensional all-LND Lie algebra containing
   delta with trivial common rational invariant field.
3. There exists a finite-dimensional solvable Lie algebra generated
   by finitely many LNDs, containing delta and having generic tangent
   rank four.

The implication 2 implies 1 is Sections 2--5. For 1 implies 2,
take the four commuting coordinate partials in q_1,q_2,q_3,s.
Their complex linear span is an abelian all-LND Lie algebra,
contains delta, and has common rational invariant field C.
The equivalence with statement 3 follows from Section 2.1 and the
rational-transitivity equivalence in Section 3. Since delta is an
LND, it may be added to the displayed LND generating list without
changing the Lie algebra.

This is a recognition criterion and a precise remaining quantifier.
It does not construct the Lie algebra in statement 2 from an
arbitrary initial ambient frame. Nor does finite-dimensional closure
of LND generators alone imply that every member of their closure
is locally nilpotent. One must retain the all-members condition or
a hypothesis, such as solvability in Section 2.1, that actually
implies it before using the integration-to-unipotence step.

The [pass-27 primitive-top control](primitive-top-slice-pass27.md)
already excludes concluding that a particular highest primitive
bracket is sliced from finite nilpotent closure. That control does
not contradict this theorem: the present conclusion comes from
the whole transitive action and subsequent recognition, not from
that selected field.

No new numerical checker was added. The task is a source-dependent
general theorem implication, with a finite PBW/matrix proof and
an exact geometric hypothesis audit; additional examples would
not certify its universal steps. A separate algebraic audit checked
the finite stable-module construction and the stabilizer-normalizer
right-action mechanism without finding a gap. General cancellation
remains unresolved because the existence assertion in statement 2
has not been established for every cylinder.
