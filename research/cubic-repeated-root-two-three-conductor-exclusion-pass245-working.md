# The quadratic first and cubic second projections are impossible

In the supplied actual chart \(q=\rho^2-t^3\), the projected degree
pair \((2,3)\) is impossible. The exact parity conductor and the
whole-line return constrain the two cubic-center factors. The
evaluated cofactor then forces a return root at the quadratic vertex,
with multiplicity three, contrary to the actual gradient and scalar
determinant identities.

All plane and parameter changes in this note are over the exact
coefficient field and are used only for this calculation. None is
asserted to be an original ambient coordinate change.

## 1. Actual assumptions and the local root test

Assume the globally sliced polynomial four-ring, supplied exact1122
coordinate frame and entire initial kernel of
[rebuilt237](repeated-root-rebuilt-chart-line-exclusion-pass237-working.md):
\[
\begin{gathered}
\mathscr B=\mathbb C[X,Y,Z,W]=\mathscr A[u],\qquad E=\partial_u,\qquad
\deg_E(X,Y,Z,W)=(1,1,2,2),\\
\ker\bigl((\mathsf X,\mathsf Y,\mathsf Z,\mathsf W)
 \mapsto(a_0\tau,b_0\tau,e_0\tau^2,h_0\tau^2)\bigr)
 =(\mathsf X^2\mathsf Z-\mathsf W^2+\mathsf X\mathsf Y^3),\\
a_0=EX,\quad b_0=EY,\quad e_0=E^2Z/2,\quad h_0=E^2W/2 .
\end{gathered}                                                     \tag{1}
\]
The variable \(\tau\) is independent. The literal selected \(Z\)
pivot has been rebuilt as in237. Its exact coefficient field and
line ring satisfy
\[
\begin{gathered}
L=\operatorname{Frac}(\ker_{\mathscr A}N),\qquad
\mathscr A_L=L[v],\quad Nv=1,\quad
[L:\mathbb C(t,\rho)]<\infty,\\
q=\rho^2-t^3,\qquad t,\rho\text{ algebraically independent},\\
Y=y+tX,\quad Z=z+\sigma X+qX^2,\quad W=w+\theta X+\rho X^2,\\
L[y,z,w]=L[v],\quad J_0=-a_0^3\in L^*,\qquad
\deg_vy=2,\quad\deg_v\theta=3 .
\end{gathered}                                                     \tag{2}
\]
The extended coefficient partials commute and fix \(v\).
The letters \(z,w,y,\theta\) in (2) are the actual projected entries.

Put \(\mathcal R=2V+k_\rho\), \(R=\mathcal R(y,\theta)\),
\(\mathcal C=L[y,\theta]\), and \(\mathcal O=L[v]\).
The complete potentials and centered polynomial of
[239](radical-minus-polynomial-centered-eliminant-pass239-working.md)
give
\[
\begin{gathered}
R\ne0,\quad
\mathcal O=\mathcal C+\mathcal Cw,\quad
R\mathcal O\subset\mathcal C,\quad
\operatorname{Frac}\mathcal C=L(v),\\
R\widetilde w=-p(y),\quad
\widetilde w=w-D(y,\theta),\quad D\in L[U,V],\quad\deg D\le1,\\
p(U)=-U^3+\text{terms of degree at most two},\\
\mathcal H(y,\theta)=0,\quad \deg\mathcal H=6,\quad\mathcal H_6=U^6,
\quad \mathcal H(U,-k_\rho/2)=p(U)^2 .
\end{gathered}                                                     \tag{3}
\]
In particular \(\mathcal O=\mathcal C+\mathcal C\widetilde w\)
too. The irreducible plane relation \(G\) has degree three, by the
birational polynomial-degree argument used in
[frontier240](cubic-repeated-root-projection-frontier-pass240-working.md).
The actual eliminant has a simple such factor and
\[
\mathcal H=GQ_1,\qquad
0\ne q_1:=Q_1(y,\theta),\qquad q_1\mid R .
\tag{4}
\]
The last assertion is the \(i\le2\) bound of
[238](repeated-root-return-excess-order-bound-pass238-working.md),
not a conclusion from birationality alone.

We recall the precise actual root test needed below. Write
\[
\begin{gathered}
B_1=f_t(y,\theta),\qquad C_1=f_\rho(y,\theta)+2w,\\
\mathcal H_V(y,\theta)=q_1G_V(y,\theta)
                    =-R(C_1^2+R\Lambda_\rho),\\
Rw_v=-B_1y_v-C_1\theta_v,\qquad R_v=2\theta_v,\\
J_0=[dy\wedge(R\,d\theta+B_1dt+C_1d\rho)\wedge dw]
                                        _{dt\wedge d\rho\wedge dv}.
\end{gathered}                                                     \tag{5}
\]
All coefficient derivatives in (5) are formed before evaluation.
At a root \(r\) of \(R\) where \(C_1(r)=0\), nonzero \(J_0\)
forces \(B_1(r)\ne0\). If \(m=\operatorname{ord}_rR\), then
\(\operatorname{ord}_r\theta_v=m-1\). Both \(Rw_v\) and
\(C_1\theta_v\) have order at least \(m\), so (5) implies
\[
C_1(r)=0,\ R(r)=0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \operatorname{ord}_r y_v\ge m .
\tag{6}
\]
These orders may be taken over an algebraic closure of \(L\).
No coefficient derivative is applied to a moving root.

## 2. Exact parity conductor and the cubic center quotient

Complete the square in the quadratic polynomial \(y(v)\) using
only a translation over \(L\):
\[
x=v+\xi_0,\quad x_v=1,\qquad y=a_2x^2+b_2,\quad a_2\in L^* .
\tag{7}
\]
Thus \(y_v=2a_2x\). We never replace the coefficient partials
fixing \(v\) by partials fixing \(x\). For formal plane calculations
put \(u=(U-b_2)/a_2\); on evaluation it equals \(x^2\).
Because \(R\) has degree three, write its even and odd parts as
\[
R=E(x^2)+xA(x^2),\qquad
A,E\in L[u],\quad \deg A=1,\quad\deg E\le1 .
\tag{8}
\]
The ring generated by \(y,\theta\) is therefore
\[
\mathcal C=L[x^2,xA(x^2)]
 =L[u]\oplus xA(u)L[u]\subset
 \mathcal O=L[u]\oplus xL[u].
\tag{9}
\]
The decomposition is direct by parity.

Its conductor inside \(\mathcal O\) is exactly
\(A(x^2)\mathcal O\). Indeed, for \(f=e(u)+xg(u)\),
membership \(f\in\mathcal C\) means \(A\mid g\);
membership \(xf\in\mathcal C\) means \(A\mid e\).
Together these are equivalent to \(f\mathcal O\subset\mathcal C\),
since \(\mathcal O\) is generated by \(1,x\) over \(L[u]\).
The inclusion \(R\mathcal O\subset\mathcal C\) in (3) thus forces
\(A\mid E\). Degree comparison gives
\[
E=sA,\quad s\in L,\qquad R=(x+s)A(x^2).
\tag{10}
\]

Since \(p(a_2x^2+b_2)\) is even and divisible by \(R\), there is
a polynomial \(Q\in L[u]\), of degree one, such that
\[
p(a_2u+b_2)=(u-s^2)A(u)Q(u),\qquad
\widetilde w=-(x-s)Q(x^2).
\tag{11}
\]
Here is a multiplicity check that includes the overlapping cases.
At nonzero \(s\), divisibility by both \(R(x)\) and \(R(-x)\)
forces the maximum of their multiplicities at each pair of opposite
roots. If \(A(s^2)=0\), at each of \(s,-s\) one of the two
polynomials has multiplicity two, giving the squared factor
\((u-s^2)^2\) required in (11). If \(s=0\) and \(A(0)=0\),
\(R\) has order three at zero, while the even polynomial on the
left has even order, hence order at least four. This again supplies
the factor \(u^2\) required in (11).
If neither overlap occurs, ordinary division by the two opposite
linear factors gives (11). These arguments can be made over an
algebraic closure; division by the displayed polynomial over
\(L[u]\) then descends the quotient to \(L\).
The degree is one because \(p(a_2u+b_2)\) has degree three.

The whole-line equality in (3) supplies an additional condition:
\[
\gcd(A,Q)=1\quad\text{in }L[u].
\tag{12}
\]
To see this, use (9) and (11) to compute the odd part of
\(\mathcal C+\mathcal C\widetilde w\).
Its coefficients of \(x\) form the ideal
\((A,Q)\subset L[u]\); the even part already contains \(L[u]\).
Equality with \(\mathcal O=L[u]\oplus xL[u]\) requires that
ideal to be all of \(L[u]\). No plane-normality hypothesis is
being substituted for the actual whole-line equality.

## 3. The exact formal relation and evaluated cofactor

Use the following normalization of the plane relation:
\[
G(U,V)=\{\mathcal R-sA(u)\}^2-uA(u)^2 .
\tag{13}
\]
It evaluates to zero by (10). It is irreducible: over \(L(u)\),
the formal variable \(\mathcal R-sA(u)\) would have to equal
\(\pm A(u)\sqrt u\), and \(u\) is not a square in \(L(u)\).
It is primitive over \(L[u]\), with leading coefficient one in
\(\mathcal R\), so Gauss's lemma applies. Thus (13) generates
the full evaluation kernel. Its total degree is three, with a
nonzero pure \(U^3\) leading term.

At \(\mathcal R=0\), formulas (3), (11), and (13) give
\[
G(U,-k_\rho/2)=-(u-s^2)A(u)^2,\qquad
Q_1(U,-k_\rho/2)=-(u-s^2)Q(u)^2 .
\tag{14}
\]
The second identity follows by polynomial cancellation from
\(\mathcal H=GQ_1\) and \(\mathcal H(U,-k_\rho/2)=p(U)^2\).

Both \(G\) and \(Q_1\) have degree three and pure \(U^3\)
top, since their product has top \(U^6\). Consequently
subtracting the second expression in (14) from \(Q_1\) removes
its degree-three part. The difference has degree at most two,
and vanishes on the line \(\mathcal R=0\). Therefore
\[
Q_1=-(u-s^2)Q(u)^2+\mathcal R L_1(U,V),
\qquad L_1\in L[U,V],\quad\deg L_1\le1 .
\tag{15}
\]
This is a formal polynomial statement, not only an equality after
evaluation.

Set
\[
b(x)=-(x-s)Q(x^2)^2+A(x^2)L_1(y,\theta).
\]
Evaluation of (15) and differentiation of (13) give the exact
identities
\[
q_1=(x+s)b(x),\qquad
G_V(y,\theta)=4xA(x^2),\qquad
\frac{\mathcal H_V(y,\theta)}R=4x\,b(x).
\tag{16}
\]
The derivative \(G_V\) holds \(U\) and every coefficient fixed,
so it uses \(\mathcal R_V=2\). No coefficient-field derivative
or moving-parameter correction enters that formal derivative.
By (4), cancellation of \(x+s\) yields
\[
0\ne b(x)\mid A(x^2).
\tag{17}
\]

## 4. Every cofactor root forces the forbidden vertex multiplicity

The polynomial \(b\) is not constant. To prove this, let \(r\)
be a root of \(A(x^2)\) in an algebraic closure of \(L\).
If \(r\ne0\), equation (12) implies \(Q(r^2)\ne0\), and
\[
b(r)-b(-r)=-2r\,Q(r^2)^2\ne0.
\]
If \(r=0\), then \(A(u)\) is a nonzero multiple of \(u\);
the derivative at zero of its contribution to \(b\) is zero,
whereas
\[
b'(0)=-Q(0)^2\ne0.
\]
This proves nonconstancy in either case.

Choose a root \(r\) of \(b\). By (17), \(A(r^2)=0\).
Equation (12) then gives \(Q(r^2)\ne0\), and the definition of
\(b\) shows
\[
0=b(r)=-(r-s)Q(r^2)^2,\qquad\text{hence }r=s .
\tag{18}
\]
In particular \(A(s^2)=0\), \(R(s)=0\), and \(b(s)=0\).
The last identity in (16), compared with (5) and evaluated at
\(x=s\), implies \(C_1(s)=0\). The root test (6) implies
\(y_v(s)=0\). By (7), this forces \(s=0\).

Since \(A\) has degree one and \(A(s^2)=0\), it follows that
\[
A(u)=a_*u,\quad a_*\in L^*,\qquad
R=a_*x^3,\qquad C_1(0)=0 .
\tag{19}
\]
Equation (6) now requires
\(\operatorname{ord}_0y_v\ge3\). But \(y_v=2a_2x\) has order
one at zero. This contradiction excludes the degree pair
\((2,3)\) under the actual hypotheses (1)--(2).

## 5. Scope

The proof uses the exact parity conductor, the monic whole-line
return, the complete cubic center, the actual cofactor divisor
and the calibrated gradient identities. A formal center relation
or conductor inclusion without these other inputs is insufficient.
All roots are evaluated after taking the original derivatives;
the translation in (7) preserves \(x_v=1\) and does not change
that convention.

This is a separate exclusion within the cubic frontier240.
It does not settle every remaining degree pair, construct an
original invariant coordinate, or establish arbitrary-frame entry
or unrestricted cancellation. No earlier source, index or packet
was edited, and no saved checker was executed, imported or compiled.
The written argument is not a claim of historical novelty,
