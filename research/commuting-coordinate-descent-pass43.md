# Commuting actions with an invariant omitting one coordinate

Three independent commuting polynomial LNDs on the complex four-ring
have a common invariant coordinate if their common kernel contains a
nonconstant polynomial independent of one actual linear coordinate.
This is a conditional dimension-four criterion. It does not assume
the commuting derivations conjecture in dimension four.

## 1. Statement

Let \(B=\mathbb C[x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4]\), and let
\(D_1,D_2,D_3\) be commuting polynomial LNDs, independent over
\(\operatorname{Frac}B\). Suppose a nonconstant polynomial \(g\)
belongs to all three kernels, and suppose \(T\) is a nonzero constant
coordinate direction with
\[
Tg=0.
\tag{1}
\]
Then their joint kernel is generated by an actual ambient coordinate
\(p\). If \(D_1\) has a polynomial slice, its kernel is
\(\mathbb C[p]^{[2]}\).

The actual constant direction is essential to the proof. A merely
rational foliation annihilating \(g\) is not a substitute.

## 2. The common polynomial and its irreducible fibers

Maubach's Proposition 3.4 applies to the affine UFD \(B\), whose
units are exactly \(\mathbb C^*\). It gives
\[
\bigcap_{i=1}^3\ker D_i=\mathbb C[p],
\qquad p-\alpha\text{ irreducible in }B
\text{ for every }\alpha\in\mathbb C.
\tag{2}
\]
The preceding independence and transcendence-degree statements in
that primary source establish that this kernel has transcendence
degree one.[^maubach]

Write \(g=H(p)\), with \(H\in\mathbb C[t]\) nonconstant.
Equation (1) gives \(H'(p)Tp=0\). Characteristic zero and the
domain property imply \(Tp=0\). After a constant linear change,
write
\[
B=R[b],\qquad R=\mathbb C[z_1,z_2,z_3],\qquad
T=\partial_b,\qquad p\in R.
\tag{3}
\]
No claim that \(p\) is a coordinate has yet been used.

## 3. All but finitely many fibers are polynomial three-rings

Choose a nonzero three-by-three minor \(\Delta\) of the four-by-three
matrix \((D_i x_j)\). There are only finitely many
\(\alpha\in\mathbb C\) for which \(p-\alpha\) divides \(\Delta\):
the elements \(p-\alpha\) are pairwise nonassociate primes of the
UFD \(B\), and a nonzero polynomial has finitely many prime factors.

For every other \(\alpha\), the ring
\(B_\alpha=B/(p-\alpha)\) is a three-dimensional complex domain,
by (2). The induced fields are commuting LNDs. Their coefficient
minor remains nonzero, so they are independent over
\(\operatorname{Frac}B_\alpha\). Maubach's Proposition 3.2,
applied with \(n=3\), now gives
\[
B/(p-\alpha)\simeq\mathbb C^{[3]}.
\tag{4}
\]
This uses independence of three derivations on a three-dimensional
domain. It does not use the unknown dimension-four coordinate
conclusion of the commuting derivations conjecture.

## 4. Surface cancellation and the three-variable coordinate theorem

By (3), equation (4) says
\[
\bigl(R/(p-\alpha)\bigr)[b]\simeq\mathbb C^{[3]}
\tag{5}
\]
for all but finitely many \(\alpha\). The established cancellation
theorem for the complex affine plane gives
\(R/(p-\alpha)\simeq\mathbb C^{[2]}\).
This is cancellation in dimension two, not the dimension-three
target under investigation.[^surface]

Kaliman's theorem for polynomials of the actual complex three-ring
with general affine-plane fibers then makes \(p\) an actual
coordinate of \(R\), and hence of \(B\). The precise version used
is Maubach's Theorem 2.4, which requires the displayed conclusion for
all but finitely many complex fibers.[^maubach]

If \(D_1\) has a polynomial slice, its original kernel is regular.
The established fixed-coordinate regular-kernel theorem therefore
gives \(\ker D_1=\mathbb C[p]^{[2]}\).[^regular]

## 5. Application to the power-law repair

In the \(T_cg=0\) branch of
[the global normalizer construction](quadratic-heisenberg-repair-pass43.md),
the first eligible triple is \(E,V_3,V_4\), if independent.
The second is \(E,V,\mathcal R\), whose independence is equivalent
to independence of \(E,Y,V\).

Every member of either eligible triple is a global polynomial LND,
all members commute, and every member kills \(g\). The actual
constant direction \(T_c\) kills \(g\) as well. Thus either
rank-three condition recognizes the original kernel.

The conclusion need not follow if both triples have rank two.
Nor does this argument establish an omitted coordinate direction
for an arbitrary common invariant, a universal bounded frame, or
unrestricted complex threefold cancellation.
