# Closed-fiber multiplicity from the actual generic degree

Every component of a closed quotient fiber in the actual nonconical
constant-acceleration setup has generic multiplicity at most six.
If one of the two relative generators survives nonconstantly on
that component, the bound improves to three. A component where
both quotient differentials vanish has multiplicity at least four;
there both relative generators collapse to constants and the
reduced component is an affine line.

These are restricted statements about the supplied original frame.
They neither construct such a frame for an arbitrary cancellation
instance nor exclude all possible fixed curves.

## 1. Hypotheses and the degree bound

Use the actual rings and elements of
[cubic-relation90](nonconical-cubic-relation-pass90.md) and
[global-Jacobian91](nonconical-global-jacobian-field-pass91.md):
\[
\begin{gathered}
B=A[u]=\mathbb C[x_1,x_2,x_3,z],\qquad
S=\mathbb C[s_1,s_2]\subset A,\qquad K=\operatorname{Frac}S,\\
R=S[A_1,A_2],\qquad A=R[c],\qquad
A_K=K[v]=K[A_1,A_2],\\
1\le d=\deg F_0=\max(\deg_v A_1,\deg_v A_2)\le3,\\
N\in\operatorname{LND}(A),\quad\ker N=S,\quad N\text{ primitive}.
\end{gathered}
\tag{1}
\]
Here A is a smooth affine complex threefold. The map
\(\operatorname{Spec}A\to\operatorname{Spec}S\) is faithfully
flat with nonempty pure curve fibers. N is the exact global
Jacobian field, not an arbitrarily selected action. The degree
of a nonconstant polynomial is unchanged by changing the generic
coordinate v affinely over K. Constants have degree zero; a zero
polynomial satisfies all displayed upper degree bounds.

For completeness, the degree needed for the third generator c
comes from the retained original-frame formulas. Write
\(b=(Ex_1,Ex_2,Ex_3)^t\), \(q=E^2z\in\mathbb C^*\),
\(a=x_{\rm tr}-bu\), and \(r=z-qu^2/2\). Over K the moving
frame gives
\[
\begin{gathered}
a=\rho b+y_1b_{s_1}+y_2b_{s_2},\qquad
r=\chi-q\Psi(y_1,y_2),\qquad \deg_Y\Psi\le2,\\
(y_1,y_2)^t=L(A_1,A_2)^t,\quad L\in\operatorname{GL}_2(K),\\
h=-\ell a,\quad \ell b=1,\quad \ell\in S^3,\qquad
c=r+\frac q2h^2,\\
\deg_v a_i,\deg_v h\le d,\qquad
\deg_v r,\deg_v c\le2d.
\end{gathered}
\tag{2}
\]
The row \(\ell\) is the first row of the actual good-speed
completion, and \(h=u-\ell x_{\rm tr}\). Thus all elements
asserted to lie in A really do. The coefficients in the first
line lie in K and have v-degree zero. Equation (2) follows by
ordinary polynomial degree and does not assume a polynomial
coordinate change on the base. On \(A_K\), N is a nonzero
constant multiple of \(\partial_v\). Consequently these are
also N-degree bounds, if desired; no such reinterpretation is
needed below.

## 2. A surviving generator and the two local rings

Fix a closed point \(\mathfrak m=(s_1-a,s_2-b)\) of S and a
height-two prime \(\mathfrak q\) minimal over \(\mathfrak m A\).
It is the generic point of an entire reduced fiber component.
At least one of \(A_1,A_2,c\) has transcendental residue in
\(A/\mathfrak q\): this one-dimensional complex affine domain
is generated by their three residues, and three algebraic residues
would all be complex constants. Choose such a generator U and put
\[
\begin{gathered}
n=\deg_v U>0,\qquad
T=(S[U])_{\mathfrak m S[U]},\qquad
k_0=\mathbb C(\bar U),\qquad F=\operatorname{Frac}T=K(U),\\
O=A_{\mathfrak q},\qquad \mathfrak n=\mathfrak qO,
\qquad J=\mathfrak mO,\qquad
\mu=\operatorname{length}_O(O/J).
\end{gathered}
\tag{3}
\]
U is transcendental over K. Otherwise it is a constant polynomial
in \(K[v]\), so \(U\in A\cap K=S\), contrary to its surviving
residue. The intersection equality follows because N kills K and
has kernel S in A. Thus S[U] is an actual polynomial ring in three
variables. Transcendence of \(\bar U\) gives the exact contraction
\(\mathfrak q\cap S[U]=\mathfrak m S[U]\).

The induced map \(T\to O\) is therefore a local injection of
regular local rings of dimension two. Their residue fields are
\(k_0\) and \(\kappa(\mathfrak q)\). Minimality of
\(\mathfrak q\) over the fiber ideal makes J primary to
\(\mathfrak n\), so \(\dim O/J=0\). Miracle flatness applies
to this precise local map: the source is regular, the target is
Cohen--Macaulay, and \(2=2+0\). Hence O is flat over T.[^flat]
This does not assert that O is finite as a T-module.

The residue extension \(\kappa(\mathfrak q)/k_0\) is finite:
both are function fields of curves over \(\mathbb C\), and
\(\bar U\) is transcendental. A composition series of the
Artinian O-module O/J therefore gives
\[
\dim_{k_0}(O/J)=\mu[\kappa(\mathfrak q):k_0],\qquad
O\otimes_T F=K(v),\qquad [K(v):K(U)]=n.
\tag{4}
\]
For the second equality, first localize A over K to get K[v].
The nonconstant polynomial U makes K[v] finite over K[U], of
rank n. Inverting every nonzero element of K[U] gives the field
K(v). The additional nonzero denominators used to form O are
already invertible in that field.

## 3. Flat associated-graded independence

The following argument supplies the needed rank inequality even
though the flat local extension need not be finite. Set
\(r_0=\dim_{k_0}(O/J)\), choose a \(k_0\)-basis
\(\bar e_1,\ldots,\bar e_{r_0}\), and choose lifts \(e_i\in O\).
Flatness applied to each pair of powers of the maximal ideal of T
gives
\[
\operatorname{gr}_{J}(O)
\simeq
\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak m T}(T)
 \otimes_{k_0}(O/J).
\tag{5}
\]
The grading on the left is J-adic, not \(\mathfrak n\)-adic.
Indeed tensoring \(\mathfrak m_T^j/\mathfrak m_T^{j+1}\) with
the flat module O identifies it with \(J^j/J^{j+1}\), and this
tensor product factors through O/J.

Suppose the lifts satisfied a nonzero F-linear relation in K(v).
Clear denominators to obtain \(\sum_i b_i e_i=0\) with
\(b_i\in T\), not all zero. Choose the least
\(\mathfrak m T\)-adic order h of its nonzero coefficients.
These orders are finite, since a regular local ring is separated
for its maximal-ideal filtration.[^graded]
Taking the degree-h initial form in (5) gives
\(\sum_i\operatorname{in}_h(b_i)\otimes\bar e_i=0\).
Linear independence of the residue basis forces every initial
coefficient to vanish, contradicting the definition of h.
Thus the lifts are F-linearly independent, and (4) proves
\[
\boxed{\quad
\mu[\kappa(\mathfrak q):\mathbb C(\bar U)]
\le\deg_v U.
\quad}
\tag{6}
\]
In particular every closed-fiber component has \(\mu\le2d\le6\).
If either \(A_i\) has nonconstant residue, choosing it as U
improves this to \(\mu\le d\le3\).

No comparison of R with A on a whole closed fiber was used. Nor
was any chosen coefficient field silently substituted into O:
the residue vector space and the flat associated-graded tensor
product suffice.

## 4. Consequences for fixed components

By [fixed-scheme92](nonconical-fixed-scheme-structure-pass92.md),
every irreducible component of the fixed scheme is an entire
component of a closed quotient fiber. Its generic whole-fiber
multiplicity is at least two. That lower bound uses the primitive
Jacobian field's nonzero action on the generic Artinian fiber,
not merely the reduced curve. Equation (6) yields
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\bar A_i\notin\mathbb C\text{ for some }i:
&2\le\mu\le d,\quad
  \kappa(\mathfrak q)=\mathbb C(\bar A_i),\\[2pt]
\bar A_1,\bar A_2\in\mathbb C:
&A/\mathfrak q=\mathbb C[\bar c],\quad
 2\le\mu\le2d.
\end{array}
\tag{7}
\]
In the first line the field degree must be one: a degree of at
least two together with \(\mu\ge2\) would exceed d. This does
not identify the entire component ring with a polynomial ring.
In the second line it does identify the ring, since A is generated
over S by \(A_1,A_2,c\), and \(\bar c\) must be transcendental.

## 5. Rank-zero quotient differential

Suppose both \(ds_1\) and \(ds_2\) vanish in
\(\Omega_{A/\mathbb C}\otimes_A\kappa(\mathfrak q)\).
This is a statement in the ambient cotangent space. Their images
in \(\Omega_{\kappa(\mathfrak q)/\mathbb C}\) always vanish
at a closed-base fiber and would give no information.

At the generic point of a curve in a smooth complex threefold,
the conormal map
\(\mathfrak n/\mathfrak n^2\to
\Omega_{O/\mathbb C}\otimes_O\kappa(\mathfrak q)\)
is injective. One can see this by restricting to the smooth open
part of the reduced curve, where its embedding in the smooth
threefold is regular and the conormal sequence is exact on the
left. Its generic point lies in that open part in characteristic
zero. Thus \(s_1-a,s_2-b\in\mathfrak n^2\), or
\(J\subset\mathfrak n^2\).

In the regular local surface O, \(O/\mathfrak n^2\) has
length three. Hence \(\mu\ge3\). Equality would imply
\(J=\mathfrak n^2\), but J has two generators and
\(\mathfrak n^2\) requires three: its quotient by
\(\mathfrak n^3\) has basis the three quadratic monomials
in two regular parameters.[^graded] Therefore \(\mu\ge4\).
Combining with (6) excludes a surviving \(A_i\), proving
\[
\boxed{\begin{gathered}
\operatorname{rank}(ds_1,ds_2)_{\mathfrak q}=0
\ \Longrightarrow\
A/\mathfrak q=\mathbb C[\bar c],\qquad 4\le\mu\le2d;\\
d=2\ \Longrightarrow\ \mu=4,
\qquad d=3\ \Longrightarrow\ \mu\in\{4,5,6\}.
\end{gathered}}
\tag{8}
\]
The d=1 case is impossible under this rank-zero hypothesis and
is already recognized independently by the linear proof91.
Equation (8) holds at a generic fiber component with the stated
rank condition, whether or not that condition was first obtained
from the fixed scheme.

## 6. Limits and source record

The multiplicity \(\mu\) is the length of the whole fiber at
its generic component, not the length of the fixed scheme there.
The latter can grow without bound in the control family of
fixed-scheme92 even when the former is two or three. Local
complete intersections of lengths four, five and six do exist;
their existence alone realizes neither the actual original frame
nor a counterexample to cancellation.

Topological restrictions on retained exceptional curves require
their own hypotheses and proof. In particular an irreducible
whole reduced fiber is a stronger condition than one selected
irreducible fixed component. No such condition was used in (6)--(8).
No novelty, human verification or formal proof status is asserted.
