# A finite algebraic certificate for the full modified Derksen invariant

Date: September 18, 2026.

Cancellation remains UNSOLVED and novelty UNKNOWN. This note supplies an
explicit finite algebraic proof of the invariant calculation in673. Unlike
the conductor argument there, it does not require transitivity, a maximal
ideal argument, or the Nullstellensatz. It is not a polynomial-coordinate
construction and is not a cancellation proof.

## 1. Ring, operators, and target

Fix any nonconstant h in C[t], with degree e>=1, and use the adapted ring

    B=C[x,y,z,t,u]/(x²y+2xzu+z²+x+h(t)).

Set

    Y=y-u², Z=z+xu,
    A'=C[x,Y,Z,t]/(x²Y+Z²+x+h(t)), so B=A'[u].             (1)

The polynomial coordinate change and its inverse are literal, as in673.
Let H=HD*(B), the C-algebra generated by kernels of all LNDs with a slice.
We will prove u in H and therefore H=B, since A' is already such a kernel.

Put a=1+xy+2zu, c=zy-2au, m=zy+2au, and use the LNDs

    tau=partial_u-x partial_z+2u partial_y,
    delta_z=x² partial_z-2(z+xu) partial_y,
    delta_t=x² partial_t-h'(t) partial_y,

    E(x)=h'(t)m, E(y)=0, E(z)=-ayh'(t),
    E(t)=ac, E(u)=-y²h'(t)/2.                             (2)

The whole polynomial flow and local nilpotence of E, including a*c=0,
are proved in673. They are the only non-elementary prerequisite within
this note's argument. For clarity, that flow uses the polynomial

    J_s=sum_(j=1)^e h^(j)(t)(ac)^(j-1)s^j/j!,

and sends (x,y,z,t,u) to

    (x+mJ_s-ay²J_s², y, z-ayJ_s, t+acs, u-y²J_s/2).

The action comes from Poloni's cubic construction;673 extends it to h.
No priority is claimed for that input or for the present derived identity.
[Primary construction](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.14090v1).

## 2. Initial entire-ideal membership

Conjugation by any automorphism preserves sliced LNDs, so it preserves H.
For any LND D and f in H, interpolation of the finite polynomial exp(sD)f
at distinct complex s extracts D(f). Thus H is D-stable. This is a finite
linear-combination operation on sliced-kernel elements, not a limit.

In (1), tau is partial_u, so A'=ker tau is contained in H. The other two
old actions kill u and preserve A', with

    delta_z(Z)=x², delta_z(Y)=-2Z, delta_t(Y)=-h'(t).

For each integer j>=0, u^j delta_z and u^j delta_t are LNDs. Apply H-stability
to Z,Y in A'. We obtain x²u^j, Zu^j, h'(t)u^j in H. Every b in B is a finite
sum of coefficients in A' times powers of u. Since A' is contained in H,

    x²B is contained in H,
    ZB is contained in H,
    h'(t)B is contained in H.                             (3)

The image-replica method is the one in BGS Theorem 1. We do not assert that
H is an ideal of B; only the three displayed entire ideals lie inside it.
[Credited image-ideal method](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.08421v2).

## 3. A whole polynomial inverse identity

Let L=[tau,E]=tau E-E tau. This is a derivation and preserves H because
both tau and E do. We do NOT claim that L is locally nilpotent.

Since tau(a)=2z and tau(c)=1-3a,

    L(t)=K=2zc+a(1-3a).                                   (4)

Define the following whole polynomials:

    k=14u²-5y,
    J=-3y²+18yu²-20u⁴,
    T0=-14u+20xu(2u²-y)+2Z(y-10u²),
    R=-1/2-xk/4,
    A0=k²/4-RJ, B0=-RT0.                                 (5)

Here R is a polynomial, not an inverse introduced by localization. Direct
expansion in the AMBIENT C[x,y,z,t,u], without the hypersurface relation,
gives

    K=-2+xk+x²J+ZT0,
    1=RK+x²A0+ZB0.                                       (6)

Indeed R(-2+xk)=1-x²k²/4; the remaining terms cancel by (5). Identity (6)
is an explicit Bezout identity modulo the already controlled ideals in(3).
It replaces any use of radical membership or division by K.

## 4. Finite differentiation recursion

Write h_j=h^(j)(t). The chain rule and (4) give L(h_j)=K h_(j+1).
For ANY b in B the product rule and (6) yield the exact identity

    h_(j+1)*b
      =L(h_j*R*b)-h_j*L(R*b)
         +x²*A0*h_(j+1)*b+Z*B0*h_(j+1)*b.                (7)

Suppose h_j B is contained in H. Then h_j Rb belongs to H and hence so
does its L-image. Also h_j L(Rb) belongs to H by the entire-ideal premise.
The last two terms lie in H by (3). Thus (7) proves

    h_j B contained in H implies h_(j+1) B contained in H.

The start j=1 is (3). After e-1 steps, h_e=e! times the leading coefficient
of h is a nonzero complex scalar. Therefore B is contained in H and H=B.
When e=1, the initial ideal h'B already equals B and no recursion is needed.
Repeated roots of h or h' do not affect any step.

In particular, substitute b=u at the final step of (7). Recursively replacing
earlier h_j multiples gives a finite expression for u using the memberships
in(3), additions, scalar multiples, and the tau/E operations. Each such
operation can itself be expanded by finite parameter interpolation into
algebraic combinations of elements of sliced kernels. This is a constructive
finite certificate procedure, not a saved proof-assistant certificate or an
explicit list of algebraically independent coordinates.
