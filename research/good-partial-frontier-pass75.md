# Common-slice obstruction and the primitive quotient test

The one-good-partial investigation now has an explicit obstruction to
common-slice selection and a complete parameter-family calculation.
It also has a sharper remaining target: a finite ideal attached to the
primitive intrinsic action, which cannot be improved by changing the
supplied slice. That ideal has not been proved to be the unit ideal
under the actual nice-action hypotheses.

The unrestricted working question remains whether A[s] isomorphic to

## 1. A proposed common-slice shortcut is false

Suppose E is a nice sliced LND in an actual four-variable polynomial
frame and T is an actual coordinate partial with ad_E^2(T)=0.
Its derived commuting LND Q=[T,E] need not be nice in that frame
or kill the supplied slice. The first
[boundary control](nice-good-partial-boundary-pass75.md) establishes
both failures in the already retained de Bondt example.

The stronger [explicit obstruction](nice-common-slice-obstruction-pass75.md)
uses a=xz-y^2+w and conic velocity parameters u=a, v=a^2+1.
Its complete polynomial inverse identifies actual coordinates
(a,U,V,S) in which
\[
E=\partial_S,\qquad
Q=(1-a^2)\partial_V+2a\partial_S,\qquad
ES=1,\quad Q(V+aS/2)=1.
\tag{1}
\]
Thus both actions have global slices. Nevertheless every E-slice is
F=S+h(a,U,V), and QF has value two modulo a-1. No choice of F
can be fixed by this unchanged Q. The original partial T is the sole
constant good direction, not an arbitrary auxiliary action inserted
afterward. All coordinate inverses and these identities are displayed
in the linked proof.

The example is fully recognized: kerE=C[a,U,V]. It is not a
cancellation counterexample. It disproves a proposed inference even
with the good-partial and two-global-slice hypotheses retained.

## 2. Exact criteria throughout the conic family

The [family proof](conic-nice-pair-classification-pass75.md) replaces
u,v by any coprime pair in C[a]. A Bezout identity ru+sv=1
gives a determinant-one two-by-two matrix and its symmetric-square
coordinate transformation. Both inverse orders are explicit.
In those actual coordinates E=partial_S and
Q=W partial_V+beta partial_S, where
\[
\begin{aligned}
W&=vu'-uv',&\quad \beta&=2(ru'+sv'),\\
Q\text{ has a slice}&\Longleftrightarrow\gcd(u',v')=1,\\
\exists F:\ EF=1, QF=0
&\Longleftrightarrow W\mid\beta
\Longleftrightarrow\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb C}\{u,v\}
\cap\mathbb C^*\ne\varnothing.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
The slice criteria in (2) concern a nonconstant pair. If both
parameters are constant, Q=0; every E-slice is fixed by Q, and
Q itself has no slice. If the parameters are not coprime, E has
no slice. These boundaries are part of the proof, not exclusions
silently handled by the computations.

The last equivalence in (2) is an all-degree polynomial argument,
not a search over slice degrees. Whenever it holds, a common slice
can even be chosen linear in the original x,y,z coordinates.
The two individual global slices in (1) do not satisfy this criterion.

For a nonconstant coprime pair and any supplied F=S+h(a,U,V),
the repaired action is
\[
Q_F=Q-(QF)E=W(\partial_V-h_V\partial_S).
\tag{3}
\]
Dividing by W yields the actual coordinate partial in V while
holding (a,U,F) fixed. This is a positive repair for every such
nonconstant coprime member, including the obstructed pair (1).
It uses the displayed inverse coordinates and is not a theorem
about all primitive centralizers.

The [general adjustment proof](good-partial-slice-adjustment-scope-pass75.md)
already gives polynomial local nilpotence for Q-(Qs)E in any
actual cylinder A[s]. It also shows that its coordinate flow degree
can be arbitrarily high even when the original Q has quadratic flow.
The family calculation must not be read as preserving a short-flow
or nice hypothesis after an arbitrary supplied-slice adjustment.

## 3. The slice-independent remaining test

The [primitive quotient argument](primitive-good-quotient-scope-pass75.md)
keeps the original ring A and the nonzero intrinsic LND d=Q|A.
Write d=hD with D primitive. If J is the ideal generated by the
six original coordinate coefficients of E wedge Q, then
\[
J=h(D(A))B,\qquad
(J:h)=(D(A))B,
\qquad (J:h)=B\Longleftrightarrow D\text{ is fixed-point-free}.
\tag{4}
\]
Here h can be computed as a gcd of those six minors in B.
The colon in (4) removes exactly that common factor. Infinite
saturation by all powers of h can erase residual information and
is not the asserted test.

Both D, up to a constant, and its fixed-locus ideal are independent
of the chosen E-slice. Consequently a further search over supplied
slices cannot change whether (4) succeeds. For the conic family,
the explicit primitive repair makes the ideal the unit ideal.
The older primitive fixed-line control fails the test, but it does
not meet the present actual nice/good-partial hypotheses.

The known Kaliman--Saveliev free-action criterion recognizes A as
a polynomial three-ring if (4) succeeds: the original cylinder
already verifies the smoothness, contractibility and domination
hypotheses.[^ks] This sufficient criterion was already established
in [pass44](heisenberg-common-base-pass44.md). The new refinement
is the canonical primitive, slice-independent test, not a new
free-action recognition theorem.

What remains is to derive (4)'s unit ideal from the additional
nice and actual-good-partial equations, or find an example meeting
those same equations with a proper primitive ideal. Neither has
been achieved. An arbitrary commuting-field control cannot decide
this narrower question. Even a negative answer would eliminate
only this choice of auxiliary action, not all possible recognizing
frames or actions.

## 4. Other construction routes and unresolved scope

The [line-family and shear proof](nice-good-direction-construction-boundary-pass75.md)
gives an exact constant-good-direction test when an affine slice
is already available. It requires an actual polynomial inverse
for the line family. Nilpotence of its Jacobian does not substitute
for that inverse. Every single elementary shear of the retained
seed that preserves niceness retains a good direction. Two shears
with different targets cannot conceal a nonnice intermediate
frame if their final frame is nice. Longer sequences and arbitrary
nonlinear frame changes remain outside that argument.

The [triangular candidate obstruction](triangular-primitive-candidate-obstruction-pass75.md)
also eliminates a proposed primitive fixed-point example. Its chosen
T is an actual sliced LND and its derived intrinsic field has a fixed
line, but no frame can make this T a coordinate partial and E nice
simultaneously. The proof computes every T-invariant polynomial
annihilated by E squared, without a degree cutoff. Those polynomials
generate an algebra of transcendence degree at most two, whereas
three T-invariant coordinate generators would be required. Changing
the T-slice cannot repair this specific candidate.

The bounded attempt to prove Q^3(x_i)=0 for every nice action
with one actual good partial did not close. The conic family
satisfies it exactly, but no complete universal proof or actual
counterexample was obtained. The missing vanishing identity is
not promoted to a theorem or counted as an additional result.

The [pass74 frontier](nonlinear-frontier-pass74.md) therefore remains:
the arbitrary-degree nice residual may have no constant good
direction or exactly one, and an arbitrary sliced action has not
been put into a nice frame at all. The all-profile recognition
threshold is still ten. No universal frame-existence or degree-bound
claim is added by this packet.
