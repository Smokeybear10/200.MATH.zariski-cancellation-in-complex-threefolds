# A sharp first-shift degree condition without a polynomial slice

## 1. Conclusion and boundary

For every integer \(n\ge1\), the homogeneous Jacobian base below admits
an ordinary-homogeneous coefficient \(\psi\) such that

- the proposed necessary first-shift degree equality holds;
- \(\psi\notin H(R)+\ker H\);
- an actual positive coordinate frame has exact Euler shift one;
- the whole four-variable LND has no polynomial slice.

Thus the sharp degree equality alone does not force an image-plus-kernel
decomposition. This is a boundary before imposing sliceness, not a
counterexample to the sliced common-kernel branch. It gives no negative
conclusion about cancellation, recognition of a sliced four-ring, or
globally degree-minimal frames.

The base belongs to the established Jacobian family discussed in the
retained [pass 588](weighted-euler-first-shift-homogeneous-recognition-disjunction-pass588-working.md)
and [pass 596](rank-three-pole-repair-priority-audit-pass596-working.md).
All properties of that base needed here are proved directly. No
classification of its kernel, published rank theorem, or novelty claim
is used. The no-slice argument is a full DVR orbit argument, not a
failure to find a slice of bounded degree.

## 2. Entire base, polynomial flow, and exact orders

Work in the actual ring \(R=\mathbb C[x,y,z]\). Set
\[
\begin{gathered}
a=2n+1,\qquad M=4n+1=2a-1,\qquad N=8n+2,\\
f=xz-y^2,\qquad r=x^a+yf^n,\\
g=\frac{f^a+r^2}{x}
  =zf^{2n}+2x^{a-1}yf^n+x^{2a-1},\\
H(P)=\det J(f,g,P).
\end{gathered}
\tag{1}
\]
In particular \(g\) is a polynomial, with no localization in its
definition. Differentiating the entire polynomials gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
Hx&=-2f^n r,\\
Hy&=2a x^{a-1}r-g,\\
Hz&=2x^{a-2}\bigl(f^{n+1}+(2a-1)yr\bigr),\\
Hf&=Hg=0,\qquad Hr=-f^n g.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
All three coordinate coefficients are ordinary homogeneous of degree
\(4n+1\), so \(H\) has ordinary derivation degree \(d_H=4n\).
The exponent \(a-2=2n-1\) is nonnegative even at \(n=1\).

For an independent flow parameter \(w\), the first identities are
\[
r(w)=r-f^ngw,\qquad
x(w)=x-2f^nrw+f^{2n}gw^2.
\tag{3}
\]
The injective localization \(R_f\) permits the exact identities
\[
y=\frac{r-x^a}{f^n},\qquad
z=\frac{g-2x^{a-1}r+x^{2a-1}}{f^{2n}}.
\tag{4}
\]
The derivation is locally nilpotent on the algebra generated by
\(f,f^{-1},g,r,x\): it kills the first three, has invariant derivative
on \(r\), and has a derivative of degree at most one in \(r\) on \(x\).
That algebra is \(R_f\) by (4). Hence \(H\) is locally nilpotent on
the original \(R\), by injectivity of localization.

Substituting (3) in (4) computes the entire finite flow, including
all its lower terms. Its highest \(w\)-coefficients on \(x,y,z\) are
respectively
\[
f^{2n}g,\qquad
-f^{\,2na-n}g^a,\qquad
f^{\,4n(a-1)}g^{2a-1}.
\tag{5}
\]
They are nonzero, and occur in degrees \(2,2a,4a-2\). Thus
\[
\deg_H(x,y,z)=(2,4n+2,8n+2)=(2,2a,N).
\tag{6}
\]
Here \(\deg_H P=\max\{j:H^jP\ne0\}\) for nonzero \(P\).
For a nonzero LND on a characteristic-zero domain this degree is
additive on products, by the unique nonzero highest Leibniz term.
Also \(\deg_H(HP)=\deg_HP-1\) when \(HP\ne0\).

All actual flow maps are the finite polynomials
\(\sum_j w^jH^j(x_i)/j!\), not maps defined only in the localization.
They satisfy the whole group identity and have inverse parameter
\(-w\). Formulas (3)--(4) are identities for this actual flow.

## 3. A uniform failure of the image-plus-kernel shortcut

Put
\[
\ell=4n+2=d_H+2,\qquad
\psi=H(z^2)+x^\ell.
\tag{7}
\]
Both summands are ordinary homogeneous of degree \(\ell\). Their
\(H\)-degrees are \(2N-1\) and \(2\ell\), respectively. The strict
inequality \(2\ell<2N-1\) holds for every \(n\ge1\). Consequently
\[
\deg_H\psi=2N-1,\qquad H\psi\ne0,\qquad
s:=\deg_H\psi+1=2N=N(\ell-d_H).
\tag{8}
\]
This is precisely the sharp degree equality under investigation.

Nevertheless \(x^\ell\notin H(R)+\ker H\). To prove it, suppose
\(x^\ell=H(Q)+K\), with \(HK=0\).
Since \(H\) is ordinary homogeneous of degree \(d_H\), the
ordinary-degree-\(\ell\) part of this equality is
\[
x^\ell=H(Q_2)+K_\ell,\qquad HK_\ell=0,
\tag{9}
\]
where \(Q_2\) is an ordinary homogeneous quadratic. The \(H\)-degree
of the left side is \(2\ell=8n+4>0\). Hence \(H(Q_2)\) has exactly
that degree, and \(Q_2\) would have \(H\)-degree \(8n+5\).

The six quadratic monomials have the following exact degrees:
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrr}
P&x^2&xy&xz&y^2&yz&z^2\\ \hline
\deg_H P&4&4n+4&8n+4&8n+4&12n+4&16n+4.
\end{array}
\tag{10}
\]
If the \(z^2\) coefficient of \(Q_2\) is nonzero, its unique highest
degree gives \(16n+4\). Otherwise a nonzero \(yz\) coefficient gives
the unique highest degree \(12n+4\). If both vanish, the degree is
at most \(8n+4\), even allowing every cancellation between the
remaining monomials. None of these possibilities is \(8n+5\).
This proves the contradiction without identifying \(\ker H\).
Subtracting the known image \(H(z^2)\) now gives
\[
\psi\notin H(R)+\ker H.
\tag{11}
\]

## 4. An actual positive frame and exact Euler shift one

In \(B=R[t]\), extend \(H\) by \(Ht=0\), and set
\[
E=H+(1+\psi)\partial_t,\qquad
(X_1,X_2,X_3,X_4)=(x+z,y+z,z,t).
\tag{12}
\]
The triangular extension \(E\) is locally nilpotent: on \(R\) it is
\(H\), and the derivatives of \(Et=1+\psi\) form a finite
\(H\)-sequence. The displayed actual linear map has full inverse
\[
x=X_1-X_3,\quad y=X_2-X_3,\quad z=X_3,\quad t=X_4.
\tag{13}
\]
By (6) and (8), its exact positive \(E\)-degree tuple is
\((N,N,N,2N)\). Its weighted Euler derivation is therefore exactly
\[
D=N(x\partial_x+y\partial_y+z\partial_z)+2Nt\partial_t.
\tag{14}
\]
No hypothetical coefficient-field coordinates enter this definition.

Use the convention \([E,D]=ED-DE\). Direct homogeneity gives
\[
C:=[E,D]=N\ell\,\partial_t-Nd_HE,\qquad [E,C]=0.
\tag{15}
\]
For clarity, this checks more than a cancellation of selected top
terms: it is an identity of the whole derivations on \(B\).
For every integer \(k\ge1\),
\[
[E^k,D]=kCE^{k-1}.
\tag{16}
\]
If \(\deg_E P=j\), formula (16) with \(k=j+2\) implies
\(E^{j+2}(DP)=0\). Thus the exact all-ring filtration shift
\[
\delta=\sup_{\substack{0\ne P\in B\\DP\ne0}}
       \bigl(\deg_E DP-\deg_E P\bigr)
\tag{17}
\]
is at most one. This implication does not assume that \(E\) has a slice.

We verify equality, rather than infer it solely from a nonzero
commutator. Make the actual shear \(T=t-z^2\). Then
\[
E=H+(1+x^\ell)\partial_T.
\tag{18}
\]
Write \(p=f^ng\), and define the polynomial
\[
J(f,r)=\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}
 \binom{\ell}{j}\frac{f^{\,a(\ell-j)}r^{2j+1}}{2j+1},\qquad
I=pg^\ell T+g^\ell r+J(f,r).
\tag{19}
\]
Since \(J_r=(f^a+r^2)^\ell=(gx)^\ell\), the full calculation gives
\[
EI=pg^\ell(1+x^\ell)-pg^\ell-pJ_r=0,\qquad
\partial_tI=pg^\ell\ne0.
\tag{20}
\]
It follows that \(E(DI)=CI=N\ell pg^\ell\ne0\) and \(E^2(DI)=0\).
Thus \(\deg_EI=0\), \(\deg_EDI=1\), and \(\delta=1\).
The invariant \(I\) is a polynomial; no reciprocal of \(f\) or \(g\)
appears in (19).

There are even two commuting homogeneous LND components of \(E\)
under (14): \(\partial_t\), of weight \(-2N\), and
\(H+\psi\partial_t\), of weight \(Nd_H\). The latter is another
triangular LND, and its coefficients are independent of \(t\).
This observation does not supply the missing polynomial slice.

## 5. A complete all-\(n\) DVR obstruction to any polynomial slice

We prove that the derivation in (18) has no polynomial slice. The
actual shear \(T=t-z^2\) then gives the same conclusion for (12).

Use an independent parameter \(F\) for values of the invariant \(f\).
Consider two endpoints with
\[
y_i=0,\qquad z_i=F/x_i,\qquad T_i=0,\qquad
r_i=x_i^a,\qquad g_i=x_i^M+F^a/x_i.
\tag{21}
\]
We will construct \(x_1,x_2\in\mathbb C[[F]]^\times\) with equal
\(g_i=g\) and with
\[
1+\frac{J(F,r_2)-J(F,r_1)}
        {g^\ell(r_2-r_1)}=0.
\tag{22}
\]
All quantities in the denominator will be units. The difference
quotient of \(J\) is itself polynomial in \(F,r_1,r_2\); only the
explicit unit denominators need localization.

Choose a primitive \(M\)-th root of unity \(\epsilon\). At \(F=0\),
the equal-\(g\) equation has the branch \(x_2=\epsilon x_1\).
On that branch (22) becomes
\[
1+Kx_1^\ell=0,\qquad
K=\frac{\epsilon^{a+1}-1}
        {(2\ell+1)(\epsilon^a-1)}\ne0.
\tag{23}
\]
Indeed, the \(F=0\) primitive is
\(J(0,r)=r^{2\ell+1}/(2\ell+1)\). Using
\(2a=M+1\) and \(\ell=M+1\), its quotient in (22) has
\(x_1\)-exponent \(\ell\), while
\(a(2\ell+1)\equiv a+1\pmod M\).
Both \(\epsilon^a-1\) and \(\epsilon^{a+1}-1\) are nonzero because
\(0<a<a+1<M\), including \(n=1\).

Select any nonzero complex solution \(c\) of \(1+Kc^\ell=0\).
The two equations defining \(x_1,x_2\) have an invertible Jacobian
in those variables at \((F,x_1,x_2)=(0,c,\epsilon c)\):
the \(x_2\) derivative of \(g_2-g_1\) is
\(M(\epsilon c)^{M-1}\ne0\); after its elimination, the derivative
of the second equation along \(x_2=\epsilon x_1\) is
\(\ell Kc^{\ell-1}\ne0\).
The formal implicit-function theorem therefore gives solutions
\[
x_1(F),x_2(F)\in\mathcal O:=\mathbb C[[F]],\qquad
x_1(0)=c,\quad x_2(0)=\epsilon c.
\tag{24}
\]
This use of the theorem is equivalently the recursive solution of
each coefficient using the same invertible complex Jacobian. It
provides an actual DVR and fraction field
\(\mathcal K=\mathbb C((F))\); no unproved passage from isolated
special points to a dominating algebraic component is needed.

The elements \(x_1,x_2,g,r_2-r_1\) are units of \(\mathcal O\).
For the last two, their constant terms are \(c^M\) and
\(c^a(\epsilon^a-1)\), respectively. Thus both whole endpoints
in (21) belong to \(\mathcal O^4\), whereas
\[
\tau=-\frac{r_2-r_1}{gF^n}\in\mathcal K,\qquad
\operatorname{ord}_F\tau=-n<0.
\tag{25}
\]

Now evaluate the actual polynomial \(E\)-flow at the first endpoint
and parameter \(\tau\). Since \(Er=-f^ng\), it reaches \(r_2\).
It retains the equal invariants \(f=F\) and \(g\). The identities
\[
x=\frac{F^a+r^2}{g},\qquad
y=\frac{r-x^a}{F^n},\qquad z=\frac{F+y^2}{x}
\tag{26}
\]
hold over \(\mathcal K\) and determine the endpoint \(x,y,z\)
uniquely when the displayed denominators are nonzero. They give
exactly \(x_2,0,F/x_2\).

The last coordinate also agrees. Along this orbit,
\(dr/dw=-F^ng\), and the primitive of \(1+x^\ell\) with respect
to \(r\) is \(r+g^{-\ell}J(F,r)\). Hence the entire displacement is
\[
T_2-T_1
=-\frac{r_2-r_1+g^{-\ell}(J(F,r_2)-J(F,r_1))}
        {F^ng}=0
\tag{27}
\]
by (22). This is a finite polynomial-flow identity over
\(\mathcal K\), not an analytic integration assumption.

If \(S\in\mathbb C[x,y,z,T]\) were any polynomial slice, then
\(\exp(wE)S=S+w\). Applying this identity to the two endpoints gives
\[
S(x_2,0,F/x_2,0)-S(x_1,0,F/x_1,0)=\tau.
\tag{28}
\]
The left side lies in \(\mathcal O\), since both endpoints are
integral and \(S\) is polynomial. The right side has negative
valuation by (25). This contradiction proves the absence of every
polynomial slice, with no bound on its degree or chosen shape.

## 6. Verification and remaining question

Fresh unsaved symbolic calculations for \(n=1,2,3\) reconstructed
the complete Jacobian coefficients, \(Hf,Hg,Hr\), the ordinary
homogeneity of \(\psi\), and the entire primitive identity
\(J_r=(f^a+r^2)^\ell\); all residuals were zero. Earlier independent
fresh checks also reconstructed the full commutator identity and the
quadratic image obstruction. These finite checks support the uniform
proofs, rather than extend finite verification to all \(n\).
No saved checker was executed, imported, or compiled.

The exact unresolved distinction is sliceness. This construction
refutes the degree-condition-only implication
\(\psi\in H(R)+\ker H\), even at actual Euler shift one. It does not
refute that implication with an additional polynomial-slice
hypothesis, and it does not settle recognition in that sliced
common-kernel branch. The no-slice theorem above is precisely why
the control cannot be transferred to that branch.
