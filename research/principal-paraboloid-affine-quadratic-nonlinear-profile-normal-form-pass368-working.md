# A normal form for the nonlinear intrinsic degree pair (1,2)

Working necessary conditions: in365's theta-dominant branch, assume
the intrinsic degrees of sigma and theta are one and two. An all-X
specialization forces the quadratic coefficient of theta, written
in sigma, to be -1 and its linear coefficient to be nonzero. The
full scalar then separates two coefficient-field alternatives.
One includes a finite quadratic extension of C(t); it is retained,
not declared impossible for being nonrational over C(t).

This note proves neither recognition nor exclusion of the surviving
profile. Coefficient-field changes remain distinct from actual
polynomial coordinate changes.

## 1. Actual hypotheses and the supplied reciprocal derivative

Retain the full actual chart264, fourth-potential theorem296, and the
complete hypotheses of
[365](principal-paraboloid-nonlinear-intercept-unequal-degree-gate-pass365-working.md).
Thus
\[
\begin{gathered}
\mathcal B=\mathbb C[X,Y,Z,W]=A[u],\quad E=\partial_u,
\quad\deg_E(X,Y,Z,W)=(1,2,2,2),\\
I_{\rm initial}=(X^2Z-Y^2)\text{ is the full initial kernel},
\quad a_0=EX\ne0,\\
O=L[v]=L[y,\sigma,\theta],\quad Nv=1,\quad
[L:\mathbb C(t,s)]<\infty,\\
K=L(v)=\mathbb C(y,z,w),\qquad k=s,
\qquad J_{t,s,v}(X)=-a_0^4\in L^*.
\end{gathered}
\tag{1}
\]
The exact coefficient partials commute. Set F=ker_L(partial_s),
finite over C(t), with t-derivative constants C. Assume
\[
n_{ss}\ne0,\qquad (\deg_v\sigma,\deg_v\theta)=(1,2).
\tag{2}
\]
By365, n_ss=-1/[2(t-tau)^2] with tau complex, and the leading
y/theta ratio is -1/[2(t-tau)]. Normalize tau by the actual map
\[
X_1=X,\quad Y_1=Y-\tau X^2,\quad
Z_1=Z-2\tau Y+\tau^2X^2,\quad W_1=W.
\tag{3}
\]
Its inverse replaces tau by -tau. It fixes X^2Z-Y^2 exactly and
leaves the literal Z-partial unchanged. Hence the selected N, L,
actual returns, and calibrated determinant are retained. It replaces
t by t-tau and leaves k=s. Drop subscripts from now on. Exact
integration in the coefficient field gives
\[
n=-s^2/(4t^2)+B(t)s+f(t),\qquad B,f\in F.
\tag{4}
\]
This is not a rational ansatz and does not identify L with F(s).

Use P=sigma as the polynomial parameter of O=L[P]. Its speed
tau0=N sigma belongs to L*. Section5 of365 gives the exact remainder
\[
\theta=A P^2+\beta P+\gamma,\quad A\in L^*,\quad
y=-\theta/(2t)+jP+k,\quad k\in L,
\qquad j=n_s+t n_{ts}=\frac{s}{2t^2}+B+tB'.
\tag{5}
\]
Indeed365's zeta=y+theta/(2t)+n_t/2-n_sP has degree one and

\[
J_{t,s,P}(X)=-a_0^4/\tau_0\in L^*.
\tag{6}

## 2. Every actual X-specialization forces A=-1 and beta nonzero

The full literal map remains
\[
\begin{aligned}
Y&=y+PX+tX^2,\\
Z&=2ty+P^2+\theta+n+(2tP+s)X+t^2X^2,\\
W&=\ell-2Py-n_tP-n_s\theta+\theta X+sX^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]
It satisfies the exact coefficient-adjoined ring equality
\[
L[X,Y,Z,W]=L[X,P].
\tag{8}
\]
One inclusion follows from (5),(7). Conversely E preserves
L[mathcal B] and kills L; since a0 belongs to L*, the actual identity
P=EY/a0-2tX puts P in that ring. Equality (8) therefore survives
every coefficient-field extension and every specialization of X.

Using (5), Z is monic quadratic in P, and W has nonzero cubic
coefficient A/t. The polynomial combination
\[
Y+\frac{A}{2t}Z
\quad\text{has P coefficient}\quad
(1+A)(X+j)-\frac{\beta}{2t}
\tag{9}
\]
and no quadratic term in P. If A differs from -1, choose X0 in L
making (9) zero. If A=-1 and beta=0, any X0 works. Either case
leaves a specialized ring generated by Z of degree two and W of
degree three. Such a pair cannot generate the entire polynomial line.
After centering the quadratic and removing the cubic's even part,
their ring is
\[
L[\xi^2,\xi(c_3\xi^2+c_1)]
=L[\xi^2]\oplus\xi(c_3\xi^2+c_1)L[\xi^2],\qquad c_3\ne0.
\tag{10}
\]
Its odd module misses xi, contradicting (8). Therefore
\[
\boxed{\theta=-P^2+\beta P+\gamma,\qquad \beta\in L^*.}
\tag{11}
\]
Combination (9) was used only over L[X], not as an ambient coordinate.
The specialization is valid even when X0 is not complex.

## 3. Two full scalar coefficients and a square presentation

Primes mean the exact t-derivative on F. Put
\[
D=(t^2B)''=t^2B''+4tB'+2B,\qquad
C=-2t^2(B+tB'),\qquad G=2tk+n-t^2j^2.
\tag{12}
\]
The formal parameter U=P+t(X+j), which is not asserted to be an
original coordinate, gives the complete expression
\[
Z=U^2+G+CX,\qquad C'=-2tD.
\tag{13}
\]
Before this parameter change, a coefficient of the full literal
(t,s,P) determinant is
\[
[XP^3]J=
-\frac{s+t^4B''+5t^3B'-2t^3k_s+2t^2B}{t^4}.
\tag{14}
\]
It vanishes by (6). Since j_s=1/(2t^2), direct differentiation gives
G_s=2tk_s-s/t^2-tB'. Hence (14) is exactly
\[
G_s=D,\qquad G=Ds+g(t),\qquad g\in F.
\tag{15}
\]
The integration constant g is in F by its zero s-derivative.
After (14), the P-fourth coefficient of the same full determinant is
\[
[P^4]J=-\frac{D(2t\beta_s-1)}{2t^3}.
\tag{16}
\]
Explicitly, before (14) its numerator, with denominator 2t^5 and
an overall minus sign, is
s+2t^5 B'' beta_s+8t^4 B' beta_s+4t^3 B beta_s+t^3B'-2t^3k_s.
Substitution gives (16). Thus the exact alternatives are
\[
D=0\qquad\text{or}\qquad \beta_s=1/(2t).
\tag{17}
\]
No division by D has been made. The parameter change P to U at fixed
actual X has triangular determinant one, so calibration (6) remains
independent of U and X.

## 4. Nonzero D retains a finite quadratic coefficient-field mode

Suppose D is nonzero. Equation (17) gives
\[
\beta=s/(2t)+\beta_0(t),\qquad \beta_0\in F.
\tag{18}
\]
Set zeta0=k+n_t/2 and Omega=n_tt/2-n_s^2+t^2 n_ts^2.
The P-squared and P coefficients of the full third level give
\[
\ell_s=-\zeta_0/t-n_{tt}/2-t^2n_{ts}^2,\qquad
\ell_t=2t n_{ts}\zeta_0+\beta\Omega.
\tag{19}
\]
For example,365's formal remainder R evaluates to
zeta0+t(ell_s+n_s^2). The P-squared coefficient gives R=-t*Omega,
and the P coefficient gives the second identity in (19).

Insert (4),(12),(15),(18) into (19), expressing
k=(Ds+g-n+t^2j^2)/(2t). Both right sides are polynomials in s
over F. Their exact mixed compatibility has coefficient
\[
[s]\{\partial_t(\ell_s)-\partial_s(\ell_t)\}
=-\frac{2tD'+3D}{2t^3}.
\tag{20}
\]
Since s is transcendental over F and the partials commute,
\[
2tD'+3D=0,\qquad t^3D^2=d_0\in\mathbb C^*.
\tag{21}
\]
The second assertion follows by differentiation and the exact
constant field of F; its value is nonzero because D and t are.
Also (t^2D)''=-D/4. Combining this with D=(t^2B)'' yields
\[
\boxed{B=b_1/t+b_2/t^2-4D,\qquad b_1,b_2\in\mathbb C,
\quad D^2=d_0/t^3.}
\tag{22}
\]
Indeed the second derivative of t^2(B+4D) is zero, so that function
is complex affine in t in the exact field F.

This is a valid finite quadratic extension mode: d0/t^3 is not a
square in C(t), but may have a square root in F. It is not an exclusion.
No equality F=C(t,D), rationality of another coefficient, or ambient
polynomiality of D is asserted. Other coefficients of (19), the fourth
level, and the full scalar remain constraints.

## 5. Zero D has a different slope condition and a surviving boundary

If D=0, exact integration gives
\[
B=b_1/t+b_2/t^2,\quad b_1,b_2\in\mathbb C,
\qquad C=2b_2,\qquad G=g(t)\in F.
\tag{23}
\]
If G'=0, then G is complex and (13) makes the irreducible actual
coordinate polynomial Z-2b2X-G a square in Frac(mathcal B).
Its prime valuation is one, whereas a square has even valuation.
Therefore this branch requires G' nonzero.

After substituting the third-level equations (19), the full scalar
in the parameter U has constant-U, cubic-X coefficient
\[
[X^3U^0]J=\frac{G'}2(t\beta_s-1).
\tag{24}
\]
The scalar and G' nonzero therefore force
\[
\beta=s/t+\beta_0(t),\qquad\beta_0\in F.
\tag{25}
\]
This differs from (18), so the zero-D boundary cannot be silently
absorbed into that case. Its cubic-U coefficient further gives
\[
[U^3X^0]J=
\frac{t^4 f''+4b_1b_2t+6b_2^2-t^3G'}{2t^6}=0.
\tag{26}
\]
The values b1=0 and b2=0 are retained. When G' is nonzero, the
presentation Z=U^2+G(t)+2b2X is not by itself the square of an actual
coordinate polynomial with complex constant term. It supplies no
recognition or contradiction without an additional argument.
