# Affine sigma is excluded by the full polynomial-line return

Working result: in the full actual264 chart, suppose
\(k=s+f\), \(f_s=n_s=0\), \(f'''\ne0\), and
\(\ell_s=e\in\mathbb C^*\).
Then \(\deg_v\sigma=1\) is impossible.

The genuine plane-gradient unit first forces \(y\) to be a
quadratic polynomial in the affine parameter \(\sigma\).
Two divided-difference equations then produce a collision, or a
common critical point, in a specialization of the three actual
coordinates. Both contradict the whole polynomial-line return.
No degree-divisibility theorem or lower scalar coefficient is
needed for this final step.

The distinct case \(\sigma\in L\) has projection degree two and
is already recognized by307. That inherited recognition is not a
new result of this note. Neither statement recognizes the remaining
nonconstant higher-degree branches or supplies arbitrary chart entry.

## 1. Full actual hypotheses and specialization over the coefficient field

Retain all hypotheses of
[264](principal-one-affine-paraboloid-chart-pass264-working.md)
and the genuine plane reduction of
[430](principal-paraboloid-arbitrary-longitudinal-plane-reduction-pass430-working.md):
\[
\begin{gathered}
\mathcal B=\mathbb C[X,Y,Z,W]=A[u],\qquad E=\partial_u,\qquad
A=\ker E,\qquad \deg_E(X,Y,Z,W)=(1,2,2,2),\\
a_0=EX\ne0,\qquad
I_{\rm initial}=(\mathsf X^2\mathsf Z-\mathsf Y^2)
\text{ is the full weighted initial kernel},\\
N=[u^4]\partial_Z|_A\ne0,\qquad
L=\operatorname{Frac}\ker_A N,\qquad
[L:\mathbb C(t,s)]<\infty,\qquad
Na_0=Nt=Ns=0,\\
O=L[A]=L[v],\qquad Nv=1,\qquad
K=\operatorname{Frac}A=L(v)=\mathbb C(y,z,w),\\
L[y,z,w]=L[y,\sigma,\theta]=O,\qquad
\det\frac{\partial(Y,Z,W)}{\partial(t,s,v)}
       =-a_0^4\in L^*.
\end{gathered}
\tag{1}
\]
The notation \(L[A]\) denotes the specified actual localization.
The coefficient partials commute and fix \(v\).
Put \(\mathbb F=\ker_L\partial_s\); this is the exact finite
extension of \(\mathbb C(t)\), not an assumed rational field.
Our specialized conditions are
\[
k=s+f,\qquad f,n\in\mathbb F,\qquad
f'''\ne0,\qquad \ell_s=e\in\mathbb C^* .
\tag{2}
\]
Primes denote exact \(t\)-derivatives of \(f,n\).
The constant-slope conclusion in(2) is available from
[431](principal-paraboloid-nonlinear-time-weighted-plane-exclusion-pass431-working.md)
and
[433](principal-paraboloid-variable-second-slope-exclusion-pass433-working.md)
when \(f'''\ell_s\ne0\), but it is retained explicitly here.

The literal rows and potentials are
\[
\begin{aligned}
Y&=y+\sigma X+tX^2,\\
Z&=z+(2t\sigma+s+f)X+t^2X^2,\\
W&=w+\theta X+sX^2,\\
z&=2ty+\sigma^2+f'\sigma+\theta+n,\\
w&=\ell-(2\sigma+f')y-\frac{f''}{2}\sigma^2-n'\sigma .
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]
They are not replaced by their leading terms.

The original global slice and \(E^2X=0\) give
\(X=a_0u+x_0\), \(x_0\in A\), \(a_0\in L^*\). Hence the actual
coefficient-adjoined ring identity is
\[
L[\mathcal B]=L[A][u]=L[v,u]=L[v,X]
             =L[X,Y,Z,W].
\tag{4}
\]
After any field extension \(M/L\), and for every \(x\in M\),
base change and evaluation at \(X=x\) therefore give
\[
M[Y(x),Z(x),W(x)]=M[v].
\tag{5}
\]
This is a polynomial-ring specialization at a coefficient value.
It does not promote \(x\) or \(X-x\) to an original complex
polynomial coordinate. In particular we may use an algebraic
closure \(M=\overline L\) without claiming that the coefficient
field of the actual chart is algebraically closed.

## 2. The unit gradient forces a genuine quadratic y

Assume \(\deg_v\sigma=1\), and use \(P=\sigma\) as the intrinsic
polynomial parameter. Thus \(L[P]=L[v]\), and \(y=g(P)\) for
some polynomial \(g\in L[P]\).
This is a coefficient-field parametrization, not an ambient reframe.

Write \(h\in L\) for the genuine third level and put
\[
S(U,P)=U^2+(f''P+n')U+\frac{f'''}6P^3+
                \frac{n''}2P^2-\ell_tP-h .
\tag{6}
\]
The third relation and430 give
\[
\theta=\frac{S(y,P)}e,\qquad
\Phi(y,P)=q,\quad q\in L,\qquad
(\Phi_U(y,P),\Phi_P(y,P))L[P]=L[P].
\tag{7}
\]
Here \(\Phi\) is the genuine fourth potential after eliminating
the third formal variable, and retains all derivatives of \(h\).

Under(2) the complete plane primitive of430 has the form
\[
\Phi(U,P)=A U^2+B(P)U+C(P),\qquad
A=\frac{f''}{2}-\frac{h_s}{e},
\tag{8}
\]
where
\[
B(P)=\frac{f'''}2P^2+
\left(n''+2e-\frac{h_s f''}{e}\right)P+
f'e-\ell_t-\frac{h_s n'}e .
\tag{9}
\]
The unused lower polynomial \(C(P)\) is retained, not set to zero.
In particular \(B\) has exact degree two.

Differentiate the formal identity \(\Phi(g(P),P)=q\) in \(P\),
holding \(L\) fixed. It gives
\(\Phi_P(g(P),P)=-g'(P)\Phi_U(g(P),P)\).
The ideal in(7) is therefore generated by \(\Phi_U(g(P),P)\)
alone, so
\[
2A g(P)+B(P)=\eta,\qquad \eta\in L^*.
\tag{10}
\]
If \(A=0\), (10) contradicts the exact degree two of \(B\).
Thus \(A\ne0\). Equation(10) then forces
\[
y=\gamma P^2+\beta P+\delta,\qquad
\gamma=-\frac{f'''}{4A}\in L^*,\qquad
\beta,\delta\in L .
\tag{11}
\]
This treats the zero-\(A\) boundary before dividing by \(A\).
No derivative of a moving intrinsic parameter or moving root was
used, and no third-level derivative was suppressed.

## 3. Full divided differences with a moving X value

For a polynomial \(F(P)\in L[P]\), let \(F^{[1]}(P_1,P_2)\)
denote the polynomial divided difference
\[
F^{[1]}=\frac{F(P_1)-F(P_2)}{P_1-P_2}.
\]
The quotient notation denotes a polynomial, also defined on the
diagonal, where its value is \(F'(P_1)\).
Put
\[
\Sigma=P_1+P_2,\qquad \Pi=P_1P_2,\qquad
L_1=y^{[1]}=\gamma\Sigma+\beta,\qquad
X_0=-L_1 .
\tag{12}
\]
At this coefficient value, \(Y(X_0)^{[1]}=0\). Since the
\(2ty\) and \(2tPX\) contributions to \(Z\) cancel in its divided
difference, its complete divided difference is
\[
Z(X_0)^{[1]}=\theta^{[1]}+\Sigma+f'.
\tag{13}
\]
Similarly, using the full expression for \(w\) in(3),
\[
W(X_0)^{[1]}-X_0 Z(X_0)^{[1]}
=-\mathcal F(\Sigma,\Pi),
\]
where
\[
\mathcal F=
\gamma(\Sigma^2-2\Pi)
+\left(\beta+\frac{f''}{2}\right)\Sigma+2\delta+n'.
\tag{14}
\]
For example the left side is \(w^{[1]}-X_0(\Sigma+f')\);
expanding \((2P+f')y+(f''/2)P^2+n'P\) proves(14).
The literal terms \(tX^2,t^2X^2,sX^2\) are constant in \(P\)
and have zero divided difference. The remaining terms involving
\(t\) cancel by the already imposed \(Y\) divided difference.

Because \(\gamma\ne0\), solve \(\mathcal F=0\) by
\[
\Pi=\frac{\Sigma^2}{2}
 +\frac{\beta+f''/2}{2\gamma}\Sigma
 +\frac{2\delta+n'}{2\gamma}.
\tag{15}
\]
We now compute the remaining equation(13) exactly.
Let \(M=y(P_1)+y(P_2)\). On \(\mathcal F=0\),
\[
M=-\frac{f''}{2}\Sigma-n',\qquad
(Py)^{[1]}=\frac{\Sigma L_1+M}{2}.
\]
Consequently
\[
\{y^2+(f''P+n')y\}^{[1]}
 =L_1\left(M+\frac{f''}{2}\Sigma+n'\right)
       +\frac{f''}{2}M
 =-\frac{(f'')^2}{4}\Sigma-\frac{f''n'}2 .
\tag{16}
\]
This identity keeps all coefficients of \(y\), including its
constant term.

Use(6),(7) to compute \(\theta^{[1]}\), then substitute(15) in(13).
The result is the polynomial
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal R(\Sigma)
:={}&e Z(X_0)^{[1]}\big|_{\mathcal F=0}\\
={}&\frac{f'''}{12}\Sigma^2\\
&+\left\{e-\frac{(f'')^2}{4}+\frac{n''}{2}
      -\frac{f'''(\beta+f''/2)}{12\gamma}\right\}\Sigma\\
&+ef'-\frac{f''n'}2-\ell_t
       -\frac{f'''(2\delta+n')}{12\gamma}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
\]
The level \(h\) disappears here only because it is constant in
the polynomial parameter and has zero divided difference.
Equation(17) is not a leading-degree approximation:
it is the complete remaining divided-difference equation.
Its quadratic leading coefficient \(f'''/12\) is nonzero.

## 4. Both collision and diagonal cases contradict whole generation

Take \(M=\overline L\). Choose a root \(\Sigma_0\in M\) of the
genuine quadratic \(\mathcal R\), and define \(\Pi_0\) by(15).
Let \(p_1,p_2\in M\) be the roots, possibly equal, of
\[
T^2-\Sigma_0T+\Pi_0=0,\qquad
x_0=-\gamma\Sigma_0-\beta .
\tag{18}
\]
The divided differences of \(Y(x_0),Z(x_0),W(x_0)\) all vanish
at \((p_1,p_2)\), by(12)--(17).

If \(p_1\ne p_2\), the three polynomials have equal values at
these distinct points. The generation identity(5), now
\(M[Y(x_0),Z(x_0),W(x_0)]=M[P]\), would express \(P\) as a
polynomial in the three equal outputs. Evaluating at \(p_1,p_2\)
would give \(p_1=p_2\), a contradiction.

If \(p_1=p_2=p_0\), the three ordinary \(P\)-derivatives instead
vanish at \(p_0\). Every polynomial in the three outputs then has
derivative zero there, by the chain rule. The polynomial \(P\)
has derivative one, again contradicting(5).

In the second case \(x_0\) is held fixed during differentiation.
It was chosen as an element of \(M\) after \(\Sigma_0\) was chosen;
it is not differentiated as a function of the running parameter.
The polynomial divided differences already include the diagonal
case, so no division by \(p_1-p_2\) is required.

This exhausts the roots of(18) and proves
\[
\boxed{\text{Under(1),(2), }\quad \deg_v\sigma\ne1.}
\tag{19}
\]

## 5. The constant-sigma boundary is inherited recognition

If \(\sigma\in L\), the plane return of430 gives \(L[y]=L[v]\),
so \(y\) is an affine polynomial parameter. Equation(6) then gives
\(\theta\) exact degree two in that parameter, with leading
coefficient \(1/e\). Thus
\[
[L(v):\operatorname{Frac}L[\sigma,\theta]]
 =[L(y):L(\theta)]=2.
\tag{20}
\]
All the actual coupled264 hypotheses are unchanged. This is
precisely the constant-sigma double-cover case recognized in
[307](principal-paraboloid-constant-sigma-double-cover-recognition-pass307-working.md).
Its original-ring conclusion and its polynomial-entry dependencies
are retained as the existing theorem, not reproved here.
No independent novelty or new coverage is claimed for this boundary.

The new conclusion(19) excludes only the affine nonconstant
\(\sigma\) branch under(2). It does not recognize higher-degree
\(\sigma\), assert existence of remaining formal families, or
promote coefficient-field polynomial variables to ambient
coordinates. The full actual initial kernel, slice, selected
derivation, calibrated potentials and polynomial returns remain
essential inputs.
