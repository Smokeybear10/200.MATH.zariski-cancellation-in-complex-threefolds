# The universal frontier as a conditional bounded-family degree problem

For a fixed finite-complexity family of sliced actions, pointwise
existence of actual invariant-coordinate witnesses over \(\mathbb C\)
already implies a uniform bound on the forward and inverse degrees
of those witnesses. The bound is conditional and non-effective.
Neither pointwise existence nor a value of the bound is proved here.

The deduction uses the exact inverse-pair equations of pass43,
constructibility, and a countable-exhaustion lemma over an uncountable
algebraically closed field. It identifies a finite-family obligation
behind the universal frame problem. It does not prove a new family
of cancellation instances, universal frame existence, or unrestricted
cancellation.

## 1. Deduplication and the remaining quantifier

Let
\[
B=\mathbb C[x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4],\qquad
E\in\operatorname{LND}(B),\qquad E(s)=1,\qquad A=\ker E.
\tag{1}
\]
The [universal search](universal-frame-search-pass43.md) already
constructs exact finite polynomial equations for every pair of
polynomial inverse maps of bounded degree. An invariant ambient
coordinate is enough to recognize the original kernel, because
the slice makes it regular and the established invariant-coordinate
theorem applies. Conversely, if \(A\) is polynomial, an adapted
frame provides such a witness. Thus pass43 already proves an exact
positive semidecision for each fixed input. It does not bound the
first successful level.

The [relative-fibration audit](universal-frame-reduction-audit-pass52.md)
separates supplied-frame recognition from universal frame existence.
Its actual primitive plinth fibers also rule out simply replacing
an invariant subalgebra by a relative polynomial base. The
[affine-lattice Jacobian condition](actual-frame-affine-ideal-pass58.md)
has a specified generic quadratic graph as a premise. It is not
available for arbitrary (1). The latest checkpointed README and
ledger retain the universal obligation separately.

There is also no new intrinsic-flow height descent hidden here.
[Cross-frame saturation](cross-frame-saturation-pass33.md), Section 3,
already observes that lifted intrinsic flows commute with \(E\)
and preserve adjoint heights. The same holds for the full stabilizer
of the embedded \(A\): if \(\phi(A)=A\), the one-variable polynomial
automorphism over that coefficient ring has
\(\phi(s)=as+b\), with \(a\in\mathbb C^*, b\in A\). Hence
\(\phi E\phi^{-1}=a^{-1}E\), so conjugation preserves every
partial's largest nonzero adjoint order.
The unit assertion uses \(A^*=\mathbb C^*\), inherited from \(A\subset B\).
This is a modest extension of the existing obstruction, not the
new mechanism of this note.

The new statement below concerns varying the coefficients of
\((E,s)\) in a finite-type family. It does not identify a successful
coordinate change for any presently unknown instance.

## 2. Countably many proper closed subsets cannot cover complex points

**Lemma.** Let \(X\) be an irreducible variety over \(\mathbb C\).
For a countable family of proper closed subsets \(Z_i\subsetneq X\),
\[
X(\mathbb C)\ne\bigcup_{i\ge1}Z_i(\mathbb C).
\tag{2}
\]

It suffices to work in a nonempty affine open of \(X\).
Each \(Z_i\) remains proper there. Write
\(X=\operatorname{Spec}(\mathbb C[x_1,\ldots,x_n]/I)\).
Choose finite generators of \(I\), and for each nonempty \(Z_i\)
choose a polynomial \(f_i\) whose class is nonzero in
\(\mathbb C[X]\) and which vanishes on \(Z_i\).
Empty \(Z_i\) can be ignored.

The coefficients of these equations and of all the \(f_i\) form
a countable set. The subfield they generate over \(\mathbb Q\)
is countable, and its algebraic closure inside \(\mathbb C\)
is a countable algebraically closed field \(k_0\).
Let \(I_0\) be the ideal generated by the selected equations
over \(k_0\), and put
\[
R_0=k_0[x_1,\ldots,x_n]/I_0.
\]
Then \(R_0\otimes_{k_0}\mathbb C=\mathbb C[X]\).
Faithful flatness of a field extension makes \(R_0\) a domain
and keeps each selected \(f_i\) nonzero in it.

The finitely generated field \(\operatorname{Frac}R_0\) embeds
into \(\mathbb C\) over \(k_0\). Explicitly, choose images in
\(\mathbb C\) of a finite transcendence basis, algebraically
independent over \(k_0\), and then extend to the remaining
finite algebraic extension using algebraic closedness.
The required independent elements exist: after adjoining finitely
many elements to a countable field, its algebraic closure remains
countable, whereas \(\mathbb C\) is uncountable.

Restricting this field embedding to \(R_0\), and using the given
inclusion \(k_0\subset\mathbb C\), produces a complex point of
the original affine \(X\). Every \(f_i\) has a nonzero value
at that point. It therefore avoids all the \(Z_i\), proving (2).

The proof does not require \(X\) or the closed subsets to be
defined over the algebraic numbers. Countably many arbitrary
complex coefficients are allowed. No claim that the avoiding
point is algebraic over \(\mathbb Q\) is made.

## 3. Increasing constructible exhaustion has a finite level

**Proposition.** Let \(X\) be a finite-type complex variety and
\[
C_1\subseteq C_2\subseteq\cdots\subseteq X
\]
be constructible subsets. If they cover all complex points, then
\[
X(\mathbb C)=\bigcup_{D\ge1}C_D(\mathbb C)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
X(\mathbb C)=C_{D_0}(\mathbb C)
\text{ for some finite }D_0.
\tag{3}
\]

For an irreducible \(X\), if no \(C_D\) were dense, their proper
closed closures would contradict (2). A dense constructible subset
contains a nonempty open subset: write it as a finite union of
locally closed pieces and choose one whose closure is \(X\).
Thus some \(C_{D_1}\) contains a dense open \(U\subset X\).

The complement \(X\setminus U\) has smaller dimension. Its induced
increasing constructible sets still cover all its complex points.
Induction on dimension gives a finite level covering that complement.
The larger of its index and \(D_1\) covers \(X\).
Dimension zero is immediate because a zero-dimensional variety has
finitely many points. A finite union of irreducible components is
handled by taking the maximum of their resulting indices.
This proves (3).

Nilpotents in a finite-type scheme do not affect this statement:
apply it to its reduction. The conclusion is about existence
at every complex point, not a single regularly varying choice
of witnesses.

## 4. An exact finite-complexity scheme of sliced actions

Fix integers \(e\ge0\), \(d\ge1\), \(N\ge1\).
Introduce coefficient parameters for four polynomials \(P_i(x)\)
of total degree at most \(e\), and a polynomial \(s(x)\) of degree
at most \(d\). Let
\[
E_P=\sum_{i=1}^4P_i(x)\partial_{x_i}.
\]
Define the affine parameter scheme \(\mathscr S_{e,d,N}\) by all
coefficients of
\[
E_P(s)-1=0,\qquad E_P^N(x_i)=0\quad(1\le i\le4).
\tag{4}
\]
These are finitely many polynomial equations over \(\mathbb Q\)
in finitely many parameters. They impose exact identities, not
vanishing on finitely many spatial test points.

Every complex point gives a genuine polynomial LND with its
displayed slice. Local nilpotence follows from the coordinate
iterate bounds: the Leibniz rule kills any fixed monomial after
more than the sum of its factors' permitted derivative orders.
The slice equation excludes the zero derivation. Some parameter
schemes, for example \(N=1\), may be empty; the statements then
hold vacuously.

Conversely every pair (1) belongs to some such family: choose its
actual finite coefficient and slice degrees and a common finite
nilpotence order on the four original generators.
This does not bound any of those quantities universally.

For each \(D\ge1\), adjoin coefficients for two four-tuples
\(F(x),G(y)\) of total degree at most \(D\), and impose
\[
G(F(x))=x,\qquad F(G(y))=y,\qquad E_P(F_1)=0.
\tag{5}
\]
Let \(\mathscr W_D\) be this affine scheme over \(\mathscr S_{e,d,N}\).
The equations are exactly the all-inverse-pairs construction of
pass43, now with \(P,s\) also varying as parameters.
Both inverse orders are retained. Neither a unit Jacobian nor
a tame-generation assertion is substituted for (5).

The image
\[
C_D=\operatorname{image}
   \bigl(\mathscr W_D\longrightarrow\mathscr S_{e,d,N}\bigr)
\tag{6}
\]
is constructible by Chevalley's theorem for a finite-presentation
map.[^chevalley] These loci are increasing: pad the coefficients
of a smaller-degree inverse pair with zeros.

For a complex parameter point, belonging to (6) is equivalent to
having a complex witness (5). Indeed its nonempty finite-type
fiber has a closed point over the algebraically closed field
\(\mathbb C\). Such a witness makes \(F_1\) an actual invariant
ambient coordinate. It is not just a smooth function or an
algebraically independent invariant.

## 5. The conditional uniform-degree equivalence

For each fixed triple \((e,d,N)\), the following are equivalent:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{(a)}&\text{Every complex point of }\mathscr S_{e,d,N}
              \text{ has polynomial kernel;}\\
\text{(b)}&\displaystyle
 \mathscr S_{e,d,N}(\mathbb C)=\bigcup_{D\ge1}C_D(\mathbb C);\\
\text{(c)}&\text{There is a finite }D_0
 \text{ with }\mathscr S_{e,d,N}(\mathbb C)=C_{D_0}(\mathbb C).
\end{array}
\tag{7}
\]

For (a) implies (b), an adapted frame of a polynomial kernel,
together with the supplied slice, has a polynomial inverse of
some finite degree. For (b) implies (a), (5) gives a fixed actual
ambient coordinate. The slice gives
\(B=(\ker E_P)[s]\), so the original kernel is regular; the
inherited invariant-coordinate regular-kernel theorem recognizes
it.[^recognition] This is exactly the recognition gate already
used by pass43. The equivalence of (b) and (c) is (3).

In particular, the new implication can be stated negatively:
if the required witness degrees are unbounded over one of these
fixed finite-complexity families, then not every action in that
family can have a polynomial kernel. Equivalently, if all those
kernels are polynomial, arbitrarily large minimal witness
degrees within that fixed family are impossible.
This is conditional logic, not a constructed counterexample
or a proof that the degrees are bounded.

The same constructibility and exhaustion argument applies to
any prescribed adjoint-height vector, by adding the exact bracket
equations from pass43 instead of \(E_P(F_1)=0\). It proves
uniformity **if that fixed target covers the entire family**.
It supplies no universal height target and does not depend on
the current supplied-frame closure.

### A stronger adapted variant with an elementary recognition gate

One can instead require the witness to be adapted to the actual
slice already supplied as a parameter. Retain both inverse equations
in (5), and replace the last equation there by
\[
E_P(F_1)=E_P(F_2)=E_P(F_3)=0,\qquad F_4=s.
\tag{8}
\]
These again give a finite-presentation witness scheme and increasing
constructible image loci \(C_D^{\mathrm{ad}}\).
The equation \(F_4=s\) is an exact coefficient equation even if
\(D<d\); it simply excludes parameters whose supplied slice cannot
be represented at that level.

For a single parameter point, a polynomial kernel
\(A=\mathbb C[a_1,a_2,a_3]\) gives the actual inverse pair
for \((a_1,a_2,a_3,s)\), by \(B=A[s]\).
Conversely, a witness (8) makes \(E_P\) equal to the fourth
coordinate partial, since it kills the first three coordinates
and sends the fourth to one. Its kernel is exactly
\(\mathbb C[F_1,F_2,F_3]\).
This equivalence uses only the slice theorem and actual inverse
equations, not the invariant-coordinate regular-kernel theorem.

Applying (3) therefore proves the same equivalence (7) with
every \(C_D\) replaced by \(C_D^{\mathrm{ad}}\).
Thus, conditional on pointwise polynomial kernels in a fixed
family, one uniform forward/inverse degree bound even exists
for coordinate systems adapted to each **supplied** slice.
There is still no value of this bound, no global algebraic choice
of the first three coordinates, and no proof of the pointwise premise.

## 6. Precise limitations and the next obligation

Three distinctions prevent (7) from becoming an unsupported
universal-recognition claim.

First, neither Noetherianity nor constructibility proves (b).
For example the constant constructible sequence
\(\mathbb A^1\setminus\{0\}\) contains the generic point but
misses the origin forever. A generic recognition argument
spreads to an open subset; every remaining closed exceptional
locus still requires an argument. A sufficient family strategy
would establish witness existence at the geometric generic
points of the closed pieces encountered by dimension induction.
This strategy is not carried out here for \(\mathscr S_{e,d,N}\).

Second, the uncountable field matters. Enumerate the nonconstant
polynomials over \(\mathbb Q\), and let \(C_D\) be the union
of the root sets of the first \(D\). These are increasing finite
closed subsets of \(\mathbb A^1\), all defined over \(\mathbb Q\).
Their \(\overline{\mathbb Q}\)-points cover
\(\overline{\mathbb Q}\), but no one level does; their complex
points do not cover \(\mathbb C\).
This is a countercontrol to inferring (3) from an algebraic-point
exhaustion, not a family of sliced-action counterexamples.
Checking every algebraic-coefficient instance would not by this
constructibility argument alone settle all complex coefficients.

Third, surjectivity of a witness morphism does not provide one
global algebraic choice. The map
\(\mathbb G_m\to\mathbb G_m,\ y\mapsto y^2\) is surjective on
complex points but has no regular section: a unit in
\(\mathbb C[t,t^{-1}]\) is \(ct^n\), whose square cannot equal \(t\).
Thus even (c) need not supply inverse coefficients that are
regular functions on the entire parameter scheme.
No simultaneous trivialization is asserted.

The remaining universal statement is still that every pair (1)
has an actual recognized coordinate witness. In the present
formulation this is equivalent to the existence, for every finite
triple \((e,d,N)\), of the conditional bound \(D_0\) in (7).
No numerical value, computable function of the triple, automatic
height-decreasing operation, or finite rejection against all
inverse degrees is obtained.

## 7. Source and verification scope

The finite-level equations and pointwise positive semidecision
are credited to the existing pass43 construction. The present
addition is the explicit bounded-family constructible exhaustion
argument, including its coefficient-field and exceptional-locus
limits. The countable-union lemma and its dimension induction
are proved above. No historical novelty claim is made.
