# Positive bad-rank normal forms for weight-four initial relations

**Status: working algebraic classification and actual-frame reduction.**

Give \(X,Y\) weight one and \(Z,W\) weight two. An irreducible
weight-four polynomial whose quadratic part in \(Z,W\) is nonzero
has the normal forms below under constant good and bad linear changes
and homogeneous quadratic translations of the bad variables.
Rank two leaves a binary quartic. Rank one with a nonzero radical
coefficient has six forms; its zero-radical boundary is a nonsquare
binary-quartic double cover.

Every change used is an actual polynomial automorphism. If the
polynomial generates the full weighted initial kernel of an actual
sliced E-time \((1,1,2,2)\) frame, none of these homogeneous changes
can lower a coordinate's time degree. The normal forms therefore
reduce that exact supplied profile, but do not recognize its kernel
or give arbitrary coordinate entry.

## 1. The group and the actual hypotheses

Work first in the formal polynomial ring over \(\mathbb C\). Write
\[
F=Q(Z,W)+A_2(X,Y)Z+B_2(X,Y)W+C_4(X,Y),
\qquad \operatorname{rank}Q\in\{1,2\}.
\tag{1}
\]
Here \(Q\) is homogeneous quadratic with constant coefficients;
the subscripts indicate homogeneous ordinary degrees in the good
variables. Terms other than \(Q\) may vanish. The allowed changes are
\[
\binom{X'}{Y'}=M\binom X Y,\qquad
\binom{Z'}{W'}=N\binom Z W+
\binom{S_2(X,Y)}{T_2(X,Y)},\qquad M,N\in\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb C).
\tag{2}
\]
The inverse first recovers \((X,Y)=M^{-1}(X',Y')\), then applies
\(N^{-1}\) after subtracting the two quadratics evaluated at that
recovered pair. This verifies both inverse orders and uses only
complex-constant denominators. Multiplying \(F\) by a nonzero
complex constant does not change its principal ideal.

For the application, retain the actual hypotheses of
[the initial-bound proof157](two-linear-two-quadratic-initial-bound-pass157-working.md):
\[
\begin{gathered}
\mathscr B=\mathbb C[X,Y,Z,W]=A[u],\qquad E=\partial_u,
\qquad A=\ker E,\qquad Eu=1,\\
(\deg_E X,\deg_E Y,\deg_E Z,\deg_E W)=(1,1,2,2),\\
a=EX,\quad b=EY,\quad e=E^2Z/2,\quad h=E^2W/2,\\
\theta:\mathbb C[\mathsf X,\mathsf Y,\mathsf Z,\mathsf W]
\longrightarrow A[\eta],\qquad
\theta(\mathsf X,\mathsf Y,\mathsf Z,\mathsf W)
=(a\eta,b\eta,e\eta^2,h\eta^2),\qquad \ker\theta=(F).
\end{gathered}
\tag{3}
\]
The source ideal is the full kernel, generated by an irreducible
polynomial of weight four. In particular the good initials are
algebraically independent. Symbols in the classification denote
formal coordinates until (2) is applied to the actual frame.
The degree profile is an E-time profile, not an adjoint-height tuple.

## 2. Rank two: a product minus a binary quartic

A nonsingular binary quadratic over \(\mathbb C\) is a nonzero
multiple of the product of two independent linear forms. A constant
bad change and a scalar therefore make its quadratic part \(ZW\).
The full polynomial becomes
\[
ZW+A_2Z+B_2W+C_4
=(Z+B_2)(W+A_2)-P_4,
\qquad P_4=A_2B_2-C_4.
\tag{4}
\]
Set \(Z'=Z+B_2\), \(W'=W+A_2\). Their inverse subtracts the
same quadratics, giving the normal form \(ZW-P_4(X,Y)\).

This form is irreducible exactly when \(P_4\ne0\).
For nonzero \(P_4\), treat it as a linear polynomial in \(Z\)
over \(\mathbb C[X,Y,W]\). Its coefficients \(W,-P_4\) are
coprime, so it is primitive and irreducible by the elementary
fraction-field linear-polynomial criterion and Gauss's lemma.
If \(P_4=0\), the displayed product is reducible.
In particular a nonzero square quartic is allowed in rank two.

The complete binary-quartic list, up to good linear changes and
nonzero scalar multiples, is
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{root multiplicities on }\mathbb P^1&P_4\\\hline
4&X^4\\
3+1&X^3Y\\
2+2&X^2Y^2\\
2+1+1&X^2Y(X-Y)\\
1+1+1+1&XY(X-Y)(X-\lambda Y),\quad\lambda\notin\{0,1\}.
\end{array}
\tag{5}
\]
To prove the list, factor the binary form into its four linear
factors, counted with multiplicity. A projective change sends any
three distinct roots to \(\infty,0,1\); the fewer-root cases need
only the corresponding part of this normalization. A scalar is
absorbed in a bad scaling. In the last row, two parameters represent
the same unordered root set exactly when they differ by one of
\[
\lambda,\quad 1-\lambda,\quad \lambda^{-1},\quad
(1-\lambda)^{-1},\quad \frac{\lambda}{\lambda-1},\quad
\frac{\lambda-1}{\lambda}.
\tag{6}
\]
This follows by choosing the ordered three roots used for the
normalization; permutations fixing the unordered four-root set
give precisely these cross-ratio values, with coincidences allowed.

There are no further identifications from quadratic bad translations.
For rank two, the critical point of \(F\) in the bad variables is
unique over \(\mathbb C[X,Y]\). Its value is \(-P_4\).
An invertible affine bad change carries that critical point to the
new one, so the critical value changes only by the scalar multiplying
\(F\) and the good-variable substitution. This also proves that
the binary-quartic data in (5)--(6) classify the rank-two forms
under the full group (2), not merely under a smaller subgroup.

## 3. Rank one: the residual quartic and irreducibility

Make the rank-one quadratic part \(Z^2\), and complete its square.
Every rank-one form becomes
\[
F=Z^2-q_2(X,Y)W-p_4(X,Y).
\tag{7}
\]
For example, completing \(Z^2+A_2Z+B_2W+C_4\) uses
\(Z'=Z+A_2/2\), \(q_2=-B_2\), and \(p_4=A_2^2/4-C_4\).

If \(q_2\ne0\), (7) is always irreducible, even when \(p_4=0\).
As a polynomial in \(W\), its coefficients are \(-q_2\) and
\(Z^2-p_4\); they are coprime because any divisor of \(q_2\)
lies in \(\mathbb C[X,Y]\), whereas the coefficient of \(Z^2\)
in the other term is one. The same primitive linear-polynomial
argument applies. Thus no condition such as coprimality of
\(q_2,p_4\) is necessary.

If \(q_2=0\), irreducibility is exactly the condition that
\(p_4\) is not the square of a binary quadratic. Indeed
\(Z^2-p_4\) is reducible over \(\mathbb C(X,Y)\) exactly when
\(p_4\) is a square there. Unique factorization implies that a
polynomial which is a rational-function square is already a
polynomial square, and a homogeneous square root has degree two.
Gauss's lemma then proves the assertion in the polynomial ring,
also after adjoining the unused variable \(W\).

For \(q_2\ne0\), changing \(W\) by a quadratic changes \(p_4\)
by a multiple of \(q_2\). This residue is the correct invariant.
More explicitly, once good variables are fixed, every bad linear
change preserving rank-one square direction has the form
\[
Z'=\alpha Z+A_2,\qquad W'=\beta W+\gamma Z+B_2,
\qquad \alpha\beta\ne0.
\tag{8}
\]
After substitution, multiplication by \(\alpha^2\), and completing
the new square, the coefficients satisfy
\[
q_2'=\frac{\alpha^2}{\beta}q_2,
\qquad
p_4'=\alpha^2p_4-\frac{\alpha^2}{\beta}q_2B_2
             +\frac{\alpha^2\gamma^2}{4\beta^2}q_2^2 .
\tag{9}
\]
The completed square variable is
\(Z'-A_2+\alpha\gamma q_2/(2\beta)\).
Thus \(p_4\bmod q_2\) changes by a nonzero scalar and a good
linear substitution only. Conversely every change by a multiple
of \(q_2\) is supplied by a quadratic \(W\)-translation.
The vanishing of \(q_2\), and its double-root or distinct-root
type when nonzero, are invariant under (2).

## 4. The six nonzero-radical rank-one forms

Suppose first \(q_2\) has distinct linear factors. Normalize it
to \(XY\). Every binary quartic is congruent modulo \(XY\) to
\(a_4X^4+b_4Y^4\). Diagonal good scalings and a bad scaling
normalize each nonzero coefficient, and interchanging the goods
interchanges the two coefficients. Hence the three forms are
\[
Z^2-XYW,\qquad Z^2-XYW-X^4,\qquad
Z^2-XYW-X^4-Y^4 .
\tag{10}
\]
The zero/nonzero pattern of the two endpoint residues, up to swap,
is invariant under the stabilizer of the pair of roots of \(XY\).
Thus these three cases are distinct under the full group.

If \(q_2\) is a square, normalize it to \(X^2\). The residual
quartic has the form \(a_4XY^3+b_4Y^4\).
The stabilizer has \(X\mapsto cX\), \(Y\mapsto dY+eX\),
with \(cd\ne0\). Modulo \(X^2\), this acts by
\[
b_4Y^4+a_4XY^3
\longmapsto b_4d^4Y^4+(a_4cd^3+4b_4d^3e)XY^3.
\tag{11}
\]
If \(b_4\ne0\), choose \(e=-a_4c/(4b_4)\) to remove the
mixed term; scaling normalizes the remaining coefficient to one.
If \(b_4=0\), the vanishing or nonvanishing of \(a_4\) gives
the other two cases. The resulting distinct forms are
\[
Z^2-X^2W,\qquad Z^2-X^2W-XY^3,\qquad
Z^2-X^2W-Y^4 .
\tag{12}
\]
The residual classes in (11) show directly why the last form
cannot be identified with either preceding form. Complex roots
used to normalize nonzero coefficients here are constants, not
roots extracted from an actual coefficient field.
Equations (10) and (12) exhaust \(q_2\ne0\).

## 5. The zero-radical rank-one boundary

For \(q_2=0\), (9) shows that \(p_4\) is classified up to scalar
and good linear substitution just as in (5). The square cases
\(X^4\), \(X^2Y^2\), and the zero quartic are reducible and must
be omitted. The irreducible algebraic normal forms are precisely
\[
Z^2-X^3Y,\qquad Z^2-X^2Y(X-Y),\qquad
Z^2-XY(X-Y)(X-\lambda Y),\quad\lambda\notin\{0,1\},
\tag{13}
\]
all independent of \(W\), with the identifications (6) in the
last family. Rank one therefore does not imply a nonzero
coefficient of its unused bad direction. Treating that boundary
as automatically reducible would discard all three families.

## 6. Exact preservation of the actual frame and ratio fields

Under (3), apply any change (2) used above to the actual coordinates.
Each new coordinate is a nonzero homogeneous source polynomial
of weight one or two. It cannot lie in \((F)\), because a nonzero
multiple of a weight-four polynomial has weight at least four.
Its leading evaluation under \(\theta\) is therefore nonzero.
Consequently every new good has exact E-degree one and every
new bad has exact E-degree two. The inverse argument is identical.
The new full initial kernel is the transformed principal ideal.

Thus these particular normalizations do not merely provide a
conditional alternative in which a degree drop might produce a
third good coordinate: in the full weight-four case such a drop
cannot happen during a homogeneous normalizing change.

Continue in any displayed normal form, and put
\[
t=b/a,\qquad q=e/a^2,\qquad \rho=h/a^2.
\tag{14}
\]
The variable \(t\) is transcendental over \(\mathbb C\), since
an algebraic value would be complex and make the good speeds
proportional. The exact projection and shift result157 yields:

- For \(F=ZW-P_4\), both bad pivots occur, both selected shifts
  are three, and \(t,\rho\) are algebraically independent.
  The full leading-ratio field is
  \(\mathbb C(t,q,\rho)=\mathbb C(t,\rho)\), with
  \(q=P_4(1,t)/\rho\).
- For \(F=Z^2-q_2W-p_4\) with \(q_2\ne0\), both shifts are
  three. The pairs \((t,q)\) and \((t,\rho)\) are algebraically
  independent, and
  \(\mathbb C(t,q,\rho)=\mathbb C(t,q)\), with
  \(\rho=(q^2-p_4(1,t))/q_2(1,t)\).
- For the boundary (13), the \(Z\) pivot has shift three and the
  omitted \(W\) pivot has shift at most two. The pair \((t,\rho)\)
  is algebraically independent, while \(q^2=p_4(1,t)\).

For example, the assertion about \((t,\rho)\) follows because
the contraction of \((F)\) to \(\mathbb C[X,Y,W]\) is zero
whenever \(F\) involves \(Z\). The fraction field of those
three initials is \(\mathbb C(a\eta,t,\rho)\), and \(a\eta\)
is transcendental over \(\operatorname{Frac}A\).
The other assertions follow by the same calculation and the
displayed relation. These are leading-ratio fields, not a claim
that \(\operatorname{Frac}(\ker_A N)\) equals one of them.

There is a further elementary integral reduction for the first
two forms in (13). Write \(p_4=L_1^2Q_2\), with \(L_1\) linear
and \(Q_2\) nonsquare quadratic. The element
\[
c=\frac{e}{L_1(a,b)}\in\operatorname{Frac}A,
\qquad c^2=Q_2(a,b),\qquad c\in A
\tag{15}
\]
is integral and hence belongs to \(A\). Here \(L_1(a,b)\ne0\).
For completeness \(A\) is normal: an element of its fraction
field integral over \(A\) is integral over the normal ring
\(\mathscr B\), hence is in \(\mathscr B\); it is killed by
\(E\), so lies in \(A\). In the initial algebra, adjoining
\(c\eta\) is an explicit integral extension satisfying
\((c\eta)^2=Q_2(a\eta,b\eta)\) and
\(L_1(a\eta,b\eta)c\eta=e\eta^2\).
Similarly the first form in (12) gives \(e/a\in A\), because
\((e/a)^2=h\).
These integral elements are not asserted to be original coordinates.

## 7. Scope of the reduction

This note classifies positive quadratic bad rank completely under
the stated homogeneous automorphism group. The zero quadratic
bad-rank case is separate. The squarefree boundary in (13) has
an additional actual rational-function obstruction, investigated
separately; that exclusion is not needed for this classification.

The normal forms and ratio fields give concrete remaining actual
\((1,1,2,2)\) subproblems. None of the equations here is promoted
from a leading relation to an invariant polynomial. In particular
neither a rational leading surface nor the integral element (15)
supplies a coordinate inverse or a cancellation theorem.
No lower-initial-rank case, arbitrary all-positive profile, or
unrestricted coordinate-entry statement is included.

The proof uses factorization of binary forms, constant linear
algebra, polynomial inverses, and the explicit retained157 shift
theorem. No new external classification theorem is invoked.
No earlier file, test, packet, or index was changed, and no checker
was run or imported. This working reduction is not a historical
novelty claim, human expert certification, or formal verification.
