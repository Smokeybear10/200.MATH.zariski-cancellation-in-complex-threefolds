# Integral topology isolates the still-compatible nodal plinth component

The fixed surface has not been embedded in affine three-space, and its
compatible action has not been extended. There is nevertheless a stronger
necessary condition on any such extension: **the prescribed nodal component
would be disjoint from every other component of the ambient plinth.**

The three free node lines pass the integral lattice test. The new exclusion
occurs if another plinth component passes through the sole fixed base point:
its doubled fiber would create a nonzero mod-two cokernel in the quotient
homology diagram. This proof needs the global absence of normalization
cycles; a local rational component count alone does not give it.

## 1. The unchanged conditional setting

Use the exact surface, gluing and primitive action of
[pass 33](nodal-plinth-realization-pass33.md):
\[
B_0=\mathbb C[u,y,z]/((u-1)(u-i)z-y(y-1)),\qquad d=u^4-1,
\]
\[
R=B_0\times_{\mathbb C[t]^6}\mathbb C[t]^3,
\qquad D=u\delta_0.
\tag{1}
\]
The conductor pairs are the unchanged pairs of normalization values
\((1,i),(1,-i),(-1,i)\), with their already specified branches and scaled
parameters. No re-pairing is allowed.

Assume a primitive LND \(E\) on \(W=\mathbb C[X,Y,Z]\), an invariant
prime \(F\), and an identification \(W/(F)=R\) taking its restriction to
\(D\). Put \(C=\ker E=\mathbb C[f,g]\), and let
\(\pi:\mathbb A^3\to\mathbb A^2\) be the actual quotient. The
[all-speed theorem](all-speed-ambient-obstruction-pass33.md) gives the
actual embedded invariant residue algebra
\[
S=C/(F)=\mathbb C+d\mathbb C[s],\qquad s=u^2,
\qquad d=s^2-1.
\tag{2}
\]
Thus \(\Gamma_0=V(F)\) is the nodal curve with normalization the
\(s\)-line and node \(b_{\mathrm n}\) identifying \(s=1,-1\).
Write \(b_0\) for its distinct smooth point \(s=0\).

The exact scheme fibers and normalization counts inherited from (1) are:

| Base point on \(\Gamma_0\) | Reduced fiber | Scheme structure | \((q_0,r_0,c)\) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| \(b_{\mathrm n}\) | three disjoint lines | reduced, translating | \((4,2,3)\) |
| \(b_0\) | one line | a doubled line, fixed | \((1,1,1)\) |
| every other point | two disjoint lines | reduced, translating | \((2,1,2)\) |

Here \(q_0\) counts points of the normalized intermediate curve over
the base point, and \(r_0\) counts analytic branches of \(\Gamma_0\).
The normalized finite map is exactly \(u\mapsto s=u^2\). The action
\(D\) is nonzero on every point in either translating row of the table.

## 2. Integral diagram and the necessary global cycle check

Choose the **fully retained** exceptional curve \(\Gamma\) of Kaliman's
Theorem 5.2(i). For a primitive action this is precisely the reduced
plinth support, as proved in
[pass 16](whole-plinth-component-pass16.md), Section 2. Every retained
component \(\Gamma_j\) has normalization \(\mathbb A^1\), and its
normalized finite intermediate map has generic degree \(N_j\ge2\).
The distinguished \(\Gamma_0\) is one of these components because its
generic quotient fiber has two lines.

Take a finite set \(\Sigma\subset\Gamma\) containing every singular,
intersection and exceptional value required for Kaliman's product and
Thom diagram. Include the particular point under consideration. Set
\(Y^*=\mathbb A^2\setminus\Sigma\),
\(X^*=\mathbb A^3\setminus\pi^{-1}(\Sigma)\), and put
\(\check Y=\mathbb A^2\setminus\Gamma\),
\(\check X=\pi^{-1}(\check Y)\simeq\check Y\times\mathbb A^1\).
All homology in this note has **integer coefficients**.

The source's pair and Thom maps give the commutative exact diagram
\[
\begin{array}{ccccc}
H_3(X^*)&\xrightarrow{\phi'}&H_1(Z^*)&\longrightarrow&H_2(\check X)\longrightarrow0\\
\downarrow\delta_3&&\downarrow\theta_*&&\downarrow\simeq\\
H_3(Y^*)&\xrightarrow{\phi}&H_1(\Gamma^*)&\longrightarrow&H_2(\check Y)\longrightarrow0.
\end{array}
\tag{3}
\]
The final zeros use \(H_2(X^*)=H_2(Y^*)=0\): removing a complex curve
from smooth contractible complex three-space does not change homology
in degree two, and removing finitely many points from complex two-space
does not either. The source uses the same vanishing in Proposition 3.2,
Step 2. Its diagram and excision argument are integral, not just rational.

Let \(P'\subset H_1(Z^*)\) be the subgroup generated by the small
normalized puncture loops. These loops are independent, and
\[
H_1(Z^*)/P'\simeq H_1(Z^{\mathrm{norm}})
\tag{4}
\]
is free abelian. The source's excision support argument gives
\(\operatorname{im}\phi'\subset P'\). Since every normalization
of \(\Gamma\) is an affine line, \(H_1(\Gamma^*)\) is generated
entirely by its puncture loops. Finiteness of \(\theta\) implies that
\(\theta_*(P')\) has finite index in \(H_1(\Gamma^*)\): each
normalized base puncture has a preimage, whose loop maps to a positive
integer multiple of that base loop.

For completeness this proves the absence of a hidden source cycle.
Given \(\alpha\in H_1(Z^*)\), choose \(m>0\) and \(v\in P'\)
with \(\theta_*(m\alpha)=\theta_*v\). By (3),
\(m\alpha-v\in\ker\theta_*\subset\operatorname{im}\phi'\subset P'\).
The torsion-free quotient (4) then gives \(\alpha\in P'\). Hence
\[
H_1(Z^*)=P',\qquad H_1(Z^{\mathrm{norm}})=0.
\tag{5}
\]
This is also the relevant conclusion of the source's Step 2. It matters
here because without (5), a cycle not generated by puncture loops could
couple different base points and invalidate a local cokernel argument.

For each \(b\in\Sigma\), let \(V_b\) and \(U_b\) be the source
and base puncture lattices over \(b\). Equations (5) and the affine-line
base normalizations give actual direct sums
\[
H_1(Z^*)=\bigoplus_bV_b,\qquad
H_1(\Gamma^*)=\bigoplus_bU_b.
\tag{6}
\]
The map \(\theta_*\) preserves these summands. Kaliman's Lemma 3.1
gives
\[
\operatorname{im}\phi=\bigoplus_b\mathbb Z\gamma_b,
\qquad \gamma_b=\sum_{\text{base branches at }b}\gamma_{b,j}.
\tag{7}
\]
There is no affine puncture relation coupling different \(b\)'s.
Taking cokernels in (3) shows the necessary **integral surjectivity**
\[
V_b\longrightarrow U_b/\mathbb Z\gamma_b
\quad\text{is surjective for every }b.
\tag{8}
\]
Here the map is induced by \(\theta_*\). This is stronger than its
surjectivity after tensoring with \(\mathbb Q\).

## 3. Other plinth components cannot meet a free fiber of (1)

The [pass-33 branch count](all-speed-ambient-obstruction-pass33.md) is
\[
c_b=q_b-r_b+1.
\tag{9}
\]
At every point of \(\Gamma_0\), the table already saturates the
contribution of this one component:
\(c_b=q_{0,b}-r_{0,b}+1\). Each other retained component has
\(q_{j,b}\ge r_{j,b}\). Therefore (9) forces separately
\[
q_{j,b}=r_{j,b}
\quad\text{for every other retained component through }b.
\tag{10}
\]
Thus each of its normalized base branches has exactly one normalized
source preimage. The ramification index of that preimage equals the
whole generic degree \(N_j\), because a finite map of smooth complex
curves has sum of ramification indices \(N_j\) over any point.

Suppose first \(b\ne b_0\). The actual entire fiber is free. The
primitive Jacobian identity \(E=\kappa\operatorname{Jac}(f,g,\cdot)\)
makes \(\pi\) a submersion at its points. At a point of any fiber
component choose a transverse complex two-disc. Its boundary represents
the integral fiber-component Thom generator, and its image is the base
linking three-sphere with degree \(+1\). Consequently (3) gives
\[
\theta_*\phi'(\text{that generator})=\gamma_b.
\tag{11}
\]
For an analytic branch of another component, (10) leaves just one
possible source puncture. Its coefficient in (11) says
\(N_j a=1\) for an integer \(a\). This contradicts \(N_j\ge2\).
This argument never infers surjectivity *after normalization* from the
surface map; it uses the integral loop coefficient instead.

Hence all other retained components are already disjoint from
\(\Gamma_0\setminus\{b_0\}\), including its node.

## 4. The doubled fiber excludes the last possible intersection

At \(b_0\), the actual fiber is one doubled line. At a point on that
line the surface \(R\) is smooth, so the pulled-back equation \(F\)
is an ambient local coordinate. A base parameter on the smooth curve
\(\Gamma_0\) restricts, up to a unit, to \(s=u^2\) on \(R\).
Choose a transverse two-disc to the line. The quotient map on this
disc has local intersection multiplicity two, since its fiber ideal
is \((F,u^2)\) in suitable analytic coordinates. Its linking sphere
therefore has degree \(+2\), not \(+1\).

The unique integral fiber-component generator consequently satisfies
\[
\theta_*\phi'(1)=2\gamma_{b_0}.
\tag{12}
\]
Equation (10) still applies. On any other normalized base branch there
is a unique source puncture, so (12) gives \(N_j a=2\) with
\(N_j\ge2\). Necessarily \(N_j=2\) and \(a=1\).
The distinguished branch also has its single index-two preimage
under \(u\mapsto u^2\). Thus, if there are \(r\) analytic branches
of the full retained curve at \(b_0\), the entire local matrix is
\[
\theta_{*,b_0}=2I_r:\mathbb Z^r\longrightarrow\mathbb Z^r.
\tag{13}
\]
Modulo the diagonal vector \(\gamma_{b_0}=(1,\ldots,1)\), its
cokernel is
\[
\frac{\mathbb Z^r}{2\mathbb Z^r+\mathbb Z(1,\ldots,1)}
\simeq(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^{r-1}.
\tag{14}
\]
Surjectivity (8) therefore forces \(r=1\). Any other component
through \(b_0\) contributes at least one additional branch, which
is impossible.

Combining Sections 3 and 4 proves the conditional global conclusion
\[
\boxed{\Gamma_0\cap\Gamma_j=\varnothing
\quad\text{for every other component of the primitive ambient plinth}.}
\tag{15}
\]
No assertion is made that the whole plinth is connected. Disjoint
additional components remain possible under this argument.

## 5. Consequences for the unavoidable first modification

Write the full plinth as in the preceding first-center proof,
\(a=F^k\beta\), with \(k\ge1\) and \(F\nmid\beta\).
Equation (15) implies that the restriction of \(\beta\) to
\(\Gamma_0\) has no zeros. The units of
\(S=\mathbb C+d\mathbb C[s]\subset\mathbb C[s]\) are exactly
the nonzero constants. It follows that
\[
\overline\beta=\kappa\in\mathbb C^*,\qquad
\beta=\kappa+FQ\quad(Q\in C).
\tag{16}
\]
Thus the extra plinth factor is a unit along the **entire** prescribed
surface, not just at a generic point. The singular first modification
from [pass 33](nodal-plinth-realization-pass33.md) remains unavoidable,
but the earlier allowance that \(\beta\) might vanish at its node is
now removed under the same actual-realization hypotheses.

There is also a limited pencil constraint. For any other retained
component with polynomial normalization \(\nu_j:\mathbb A^1\to\Gamma_j\),
the polynomial \(F\circ\nu_j\) has no zeros by (15). It is therefore a
nonzero constant \(a_j\). Hence \(\Gamma_j\) is an irreducible component
of \(F^{-1}(a_j)\). This does not make \(F-a_j\) irreducible or say
that \(\beta\) is a polynomial in \(F\).

## 6. The three node lines themselves pass the integral test

Order the normalized node punctures as \((1,-1,i,-i)\), with base
branches \(s=1,-1\). The finite loop map is
\[
\Theta=\begin{pmatrix}1&1&0&0\\0&0&1&1\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{17}
\]
The three actual paired node lines give the columns
\[
P=\begin{pmatrix}
1&1&0\\0&0&1\\1&0&1\\0&1&0
\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\Theta P=\begin{pmatrix}1&1&1\\1&1&1\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{18}
\]
The coefficient one on each of a pair of branches comes from the
ordinary crossing and the submersive quotient at the translating
line. The unchanged scaling of its orbit parameter does not alter
this complex intersection number.

These columns form a full integral basis of
\[
\Lambda=\{(a,b,c,e)\in\mathbb Z^4:a+b=c+e\}.
\tag{19}
\]
Indeed every such vector equals \(P(c-b,e,b)^{\mathsf T}\), and
these coefficients are unique. There is no finite index or hidden
torsion. The induced map from the quotient by \(\Lambda\) to
\(\mathbb Z^2/\mathbb Z(1,1)\) is an isomorphism. Thus the fixed
pairing tree is fully compatible with this integral diagram.

When no other component meets \(b_0\), its remaining one-dimensional
matrix is \((2)\). Both local quotient lattices are zero, so its
degree-two ramification also gives no contradiction by itself.
The new obstruction is to an **intersection** with another retained
component, not to the prescribed doubled fiber alone.
