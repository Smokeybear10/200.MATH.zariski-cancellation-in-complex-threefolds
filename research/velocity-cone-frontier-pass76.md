# The velocity cone and the remaining primitive fixed-point problem

The strongest new reduction identifies good constant directions with
translation symmetries of one explicitly characterized algebraic cone.
This excludes the exactly-two-direction case and supplies a new
leading-coefficient recognition test. Several larger candidate families
also fail to produce a primitive fixed point under the exact required
hypotheses. None of these results solves unrestricted cancellation.

The full working question remains A[s]=C^[4] implies A=C^[3], under
the still-unconfirmed complex-threefold interpretation of the professor's
email. Producing a nice frame for an arbitrary sliced action is still
unproved. The deductions below start only after such an actual frame
has been supplied.

## 1. The new geometric identification

Let
\[
B=\mathbb C[x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4],\qquad
E=P\cdot\partial,\quad Es=1,\quad E(P)=0,\quad
J=\operatorname{Jac}P,\quad A=\ker E.
\tag{1}
\]
The retained pass74 argument gives J^3=0 and rank J at most two.
The square-zero case is already recognized. In the remaining case,
rank J=2, rank J^2=1 and rank[J|P]=3.

The [leading-boundary proof](nice-leading-boundary-pass76.md) applies
de Bondt's homogeneous zero-set component theorem after checking the
full augmented rank, gcd and dimension hypotheses. The cone over the
velocity image is an irreducible hypersurface V(F). The radical of
the ideal of the degree-d leading velocity parts is generated by F
times distinct linear factors. If the leading gcd has only linear
irreducible factors, then F is linear and the original kernel is
polynomial. The constant-gcd alternative also recognizes the action.

The [apex proof](nice-leading-apex-pass76.md) gives the sharper identity
\[
G_2=\{c\in\mathbb C^4:J^2c=0\}
   =\{c:T_cF=0\},\qquad
\dim G_2\in\{0,1,3\}\quad(J^2\ne0).
\tag{2}
\]
Its key step identifies two nonzero rows in the one-dimensional
annihilator of im J plus the fraction-field line spanned by P. Cone density and
homogeneity then turn a relation evaluated at P into a polynomial
identity. No pointwise rank condition or unknown unit multiplier is
used to assert a global inverse.

Thus the three-dimensional alternative means F is linear and is
already recognized. A nonlinear F has no apex or exactly one
independent apex. The known conic action realizes the latter, while
already having a polynomial kernel. In particular, a nonlinear F
is not a counterexample certificate, and F(P)=0 does not imply E(F)=0.

## 2. What an existing good direction additionally forces

Suppose F is nonlinear and a constant good direction is supplied.
Put that direction in the w-coordinate by an actual constant change.
The [invariant-omission proof](nice-good-direction-invariant-omission-pass76.md)
checks that de Bondt's Theorem 5.1(iv) now applies to the reindexed
five-variable homogenization. The theorem allows the one linear
invariant z that is actually present. Its distinct no-linear-invariant
corollary is not used.

Homogenizing an original invariant expressible in three linear forms
then gives
\[
\ker E\cap\ker T_c=\mathbb C
\quad\text{for every nonzero constant }c,\qquad
\gcd(P_1,P_2,P_3)=1
\quad(T=\partial_w).
\tag{3}
\]
The full transverse gcd is treated directly by invariant-factor
closure and derivative degree, not equated without proof to the
factor in a source parametrization. Equation (3) is about polynomial
invariants and content only. It does not say that the transverse
velocity ideal is the unit ideal or that a primitive derived action
has no fixed points.

These deductions sharpen the residual problem. With no good direction,
one must establish a cone apex or find another recognition route.
With one good direction and nonlinear F, a nonconstant invariant
omitting any constant direction would already recognize the action.
Otherwise a genuine proof must handle invariants using all four
original variables and the possible codimension-two fixed locus.

## 3. Construction routes now excluded

The following statements have different hypotheses. They must not be
combined into a classification of every nice sliced action.

| [Triangular f,g pencil](triangular-nice-pencil-classification-pass76.md) | A simultaneous partial/nice frame exists exactly when f,g are constant-linearly dependent, including zero boundaries. Every compatible nonzero primitive field has a slice. | This iff is not asserted after adding k(A). |
| [Triangular f,g,k pencil](triangular-pencil-fixed-point-exclusion-pass76.md) | Any primitive fixed point makes every T-invariant nice polynomial lie in C+Cs+Cb, contradicting a three-generator invariant coordinate frame. | This proves primitive freeness under compatibility, not an iff frame criterion or a new global slice classification. |
| [Linearly triangular original nice action](triangular-nice-primitive-freeness-pass76.md) | Every independent constant-good derived field has an explicit primitive intrinsic slice. Nonunit first speeds and every zero branch are handled. | A nonlinear triangularization need not keep the given T constant. |
| [Variable-direction commuting-slice extension](commuting-slice-family-exclusion-pass76.md) | Every independent constant-good derived field has a primitive slice, including members not affine-linearly triangularizable. | This explicit family is not every nice action with an affine slice. |

The k-pencil argument is stronger than the first origin-jet test.
At a hypothetical primitive fixed point, exact vanishing orders force
one of two nonresonant coefficient eliminations or a resonant polynomial
equation 3b p'+2p=0. All three branches eliminate the missing invariant
generator. The proof permits arbitrary degrees and arbitrary k satisfying
the forced local orders. A finite degree search would not give that
conclusion.
