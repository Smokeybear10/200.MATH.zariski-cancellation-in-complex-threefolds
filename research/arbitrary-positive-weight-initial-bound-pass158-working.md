# Initial relations in arbitrary positive E-time weights

**Status: working proof of necessary conditions on an actual sliced frame.**

For a sliced complex polynomial four-ring, if all four supplied
coordinates have positive E-time degree, their leading coefficients
have transcendence degree at most two. The algebra generated by
their four weighted initial forms therefore has transcendence degree
at most three.

If its full prime kernel has height one, its irreducible relation
has weight at most the sum of the four time degrees minus two.
Every coordinate partial that differentiates that relation attains
the maximum all-ring shift allowed by the other three time degrees.
The proof uses actual cofactors, a leading LND on the original
ring, and an invariant-growth argument. It does not bound the sum
of the time degrees or recognize an arbitrary kernel.

## 1. Actual positive time degrees

Assume
\[
\begin{gathered}
B=\mathbb C[X_1,X_2,X_3,X_4],\qquad
E\in\operatorname{LND}_{\mathbb C}(B),\qquad Eu=1,\qquad
A=\ker E,\qquad B=A[u],\\
d_i:=\deg_E X_i\ge1,\qquad S=d_1+d_2+d_3+d_4,\\
X_i=\sum_{r=0}^{d_i}c_{i,r}u^r,\qquad
c_i:=c_{i,d_i}\in A\setminus\{0\},\qquad
c_i=E^{d_i}X_i/d_i! .
\end{gathered}
\tag{1}
\]
The displayed generators are actual polynomial coordinates.
The slice \(u\) is not assumed to be an ambient coordinate.
All four weights are the exact E-time degrees, not selected
adjoint degrees or ordinary polynomial degrees.

Write \(K=\operatorname{Frac}A\). The algebra
\(A\simeq B/(u)\) is finitely generated and has transcendence
degree three. Choose a separating transcendence basis
\(\xi_1,\xi_2,\xi_3\) of \(K/\mathbb C\).
Coordinate derivatives extend through the finite separable
extension of \(\mathbb C(\xi_1,\xi_2,\xi_3)\).
The differentials \(d\xi_1,d\xi_2,d\xi_3,du\) form a basis
over \(K(u)\). This does not assert that \(A\) is a polynomial
ring or that these derivatives preserve \(A\).

## 2. Cofactors and the actual all-ring leading derivation

The original coordinate volume satisfies
\[
dX_1\wedge dX_2\wedge dX_3\wedge dX_4
=J(u)\,d\xi_1\wedge d\xi_2\wedge d\xi_3\wedge du,
\qquad J(u)=J_0\in K^* .
\tag{2}
\]
Initially the determinant is a nonzero polynomial in \(u\)
over \(K\). The actual flow \(\exp(tE)\) has inverse
\(\exp(-tE)\) over \(B[t]\). Its original-coordinate Jacobian
is a unit of \(B[t]\), and evaluation at \(t=0\) makes it one.
The flow fixes \(K\) and translates \(u\), so invariance of
the volume gives \(J(u+t)=J(u)\). This proves (2).
It does not make \(J_0\) a complex constant.

For each \(j\), let \(T_j=\partial_{X_j}\) be the actual
coordinate partial, and put \(R_j=S-d_j\).
The cofactor for \(T_j(\xi_\ell)\) uses the other three
coordinate rows and one \(u\)-derivative column.
Their degree sum is \(R_j\); the \(u\)-derivative lowers
one entry's bound by one. The cofactor for \(T_j(u)\) uses
only horizontal columns. Thus
\[
T_j(A)\subset A[u]_{\le R_j-1},\qquad
T_j(u)\in A[u]_{\le R_j}.
\tag{3}
\]
The cofactor calculation first proves these bounds over \(K\).
The separating-basis chain rule extends the horizontal bound
to every element of \(K\). Actual membership in \(B=A[u]\)
then returns the coefficients to \(A\) by uniqueness in \(K[u]\).

There is always nonzero horizontal action.
If \(T_j(A)=0\), choose any \(i\ne j\).
Because \(d_i>0\), \(\partial_u X_i\ne0\); hence
\(0=T_j(X_i)=(\partial_uX_i)T_j(u)\) forces \(T_j(u)=0\).
That would make \(T_j=0\) on \(A[u]\), a contradiction.

Fix \(j\). Let \(k_0\ge0\) be the largest degree of the images
of finite algebra generators of \(A\). The derivation rule
gives the same bound on all of \(T_j(A)\).
With degree of zero equal to \(-\infty\), put
\(k=\max\{k_0,\deg_uT_j(u)-1\}\).
Then \(0\le k\le R_j-1\).
Write \(T_j|_A=\sum_{r=0}^{k}u^rD_r\), with \(D_r\) derivations
of \(A\), and \(T_j(u)=\sum_{r=0}^{k+1}h_ru^r\).

Adjoin an invariant indeterminate \(\varepsilon\), and on the
Laurent extension let \(\varphi\) fix \(A,\varepsilon\) and
send \(u\) to \(u/\varepsilon\). Then
\[
\begin{gathered}
(T_j)_\varepsilon=\varepsilon^k\varphi T_j\varphi^{-1},\\
(T_j)_\varepsilon(f)
=\sum_{r=0}^{k}D_r(f)u^r\varepsilon^{k-r}\quad(f\in A),\\
(T_j)_\varepsilon(u)
=\sum_{r=0}^{k+1}h_ru^r\varepsilon^{k+1-r}.
\end{gathered}
\tag{4}
\]
This is an actual conjugate LND multiplied by an invariant
unit. It preserves \(B[\varepsilon]\); its restriction is
locally nilpotent by injection into the Laurent ring.
Specializing \(\varepsilon=0\) gives a nonzero LND
\(\mathcal D\) of \(B\), with
\(\mathcal D|_A=u^kD_k\) and
\(\mathcal D(u)=h_{k+1}u^{k+1}\).

For an LND on a characteristic-zero domain,
\(f\mid\mathcal D(f)\) implies \(\mathcal D(f)=0\):
otherwise additivity of LND degree on products contradicts
the degree drop of a nonzero derivative.
Apply this to \(u\) to get \(h_{k+1}=0\).
Nonvanishing of \(\mathcal D\) now gives \(D_k\ne0\),
\(k=k_0\), and \(\deg_uT_j(u)\le k\).
The identity
\(\mathcal D^r(f)=u^{kr}D_k^r(f)\) for \(f\in A\)
proves that \(N_j:=D_k\) is a nonzero LND of \(A\).
This is the actual leading mechanism125/135/157, reconstructed
with arbitrary positive row degrees.[^1]

For every coordinate partial the conclusion is
\[
\begin{gathered}
0\le k_j\le S-d_j-1,\qquad
0\ne N_j=[u^{k_j}]T_j|_A\in\operatorname{LND}(A),\qquad
\deg_uT_j(u)\le k_j,\\
\deg_E T_j(f)\le\deg_E f+k_j
\quad(0\ne f\in B,\ T_j(f)\ne0),\\
N_j(c_i)=0\quad(1\le i\le4).
\end{gathered}
\tag{5}
\]
The shift is exact because it is attained on an element of
\(A\) on which \(N_j\) is nonzero.
To check the last line, the coefficient of \(u^{d_i+k_j}\)
in \(T_j(X_i)\) is \(N_j(c_i)\).
Differentiating \(u\) contributes only smaller degrees.
But \(T_j(X_i)\) is zero or one and \(d_i+k_j>0\).
This is where positivity of every weight is essential.

## 3. Rank of the generated coordinate initials

Use formal source variables and a new independent \(\eta\):
\[
\begin{gathered}
\mathscr P=\mathbb C[\mathsf X_1,\mathsf X_2,\mathsf X_3,\mathsf X_4],
\qquad \operatorname{wt}\mathsf X_i=d_i,\\
\theta:\mathscr P\longrightarrow A[\eta],\qquad
\mathsf X_i\longmapsto c_i\eta^{d_i},\\
\operatorname{trdeg}_{\mathbb C}\mathbb C(c_1,c_2,c_3,c_4)\le2,
\qquad
1\le\operatorname{trdeg}_{\mathbb C}\operatorname{im}\theta\le3 .
\end{gathered}
\tag{6}
\]
For any \(j\), the local-slice theorem gives
\(\operatorname{trdeg}_{\mathbb C}\ker_A N_j=2\).
All four coefficients belong to that kernel by (5).
This proves the upper bounds without a polynomial presentation
or finite-generation assumption on \(\ker_A N_j\).
The lower bound follows from \(\eta\) being independent and
each \(c_i\eta^{d_i}\) being nonconstant and transcendental
over \(\mathbb C\).

The subalgebra in (6) is generated only by the four coordinate
initials. It is not identified with the whole associated graded
algebra \(\operatorname{gr}_E B=A[\eta]\).
The latter has transcendence degree four.

Suppose the image in (6) has transcendence degree three.
Then \(\ker\theta\) is a height-one prime in the UFD
\(\mathscr P\), generated by an irreducible weighted homogeneous
polynomial \(F\).
The kernel is graded, and irreducible factors of a weighted
homogeneous polynomial with positive weights are weighted
homogeneous, which justifies this choice.
Write \(D=\operatorname{wt}F\).
For every \(j\) with \(F_{\mathsf X_j}\ne0\), we will prove
\[
D\le d_j+k_j-1\le S-2 .
\tag{7}
\]
No principal-kernel assertion is made when the initial rank
is one or two.

## 4. Exact derivative multiplicity

Fix an occurring pivot \(j\). Let \(P\in\mathscr P\) have
weighted degree \(M\), and suppose its top weighted part
is \(P_M=F^\ell G\) with exact multiplicity
\(\ell\ge1\), \(F\nmid G\).
In characteristic zero \(F_{\mathsf X_j}\notin(F)\), because
\(F\) depends on \(\mathsf X_j\).
The usual product rule gives
\[
\begin{gathered}
\partial_{\mathsf X_j}^{\ell}P_M
\equiv\ell!\,G(F_{\mathsf X_j})^\ell\pmod{F},\\
\deg_E\bigl(T_j^\ell P(X_1,X_2,X_3,X_4)\bigr)=M-d_j\ell,\\
\deg_E P(X_1,X_2,X_3,X_4)\ge M-(d_j+k_j)\ell .
\end{gathered}
\tag{8}
\]
The displayed residue is nonzero in the domain
\(\mathscr P/(F)\). It has weight \(M-d_j\ell\).
Lower weighted parts of \(P\), after the same derivatives,
have strictly smaller weight or vanish.
Thus the top nonzero initial term really gives the exact
E-degree in (8); no cancellation with a lower source part
is possible.
Iterating the all-ring shift in (5) gives the last inequality.
This is the derivative-multiplicity argument133/157 with
the actual pivot cost \(d_j\), not a fixed quadratic cost.[^2]

## 5. Three invariant variables force a strict inequality

For \(f\in A\setminus\mathbb C\), let \(P\) be its unique
polynomial in the original coordinates and \(M>0\) its
weighted degree. Since \(f\) has E-degree zero, its top
weighted part lies in \(\ker\theta=(F)\).
Writing its exact multiplicity as above gives
\[
\ell D\le M\le(d_j+k_j)\ell .
\tag{9}
\]
Existence of a nonconstant invariant first yields
\(D\le d_j+k_j\).

If \(D=d_j+k_j\), equality in (9) forces
\(M=\ell D\) and \(G\) to have weight zero, so \(G\)
is a nonzero complex constant.
Every invariant top is therefore a scalar power of \(F\).
Let \(\mathcal F_MA\) be the invariants of original weighted
degree at most \(M\).
Its successive graded quotient injects into the corresponding
space of weighted top polynomials, and has dimension at
most one for multiples of \(D\), zero otherwise.

Choose three algebraically independent invariants, and let
\(C_0>0\) be the largest of their original weighted degrees.
Their monomials of total exponent at most \(n\) give
\[
\binom{n+3}{3}
\le\dim_{\mathbb C}\mathcal F_{C_0n}A
\le\left\lfloor\frac{C_0n}{D}\right\rfloor+1
\qquad(n\ge0),
\tag{10}
\]
which is impossible for large \(n\).
Hence \(D\le d_j+k_j-1\), proving (7).
This argument needs no finite set of initial generators for
the invariant ring and no subduction algorithm.

## 6. The exact maximal shift is equivalent to an independent projection

Fix \(j\), and list the other three indices in their original
order. Write their leading coefficients as
\(\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell_3\), their weights as
\(w_1,w_2,w_3\), and \(R=w_1+w_2+w_3=S-d_j\).
Their leading differential wedge is
\[
\begin{gathered}
d(\ell_1u^{w_1})\wedge d(\ell_2u^{w_2})\wedge d(\ell_3u^{w_3})
=u^R\,d\ell_1\wedge d\ell_2\wedge d\ell_3
 +u^{R-1}\Omega_j\wedge du,\\
\Omega_j
=w_3\ell_3\,d\ell_1\wedge d\ell_2
 -w_2\ell_2\,d\ell_1\wedge d\ell_3
 +w_1\ell_1\,d\ell_2\wedge d\ell_3,\qquad
d\ell_1\wedge d\ell_2\wedge d\ell_3=0 .
\end{gathered}
\tag{11}
\]
The last vanishing follows from the coefficient-field bound
in (6).
In the separating differential basis, the coefficients of
\(\Omega_j\), up to their signs and division by \(J_0\),
are exactly the degree-\(R-1\) horizontal cofactors for \(T_j\).
Lower time coefficients of the actual coordinates cannot
contribute at that maximal degree.
Thus \(\Omega_j\ne0\) is equivalent to a nonzero maximal
horizontal coefficient.
The quotient rule ensures that nonvanishing detected on \(K\)
also occurs on some element of \(A\).

Apply the same calculation with the independent variable
\(\eta\) in place of \(u\).
In characteristic zero the three initials are algebraically
independent exactly when their differential wedge is nonzero.
Because its horizontal term vanishes in (11), this is
equivalent to \(\Omega_j\ne0\). Consequently
\[
k_j=S-d_j-1
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\operatorname{trdeg}_{\mathbb C}
\mathbb C(c_i\eta^{d_i}:i\ne j)=3 .
\tag{12}
\]
This argument introduces neither weighted roots nor a
choice of ratio-field generators.

Under the full height-one kernel \((F)\), the other three
initials are independent exactly when \(F\) involves
\(\mathsf X_j\).
Indeed their kernel is the contraction of \((F)\) to the
source subring omitting \(\mathsf X_j\).
If \(F\) involves that variable, a nonzero multiple has
positive degree in it and cannot belong to the smaller
subring. Otherwise \(F\) itself lies there.

Therefore every appearing pivot has the exact maximal
shift \(k_j=S-d_j-1\), and the first inequality in (7)
becomes precisely \(D\le S-2\).
An omitted pivot instead has
\(k_j\le S-d_j-2\).
If the full initial rank is at most two, the latter bound
holds for every coordinate partial.
It does not make that initial kernel principal.

## 7. What the statement does not supply

All weights in this theorem are positive.
This cannot be silently removed: in the actual translation
frame \((u,a,b,c)\) with \(E=\partial_u\), the weights are
\((1,0,0,0)\). The four leading coefficients are
\((1,a,b,c)\), whose field has transcendence degree three;
the generated initial algebra \(\mathbb C[\eta,a,b,c]\)
has transcendence degree four.
This is outside (1), not a contradiction to (6).

The height-one condition in Sections 3--6 is also essential
to the stated multiplicity argument.
When the initial rank is smaller, a factor not divisible by
a selected relation can still be another element of the
full initial kernel. The exact derivative degree in (8)
would then fail. Actual157 contains such a height-two
boundary; no assertion about a principal factor can replace
the full-kernel hypothesis.[^3]

Even in height one, \(D\le S-2\) does not produce an
invariant correction to \(F\), a new coordinate, or a
degree-lowering automorphism. It imposes no upper bound
on \(S\), and does not show that a globally minimizing
frame has a prescribed small profile.
This is a necessary condition on every actual all-positive
frame, not universal entry into a recognized class.

The proof is specifically stated for the complex four-ring:
the strict growth step used three algebraically independent
invariants. No unexamined extension to a different dimension
is asserted.
No old adjoint-height profile is invoked.
There is no claim of historical novelty, human expert review,
formal proof-assistant certification, or unrestricted cancellation.
All157 and earlier artifacts are unchanged.
