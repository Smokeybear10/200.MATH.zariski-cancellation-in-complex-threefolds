## 4. Both exact first integrals of the xi-squared coefficient

Put
\[
\Lambda=2H_cX+2\alpha z+\Lambda_0,
\qquad M=\overline T_t-\Lambda v_t+2\mathfrak a\alpha\overline V.
\tag{18}
\]
All t derivatives in this and subsequent matrices hold z,X fixed.
From (13),(17), \(w'+h''v=\mathfrak a\Lambda\) and
\(\overline T_z=\Lambda\). The complete corrected determinant
in (11) is therefore one half of
\[
\mathscr D=\det
\begin{pmatrix}
\overline U_t&\overline U_z&2\xi\\
\mathfrak a\xi^2+v_t\xi+\overline V_t+\mathfrak a\overline U
                         &\xi+\overline V_z&v\\
\mathfrak a\Lambda\xi+\Lambda v_t+M&\Lambda&w
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{19}
\]
The xi in the middle entry of the second row is essential.
The precise calibration is
\[
\mathscr D/2=J_{t,z,\xi}(X)
                 =-a_0^4S_z/\rho\in L^*.
\tag{20}
\]
This value may depend on t,z, but not on X or xi. In particular
no constant coefficient in t,z is artificially set to zero.

The cubic-xi coefficient in (19) cancels identically. The complete
quadratic-xi coefficient is
\[
[\xi^2]\mathscr D
 =-\mathfrak a w\overline U_z
       -2\mathfrak a\Lambda\overline V_z-2M=0.
\tag{21}
\]
Its X-squared coefficient cancels by (17). For the other two
coefficients define
\[
\begin{gathered}

The labels Lz,Ez,Fz are polynomials, not derivative notation.
Exact comparison in (21), dividing only by the nonzero

\[
\begin{aligned}
E_z(q_{\rm r})_z-2H_c(R_{\rm r})_z-2\alpha q_{\rm r}
 &=\frac{2\alpha(t\mathfrak a+2a)}{\mathfrak a}z
                                      +\frac{M_{10}}{\mathfrak a},\\

\end{aligned}
\tag{24}
\]
Neither iota coefficient is assumed complex or nonzero.

Eliminating Rr without dividing any root-dependent factor gives

\Delta_zq_{\rm r}=2H_cR_3-L_zR_2,\qquad

## 5. The full constant-xi scalar removes all finite poles

The ENTIRE constant-xi coefficient of (19), before its factor one
half, is
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathscr D_0={}&w\{\overline U_t\overline V_z
             -\overline U_z(\overline V_t+\mathfrak a\overline U)\}\\
 &+v\overline U_z(\overline T_t+2\mathfrak a\alpha\overline V)
                         -\Lambda v\overline U_t.
\end{aligned}
\tag{27}
\]
Set
\[
\mathcal K=t\mathfrak a+2a,\qquad
\mathcal G=-16H_c^2\delta\delta'+4H_c\mathfrak a u_1
                                      +4H_cv_{10}'+2u_0'\in F_0.
\tag{28}
\]
Substitution of the complete support (14) into (27), with (17),
gives the exact coefficient
\[
\boxed{[X^3]\mathscr D_0
 =4t\mathfrak a\alpha q_{\rm r}(q_{\rm r})_z
 -4tH_c\mathcal K(R_{\rm r})_z
 +\mathcal G(q_{\rm r})_z-8tH_c^2\delta\mathcal K=0.}
\tag{29}
\]
All \((q_{\rm r})_t\) and \((R_{\rm r})_t\) terms cancel
identically BEFORE using (24) or (26). The X-fourth coefficient
also cancels, using the unconditional u1-prime identity (17).
No division by \((q_{\rm r})_z\), \(\mathcal K\) or a moving root
is involved. The factor four in (29) belongs to \(\mathscr D\),
not the scalar \(J_{t,z,\xi}=\mathscr D/2\).

We can now work over an algebraic closure of F0 solely to inspect
rational functions of z. Extend the z derivative to kill this
constant field. Suppose qr has a finite pole at \(z=r_*\) of
order \(n\ge1\). Write its leading Laurent term as
\(c_*(z-r_*)^{-n}\), with \(c_*\ne0\). Then
\(q_{\rm r}(q_{\rm r})_z\) has pole order \(2n+1\) with
nonzero leading coefficient \(-nc_*^2\). The first identity (24)
shows that Rr has pole order at most n, so its z derivative has
pole order at most n+1. The same n+1 bound holds for qr's
derivative. All other coefficients in (29) lie in F0. Since
\(4t\mathfrak a\alpha\ne0\), its first term has uniquely
largest pole order, a contradiction.

This argument allows repeated Delta-z roots and roots algebraic
over F0 that move under t differentiation. No t derivatives of
qr or Rr remain in (29); we never differentiate a Laurent expansion
in t. It is therefore not an assumption that such roots are complex
constants. Nor is any ambient localization at a root performed.

Thus qr has no finite poles. A reduced nonconstant denominator in
F0[z] would have a root over its algebraic closure, so qr belongs
to F0[z]. From the numerator and denominator degree bounds (26),
\[
q_{\rm r}=q_1z+q_0,\qquad q_1,q_0\in F_0.
\tag{30}
\]
This includes qr constant or zero and q1=0. The first identity (24)
then gives \(R_{\rm r}\in F_0[z]_{\le2}\). Writing its z-squared
coefficient as r2, including r2=0, one gets
\[
r_2=-\frac{\alpha}{2H_c}
                  \left(2q_1+\frac{\mathcal K}{\mathfrak a}\right).
\tag{31}
\]

## 6. The last coefficient contradicts the unit multiplier

The coefficient of z in the COMPLETE identity (29) is
\[
4t\mathfrak a\alpha q_1^2-8tH_c\mathcal K r_2
       =\frac{4t\alpha}{\mathfrak a}
                       (\mathfrak a q_1+\mathcal K)^2=0.
\tag{32}
\]
The equality uses (31), not a division by q1 or \(\mathcal K\).
Characteristic zero and the proved nonzero \(t,\alpha,\mathfrak a\) give
\[
q_1=-t-\frac{2a}{\mathfrak a},\qquad
S_z=t+q_1=-\frac{2a}{\mathfrak a}\ne0,\qquad
z_s=-\frac{\mathfrak a}{2a}.
\tag{33}
\]
Finally the genuine494 multiplier in (8) becomes
\[
\kappa=ta\left(z_s+\frac{\mathfrak a}{2a}\right)=0,
\tag{34}
\]
contrary to \(\kappa\in L^*\). This proves that the assumed
h''-nonzero branch cannot occur. Equation (3) then gives f''''=0,
proving precisely (1). No recognition of the remaining h''=0
case is asserted here.
