# The exact radial-center patching obstruction

The two actual radial divisibility tests form one finite linear
congruence problem over the invariant base. Height-one local solutions
leave an exact finite-length obstruction at a closed point. Its
vanishing, not just local solvability, is equivalent to a single
regular center.

In the actual remaining plinth sector there is a stronger limitation:
a regular radial center would give an algebra retraction to the base,
which the whole divisor fiber forbids. Thus the conditional patching
problem below is not an unfinished sufficient repair expected to work
at those plinth points. Local existence already fails there.

## 1. Actual hypotheses and the full denominator

Retain the supplied independent degree-two frame and the actual
determinant fields of
[the generic bad-pair theorem](generic-bad-pair-pass44.md).
Let \(C=\ker D\simeq\mathbb C^{[2]}\), let
\[
R=C_{\mathfrak m},\qquad \mathfrak mR=(a,b),\qquad
L=\operatorname{Frac}R,
\]
where \(a,b\) are a regular parameter pair. Write
\(\mathcal B=B_R=\mathcal A[s_*]\), with \(\mathcal A=A_R\)
and \(Es_*=1\). The inherited generic identifications are
\[
\mathcal B_L=L[x_3,x_4],\qquad
\mathcal B\cap L=R,\qquad
x_i=\mathcal Q_i(x_3,x_4),\quad
\deg\mathcal Q_i\leq2\quad(i=1,2).
\tag{1}
\]
The four \(x_i\) are the actual original polynomial generators.
The parameter pair \(a,b\) is not being identified with the original
bad speeds.

There is a nonzero \(\Delta\in R\) and actual derivations
\[
K_3=-\Delta\partial_{x_4},\qquad
K_4=\Delta\partial_{x_3}
\quad\hbox{on }\mathcal B_L,
\tag{2}
\]
both killing \(R\). For a center \(\eta=(\eta_3,\eta_4)\in R^2\),
put
\[
\Theta_\eta=
\frac{(x_3-\eta_3)K_4-(x_4-\eta_4)K_3}{\Delta}.
\tag{3}
\]
The complete
[radial descent proof](radial-slice-descent-pass55.md)
gives the exact condition
\[
\Theta_\eta(\mathcal B)\subseteq\mathcal B
\iff
(x_3-\eta_3)K_4x_i-(x_4-\eta_4)K_3x_i
\in\Delta\mathcal B\quad(i=1,2).
\tag{4}
\]
All multiplicities of \(\Delta\) remain in (4).
At a height-one prime \(\pi\), if
\(\Delta=u\pi^m\) in \(R_{(\pi)}\), the test is modulo
\(\pi^m\mathcal B_{(\pi)}\), not modulo its radical.

A formally rational center cannot enlarge the class of actual
regular centers: regularity implies
\(\eta_i=x_i-\Theta_\eta x_i\in\mathcal B\cap L=R\).

## 2. A finite cyclic obstruction

Use the \(R\)-module
\[
\mathcal T=(\mathcal B/\Delta\mathcal B)^2
\]
and the following three elements, whose two entries have indices
\(i=1,2\):
\[
\begin{aligned}
u&=(K_4x_i)_i\bmod\Delta\mathcal B,\\
v&=(-K_3x_i)_i\bmod\Delta\mathcal B,\\
n&=(x_3K_4x_i-x_4K_3x_i)_i\bmod\Delta\mathcal B.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]
Define
\[
\phi:R^2\longrightarrow\mathcal T,\qquad
\phi(\eta_3,\eta_4)=\eta_3u+\eta_4v,
\]
\[
M=\ker\phi,\qquad
P=Rn+Ru+Rv,\qquad
Q=P/(Ru+Rv).
\tag{6}
\]
Although \(\mathcal T\) need not be finite over \(R\), \(P\)
is generated by three elements and \(Q\) is cyclic. In particular,
\[
Q\simeq R/I,\qquad
I=\{c\in R:cn\in Ru+Rv\}.
\tag{7}
\]
No finiteness of \(\mathcal B\) over \(R\) is used.

Equations (4)--(6) prove
\[
\boxed{\text{an \(R\)-valued regular center exists}
\iff \phi(\eta)=n\text{ has a solution}
\iff Q=0.}
\tag{8}
\]
If one solution exists, every solution is its translate by \(M\).
Both \(P\) and \(Q\) are annihilated by \(\Delta\), while
\(\Delta R^2\subseteq M\).

Localization is exact, so a regular center over \(R_{(\pi)}\)
exists if and only if \(Q_{(\pi)}=0\). Consequently only the
finitely many prime divisors of \(\Delta\) require a nontrivial
height-one test. If all these tests have solutions, then
\[
\operatorname{Supp}Q\subseteq\{\mathfrak mR\}.
\tag{9}
\]
Indeed \(Q\) also vanishes at the generic point, since
\(\Delta Q=0\). On the spectrum of the two-dimensional local
domain, no other prime remains. Thus \(Q\) has finite length,
and \(I\) is either the unit ideal or an
\(\mathfrak mR\)-primary ideal. Finite length is not the same
as vanishing.

## 3. The precise overlap class

Assume the height-one solutions in Section 2 exist.
Equation (9) implies \(Q_a=Q_b=0\). Hence one can choose
actual solutions
\[
\eta^{(a)}\in R_a^2,\qquad
\eta^{(b)}\in R_b^2
\tag{10}
\]
on these two entire principal opens, not just at individual
height-one stalks. Their difference lies in \(M_{ab}\).
The class
\[
\omega=
[\eta^{(a)}-\eta^{(b)}]
\in M_{ab}/(M_a+M_b)
\tag{11}
\]
is independent of the choices, because a change of either
local solution is a local element of \(M\).

The exact patching criterion is
\[
\boxed{Q=0\iff\omega=0.}
\tag{12}
\]
For the nontrivial implication, if
\(\eta^{(a)}-\eta^{(b)}=m_a-m_b\) with
\(m_a\in M_a\) and \(m_b\in M_b\), then
\(\eta^{(a)}-m_a=\eta^{(b)}-m_b\). This common pair belongs to
\[
R_a^2\cap R_b^2=R^2\quad\hbox{inside }L^2.
\]
The intersection equality follows from unique factorization:
an element whose denominators can be powers of \(a\) and
also powers of \(b\) has no nonunit denominator, since
\(a,b\) are nonassociate prime parameters.
The common pair solves the equation on both principal opens.
Every height-one prime belongs to at least one of them, since
no height-one prime contains both \(a\) and \(b\).
Apply the actual embedded intersection (13) below to the two
numerators in (4), divided by \(\Delta\): they belong to every
\(\mathcal B_{(\pi)}\), hence to \(\mathcal B\).
Thus the common pair solves \(\phi(\eta)=n\) globally.
Conversely a global solution makes the difference in (11)
a sum of local elements of \(M\).

Thus (11) is the usual explicit overlap obstruction on the
punctured local base. No vanishing assertion about that quotient
is implicit. In particular, even freeness of the translation
module would not by itself force \(\omega=0\).
The equivalence (12) uses the actual intersection just invoked;
it is not asserted for an unrestricted abstract target module.
Such a target could contain nonzero classes supported only at
the closed point, invisible on both principal opens.

There is no further obstruction to testing a *fixed* center
at all divisors. The inherited embedded intersection
\[
\mathcal B=\bigcap_{\operatorname{ht}\pi=1}
\mathcal B_{(\pi)}
\quad\hbox{inside }\mathcal B_L
\tag{13}
\]
implies that a fixed \(\eta\in R^2\) satisfying (4) at every
height-one prime satisfies it globally: apply (13) to each
numerator divided by \(\Delta\).
The intersection follows either coefficientwise from
\(\mathcal B=\mathcal A[s_*]\) and
[the actual invariant-ring intersection](minimal-quadratic-slice-pass46.md),
or directly by factoring invariant denominators in the actual
factorial ring. The distinction is between one fixed center
passing all tests and unrelated centers passing different tests.

## 4. A formal congruence control, not an actual frame

Let \(R=\mathbb C[a,b]_{(a,b)}\) and consider the elementary
congruence
\[
b\eta_3=1\pmod{aR},\qquad \eta_4\text{ arbitrary}.
\tag{14}
\]
This is of the linear-module form (6), with
\(\mathcal T=R/aR\), \(u=b\), \(v=0\), \(n=1\), and
\(\Delta=a\). Its exact data are
\[
M=aR\oplus R,\qquad Q=R/(a,b).
\tag{15}
\]
Here \(M\) is free. At the only relevant height-one prime
\((a)\), \(b\) is a unit and \((1/b,0)\) is a solution.
At every other height-one prime the target module vanishes.
Yet there is no solution over \(R\), since (14) reduced
modulo \((a,b)\) would say \(0=1\).

On \(D(a)\) choose the zero pair and on \(D(b)\) choose
\((1/b,0)\). In the basis \((a,0),(0,1)\) of \(M\), their
overlap difference has first coefficient \(-1/(ab)\).
Its nonzero overlap class is precisely the obstruction (11).
This example proves that the elementary module properties
already listed do not force patching.

It is only a finite linear-congruence control. No ring
\(\mathcal B\), actual coordinate frame, faithful-flat
quadratic graph, or sliced action realizing (14) is asserted.
It therefore does not disprove a further actual-frame
compatibility theorem in a sector where local centers exist.

## 5. Why the remaining plinth sector has no local centers

The actual fiber obstruction is stronger than the abstract
patching limitation. If (3) is regular, finite polynomial
spectral projections in \(\Theta_\eta\) preserve
\(\mathcal B\). For any \(F\in\mathcal B\) of generic degree
at most \(d\), its constant homogeneous component is
\[
\prod_{k=1}^{d}\frac{\Theta_\eta-k}{-k}\,F
=F(\eta_3,\eta_4)\in\mathcal B\cap L=R.
\tag{16}
\]
The empty product handles \(d=0\).
The operator used to compute the component depends on \(d\),
but its value is ordinary evaluation. Therefore these values
define one multiplicative \(R\)-algebra retraction
\[
\operatorname{ev}_\eta:\mathcal B\longrightarrow R.
\tag{17}
\]

At a genuine primitive plinth prime \(\pi\), apply the same
argument over \(R_{(\pi)}\), with residue field \(\kappa\).
The inherited whole residue statement is
\[
\mathcal B_{(\pi)}/\pi\mathcal B_{(\pi)}
=F_\pi[t,s_*],\qquad
[F_\pi:\kappa]>1.
\tag{18}
\]
The field-degree and plinth-support gates are proved in
[the residue-degree note](quadratic-residue-degree-pass46.md).
The argument here needs only the strict inequality in (18),
not a degree-two classification.
A retraction (17) would induce a unital \(\kappa\)-algebra
map \(F_\pi[t,s_*]\to\kappa\). Its restriction to the field
\(F_\pi\) is injective and fixes \(\kappa\), which is
impossible for a nontrivial finite extension.
This uses the entire scheme fiber in (18), not a presumed
surjectivity statement for the invariant ring of a reduction.

Consequently no regular radial center exists at any genuine
plinth divisor. If a center existed over \(C_{\mathfrak m}\),
it would localize to a center at every such divisor through
\(\mathfrak m\). Thus height-one local radial existence in
this actual setting already forces the full primitive
plinth \(p\) to be a unit in \(R\).

The augmentation argument in this section was independently
reconstructed in coordination with the geometry and local
radial audits; the separate complete proof and actual
polynomial-cylinder control are in
[the radial DVR scope note](radial-dvr-scope-pass56.md).
The patching calculation does not replace its local obstruction.

## 6. Scope and the resulting research decision

The module \(Q\) and the overlap class (11) give exact
conditional patching criteria, retaining the actual full
\(\Delta\)-multiplicities. They do not prove that arbitrary
height-one centers patch.

More importantly, a radial-center proof cannot address the
remaining actual second-image/plinth obstruction: there is
already no center at its genuine plinth divisor. A small
affine generic slice need not produce a regular radial
operator. The next slice-selection question must therefore
be studied without importing radial regularity as a necessary
condition.

All calculations here are elementary module, localization,
and spectral-projection arguments using the linked actual
frame and residue theorems. No additional numerical test
suite or global recognition theorem is asserted.
