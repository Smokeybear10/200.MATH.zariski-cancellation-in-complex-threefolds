## 3. Commuting coefficient derivatives force H to be complex quadratic

Differentiate \(D_n(c)=0\) in \(t\), and \(D_t(c)=0\) in \(n\).
The mixed formal derivatives cancel, leaving
\[
D_{nU}(c)c_t-D_{tU}(c)c_n=0.
\tag{13}
\]
Directly from (6),
\[
\begin{aligned}
D_{nU}(c)&=4cH_{nnn}-2H_{nnt},\\
D_{tU}(c)-2cD_{nU}(c)&=-4D_n(c)=0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
\]
Equations (11)--(14) and \(R\ne0\) give
\[
H_{nnt}=2cH_{nnn}.
\tag{15}
\]
Using \(D_n(c)=D_t(c)=0\) again yields
\[
H_{ntt}=4c^2H_{nnn},\qquad H_{ttt}=8c^3H_{nnn}.
\tag{16}
\]
These are identities in \(L\), with all subscripts denoting the
commuting exact coefficient derivatives.

There is a short compatibility obstruction to a nonzero \(H_{nnn}\).
The first two identities imply the equality of coefficient-field
differentials
\[
d(H_{nt})=2c\,d(H_{nn}).
\tag{17}
\]
Take the exterior derivative. Since
\(L/\mathbb C(t,n)\) is finite and separable,
\(dt,dn\) are a basis of \(\Omega_{L/\mathbb C}\), and
\(dt\wedge dn\ne0\). Therefore
\[
0=2\,dc\wedge d(H_{nn})
  =2R H_{nnn}\,dt\wedge dn.
\tag{18}
\]
By \(R\ne0\), \(H_{nnn}=0\). Equations (15)--(16) then make
every third coefficient derivative of \(H\) zero.

The common constants of \(\partial_t,\partial_n\) in \(L\)
are exactly \(\mathbb C\).
For an explicit justification, differentiate a common constant's
monic minimal polynomial over \(\mathbb C(t,n)\) by both partials.
Minimality makes every coefficient constant in both variables,
hence complex. The element is algebraic over the algebraically
closed field \(\mathbb C\), so it is complex.
It follows that every entry of the Hessian of \(H\) is complex.
Subtract its quadratic polynomial primitive; the remaining gradient
is complex, and subtracting the affine primitive leaves a complex
constant. Thus, with complex constants,
\[
H=\frac{\alpha}{2}n^2+\beta tn+\frac{\gamma}{2}t^2
    +\delta n+\epsilon t+\zeta.
\tag{19}
\]
This is exact integration in the finite field; no hidden algebraic
integration mode or rationality assumption is discarded.
