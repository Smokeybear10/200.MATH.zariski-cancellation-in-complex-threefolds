# Independent audit of the affine-linear-plane obstruction

The displayed slice LND below has no nonconstant invariant polynomial
in the algebra generated by any two independent affine-linear forms
in its displayed coordinates. The proof is exact for every such pair;
it uses no finite grid or bounded search. The kernel is explicitly a
polynomial three-ring, so this is an obstruction to an affine-linear
plane search, not a cancellation counterexample or a novelty claim.

## 1. Polynomial coordinates and the slice derivation

Start with coordinates \((A,B_0,C,s)\) and set
\[
x_1=A+s^2,\qquad x_2=B_0+s^3,\qquad x_3=C+s^4,
\qquad x_4=s+x_1x_2.
\tag{1}
\]
These are polynomial coordinates, with the two-sided inverse
\[
s=x_4-x_1x_2,\qquad A=x_1-s^2,
\qquad B_0=x_2-s^3,
\qquad C=x_3-s^4.
\tag{2}
\]
Let \(E=\partial_s\) in the original coordinates. In the displayed
\(x\)-frame,
\[
E(x)=\bigl(2s,3s^2,4s^3,1+2x_2s+3x_1s^2\bigr),
\qquad s=x_4-x_1x_2.
\tag{3}
\]
Thus \(E\) is an LND, \(E(s)=1\), and
\[
\ker E=\mathbb C[A,B_0,C].
\tag{4}
\]
In particular the example already has nonlinear invariant ambient
coordinates.

## 2. Planes whose generators avoid \(x_4\)

In fact the stronger statement
\[
\mathbb C[x_1,x_2,x_3]\cap\ker E=\mathbb C
\tag{5}
\]
holds. For \(H\in\mathbb C[x_1,x_2,x_3]\), equation (3) gives
\[
E(H)=2sH_{x_1}+3s^2H_{x_2}+4s^3H_{x_3}.
\tag{6}
\]
The variables \((x_1,x_2,x_3,s)\) are also polynomial coordinates,
since \(x_4=s+x_1x_2\). Therefore the three powers of \(s\)
in (6) are independent over \(\mathbb C[x_1,x_2,x_3]\).
If \(E(H)=0\), each displayed derivative vanishes, and \(H\)
is constant. This covers every affine-linear plane with both
generators avoiding \(x_4\), including arbitrary constant terms.

## 3. Planes involving \(x_4\): the exact restriction map

For any remaining plane, an invertible affine change of its two
generators puts them in the form
\[
U=x_4+\alpha x_1+\beta x_2+\gamma x_3+u_0,
\qquad
V=\ell_1x_1+\ell_2x_2+\ell_3x_3+v_0,
\tag{7}
\]
where \((\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell_3)\ne0\). All parameters here are
arbitrary complex constants.

On the hypersurface \(s=0\), or \(x_4=x_1x_2\), the restrictions
are
\[
U_0=x_1x_2+\alpha x_1+\beta x_2+\gamma x_3+u_0,
\qquad V_0=\ell_1x_1+\ell_2x_2+\ell_3x_3+v_0.
\tag{8}
\]
They are algebraically independent. To verify this without choosing
parameter values, take the three two-by-two minors of their Jacobian:
\[
\begin{aligned}
M_{12}&=\ell_2x_2-\ell_1x_1+\alpha\ell_2-\beta\ell_1,\\
M_{13}&=\ell_3x_2+\alpha\ell_3-\gamma\ell_1,\\
M_{23}&=\ell_3x_1+\beta\ell_3-\gamma\ell_2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]
If all minors vanished identically, the coefficients of \(x_1,x_2\)
in \(M_{12}\) would force \(\ell_1=\ell_2=0\), and the coefficient
of \(x_2\) in \(M_{13}\) would force \(\ell_3=0\). This contradicts
(7). The Jacobian has rank two over the rational function field,
so characteristic zero gives algebraic independence.

Equivalently, the substitution map
\[
\mathbb C[\mathsf U,\mathsf V]\longrightarrow
\mathbb C[x_1,x_2,x_3],\qquad
(\mathsf U,\mathsf V)\longmapsto(U_0,V_0)
\tag{10}
\]
is injective. This is the precise restriction property required below.

## 4. Restricting an invariant polynomial forces it to be constant

Equation (3) implies
\[
E(U)|_{s=0}=1,\qquad E(V)|_{s=0}=0,
\tag{11}
\]
and, as a polynomial on the whole ambient space,
\[
E(V)=2\ell_1s+3\ell_2s^2+4\ell_3s^3\ne0.
\tag{12}
\]
Let \(P\in\mathbb C[\mathsf U,\mathsf V]\) satisfy
\(E(P(U,V))=0\). The chain rule, followed by restriction to \(s=0\),
gives
\[
P_{\mathsf U}(U_0,V_0)=0.
\]
Injectivity of (10) forces \(P_{\mathsf U}=0\), hence
\(P=p(\mathsf V)\) for some one-variable polynomial \(p\).
Returning to the full ring gives
\[
0=p'(V)E(V).
\]
The ring is a domain and (12) is nonzero, so \(p'(V)=0\).
The affine-linear polynomial \(V\) is transcendental over
\(\mathbb C\), whence \(p'=0\), and characteristic zero makes
\(p\) constant.

Combining this with Section 2 proves
\[
\mathbb C[U,V]\cap\ker E=\mathbb C
\tag{13}
\]
for every pair of independent affine-linear forms \(U,V\) in the
displayed frame.

## 5. Projective-direction consequence and limits

The known quasi-extension and plane-LND results imply that projectable
coefficient direction on an actual coordinate plane produces a
nonconstant plane invariant. The precise reformulation and sources
are in
[the projective-plane recognition note](pass14-projective-plane-recognition.md),
using
[Freudenburg and Moser-Jauslin, Lemma 2.1](https://moser.perso.math.cnrs.fr/adjoinedroots.pdf)
and
[Freudenburg, Corollary 5.43](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-662-55350-3_5).
Every independent affine-linear pair extends to ambient linear
coordinates, so (13) excludes projectability for every affine-linear
plane in this frame.

The direct proof of (13) did not require those recognition results.
They are used only to translate the invariant obstruction into the
projective-direction formulation. No bounded sampling of plane
parameters is used at either stage.

This does not obstruct a search over all polynomial coordinate planes.
For example \(\mathbb C[A,B_0]\) is an invariant coordinate plane
by (2) and (4). Consequently the example cannot establish a lower
bound over all polynomial frames or contradict cancellation.

## 6. Exact checks performed

An independent inline SymPy run using
`/opt/anaconda3/bin/python` passed 18 grouped exact checks in
0.153 seconds. Six groups concern this example: the two-sided inverse,
the adapted derivation and slice, the restrictions in (11) and (12),
all three symbolic Jacobian minors, the coefficient consequences of
their identical vanishing, and the independent-powers identity (6).
The other twelve groups check the separate controls in the
projective-plane recognition note.

These symbolic identities support the hand proof. They are not a
finite-grid substitute for the universal argument in Sections 2–4.
