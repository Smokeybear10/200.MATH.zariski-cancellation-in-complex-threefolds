# Actual cubic cusp charts: coefficient forcing and a projected coordinate

Status: working proof pending independent review of this saved artifact.
The result is conditional on the retained actual cusp chart and a cubic
graph in its fixed projected axes. It constructs a coordinate of the
projected polynomial ring, not a coordinate of the original four-ring.
No global recognition, unrestricted entry, or historical novelty claim
is made.

## 1. Exact actual hypotheses and the whole cubic relation

Retain the setup of [the actual cusp chart159](cusp-generic-chart-first-reductions-pass159-working.md):
\(B=A[u]=\mathbb C[X,Y,Z,W]\), \(E=\partial_u\), exact time
degrees \((1,1,2,2)\), and full weighted initial kernel
\((XZ-Y^3)\). Write \(a=EX\ne0\), let
\(N=[u^3](\partial_Z|_A)\), and put
\(S=\ker_A N\), \(L=\operatorname{Frac}S\).
In particular \(Na=0\). The actual projected coordinates satisfy
\[
\begin{gathered}
A_a=\mathbb C[y,z,w]_{a_0},\qquad a_0(y,z,w)=a,\\
\operatorname{Frac}A=\mathbb C(y,z,w)=L(v),\qquad
L[y,\vartheta]=L[v],\\
Y=y+\tau X,\quad Z=z+(3\tau^2y+k)X+\tau^3X^2,
\quad W=w+\vartheta X+\rho X^2,\\
z=3\tau y^2+k_\tau y+r\vartheta+n,\qquad
rw+y^3+\tfrac12k_{\tau\tau}y^2+k_{\tau\rho}y\vartheta
 +\tfrac12k_{\rho\rho}\vartheta^2+n_\tau y+n_\rho\vartheta=\ell,
\quad r=k_\rho\ne0.
\end{gathered}
\tag{1}
\]
The coefficient field \(L\) is finite separable over
\(\mathbb C(\tau,\rho)\), and its commuting extended coefficient
partials have joint constants \(\mathbb C\).

Assume the fixed-axis cubic graph case
\(\vartheta=P(y)\), \(P\in L[U]\), \(\deg P=3\).
Then \(L[y]=L[v]\), so \(y\) is an affine parameter over \(L\).
The selected plane kernel is generated by \(V-P(U)\), of total
degree three. The unit-gradient and remainder argument of
[the selected-factor reduction159](cusp-generic-selected-factor-reduction-pass159-working.md)
makes the whole potential relation
\[
\Phi-m=\lambda\bigl(V-P(U)\bigr),\qquad \lambda\in L^*.
\tag{2}
\]
Indeed a nonconstant extra cofactor would give degree at least six,
whereas \(\deg\Phi\le4\). No coordinate change over \(L\) is
being performed on the original ring.

## 2. Exact lower equations and reciprocal-linear lambda

The quartic part of \(\Phi\) is
\(-U^3(r_\tau U+r_\rho V)/r\). By (2) it vanishes, hence
\(r\in\mathbb C^*\). Put \(K=\ker_L\partial_\rho\), and use a
prime for \(\partial_\tau\) on \(K\). One has
\([K:\mathbb C(\tau)]<\infty\) and \(\ker_K\partial_\tau=\mathbb C\).
For completeness, differentiating the monic minimal polynomial of an
element of \(K\) over \(\mathbb C(\tau,\rho)\) shows that all its
coefficients belong to \(\mathbb C(\tau)\). Linear independence over
\(\mathbb C(\tau)\) persists after adjoining the transcendental
\(\rho\), giving the uniform bound by
\([L:\mathbb C(\tau,\rho)]\). The assertion about constants follows
from the joint constants in (1).

Write \(k=r\rho+k_0\), \(k_0\in K\). The coefficients of
\(V^2\) and \(UV\) in (2), respectively, give
\(n_{\rho\rho}=0\) and \(n_{\tau\rho}=0\). Consequently
\(n=s\rho+n_0\), where \(s\in\mathbb C\), \(n_0\in K\).
The remaining literal coefficients are
\[
\begin{gathered}
P=p_3U^3+p_2U^2+p_1U+p_0,\qquad p_3\ne0,\\
\lambda=-s^2/r-\ell_\rho,\qquad
p_3=\frac{s/r-k_0'''/6}{\lambda},\qquad
p_2=\frac{s k_0''/(2r)-n_0''/2}{\lambda},\qquad
p_1=\frac{s n_0'/r+\ell_\tau}{\lambda}.
\end{gathered}
\tag{3}
\]
These formulas follow directly by expanding the potential in159; they
do not impose a choice of its integration constant \(m\).

The constant determinant, now using \(y\) as the third separating
parameter, says that the following polynomial in \(U\) is a nonzero
element of \(L\):
\[
\mathcal S=P_\tau+P_U P_\rho+(s/r)P_U+
                  (3U^2+k_0''U+n_0')/r\in L^* .
\tag{4}
\]
In detail the determinant is \(-\lambda\mathcal S\), equal to
\(-a^3/y_v\in L^*\), not necessarily to \(-a^3\) in this parameter.
The coefficient of \(U^5\) is \(3p_3(p_3)_\rho\), so (3)
implies \(\lambda_\rho=0\). Thus
\[
\begin{gathered}
\ell=(-\lambda-s^2/r)\rho+\ell_0,\quad \ell_0\in K,\\
\alpha=-\lambda'/\lambda,\qquad
p_1=\alpha\rho+b,\quad
b=(s n_0'/r+\ell_0')/\lambda,\qquad p_2,p_3\in K.
\end{gathered}
\tag{5}
\]
The \(U^2\) coefficient of (4) makes \((p_0)_\rho\in K\), since
\(p_3\ne0\). Therefore \(p_0=e\rho+d\) for \(e,d\in K\).
The remaining positive coefficients give
\[
\begin{gathered}
p_3'+3\alpha p_3=0,\\
p_2'+2\alpha p_2+3p_3(e+s/r)+3/r=0,\\
\alpha'+\alpha^2=0,\qquad
b'+\alpha b+2p_2(e+s/r)+k_0''/r=0.
\end{gathered}
\tag{6}
\]
To separate the last line, its first equation is the coefficient of
\(\rho\) in the \(U\) coefficient of (4). The variable \(\rho\)
is transcendental over \(K\), so this coefficient comparison is valid.

Put \(\chi=1/\lambda\). Then \(\chi''=0\), and exact constants
give \(\chi=A\tau+B\), \(A,B\in\mathbb C\), not both zero.
The first equation of (6) gives \((\chi^3p_3)'=0\). Hence
\[
\chi=A\tau+B,\qquad p_3=C/\chi^3,\quad C\in\mathbb C^*,\qquad
k_0'''=6s/r-6C/\chi^4.
\tag{7}
\]
No rationality of \(K\) has been assumed to obtain (7).

## 3. The full coefficient field and the line pencil

All coefficients of \(P,k,n,\ell\) belong to \(K[\rho]\).
Equation (1) therefore puts \(z,w\) in \(K(\rho)[y]\).
Actual field return now gives
\(L(y)=\mathbb C(y,z,w)\subset K(\rho)(y)\subset L(y)\).
Intersecting with \(L\), since \(y\) is transcendental over it,
proves \(L=K(\rho)\).

Set \(H=\chi(e+s/r)\in K\) and introduce the projected linear
change \(w_*=w+(s/r^2)z\). Direct use of (1) gives
\[
\begin{gathered}
w_*=V_0-\frac{\rho}{r\chi},\qquad
z=Z_0+(r\alpha y+re+s)\rho,\\
V_0=-y^3/r+(-k_0''/(2r)+3s\tau/r^2)y^2
       +(-n_0'/r+s k_0'/r^2)y+\ell_0/r+s n_0/r^2,\\
Z_0=rp_3y^3+(3\tau+rp_2)y^2+(k_0'+rb)y+n_0+rd,\\
z=f w_*+g,\qquad
f=-r^2Ay-r^2H,\qquad g=Z_0-fV_0\in K[y].
\end{gathered}
\tag{8}
\]
In particular \(w_*\) is transcendental over \(M=K(y)\).
With \(k_*:=\mathbb C(y)\), actual field return is the exact equality
\(k_*(z,w_*)=M(w_*)\).

Suppose \(H\notin\mathbb C\). Then \(f\) is transcendental over
\(k_*\). The inclusions
\(k_*(z,w_*)\subset k_*(f,g)(w_*)\subset M(w_*)\) are equalities,
and intersection with \(M\) gives \(M=k_*(f,g)\).
Apply the elementary pencil-degree lemma proved in
[the actual-field argument166](cusp-quartic-actual-field-recognition-pass166-working.md),
Section 3. For the full irreducible relation \(R(f,g)=0\), the
degree of \(M(w_*)/k_*(w_*,z)\) is the total degree of \(R\).
That degree is one here. Thus \(g\in k_*+k_*f\), or
\(g\in\mathbb C(y)+H\mathbb C(y)\).
Since \(g\in K[y]\), each of its coefficients belongs to
\(\mathbb C+\mathbb C H\). This follows by expanding in a finite
complex basis for the span of \(1,H\) and those coefficients;
the basis remains independent over \(\mathbb C(y)\).

The coefficient of \(y^2\) in (8) is \(T+JH\), where
\[
\begin{gathered}
T=3\tau+rp_2-rA n_0'+As k_0',\qquad
J=-rk_0''/2+3s\tau,\\
T'=-3rCH/\chi^4,
\qquad J'=3rC/\chi^4\ne0,
\qquad (T+JH)'=JH'.
\end{gathered}
\tag{9}
\]
For the first derivative identity, substitute (6), (3) and (7):
the terms involving \(n_0''\) and \(k_0''\) cancel, leaving
\(-3rp_3H/\chi\). The second follows immediately from (7).
The coefficient-span assertion gives \(T+JH=c_0+c_1H\),
\(c_0,c_1\in\mathbb C\). Differentiating implies
\((J-c_1)H'=0\). But \(H\notin\mathbb C\) gives \(H'\ne0\),
whereas \(J'\ne0\) excludes \(J=c_1\). This contradiction proves
\[
H=\chi(e+s/r)\in\mathbb C.
\tag{10}
\]
The argument includes \(A=0\); neither (8) nor (9) divides by \(A\).
It does not replace actual field return by a nonzero determinant.

## 4. A projected coordinate and proved rationality of K

Now \(H\) is complex. The top three coefficients of \(g\) in (8) are
\[
\begin{gathered}
[y^4]g=-rA,\\
[y^3]g=rp_3-rA k_0''/2+3As\tau-rH,\\
[y^2]g=T+JH.
\end{gathered}
\tag{11}
\]
The cubic coefficient has derivative zero by (7) and
\(p_3'=-3Ap_3/\chi\); the quadratic coefficient has derivative
zero by (9). They are therefore complex constants.
The linear coefficient is
\(k_0'+rb+rA\ell_0+Asn_0-rHn_0'+Hs k_0'\).
Its derivative is zero as well: use the last equation of (6),
\(b/\chi=s n_0'/r+\ell_0'\), and
\(2rp_2/\chi=s k_0''-rn_0''\) to cancel every term.
Consequently there is \(P_*\in\mathbb C[y]\), with zero constant
term and degree at most four, such that
\[
g=P_*(y)+g_0,\qquad
g_0=(1+sH)n_0+rd+rH\ell_0\in K.
\tag{12}
\]
Since \(f\in\mathbb C(y)\), the same actual field equality now gives
\(K(y)(w_*)=\mathbb C(y,g_0)(w_*)\). Intersect first with
\(K(y)\), and then with \(K\), to obtain
\(K=\mathbb C(g_0)\). In particular \(g_0\notin\mathbb C\).
This rationality is a consequence, not an additional hypothesis.

More precisely, the projected polynomial ring has the two-sided
coordinate presentation
\[
\begin{gathered}
g_0=(1+sH+sAy)z+r^2(Ay+H)w-P_*(y),
\qquad w_*=w+(s/r^2)z,\\
z=g_0-r^2(Ay+H)w_*+P_*(y),\qquad
w=w_*-(s/r^2)z,\\
\mathbb C[y,z,w]=\mathbb C[y,w_*,g_0],\qquad
K=\mathbb C(g_0),\qquad L=\mathbb C(g_0,\rho).
\end{gathered}
\tag{13}
\]
The displayed recovery formulas verify both polynomial composition
orders. The original projected triple is algebraically independent
over \(\mathbb C\), by (1), so this really is a polynomial three-ring.
Also \(g_0\in A_a\cap L=S_a\), because \(Na=0\).
This does not assert \(g_0\in A\).

## 5. An additional localized divisor condition and exact scope

The last equation of (7) can also be integrated explicitly without
assuming rationality of \(K\). If \(A\ne0\), it gives
\[
k_0=(s/r)\tau^3+C/(A^3\chi)+Q_2(\tau),
\qquad Q_2\in\mathbb C[\tau],\quad \deg Q_2\le2.
\tag{14}
\]
The remaining third derivative zero integrates by exact constants
three times. If \(A=0\), the corresponding formula is
\(k_0=(s/r-C/B^4)\tau^3+Q_2(\tau)\).
In the first case \(k_0=k-r\rho\in S_a\), and
\(\tau,\rho\in S_a\). As \(C/A^3\ne0\), (14) implies
\[
\chi\in S_a^*,\qquad A b+B a=a\chi\in S_a^*,\qquad b=EY.
\tag{15}
\]
Here the \(b\) in the last display is the original speed, not the
coefficient named \(b\) in (5). No assertion that \(a\), or that
speed combination, is a unit in the unlocalized ring is made.

The theorem establishes (7), (10), and the actual projected-coordinate
and field identifications (13), under the cubic graph assumptions.
It neither constructs a global invariant coordinate of \(B\) nor
identifies \(A\) with a polynomial ring over \(S\), proves that
\(a\) is a unit, or shows that a generic
parameter extends across \(a=0\). In fact \(g_0\) belongs to the
coefficient field, not to its generic line-parameter direction.
The remaining task is an integral correction in the original frame.
Other graph degrees, other initial configurations, and unrestricted
coordinate entry are outside this result.
