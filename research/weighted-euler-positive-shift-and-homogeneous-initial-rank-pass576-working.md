# Weighted Euler dichotomy for an arbitrary sliced polynomial ring

## 1. Statement

Let
\[
 B=\mathbf C[X_1,\ldots,X_n]=A[u],\qquad
 E=\partial_u,\qquad A=\ker_B E,\qquad n\ge2.
\tag{1}
\]
The \(X_i\) are actual polynomial coordinates, and \(u\) is the given
global slice, \(Eu=1\). No principal initial ideal, degree bound, chosen
chart, or prior polynomiality of \(A\) is assumed. Suppose the actual
coordinate degrees
\[
 d_i=\deg_E X_i
\tag{2}
\]
are all positive. A zero-degree actual coordinate would already be an
invariant ambient coordinate and is outside this all-positive setup.
Use the convention \(\deg_E0=-\infty\).

An all-positive frame is always available for a nonzero sliced \(E\).
Starting from any polynomial coordinates, choose \(X_j\) with
\(\deg_E X_j>0\), which exists because \(E\ne0\). Replace every
other degree-zero coordinate \(X_i\) by \(X_i+X_j\), fixing \(X_j\)
and all already positive-degree coordinates. This simultaneous
invertible complex linear shear makes each replaced degree equal
to \(\deg_E X_j\). It need not preserve a globally minimizing
frame, a special initial ideal, or the Euler shift of the old
frame. Thus availability of an all-positive frame is not an extra
entry assumption for this dichotomy.

Consider the actual weighted Euler derivation
\[
 D=\sum_{i=1}^n d_iX_i\frac{\partial}{\partial X_i}.
\tag{3}
\]
It is locally finite and diagonalizable on \(B\): each source monomial
is an eigenvector of the indicated nonnegative integer weight. Let
\(\delta=\deg_E D\) be its exact filtration shift, defined and proved
finite below. There is the following dichotomy.

* If \(\delta=0\), then \(A\) is a polynomial ring in \(n-1\) variables.
  There exist actual invariant polynomials \(f_1,\ldots,f_{n-1}\) such
  that \((f_1,\ldots,f_{n-1},u)\) is an ambient polynomial coordinate
  system.
* If \(\delta>0\), the nonzero induced derivation on
  \(\operatorname{gr}_E B=A[\eta]\) has the form
  \[
   \overline D=\eta^\delta N,\qquad
   0\ne N\in\operatorname{LND}(A),\qquad \overline D\eta=0.
  \tag{4}
  \]
  For every nonzero polynomial homogeneous in the fixed source weights
  \(d_i\), its entire evaluated \(E\)-initial belongs to
  \((\ker_A N)[\eta]\). Thus the field generated by **all** such initials,
  not only a finite list of relation remainders, has transcendence degree
  at most \(n-1\) over \(\mathbf C\).

The shift is always nonnegative, so these are all cases. The positive
case does not assert a slice for \(N\), an actual coordinate replacement,
or termination of a degree-lowering process. Explicit actual controls
below show why these limitations matter. The proof is self-contained;
no claim of novelty is made for the grading or locally finite
derivation arguments.

## 2. Finiteness of the shift on the whole ring

The slice presentation in (1) identifies \(A\) with \(B/(u)\) by
evaluation at \(u=0\). In particular \(A\) is a finitely generated
\(\mathbf C\)-domain. Equivalently, the finite Taylor projection
\[
 \pi(f)=\sum_{j\ge0}\frac{(-u)^j}{j!}E^j(f)
\tag{5}
\]
is the evaluation homomorphism onto \(A\), and the finitely many
\(\pi(X_i)\) generate \(A\). The sum is finite separately for each
polynomial. Formula (5) uses the given slice, not an assertion that
it is already an ambient coordinate.

Choose any finite algebra generating set \(a_1,\ldots,a_\ell\) of
\(A\). Ignore zero derivative values in the following maximum:
\[
 \delta_0=
 \max\left\{\deg_E D(a_j):1\le j\le\ell,\quad
                         \deg_E D(u)-1\right\}.
\tag{6}
\]
At least one derivative is nonzero because these elements generate
\(B\) and \(D\ne0\). The maximum is therefore a finite integer.

For every \(a\in A\), the product rule in its polynomial expression
in the \(a_j\) shows that \(\deg_E D(a)\le\delta_0\): all
undifferentiated coefficients have degree zero. For
\(f=\sum_{j=0}^e b_j u^j\), \(b_j\in A\), the complete derivative is
\[
 Df=\sum_{j=0}^e D(b_j)u^j
                    +\sum_{j=1}^e j b_j u^{j-1}D(u).
\tag{7}
\]
Both sums have degree at most \(e+\delta_0\). The generators attaining
the maximum in (6) give equality in the corresponding shift. Hence
\[
 \delta=\delta_0=
 \sup_{\substack{f\ne0\\Df\ne0}}
           \{\deg_E Df-\deg_E f\}<\infty.
\tag{8}
\]
This is an all-ring bound, not a bound only on the displayed \(X_i\).
Since \(DX_i=d_iX_i\ne0\), their degree differences are zero.
Consequently \(\delta\ge0\).

The coefficient description \(B=A[u]\) gives the entire associated
graded ring
\[
 \operatorname{gr}_E B=A[\eta],\qquad
 \operatorname{in}_E\!\left(\sum_{j=0}^e b_ju^j\right)=b_e\eta^e.
\tag{9}
\]
No identification of a smaller coordinate-initial algebra with (9)
is being made.

## 3. Positive shift produces a homogeneous locally nilpotent derivation

Assume \(\delta>0\). The filtration inequality defines a degree-\(\delta\)
derivation \(\overline D\) on (9). On a homogeneous initial of degree
\(e\), it is the class of \(Df\) at degree \(e+\delta\), which may be
zero. Equality somewhere in (8) makes \(\overline D\ne0\).

We verify local nilpotence, although \(D\) itself is generally
semisimple rather than locally nilpotent. For each \(f\in B\), local
finiteness puts \(f,Df,D^2f,\ldots\) in a finite-dimensional vector
space. The \(E\)-degrees of this orbit are bounded, since a finite
basis has a finite maximum degree. If
\(\overline D^{\,j}\operatorname{in}_E f\ne0\), the successive
nonvanishing initial identities force
\[
 \deg_E D^j f=\deg_E f+j\delta.
\tag{10}
\]
This cannot hold for arbitrarily large \(j\). Thus \(\overline D\)
is locally nilpotent on every homogeneous element of \(A[\eta]\);
finite sums give the same conclusion on the whole graded ring.

Homogeneity gives
\(\overline D(\eta)=b\eta^{\delta+1}\) for some \(b\in A\), possibly
zero. We use the following elementary divisibility fact. If \(L\)
is an LND on a characteristic-zero domain and \(L(f)=fg\), then
\(L(f)=0\). Otherwise the finite \(L\)-degree satisfies
\[
 \deg_L L(f)=\deg_L f-1,\qquad
 \deg_L(fg)=\deg_L f+\deg_L g,
\tag{11}
\]
which is impossible. The product formula follows by taking the
last nonzero term in the Leibniz expansion; its binomial coefficient
is nonzero in characteristic zero. Applying this fact to
\(f=\eta\) proves \(\overline D\eta=0\).

For \(a\in A\), homogeneity now uniquely defines a derivation \(N\)
of \(A\) by
\[
 \overline D(a)=\eta^\delta N(a).
\tag{12}
\]
The product rule proves that it is a \(\mathbf C\)-derivation.
It is nonzero because \(\overline D\ne0\) and
\(\overline D\eta=0\). Moreover
\[
 \overline D^{\,j}(a)=\eta^{j\delta}N^j(a),
\tag{13}
\]
so the domain property and local nilpotence of \(\overline D\)
show that \(N\) is locally nilpotent on \(A\). This proves (4).
No unique highest coordinate-coefficient shift was needed.

## 4. All fixed-source-homogeneous initials lie in one smaller field

Let \(H\in\mathbf C[\mathsf X_1,\ldots,\mathsf X_n]\) be a nonzero
polynomial homogeneous of source weight \(M\) for the fixed weights
\(d_i\), and set \(f=H(X_1,\ldots,X_n)\). Actual polynomial
coordinates ensure \(f\ne0\), and
\[
 Df=Mf.
\tag{14}
\]
Because \(\delta>0\), the right side has no component at degree
\(\deg_E f+\delta\). Therefore
\(\overline D(\operatorname{in}_E f)=0\). Writing
\(\operatorname{in}_E f=c\eta^e\) and using (4) yields \(N(c)=0\).
This includes a homogeneous source relation whose actual evaluation
has smaller positive degree, and also a homogeneous evaluation
of degree zero.

Define \(\mathscr H\) to be the algebra generated by all these
initials. We have the exact containment
\[
 \mathscr H\subseteq(\ker_A N)[\eta].
\tag{15}
\]
There is no finite-generation assumption on either algebra.
For clarity, the usual local-slice argument for a nonzero LND on
the domain \(A\) gives
\(\operatorname{trdeg}_{\mathbf C}\ker_A N=\dim A-1=n-2\).
Indeed choose \(b\) with \(N^2b=0\) and \(a=Nb\ne0\);
in \(A_a\), \(b/a\) is a slice. The finite Taylor projection gives
\(A_a=(\ker_A N)_a[b/a]\), and passing to fraction fields gives
the stated transcendence degree. Consequently
\[
 \operatorname{trdeg}_{\mathbf C}\operatorname{Frac}\mathscr H
 \le n-1.
\tag{16}
\]
This bound concerns the entire homogeneous-initial algebra, not
just chosen generators of an initial ideal.

The homogeneity in this statement is always that of the original
Euler derivation (3). If a cancelled polynomial of original Euler
weight \(M\) has \(E\)-degree \(e<M\), a newly adjoined presentation
variable with weight \(e\) is not an Euler eigenvector of weight
\(e\). A relation homogeneous in those new presentation weights
can mix different original Euler weights. Such a relation need
not satisfy (14), and (15) must not be applied to it without
checking its actual Euler homogeneity.

## 5. Zero shift recognizes the invariant ring

Now assume \(\delta=0\). Since every element of \(A\) has
\(E\)-degree zero, (8) shows
\[
 D(A)\subseteq A.
\tag{17}
\]
In the actual coordinates \(X_i\), \(D\) gives the nonnegative
grading of \(B\) with positive weights \(d_i\), so \(B_0=\mathbf C\).
The \(D\)-stability of \(A\) implies that it is the direct sum of
its intersections with the weight spaces of \(B\). To see this
without an invariant-subspace assumption about an infinite direct
sum, take a single \(a\in A\). It has finite weight support in
\(B\), and each spectral projection on that support is a polynomial
in \(D\), obtained by Lagrange interpolation in the distinct
integer eigenvalues. By (17), every homogeneous component of
\(a\) is still in \(A\). Thus
\[
 A=\bigoplus_{j\ge0}A_j,\qquad A_0=\mathbf C.
\tag{18}
\]
Decomposing finitely many algebra generators of \(A\) provides
finitely many homogeneous generators.

The ring \(A\) is smooth. One direct proof uses
\(A\simeq B/(u)\). In the actual coordinates, write
\(E=\sum_i E(X_i)\partial_{X_i}\). The equality \(Eu=1\) is
\[
 \sum_i E(X_i)\frac{\partial u}{\partial X_i}=1.
\tag{19}
\]
Hence the gradient ideal of \(u\) is the unit ideal. The
characteristic-zero hypersurface Jacobian criterion makes \(u=0\)
smooth everywhere, without presuming that \(u\) was a coordinate.

Let \(\mathfrak m=\bigoplus_{j>0}A_j\), the homogeneous vertex.
It is a maximal ideal with residue field \(\mathbf C\).
Smoothness at this point and \(\dim A=n-1\) give
\(\dim_{\mathbf C}\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2=n-1\).
Choose homogeneous \(f_1,\ldots,f_{n-1}\in\mathfrak m\) lifting
a homogeneous basis of that vector space.

These elements generate \(A\). For a homogeneous element of
positive weight, subtract the matching-weight linear combination
of the \(f_i\) representing its class modulo \(\mathfrak m^2\).
The remainder is a sum of products of positive-weight homogeneous
elements of strictly smaller weights. Induction on the positive
integer weight expresses it in the \(f_i\). Constants are the
weight-zero starting case. We obtain a surjection
\[
 \mathbf C[T_1,\ldots,T_{n-1}]\longrightarrow A,\qquad T_i\mapsto f_i.
\tag{20}
\]
Both domains have dimension \(n-1\), so its prime kernel has
height zero and is zero. Therefore \(A=\mathbf C[f_1,\ldots,f_{n-1}]\)
is a polynomial ring. Using the given slice presentation,
\[
 B=\mathbf C[f_1,\ldots,f_{n-1},u]
\tag{21}
\]
is an actual polynomial coordinate system.

The proof supplies a finite homogeneous-generator recognition step
under \(\delta=0\). It does not prove that repeated changes of an
arbitrary positive-shift frame ever reach this case.

## 6. A sharp positive-shift actual frame and mixed-weight escape

In the known translation ring \(\mathbf C[a,b,c,u]\), take
\[
 X=a+u^4,\quad Y=b+u^5,\quad Z=c+u^6+u^3,\quad
 W=-(u+Y^2-XZ).
\tag{22}
\]
This is the \(n=4\) instance of the completely read source565,
with the last coordinate rescaled by \(-1\). Its whole inverse is
\[
 U=-W-Y^2+XZ,\qquad
 u=U,\quad a=X-U^4,\quad b=Y-U^5,\quad c=Z-U^6-U^3.
\tag{23}
\]
Substitution in the forward direction first gives \(U=u\) and
then \(a,b,c\). Conversely these formulas recover \(X,Y,Z\)
and finally \(-[U+Y^2-XZ]=W\). Both orders are polynomial
identities on the entire fourfold.

The degrees are \((4,5,6,7)\), all with leader one. Put
\(F=Y^2-XZ\) and \(Q=7u-3F\). The entire transported Euler
derivation for these weights is
\[
 \begin{split}
 D u&=Q,\\
 D a&=4a+4u^4-4u^3Q,\\
 D b&=5b+5u^5-5u^4Q,\\
 D c&=6c+6u^6+6u^3-(6u^5+3u^2)Q.
 \end{split}
\tag{24}
\]
These formulas follow by differentiating the whole inverse.
In the order \(a,b,c,u\), their exact degree/leader pairs are
\[
 (10,-12),\quad(11,-15),\quad(12,-18),\quad(7,3).
\tag{25}
\]
Thus \(\delta=12\) and \(N=-18\partial_c\).
Every fixed-source-homogeneous initial belongs to
\(\mathbf C[a,b,\eta]\).

This rank-three bound is attained by such initials. Besides \(X\),
use the homogeneous source polynomials
\[
 \begin{split}
 H_a&=YZ-XW,\\
 H_b&=5WX^2Z-5WXY^2-X^4Y-6XYZ^2+7Y^3Z.
 \end{split}
\tag{26}
\]
Their source weights are eleven and twenty-one, and their entire
actual remainders have degrees ten and sixteen, with leaders
\(-a\) and \(5a^2-6b\). The three initials
\[
 \eta^4,\quad -a\eta^{10},\quad(5a^2-6b)\eta^{16}
\tag{27}
\]
are algebraically independent: their Jacobian in \((\eta,a,b)\)
is \(24\eta^{29}\ne0\). Hence \(\mathscr H\) has rank exactly
three. Even the collection of every homogeneous source polynomial
cannot recover a fourth independent initial here.

Nevertheless the mixed-weight polynomial \(U=-W-Y^2+XZ\)
is the actual slice. Its terms have original Euler weights seven
and ten. The elementary replacement
\(W\mapsto W+Y^2-XZ=-u\), fixing \(X,Y,Z\), decreases its degree
from seven to one. This is a genuine polynomial automorphism,
not a coordinate-field promotion. The fixed homogeneous bound
does not prevent that move. The frame is explicitly not globally
minimizing.

## 7. The induced N need not itself have a slice

A second actual translation frame is
\[
 X=a+u^4,\quad Y=b+u^5,\quad
 Z=c+a^2u^6+u^3,\quad W=u+Y^2-XZ.
\tag{28}
\]
Its complete inverse is
\[
 U=W-Y^2+XZ,\quad
 a=X-U^4,\quad b=Y-U^5,\quad
 c=Z-(X-U^4)^2U^6-U^3,\quad u=U.
\tag{29}
\]
Again both inverse orders follow by first recovering \(U\) and
then substituting each unchanged coordinate. No localization is
used. The exact degree tuple is \((4,5,6,10)\), with leaders
\((1,1,a^2,1-a^2)\), all nonzero polynomials.

For this frame the complete weighted Euler derivation satisfies
\[
 \begin{split}
 D u&=10u,\\
 D a&=4a-36u^4,\qquad D b=5b-45u^5,\\
 D c&=6c-62a^2u^6+72au^{10}-24u^3.
 \end{split}
\tag{30}
\]
Thus \(\delta=10\) and \(N=72a\partial_c\). This nonzero LND
has no slice: every image lies in the proper ideal \(aA\), so
one is not an image. It also vanishes on the divisor \(a=0\).
This control shows that a sliced derivation on the ambient
polynomial ring does not make the raw Euler-induced \(N\)
automatically sliced or fixed-point-free.

In this particular example its common factor can be removed to
give \(\partial_c\); the example does not deny the possibility of
a separately justified primitive normalization. It supplies no
general theorem about what such a normalization would do.
Here \(A\) is already polynomial, and no global degree-minimum
claim is made.

## 8. Zero-shift boundary and exact limitations

The positive-shift hypothesis in (16) cannot simply be omitted.
In the same known translation ring use the actual frame
\[
 (T_1,T_2,T_3,T_4)=(u,a+u^2,b+u^3,c+u^4).
\tag{31}
\]
Its inverse subtracts \(T_1^2,T_1^3,T_1^4\) from the last
three coordinates, with \(u=T_1\). The Euler weights are
\((1,2,3,4)\), and in the old coordinates
\[
 D(a,b,c,u)=(2a,3b,4c,u),\qquad \delta=0.
\tag{32}
\]
The homogeneous polynomials \(T_1(T_i-T_1^i)\), \(i=2,3,4\),
have actual values \(au,bu,cu\), all of positive degree one.
Together with \(T_1\), their initials are
\(\eta,a\eta,b\eta,c\eta\), of rank four; their Jacobian in
\((\eta,a,b,c)\) is \(\eta^3\).
This agrees with the recognition alternative and contradicts
any unconditional rank-three version for these initials.

The theorem improves a bound on the entire fixed homogeneous
collection in the positive-shift case. It does not claim that a
remainder is a coordinate, that the invariant kernel of \(N\)
is polynomial, or that a finite sequence of remainder adjunctions
or coordinate changes finds an adapted frame. New presentation
weights and original Euler weights must remain distinct.
The nonprincipal-ideal and degree-zero boundaries in573 are
therefore not removed by silently changing the meaning of
homogeneity.

All three controls are actual polynomial translation frames with
complete inverses, not embeddings of a single orbit. Their known
polynomial kernels and available lower frames are retained. They
are not counterexamples to cancellation or to an entry statement
about globally minimizing frames.
