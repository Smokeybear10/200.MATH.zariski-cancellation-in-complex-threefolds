# The smooth-generator quartic forces the residue basis and a strict length bound

In the smooth-a subcase of the remaining actual mixed fiber, the new
quartic U vanishes on the whole fiber but remains nonzero in the
internal degree-module fiber. This determines the entire fourth-degree
residue space, forces W3=D, and makes the fourth-image length bound
strict. It does not exclude this smooth-a subcase.

## 1. Inputs from the actual one-quartic construction

Use R=S_m with maximal ideal n, the actual faithfully flat domain A_R,
and the smooth-a normalization in
[one-quartic108](mixed-smooth-generator-quartic-pass108.md). The whole
fiber is D[c], with D=C[G]/(G²(G−1)), and every Fj=ker N^(j+1)
is free of rank j+1. The actual algebra is generated by F4. In the
normalized basis the retained equations include
\[
\begin{gathered}
F_2=R\oplus RG\oplus RH,\quad r=aG+bH,\quad Nr=P,\\
NG=\epsilon br+a,\quad NH=-\epsilon ar+B_2,
\quad \epsilon\in R^*,\quad a\in\mathfrak n\setminus\mathfrak n^2,
\quad J_2=(a,b),\\
B_2=a\beta+b^2\delta,\quad Q=a\zeta+b^2q,
\quad\beta,\zeta\in\mathfrak n,\quad\delta,q\in\mathfrak n^2,\\
aU=\epsilon H^2-2\delta G-q,\quad U\in F_4,
\quad N^4U=6\epsilon^3aP^2,
\quad H\in\mathfrak nA_R\cap\mathfrak nF_4,\\
I_4=N^4(F_4)=P^2J_4,\quad
J_4=(a,t^n)\subsetneq R,\quad
1\le n\le2k,\quad k=\operatorname{length}_R R/J_2.
\end{gathered}
\tag{1}
\]
Here t is a complementary regular parameter and the image of b in
the DVR R/(a) has order k. The normalization does not rescale N.
The exact divisor valuations and nonprincipality106 are the retained
inputs for J4; the existence of U is an actual regular-sequence
division, not a completed-ring construction.

The retained third-image result105 says that every F3 element has
constant values on both reduced components. It does not initially
rule out a parameter-dependent coefficient in the double nilradical.
That distinction will matter below.[^third]

## 2. U has zero whole residue but nonzero internal class

The right side of aU in (1) belongs to n²A_R, since H belongs to
nA_R and delta,q belong to n². Flatness supplies the exact colon
calculation
\[
(\mathfrak n^2A_R:a)=\mathfrak nA_R,
\qquad
\boxed{\quad U\in\mathfrak nA_R
\quad\text{but}\quad U\notin\mathfrak nF_4.\quad}
\tag{2}
\]
Indeed, multiplication by a gives an injective map
R/n to R/n², because (n²:a)=n for a regular parameter of the
regular local surface. Tensoring this map with the flat module A_R
gives the displayed colon identity. This argument is valid over the
whole nonreduced fiber.

For the nonmembership, if U were in nF4, applying N to the fourth
power would put 6epsilon³aP² in nI4=P²nJ4. Cancelling P² gives
a in nJ4, which is contained in n², a contradiction. Thus the two
quotient maps in (2) genuinely distinguish U. Neither is replaced by
a general commutation of degree kernels with reduction.

## 3. The actual fourth-degree basis and algebra generators

The four whole-fiber residues 1,G,G²,c are independent over C.
Suppose a relation among their internal classes and [U] vanishes in
F4/nF4. Its image in the whole fiber first makes all four coefficients
of 1,G,G²,c zero. The nonzero internal class [U] from (2) then
makes the remaining coefficient zero. Since F4 is free of rank five,
these five elements are an actual basis. Consequently
\[
\begin{gathered}
F_4=R\oplus RG\oplus RG^2\oplus Rc\oplus RU,\\
\boxed{\quad W_4=\mathcal D\oplus\mathbb C c,
\qquad \dim_{\mathbb C}W_4=4,\qquad A_R=R[G,c,U].\quad}\\
L=N^4c/P^2\in R,\qquad J_4=(a,b^2,L).
\end{gathered}
\tag{3}
\]
The algebra generation follows from the actual A_R=R[F4], not from
a formal solution of the conic. In particular no division by
1+epsilon bG occurs. The ideal equality follows by applying N4 to
this basis and using N4(G²)=6epsilon²b²P². The units 6epsilon²
and 6epsilon³ can be absorbed into ideal generators.

Thus the only kernel direction of the whole-fiber map
alpha:F4/nF4 to A_R/nA_R is the line C[U]. In particular the
fourth residue space has no extra independent parameter-dependent
nilpotent direction.

## 4. Internal reduction forces W3 to be the full Artin algebra

Let V=F4/nF4 and let beta:V to I4/nI4 be the surjective map
induced by N4. The exact sequence F3 to F4 to I4 gives
ker(beta)=image(F3/nF3 to V). The target has dimension two,
because J4=(a,t^n) is a minimally two-generated ideal. Thus
ker(beta) has dimension three.

Moreover beta([U]) is the nonzero class of 6epsilon³aP². Since
ker(alpha)=C[U], alpha is injective on ker(beta). Its image there
is exactly W3. This proves dimension three without identifying the
two reduction maps. By (3), every W3 element is of the form d+gamma c,
with d in D and gamma in C. The retained constant reduced values
force gamma=0. Therefore
\[
\boxed{\quad
\dim\ker\beta=3,\qquad
\ker\alpha\cap\ker\beta=0,\qquad
W_3=\mathcal D.
\quad}
\tag{4}
\]
No equality between the double and simple translation speeds is
needed. In particular a surviving branch with dim W3=2 cannot have
ord_n(a)=1 in the present proper-ideal coordinates.

## 5. The exponent bound is strict

Choose T in F3 with whole residue G², using (4). Its expression in
the basis (3) is G²+nu U+F with nu in R and
F in n(R+RG+RG²+Rc). Apply N4 and divide by P². Modulo nJ4 this
gives
\[
6\epsilon^2b^2+6\epsilon^3\nu a\in\mathfrak nJ_4,
\qquad \nu\in\mathfrak n,
\qquad
\boxed{\quad b^2\in\mathfrak nJ_4,\qquad J_4=(a,L).\quad}
\tag{5}
\]
To justify the middle assertion, b² and nJ4 are contained in n².
Since a has order one and epsilon is a unit, the membership forces
nu into n. Its a term then belongs to nJ4, giving the first boxed
membership. Apply Nakayama to J4=(a,L)+(b²) and that membership
to obtain the second boxed equality.

Reducing b² in nJ4 modulo a in the DVR R/(a), its order 2k is at
least n+1. The previously weak length bound is therefore
\[
\boxed{\quad
1\le n\le2k-1,\qquad
\operatorname{ord}_{R/(a)}(L\bmod a)=n,\qquad
k=1\ \Longrightarrow\ J_4=\mathfrak n.
\quad}
\tag{6}
\]
The order of L follows from J4=(a,L), so its image is nonzero and
generates the ideal (t^n). This gives a sharp identification of the
selected c derivative as the second minimal generator in this
conditional setting. No assertion determines n for k greater than one.

## 6. What remains open

The new conclusions use the whole-fiber vanishing of the constructed
U, the actual free degree modules, the two-generated fourth image,
and the independent retained reduced-value restriction on F3.
The last restriction is not a consequence of the ranks alone.

The actual presentation A_R=R[G,c,U] is only a generating-set
statement. No defining ideal or polynomial inverse over R is supplied,
and no relation is solved by inverting 1+epsilon bG. The theorem does
not show that U lies in nF4; it proves the opposite. It does not force
the entire F4 to be pure, even though its four-dimensional residue-basis
piece is pure. No mixed-fiber exclusion or global recognition follows.
