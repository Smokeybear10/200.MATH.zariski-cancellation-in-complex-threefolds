# Four global generators of the fixed conductor surface

The actual pinched surface of passes 20–22 admits a closed embedding
into \(\mathbb A^4\). The reduction changes the two functions used to
label and parametrize its conductor; it is not the deletion of one
function from the earlier five-generator list.

This improves the established algebra-generator bound to
\[
3\le \mu_{\mathbb C}(R)\le4.
\]
It does not determine which endpoint is sharp, give an
\(\mathbb A^3\) embedding, or realize this surface as an exceptional
surface of an ambient locally nilpotent derivation. No priority claim
or human expert verification is asserted.

## 1. The same ring and the same gluing

Use the uniformly normalized coordinates of
[the five-generator proof](nodal-generators-pass22.md), Section 1:
\[
B=\mathbb C[u,y,z]/(A(u)z-y(y-1)),\qquad
A(u)=(u-1)(u-i),\qquad d=u^4-1.
\tag{1}
\]
This is the normalization of the fixed pinched ring
\[
R=B\times_{\mathbb C[t]^6}\mathbb C[t]^3,\qquad
I=dB=(d,du)R.
\tag{2}
\]
The original uniform rescaling has not changed: \(u=w/a_0\),
\(z=a_0^2z_{\rm old}\), \(a_0^4=3\), and the shared parameter is
\(t=a_0^3t_{\rm old}\).

The three pairs are, in their original order,
\[
((1,0),(i,0)),\qquad ((1,1),(-i,*)),\qquad
((-1,*),(i,1)).
\tag{3}
\]
Here \((r,\epsilon)\) denotes the split line \(u=r,y=\epsilon\);
\((r,*)\) denotes the entire unsplit fiber at \(u=r\). On these lines
\[
t=\frac{z}{r(2\epsilon-1)}
\quad\hbox{and}\quad
t=\frac{y}{rA(r)},
\tag{4}
\]
respectively. In particular, \(u\in B\) but \(u\notin R\).
The conductor element \(du\), rather than \(u\), is an available
function in \(R\).

## 2. Four functions

Put
\[
\begin{split}
b(u)&=\frac{1-i}{4}(u+1)(u+i),\\
a(u)&=-\frac i2A(u)\left(u+\frac{1+i}{2}\right),\\
q(u)&=-u\,b(u)^2,\qquad r(u)=\frac i8uA(u).
\end{split}
\tag{5}
\]
The letter \(r(u)\) in (5) denotes a coefficient polynomial, not a
chosen root \(r\) in (4). Define
\[
e=du,\qquad g=a(u)+b(u)y,\qquad h=q(u)z+r(u)y.
\tag{6}
\]

**Theorem.**
\[
\boxed{R=\mathbb C[d,e,g,h].}
\tag{7}
\]

First, all four functions belong to the fixed \(R\). The conductor
elements \(d,e\) vanish on every line. Direct substitution of (4)
gives the following restrictions on each of the two members of a pair:

| Pair | \(g\) | \(h\) | \(d,e\) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | \(0\) | \(t\) | \(0,0\) |
| 2 | \(1\) | \(-t\) | \(0,0\) |
| 3 | \(i\) | \(-t\) | \(0,0\) |

The sign of \(h\) on the last two pairs changes the function, not
the gluing: the equality condition still uses the same \(t\) on
both members of every pair.

## 3. Explicit polynomial recovery of the normalization

Write \(S=\mathbb C[d,e,g,h]\subseteq R\). Two polynomial identities
provide global recovery, including all exceptional roots. Define
\[
\begin{split}
U_y(u)={}&\frac{-1+i}{4}u^4+\frac32u^3
-\frac{9(1+i)}4u^2+4iu+2-2i,\\
U_z(u)={}&\frac i8u^4+\frac{-3+3i}{8}u^3
-\frac98u^2-(1+i)u-i.
\end{split}
\tag{8}
\]
Then
\[
bU_y-uAr=1,\qquad AU_z-uq=1.
\tag{9}
\]
Equivalently, the two apparent quotients
\((1+uAr)/b\) and \((1+uq)/A\) are exactly the polynomials
\(U_y,U_z\) in (8). No localization is implicit.

Set \(v=g-a(u)=b(u)y\). The surface equation and (5)–(6) give
\[
Ar\,y=Ah+u\,v(v-b).
\tag{10}
\]
Consequently
\[
y=U_yv-u\bigl(Ah+u\,v(v-b)\bigr).
\tag{11}
\]
Likewise \(Az=y(y-1)\) and \(qz=h-ry\) give
\[
z=U_z\,y(y-1)-u(h-ry).
\tag{12}
\]
All coefficients in (11)–(12) are polynomials in \(u\). Thus
\[
B=S[u].
\tag{13}
\]
This is a polynomial recovery statement, not just equality of
fraction fields.

Since \(u^4-d-1=0\), the normalization \(B\) is a finite
\(S\)-module generated by \(1,u,u^2,u^3\). No freeness is claimed.
The intermediate ring \(R\) is an \(S\)-submodule of the finite
module \(B\). Because \(S\) is Noetherian, \(R\) is finite over \(S\).

## 4. Global points and the smooth complement

Outside the conductor, (2) identifies \(R_d=B_d\). Moreover
\[
u=e/d\in S_d.
\]
Combining this with (13) gives
\[
S_d=R_d=B_d.
\tag{14}
\]
Thus the finite morphism \(\operatorname{Spec}R\to\operatorname{Spec}S\)
is already an isomorphism off the conductor. In particular, there
is no unexamined smooth exceptional locus or missing point at infinity
there.

On the conductor, the table in Section 2 separates its three components
by \(g\), and \(h=\pm t\) separates points within each component.
These are exactly the identifications defining \(R\), with no new
point identifications. A point off the conductor cannot share its
image with a conductor point because their \(d\)-values differ.
The finite map is therefore injective on closed points. It is
surjective by integrality, and all closed residue fields are
\(\mathbb C\).

## 5. The entire pinched cotangent space

At every conductor point the completed local ring of the fixed
surface is
\[
\mathbb C[[\xi,\eta,t]]/(\xi\eta).
\tag{15}
\]
This is the actual two-branch local ring from the conductor
construction, not either normalization branch alone.

Let the two distinct normalized roots in a pair be \(u_j,u_k\).
On either smooth normalization branch, \(u-u_j\) and \(h\) are
local coordinates along the conductor. On a split line this follows
from \(2y-1\ne0\) and \(q(u_j)\ne0\); on an unsplit line it follows
from \(A(u_j)\ne0\) and \(r(u_j)\ne0\). These coefficients are
nonzero at every point of the relevant line.

Keeping the common \(t\) fixed, the two normal derivatives of
\((d,e)\) are
\[
\begin{pmatrix}
4u_j^3&4u_k^3\\
4u_j^4&4u_k^4
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{16}
\]
Its determinant is
\[
16u_j^3u_k^3(u_k-u_j)\ne0.
\tag{17}
\]
Also \(h=\sigma t\) along the pair, with
\(\sigma=1,-1,-1\), respectively. Thus \(d,e,h\) span both normal
directions and the common tangent direction in the three-dimensional
cotangent space of (15). In particular, the map on cotangent spaces
is surjective at every conductor point.

The unknown normal derivatives of \(h\) do not affect this
determinant: \(d,e\) have zero common tangent derivative, and
the common tangent derivative of \(h\) is the nonzero constant
\(\sigma\).

## 6. Equality of rings

There are two independent recognition arguments once the polynomial
recovery and finiteness of Section 3 are established.

### 6.1. A direct full-conductor argument

The table in Section 2 implies that
\[
S\longrightarrow R/I\cong\mathbb C[t]^3
\tag{18}
\]
is surjective. Indeed, Lagrange polynomials in \(g\), evaluated
at its three distinct values \(0,1,i\), give the three idempotents.
Multiplying them by \(h\) gives respectively \(t,-t,-t\) on
their supported components. Thus these restrictions generate the
entire product ring, not merely its closed points.

Let \(M=R/S\), a finite \(S\)-module. By (2) and (18),
\[
R=S+(d,e)R,\qquad M=(d,e)M.
\tag{19}
\]
The second identity is an identity of \(S\)-modules:
\(d,e\in S\), so their multiplication descends to \(R/S\).
No \(R\)-module structure on \(R/S\) is assumed.

Equation (14) gives \(M_d=0\). At a maximal ideal
\(\mathfrak m\subset S\) containing \(d\), the identity
\[
e^4=d^4(d+1)
\tag{20}
\]
forces \(e\in\mathfrak m\). Localizing (19), Nakayama over
\(S_{\mathfrak m}\) gives \(M_{\mathfrak m}=0\).
At maximal ideals not containing \(d\), the same vanishing follows
from \(M_d=0\). Therefore \(M=0\), proving \(R=S\).

This proof uses the exact conductor ideal \(I=(d,e)R\), its full
quotient, and polynomial normalization recovery. It does not
replace the conductor ideal by its support.

### 6.2. Independent finite/cotangent recognition

For completeness, the geometric finite recognition argument follows.
For a maximal ideal \(\mathfrak m\) of \(S\), let
\[
A_*=S_{\mathfrak m},\qquad B_*=R\otimes_S S_{\mathfrak m}.
\]
The finite map is bijective on closed points, so \(B_*\) is local,
with a unique maximal ideal \(\mathfrak n\) and residue field
equal to that of \(A_*\). Cotangent surjectivity yields
\[
\mathfrak n=\mathfrak mB_*+\mathfrak n^2.
\]
Nakayama over \(B_*\), applied to
\(\mathfrak n/\mathfrak mB_*\), gives
\(\mathfrak n=\mathfrak mB_*\). Residue-field equality then gives
\(B_*=A_*+\mathfrak mB_*\). Apply Nakayama over \(A_*\) to
the finite module \(B_*/A_*\): it vanishes. Therefore the finite
module \(R/S\) vanishes at every maximal ideal of \(S\), proving
\(R=S\).

Finiteness is supplied by the explicit identities (9)–(13);
point separation and cotangent surjectivity alone would not be enough.

## 7. A four-variable elimination presentation

The proof also specifies an exact presentation. Let
\[
P=\mathbb C[D,E,G,H]
\]
and adjoin an independent variable \(U\). Evaluate every coefficient
polynomial from (5), (8) at \(U\), and define
\[
\begin{split}
v_*&=G-a(U),\\
y_*&=U_y(U)v_*-U\bigl(A(U)H+U v_*(v_*-b(U))\bigr),\\
z_*&=U_z(U)y_*(y_*-1)-U(H-r(U)y_*).
\end{split}
\tag{21}
\]
In \(P[U]\), take the ideal \(J\) generated by
\[
\begin{split}
&A(U)z_*-y_*(y_*-1),\\
&G-a(U)-b(U)y_*,\\
&H-q(U)z_*-r(U)y_*,\\
&D-U^4+1,\qquad E-UD.
\end{split}
\tag{22}
\]
The recovery identities give mutually inverse homomorphisms
\[
P[U]/J\cong B:
\]
send \(U\) to \(u\), \(D,E,G,H\) to \(d,e,g,h\), and conversely
send \(u,y,z\) to \(U,y_*,z_*\). Every relation and every generator
is recovered by (21)–(22). In particular,
\[
R\cong P/(J\cap P).
\tag{23}
\]
This is a concrete prime elimination ideal. It is not a computed
minimal list of relations, and it has no unstated saturation.

## Verification and boundary

The standalone [exact checker](nodal_elimination_checks_pass23.py)
uses only SymPy and the standard library, with seven test methods.
It reconstructs the six original line parametrizations, both Bézout
identities, polynomial recovery before taking the quotient, finiteness
and off-conductor recovery identities, conductor point separation,
the full conductor quotient's idempotents, the base relation (20),
and the complete conductor cotangent determinant.

/opt/anaconda3/bin/python research/nodal_elimination_checks_pass23.py
~~~

The targeted run passed all seven methods in 0.274 seconds.

The two finite/local ring arguments above are the human-readable part of
the verification, not a numerical test assertion. The only inherited
geometric premises are the fixed ring, its conductor, and its local
two-branch model, proved in
[the conductor construction](nodal-conductor-pass20.md) and used in
[the independently audited five-generator proof](nodal-generators-pass22.md).
No new external classification theorem is invoked.

The local cotangent dimension still gives the lower bound \(3\).
The earlier argument excluding every triple \(\{d,du,f\}\) still
applies, because \(R/(d,du)\cong\mathbb C[t]^3\) is not
one-generated. Consequently a three-generator presentation, if one
exists, must make a further substantive change of generators. This
pass stops at the proved four-generator reduction.
