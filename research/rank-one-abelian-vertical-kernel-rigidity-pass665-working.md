# A nonzero vertical kernel collapses an abelian ambient family

Date: September 18, 2026.

Under the explicit rank-one and non-fence plinth hypotheses below,
an abelian family of ambient locally nilpotent derivations has two
possibilities. Its map taking a field to its value on the cylinder
variable is injective, or the entire family centralizes the unique
intrinsic direction and lies in a specified rank-two module. Thus
cancellation between several commuting conjugation orbits cannot
produce an intrinsic direction transverse to that unique line.

This is a conditional structural restriction, not a proof that a
rank-one cancellation candidate exists or is impossible. No useful
ambient family is constructed for an arbitrary polynomial cylinder.
The main cancellation assertion remains UNSOLVED; novelty is UNKNOWN.

## 1. All hypotheses and the statements

Let \(A\) be a finitely generated complex domain and a UFD. Assume
that \(0\ne D\in\operatorname{LND}(A)\) is primitive and
\[
 C:=\ker_A D=\mathbb C[a,b],\qquad
 \operatorname{pl}_A(D):=D(A)\cap C=pC,
 \quad 0\ne p\notin C^*.
 \tag{1}
\]
Primitive means that no nonunit divides every value of \(D\), not
that its image ideal is the unit ideal. Impose the non-fence condition
\[
 p\notin\mathbb C[g]\quad\text{for every plane coordinate }g
 \text{ of }C.
 \tag{2}
\]
Here a plane coordinate means \(C=\mathbb C[g,h]\) for some \(h\).
Condition (2) is the precise meaning of non-fence used in this note;
no irreducibility or squarefreeness of \(p\) is assumed.
For the abelian-family dichotomy, additionally assume the full
rank-one description
\[
 \operatorname{LND}(A)=CD,
 \tag{3}
\]
including the zero derivation. Put \(B=A[u]\), extend \(D\) by
\(D(u)=0\), and set \(E=\partial_u\).

Let \(\mathfrak H\) be a finite-dimensional complex vector space
of pairwise commuting LNDs of \(B\), including zero. Equivalently,
one can take the constant span of finitely many commuting LNDs:
every such sum is again LND, by the multinomial formula. Define
\[
 \nu:\mathfrak H\longrightarrow B,\qquad \nu(W)=W(u),
 \quad \mathcal K=\ker\nu.
 \tag{4}
\]

**Centralizer statement.** Under (1)--(2), without needing (3),
\[
 \{W\in\operatorname{LND}(B):[D,W]=0\}
       =C[u]D+CE.
 \tag{5}
\]
The right side is a complex vector space and a \(C\)-module of
LNDs, not an abelian Lie algebra in general.

**Vertical-kernel dichotomy.** Under (1)--(3), either \(\nu\) is
injective, or
\[
 \mathcal K\ne0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 [D,\mathfrak H]=0,\quad \mathfrak H\subset C[u]D+CE,
 \quad\nu(\mathfrak H)\subset C,
 \quad\operatorname{rank}_{\operatorname{Frac}B}\mathfrak H\le2.
 \tag{6}
\]
No normalization assumption on \(\mathfrak H\) is needed for (6).
Section 6 records the additional consequence when
\([D,\mathfrak H]\subset\mathfrak H\), as in commuting conjugation
families. The two alternatives do not claim that an injective
vertical map has orbit rank greater than two.

The proof works under the displayed algebraic hypotheses without
assuming \(B\) polynomial. In the intended application
\(A[u]\simeq\mathbb C^{[4]}\), the UFD and other inherited properties
are available, while (3) specifies the hypothetical rank-one branch.
The principal full plinth and condition (2) are explicit assumptions
here, not silently deduced from polynomiality of the cylinder.

## 2. Primitive saturation and the retained relative classification

We spell out the algebra used twice below. Choose algebra generators
\(a_1,\ldots,a_m\) of \(A\). The ideal generated by all values of
\(D\) is generated by \(D(a_1),\ldots,D(a_m)\), by the product rule.
Their gcd is one in \(A\). It stays one in the UFD \(B=A[u]\):
a nonconstant-in-\(u\) irreducible cannot divide a nonzero element
of \(A\), and a common irreducible from \(A\) is already excluded.

Consequently, if \(\eta\in\operatorname{Frac}B\) and \(\eta D\)
is a regular derivation of \(B\), then \(\eta\in B\). Write
\(\eta=h/k\) in coprime UFD form. Regularity says
\(k\mid hD(a_i)\) for every \(i\), hence \(k\mid D(a_i)\) for
every \(i\). The gcd condition forces \(k\) to be a unit. This
does not require the image ideal of \(D\) to be all of \(B\).

We also use two elementary LND facts. Its degree is additive on
products of nonzero elements of a characteristic-zero domain.
Thus \(W(q)\in qB\) implies \(W(q)=0\): otherwise its degree
would be both \(\deg_W(q)-1\) and at least \(\deg_W(q)\).
Additivity also proves factorial closure of \(\ker W\).

By (1), choose \(r\in A\) with \(Dr=p\). The local-slice theorem
gives
\[
 A_p=C_p[r],\qquad B_p=C_p[r,u],\qquad D=p\partial_r.
 \tag{7}
\]
For completeness, \(r/p\) is a slice on \(A_p\). Subtracting
\(D^n(v)(r/p)^n/n!\) from an element of \(D\)-degree \(n\)
lowers its degree; induction yields the polynomial slice expansion.
Its coefficients are in \((\ker D)_p=C_p\). Differentiating a
polynomial relation in the slice proves algebraic independence.

If \(0\ne q\in B\) and \(qD\) is LND, then \(Dq=0\).
Indeed \(qD\) fixes \(p\), so is still LND on (7). Over the
fraction field of \(C_p[u]\), a derivation \(a(r)\partial_r\)
with a positive-degree polynomial coefficient is not LND: its
successive values on \(r\) have nonzero leading coefficients.
Thus \(q\) has degree zero in \(r\), giving \(Dq=0\).

The exact relative classification already proved in
[35, Section 1](rank-one-cylinder-pass35.md) is
\[
 \{W\in\operatorname{LND}(B):W(u)=0\}=C[u]D.
 \tag{8}
\]
Here is its reconstruction using (3). Specializing such a \(W\)
at every scalar \(u=c\) gives an LND \(W_c\) of \(A\), hence
a member of \(CD\). Choose \(a_0\) with \(Da_0\ne0\). For every
generator \(a_i\), the polynomial
\[
 D(a_0)W(a_i)-D(a_i)W(a_0)\in A[u]
 \tag{9}
\]
vanishes at every complex value of \(u\), so is zero. Therefore
\(W=(W(a_0)/D(a_0))D\) on the fraction field. Saturation makes
the multiplier polynomial; the preceding LND fact makes it
\(D\)-invariant, hence an element of \(C[u]\). Conversely every
such multiple is LND. This is an inherited theorem, not a new
relative-stability assertion.

## 3. The non-fence plane lemma, without plane preservation

We need the following consequence of the plane embedding theorem.
Let \(R\) be any finitely generated complex domain containing an
embedded plane \(C=\mathbb C[a,b]\). If an LND \(\delta\) of \(R\)
kills a nonconstant \(p\in C\) satisfying (2), then it kills \(C\).
There is no assumption that \(\delta(C)\subset C\).

Suppose otherwise, and select a nonzero one of \(\delta(a),\delta(b)\).
The polynomial \(p:\mathbb A^2\to\mathbb A^1\) has finitely many
critical values. Its critical locus has finitely many irreducible
components, and the differential of \(p\) vanishes on each of them.
In characteristic zero its restriction is constant on each
positive-dimensional component; isolated components add finitely
many values. The product of the chosen nonzero derivative with
all factors \(p-\lambda\) for these critical values is nonzero in
the domain \(R\). Choose a complex closed point where it does not
vanish. This is legitimate for a nonzero element of a finite-type
complex domain.

The \(\delta\)-orbit of that point is a polynomial map from
\(\mathbb A^1\). Its projection to the plane is nonconstant, because
its initial projected derivative is nonzero, and it lies in a smooth
irreducible component \(\Gamma\) of a fiber \(p=\lambda_0\).
The map extends to a nonconstant map from \(\mathbb P^1\) to the
smooth projective completion \(\overline\Gamma\). Such a map is
surjective and forces \(\overline\Gamma\) to have genus zero.
Every point of \(\overline\Gamma\setminus\Gamma\) has a nonempty
preimage contained in the single source point at infinity. Thus
there is at most one omitted point. There is at least one because
\(\Gamma\) is a positive-dimensional affine curve. Consequently
\(\Gamma\simeq\mathbb A^1\).

Abhyankar--Moh--Suzuki now makes an irreducible equation \(g\) of
\(\Gamma\) a coordinate of the plane: \(C=\mathbb C[g,h]\).[^ams]
Since \(g\) divides the invariant polynomial \(p-\lambda_0\),
factorial closure of the LND kernel in \(R\) gives \(\delta g=0\).
If \(\delta\) still moves the plane, then \(\delta h\ne0\), and
\[
 0=\delta p=p_h\,\delta h
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad p_h=0
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad p\in\mathbb C[g].
 \tag{10}
\]
The last implication is characteristic zero. It contradicts (2),
proving the lemma. This repeats the orbit-to-smooth-fiber and plane
embedding mechanism of [651, Section 5](constant-speed-cuspidal-quotient-plinth-pass651-working.md);
no generic connectedness, normality of \(R\), or surface-classification
theorem is being inserted.

## 4. Full plinth preservation and the ambient centralizer

Extending \(D\) by zero on \(u\) gives, coefficient by coefficient,
\[
 \ker_B D=C[u],\qquad \operatorname{pl}_B(D)=pC[u].
 \tag{11}
\]
For the second equality, if \(D(\sum a_j u^j)=\sum h_j u^j\)
is invariant, then each \(h_j=D(a_j)\) lies in \(C\), hence in
the full plinth \(pC\). Conversely \(D(rq)=pq\) for \(q\in C[u]\).

Let \(W\) be LND and commute with \(D\). It preserves both the
kernel and full plinth of \(D\): if \(h=Dv\) is invariant, then
\(Wh=D(Wv)\) is invariant as well. In particular
\(Wp\in pC[u]\). LND divisibility gives \(Wp=0\). Restricting
\(W\) to the stable polynomial subring \(C[u]\) and applying
Section 3 proves \(W(C)=0\).

This restriction is a \(C\)-linear LND of \(C[u]\), so
\(W(u)=\beta\in C\). To justify the assertion, pass to
\(\operatorname{Frac}(C)[u]\); a positive-degree coefficient of
\(\partial_u\) would have nonvanishing iterates on \(u\), as in
Section 2. The polynomial coefficient must therefore have degree zero.

The regular derivation \(W-\beta E\) kills \(C\) and \(u\).
On \(\operatorname{Frac}B=\operatorname{Frac}(C)(r,u)\), it is
a rational multiple \(qD\). Primitive saturation gives \(q\in B\).
Both \(W\) and \(\beta E\) commute with \(D\), so
\(D(q)D=0\); hence \(q\in C[u]\). This step does not assume
that the difference \(W-\beta E\) is LND.

Conversely \(q(u)D+\beta E\), for \(q\in C[u]\) and \(\beta\in C\),
preserves \(B\) and fixes \(p\). On (7) it is the triangular field
\[
 p q(u)\partial_r+\beta\partial_u
 \tag{12}
\]
over \(C_p\), which is LND. Restriction through the injective
localization proves it is LND on \(B\). It commutes with \(D\),
proving (5). In this module the exact bracket is
\[
 [qD+\beta E,\ q'D+\beta'E]
       =(\beta\partial_u q'-\beta'\partial_u q)D.
 \tag{13}
\]
Thus membership alone does not assert pairwise commutativity.

## 5. A nonzero vertical kernel forces centralization

Assume (3) and \(\mathcal K\ne0\). By (8), choose
\(0\ne qD\in\mathcal K\), with \(q\in C[u]\). For any
\(W\in\mathfrak H\), abelianness gives
\[
 0=[W,qD]=W(q)D+q[W,D],\qquad
 [W,D]=-\frac{W(q)}qD.
 \tag{14}
\]
The left side of the second equality is a regular derivation, so
primitive saturation proves that \(W(q)/q\) is polynomial. Thus
\(q\mid W(q)\). Since \(W\) is LND, divisibility forces \(W(q)=0\),
and (14) gives \([W,D]=0\). This holds for every member of the
family, without needing an initial normalization hypothesis.

Section 4 now places \(\mathfrak H\) in \(C[u]D+CE\), and its
vertical values in \(C\). The fields \(D,E\) are independent over
\(\operatorname{Frac}B\), because \(D(u)=0\), \(E(u)=1\), and
\(D\ne0\). This proves the rank bound in (6). The commuting flows
define an additive algebraic group action; this rank is its generic
orbit dimension. The complex dimension of \(\mathfrak H\) itself
need not be at most two.

For an LND, preservation of \((u)\) is equivalent to killing \(u\).
Consequently \(\mathcal K\) is exactly the set of descending constant
combinations in this family. Every member is \(q(u)D\), and its
action on \(B/(u)=A\) is \(q(0)D\). This may be zero, but it is
never transverse to \(D\).

## 6. The additional normalized-family consequence and remaining gap

Suppose also \([D,\mathfrak H]\subset\mathfrak H\). Since \(D(u)=0\),
\[
 \nu([D,W])=D(\nu(W)).
 \tag{15}
\]
Finite conjugation by the LND \(D\) makes \(\operatorname{ad}D\)
locally nilpotent on regular derivations of the finitely generated
ring \(B\); on the finite-dimensional stable space \(\mathfrak H\)
it is nilpotent. This is the finite-adjoint argument retained in
[662](commuting-orbit-coefficient-tangent-test-pass662-working.md).

If the family is not centralized by \(D\), (6) forces \(\nu\) to be
injective. Equation (15) then identifies its adjoint Jordan chains
with the corresponding \(D\)-chains of vertical values. In particular,
for every nonzero \(W\),
\[
 \deg_{\operatorname{ad}D}W=\deg_D(Wu),\qquad Wu\ne0.
 \tag{16}
\]
If the family is centralized instead, both degrees are zero whenever
\(Wu\ne0\); any nonzero member with \(Wu=0\) has adjoint degree zero.
These are statements about the supplied finite commuting family,
not about arbitrary sums or brackets of unrelated ambient LNDs.

The argument does not exclude the injective branch, construct a
family with a useful vertical kernel, or force generic orbit rank
at most two in the injective branch. The independent unipotent
criterion of [35](rank-one-cylinder-pass35.md) requires containment
of \(E\); normalization by \(D\) does not supply that hypothesis.
The symmetry boundary in [30](intrinsic-symmetry-pass30.md) likewise
does not transfer arbitrary cylinder actions to the chosen quotient.
No conclusion about affine-linear intrinsic actions is used here.

The relative classification (8), primitive saturation, and the
orbit-to-plane mechanism are inherited and have been reconstructed
explicitly. The bounded consequence is their application to the
vertical kernel of an abelian family, together with (5). This is not
a literature-priority determination. No saved checker was executed,
imported, compiled or edited, and no canonical file was changed.
The complete source passed two independent internal reviews; no
human expert review or formal certification is claimed.
