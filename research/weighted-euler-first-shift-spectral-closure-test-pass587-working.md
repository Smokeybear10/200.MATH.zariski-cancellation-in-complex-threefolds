# A first-shift spectral closure test and its actual boundaries

## 1. The bounded result

Let
\[
 B=\mathbb C[X_1,\ldots,X_n]=A[u],\qquad
 E=\partial_u,\quad Eu=1,\quad A=\ker E,
 \qquad d_i=\deg_E X_i>0,
 \qquad D=\sum_i d_iX_i\partial_{X_i}.
\tag{1}
\]
The displayed frame is an actual polynomial coordinate frame, and assume
the exact whole-ring shift of \(D\) for the \(E\)-filtration is one.
No polynomial presentation of \(A\) is assumed.

There is a finite sufficient test for a commuting decomposition of
\(E\) into locally nilpotent \(D\)-weight components. Take its distinct
adjoint weights \(\lambda\), and form the sums \(\lambda+\mu\) over
unordered pairs of distinct weights. If these pair sums are all
distinct, the weight components commute and are all locally nilpotent.
The complex Lie algebra generated by \(E,D\) then has dimension one
more than the number of components. Their flows give an additive-group
action normalized by the actual Euler torus. In particular the test
always succeeds when there are at most three distinct weights.

The pair-sum condition cannot simply be omitted from the commuting
conclusion. The complete actual first-shift frame583 has five weights
whose components do not commute and need not preserve \(A\). Its Lie
algebra is nevertheless finite dimensional; an exact nine-dimensional
description is given below. Thus it is not a counterexample to general
finite-dimensional closure at shift one.

Even success of the commuting test does not guarantee an Euler-
homogeneous slice for \(E\). The revised actual control582 has three
commuting components and no such slice. A coordinate slice for one
component is not automatically a slice for their sum. These statements
do not prove or disprove general recognition of \(A\), general
first-shift Lie closure, or cancellation. No novelty is claimed.

The whole sources576,577,582 and583 supply the directly relevant
contracts and controls. Their needed identities are rederived here.
The high-shift spectral control584 is not used to infer any first-shift
counterexample.

## 2. Exact first shift gives the double-commutator relation

Since \(D\) has shift one, its complete coefficient form in \(A[u]\)
initially has the shape
\[
 D|_A=M+uN,\qquad
 D(u)=b_2u^2+b_1u+b_0,
 \quad M,N\in\operatorname{Der}_{\mathbb C}(A),\quad b_i\in A.
\tag{2}
\]
These are whole-ring coefficient identities, not assignments of
leading vectors. The induced nonzero degree-one derivation on
\(\operatorname{gr}_E B=A[\eta]\) is locally nilpotent. Indeed, if
arbitrarily many of its iterates on an initial form were nonzero,
the corresponding iterates of \(D\) would have unbounded \(E\)-degree.
This contradicts the finite-dimensional orbit of that polynomial under
the actual locally finite Euler derivation.

This induced LND takes \(\eta\) to \(b_2\eta^2\), so it kills
\(\eta\). The elementary divisibility fact used here is that an LND
\(L\) on a characteristic-zero domain cannot satisfy
\(L(f)=fg\ne0\): the additive LND degree gives
\(\deg_L L(f)=\deg_L f-1\), whereas
\(\deg_L(fg)=\deg_L f+\deg_L g\). Consequently \(b_2=0\).
The induced derivation is \(\eta N\), with \(N\ne0\) locally
nilpotent on \(A\), and the full identities are
\[
 D|_A=M+uN,\qquad D(u)=b_1u+b_0,
 \qquad [E,D]=N+b_1E,
 \qquad [E,[E,D]]=0.
\tag{3}
\]
Here the extension of \(N\) kills the supplied slice \(u\), and the
bracket convention is \([P,Q]=PQ-QP\). No constancy of \(b_1\),
local finiteness of \(M\), or slice for \(N\) is inserted.

The proof below uses only the last relation of (3), the actual Euler
spectral decomposition, and local nilpotence of \(E\). Its hypotheses
have now been verified in the full first-shift setup (1).

## 3. The spectral identity and a finite noncollision test

For a monomial vector field in the actual coordinates,
\[
 [D,X^\alpha\partial_{X_i}]
   =\left(\sum_j d_j\alpha_j-d_i\right)
       X^\alpha\partial_{X_i}.
\tag{4}
\]
Every polynomial derivation therefore has finite integral adjoint
weight support. Write the nonzero components of \(E\) as
\[
 E=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal S}E_\lambda,
 \qquad [D,E_\lambda]=\lambda E_\lambda,
 \qquad 0<|\mathcal S|<\infty.
\tag{5}
\]
They are genuine polynomial derivations on \(B\). Their definition
does not imply that they preserve \(A\) or kill \(u\).

A bracket \([E_\lambda,E_\mu]\) has weight \(\lambda+\mu\).
Grouping the entire relation (3) by this weight gives, for every
integer \(s\),
\[
 \sum_{\substack{\lambda<\mu\\\lambda+\mu=s}}
    (\mu-\lambda)[E_\lambda,E_\mu]=0.
\tag{6}
\]
There is no contribution from a diagonal pair, because its bracket
is zero. Formula (6) follows by expanding both ordered terms for
each unordered pair; multiplying the entire double commutator by
minus one gives the displayed sign convention.

Suppose the map
\[
 \{\lambda,\mu\}\longmapsto\lambda+\mu,
 \qquad \lambda,\mu\in\mathcal S,\quad\lambda\ne\mu,
\tag{7}
\]
is injective. Each nonempty sum in (6) then contains one term.
Characteristic zero and \(\lambda\ne\mu\) imply
\([E_\lambda,E_\mu]=0\). This proves pairwise commutation,
including the vacuous one-component case. With two or three weights,
two distinct unordered pairs would share a weight, so equality of
their sums would force their other weights to agree. Thus (7) is
automatic when \(|\mathcal S|\le3\).

The test is finite once the polynomial coefficients of \(E\) in the
actual frame are given: (4) extracts their finite weight support and
(7) compares a finite list of integers. Failure of (7) is inconclusive
about commutation; it merely leaves room for cancellation in (6).

## 4. Why the commuting components are LNDs

Let \(\tau_t\), \(t\in\mathbb C^*\), be the actual Euler torus
automorphism \(\tau_t(X_i)=t^{d_i}X_i\). Its conjugates of \(E\)
are
\[
 E^{(t)}=\tau_tE\tau_t^{-1}
       =\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal S}t^\lambda E_\lambda.
\tag{8}
\]
Every \(E^{(t)}\) is locally nilpotent. Under (7), all these
conjugates commute because the \(E_\lambda\) commute.

Put \(r=|\mathcal S|\). The distinct Laurent monomials
\(t^\lambda\) are linearly independent as functions on
\(\mathbb C^*\). Hence one can choose nonzero scalars
\(t_1,\ldots,t_r\) for which the matrix
\((t_j^\lambda)_{j,\lambda}\) is invertible. For completeness,
if the evaluation vectors spanned a proper subspace of \(\mathbb C^r\),
a nonzero linear functional annihilating that subspace would give a
nonzero Laurent polynomial vanishing at every nonzero complex number,
which is impossible. Thus (8) expresses each \(E_\lambda\) as a
finite complex linear combination of commuting LNDs.

Such a linear combination is locally nilpotent. Given a polynomial
\(f\), choose \(k_j\) so that \((E^{(t_j)})^{k_j}f=0\).
In a sufficiently high power of their linear combination, every
multinomial term contains at least \(k_j\) copies of one derivation.
Commutation moves those copies next to \(f\), annihilating that term.
This proves local nilpotence on every \(f\), without assuming that
local nilpotence survives arbitrary noncommuting projection.

Conversely, each spectral component lies in the complex Lie algebra
generated by \(D,E\), through the exact finite interpolation formula
\[
 E_\lambda=
 \prod_{\substack{\mu\in\mathcal S\\\mu\ne\lambda}}
       \frac{\operatorname{ad}_D-\mu}{\lambda-\mu}\,(E).
\tag{9}
\]
Consequently the full Lie algebra under (7) is
\[
 \operatorname{Lie}_{\mathbb C}(D,E)
   =\mathbb C D\ltimes
           \bigoplus_{\lambda\in\mathcal S}\mathbb C E_\lambda,
 \qquad \dim\operatorname{Lie}_{\mathbb C}(D,E)=r+1.
\tag{10}
\]
Distinct nonzero weight components are linearly independent. Also
\(D\) is not in their span: that span consists of LNDs by the same
commuting argument, while the nonzero positive Euler derivation is
not locally nilpotent.

The finite product
\(\prod_\lambda\exp(z_\lambda E_\lambda)\) defines a polynomial
action of \(\mathbb G_a^r\) on the whole affine \(n\)-space.
The Euler torus normalizes this action by
\(z_\lambda\mapsto t^\lambda z_\lambda\). Thus (10) comes with
an actual algebraic group action, not merely a formal vector-field
decomposition. No orbit quotient or coordinate descent is inferred
from that action alone.

## 5. Actual first shift with noncommuting components

Use the complete accepted583 frame in
\(\mathbb C[a,b,c,u]\), with \(E=\partial_u\):
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 X&=u,&Y&=a+u^2,&Z&=b+au+u^4,\\
 W&=c-\frac13b^2u+\frac13ab u^2-\frac13a^2u^3+u^6.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]
Its entire inverse is obtained successively from
\[
 u=X,\quad a=Y-X^2,\quad b=Z-aX-X^4,
 \quad c=W+\frac13b^2X-\frac13abX^2+\frac13a^2X^3-X^6.
\tag{12}
\]
Both inverse orders are polynomial identities, with Jacobian \(-1\)
in the ordered old coordinates \((a,b,c,u)\). The exact actual
degrees are \((1,2,4,6)\). Its entire weighted Euler field is
\[
 D=2a\partial_a+(4b+au)\partial_b+(6c+b^2u)\partial_c
       +u\partial_u.
\tag{13}
\]
Checking the four complete eigen-equations verifies (13), including
all lower terms in \(W\). The horizontal coefficients have
\(u\)-degree at most one, and the vertical coefficient has degree
one. Equality on \(b\) proves exact shift one. Moreover
\([E,D]=E+a\partial_b+b^2\partial_c\), and its next bracket
with \(E\) is zero.

In the ordered coefficient variables \((u,a,b,c)\), the complete
spectral decomposition of this same \(E\) is
\[
\begin{aligned}
 E_{-2}&=a\partial_b-
        \frac{au(au-2b)}3\partial_c,\\
 E_{-1}&=\partial_u-a\partial_b+
        \frac{(2au-b)^2}3\partial_c,\\
 E_0&=-2a^2u^2\partial_c,\\
 E_1&=\frac{4au(au+b)}3\partial_c,\\
 E_2&=-\frac{(au+b)^2}3\partial_c.
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
\]
They sum to \(E\), and direct brackets with the entire field (13)
give \([D,E_\lambda]=\lambda E_\lambda\). Therefore (14)
is the actual source-weight decomposition, despite being written
in coefficient-slice variables for readability.

Its only nonzero unordered component brackets are
\[
\begin{aligned}
 [E_{-2},E_1]&=\frac43a^2u\partial_c,&
 [E_{-1},E_0]&=-4a^2u\partial_c,\\
 [E_{-2},E_2]&=-\frac23a(au+b)\partial_c,&
 [E_{-1},E_1]&=\frac43a(au+b)\partial_c.
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
\]
The repeated pair sums are essential: the first row satisfies
\(3[E_{-2},E_1]+[E_{-1},E_0]=0\), and the second satisfies
\(4[E_{-2},E_2]+2[E_{-1},E_1]=0\), exactly as in (6).
Thus the double-commutator relation does not imply pairwise
commutation at first shift.

The same example disproves automatic preservation of the original
invariant ring by a spectral component. In fact
\[
 E_{-2}(c)=-\frac13a^2u^2+\frac23abu\notin A,
 \qquad [E,E_{-2}](c)=-\frac23a^2u+\frac23ab\ne0.
\tag{16}
\]
It does not disprove local nilpotence of individual components.
All five fields in (14) strictly lower the nonnegative grading in
\(u,b,c\) of weights \((1,1,3)\), over \(\mathbb C[a]\).
Hence all five are LNDs.

## 6. This noncommuting example has finite Lie closure

To record the precise boundary, let \(L=a\partial_b\) and put
\[
 \mathcal V=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb C}
 \{b^2,\ abu,\ a^2u^2,\ ab,\ a^2u,\ a^2\}\partial_c.
\tag{17}
\]
Then the exact complex Lie algebra of (11) is
\[
 \operatorname{Lie}_{\mathbb C}(D,E)
   =\mathbb C D+\mathbb C E+\mathbb C L+\mathcal V,
 \qquad \dim\operatorname{Lie}_{\mathbb C}(D,E)=9.
\tag{18}
\]
Here is a direct verification of both inclusions. The recovered
\(N=[E,D]-E=L+b^2\partial_c\) is in the Lie algebra and
\([N,D]=2L-2b^2\partial_c\). Therefore
\(L=N/2+[N,D]/4\) and \(b^2\partial_c=N-L\) belong to it.
Next
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 [D,b^2\partial_c]&=2b^2\partial_c+2abu\partial_c,\\
 [D,abu\partial_c]&=abu\partial_c+a^2u^2\partial_c.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{19}
\]
Bracketing these quadratic vertical fields with \(E\) produces
\(ab\partial_c\) and \(a^2u\partial_c\); then bracketing
\(ab\partial_c\) with \(L\) produces \(a^2\partial_c\).
This gives every displayed generator of \(\mathcal V\).

For the opposite inclusion, the vertical fields commute. Brackets
with \(E\) and \(L\) differentiate their coefficients by
\(\partial_u\) and \(a\partial_b\), preserving \(\mathcal V\).
Besides (19), the remaining \(D\)-brackets on its ordered monomials
are
\[
\begin{aligned}
 [D,a^2u^2\partial_c]&=0,&
 [D,ab\partial_c]&=a^2u\partial_c,\\
 [D,a^2u\partial_c]&=-a^2u\partial_c,&
 [D,a^2\partial_c]&=-2a^2\partial_c.
\end{aligned}
\tag{20}
\]
Finally \([D,E]=-E-L-b^2\partial_c\),
\([D,L]=-2L-2abu\partial_c\), and \([E,L]=0\).
The displayed span is therefore Lie closed. Its nine generators
are independent: the \(a\), then \(u\), then \(b\) components
separate \(D,E,L\), and the six vertical monomials are independent
over \(\mathbb C\). This proves (18).

The known polynomial invariant ring and complete inverse remain
visible throughout. This control defeats the proposed commutation
and invariant-subring conclusions, but not finite-dimensional closure
or recognition by other actual coordinate changes.

## 7. A component's homogeneous slice need not be an E-slice

The revised control582 gives a separate obstruction to overinterpreting
the positive result. Start in the actual coordinates \((a,x,z,c)\),
and set
\[
 E=(1+a^2)\partial_x+a\partial_z,
 \qquad u=x-az,
 \qquad b=ax-(1+a^2)z.
\tag{21}
\]
It is a sliced LND, with the entire inverse
\(x=(1+a^2)u-ab\), \(z=au-b\). Thus
\(B=\mathbb C[a,b,c,u]\), and \(A=\mathbb C[a,b,c]\).
The actual frame \((x,a+x,z,c+x)\) has degrees \((1,1,1,1)\);
its Euler field is the standard Euler field in \((a,x,z,c)\).
Its whole coefficient identities include
\[
 D(a)=a,\quad D(c)=c,\quad
 D(u)=(1-a^2)u+ab,\quad
 D(b)=(1+a^2)b+a(1-a^2)u.
\tag{22}
\]
They prove exact shift one, including the nonzero horizontal top.

Here the spectral components are just
\[
 E_{-1}=\partial_x,\qquad E_0=a\partial_z,
 \qquad E_1=a^2\partial_x.
\tag{23}
\]
They commute, satisfy (7), and generate with \(D\) the
four-dimensional Lie algebra described by (10). The component
\(\partial_x\) has the actual homogeneous coordinate slice \(x\).
But \(E(x)=1+a^2\), not one.

There is no other homogeneous \(E\)-slice. Evaluating \(Ev=1\)
at the Euler origin shows that a homogeneous such \(v\) would need
a nonzero linear term, so with all weights one it must be linear.
For every complex linear form in \((a,x,z,c)\),
\[
 E(\alpha x+\beta z+\gamma a+\epsilon c)
       =\alpha(1+a^2)+\beta a.
\tag{24}
\]
Equality to one would force both \(\alpha=1\) and \(\alpha=0\).
Thus even the commuting finite-action conclusion does not supply an
Euler-homogeneous slice for the original sliced derivation.

An actual polynomial \(v\) satisfying both \(Ev=1\) and
\(Dv=\lambda v\) would be sufficient additional recognition input,
by the finite test and explicit completion in582. This control shows
that the extra input cannot be inferred merely from (10) or from a
coordinate slice for one \(E_\lambda\). Its own \(A\) is polynomial,
so it does not disprove recognition by another argument.

## 8. Status and exact remaining question

The established sufficient condition is the finite noncollision
test (7), not a general theorem about all first-shift frames. In the
repeated-pair-sum case, it remains unproved here whether the entire
Lie algebra must be finite dimensional or whether every spectral
component must be locally nilpotent. The actual example (11) only
settles the failures recorded in (15)--(16). The high-shift example584
cannot settle those remaining first-shift questions.

Fresh unsaved algebra reconstructs the full inverse and Euler field
of (11), its exact spectral decomposition, every unordered spectral
bracket, and the entire nine-dimensional bracket-closed span. It also
reconstructs both inverse orders and all first-shift and homogeneous-
slice identities of (21)--(24). The general spectral and LND arguments
are proofs and do not rely on finite sampled controls.

No saved checker was executed, imported or compiled, no canonical file
was edited, and no unfinished source was read. This bounded working
source makes no general coordinate-entry, plateau-descent or
cancellation claim.
