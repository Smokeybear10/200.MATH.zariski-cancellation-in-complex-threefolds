# Exact finite-closure dichotomy for two polynomial shears

Work over a field of characteristic zero. The result below is an elementary
fixed-frame obstruction, not a cancellation theorem or a novelty claim.

## The actual coordinate derivation

For positive integers m,n put

\[
u=x+y^m,\qquad s=y+u^n.
\]

These are polynomial coordinates, with inverse
\(y=s-u^n\), \(x=u-(s-u^n)^m\). The derivation that is
\(\partial_u\) in the frame (u,s) is therefore

\[
\delta=\bigl(1+mn y^{m-1}(x+y^m)^{n-1}\bigr)\partial_x
       -n(x+y^m)^{n-1}\partial_y.
\]

In particular, it is locally nilpotent, has the actual slice u, and has
kernel k[s]. No kernel-recognition assertion is needed for this example.
Let g(m,n) be the Lie algebra over k generated by this delta and the two
partials in the original frame (x,y).

**Proposition.** Its dimension and type are exactly as follows:

| Parameters | Lie algebra in the original frame |
| --- | --- |
| m=1 | Nilpotent, dimension n+1, nilpotency class n |
| n=1 | Nilpotent, dimension m+1, nilpotency class m |
| m=n=2 | Dimension five, isomorphic to sl2 semidirect its standard two-dimensional module |
| m,n at least two and m+n greater than four | Infinite-dimensional |

The first two rows agree when m=n=1. Class one means abelian.

## Extracting the basic fields

In the intermediate polynomial coordinates (u,v)=(x+y^m,y), write
P=partial_u and Q=partial_v. The three generators become

\[
P,\qquad Q+mv^{m-1}P,\qquad P-nu^{n-1}Q.
\]

If n is at least two, repeated bracketing with P gives
\((\operatorname{ad}P)^{n-1}\delta=-n!Q\). If n=1, Q=P-delta
instead. Thus Q always belongs to g(m,n), as do
\(E=u^{n-1}Q\) and \(F=v^{m-1}P\). Conversely P,Q,E,F span a
generating set because the displayed original generators are their constant
linear combinations. All statements concern Lie algebras over k, not
modules over the polynomial ring.

## Finite cases

For m=1, the fields P and \(u^jQ\), for 0 at most j at most n-1,
are a linearly independent bracket-closed generating list. The only
nonzero brackets of this list are
\([P,u^jQ]=j u^{j-1}Q\). The longest nonzero iterated bracket of
P with the highest field has n-1 copies of P and n total entries.
Every bracket with n+1 entries vanishes. This proves the dimension and
the nilpotency class. Exchanging the roles of the variables proves the
n=1 case.

For m=n=2 use P,Q,E=uQ,F=vP and H=uP-vQ. Direct calculation gives

\[
[E,F]=H,\quad [H,E]=2E,\quad [H,F]=-2F,
\]

\[
[P,E]=Q,\quad [Q,F]=P,\quad [H,P]=-P,\quad [H,Q]=Q.
\]

All other unordered pairs either vanish or are determined by these
identities. Thus E,F,H form sl2, P,Q form its standard abelian module,
and all five fields belong to the derived algebra. In particular this
finite-dimensional example is perfect, not solvable.

## A Lie word of arbitrarily high degree

Assume m,n are at least two. For a polynomial f(u,v) define

\[
V_f=f_u Q-f_v P,\qquad
\{f,g\}=f_u g_v-f_v g_u.
\]

The Leibniz rule gives \([V_f,V_g]=V_{\{f,g\}}\).
Both \(V_{u^n}=nE\) and \(V_{v^m}=-mF\) belong to g(m,n).
Starting with \(h_0=u^n\), define recursively

\[
h_{r+1}=\{u^n,\{v^m,h_r\}\}.
\]

For any nonnegative a,b with a positive, direct differentiation gives

\[
\{u^n,\{v^m,u^av^b\}\}
 =-nm a(b+m-1)u^{a+n-2}v^{b+m-2}.
\]

Consequently for every r at least zero,

\[
h_r=c_r u^{n+r(n-2)}v^{r(m-2)},\qquad c_0=1,
\]

\[
c_{r+1}=-nm\bigl(n+r(n-2)\bigr)
               \bigl(r(m-2)+m-1\bigr)c_r.
\]

All factors in this coefficient recurrence are nonzero in characteristic
zero. The vector field V(h_r) has total coefficient degree
\(n-1+r(m+n-4)\). If m+n is greater than four, these degrees strictly
increase, so the fields are linearly independent. This proves infinite
dimension for the entire parameter range, not just the values or bracket
depths used in the companion tests. At (2,2) the degrees do not increase,
which agrees with the five-dimensional calculation.

## Four-dimensional and coordinate-change consequences

Adjoin independent passive variables z1,z2 and let delta kill them.
The closure with all four original coordinate partials is the direct sum
of g(m,n) and the two-dimensional central space of passive translations.
Nevertheless delta has the explicit polynomial kernel k[s,z1,z2].
For example, (m,n)=(2,3) is a sliced coordinate LND on affine four-space
whose closure with the prescribed four partials is infinite-dimensional.

An invertible affine-linear change of the prescribed frame preserves the
vector space of constant translation fields. Hence it preserves the
generated Lie algebra and cannot repair this infinite-dimensional closure.
The nonlinear frame (u,s,z1,z2), on the other hand, gives an abelian
four-dimensional closure because delta is its u-partial.

This refutes automatic finite closure in a specified frame, including
every affine-linear replacement of that frame. It does not refute the
existence of a useful nonlinear polynomial frame, and it does not give a
counterexample to cancellation. The universal existence question from
[the finite-closure criterion](levi-bridge-pass29.md) remains unresolved.

## Relation to published work

This dichotomy is a special case of existing work, not a plausibly new
finite-dimensionality theorem. Arzhantsev, Gaifullin and Lopatkin,
[Theorem 3.1 and its full proof](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.05627),
give a directed-cycle criterion for homogeneous LND generators over any
characteristic-zero field, crediting earlier results of Arzhantsev,
Liendo and Stasyuk and of Arzhantsev and Zaidenberg. For the extracted
generators P,Q,E,F here, only E and F can lie on a directed cycle.
They do so precisely when m,n are at least two, and both then have
weight zero precisely when m=n=2. Their criterion thus gives exactly
the finite/infinite boundary above. The explicit bases and all-depth
recurrence make this application independently inspectable. The useful
research conclusion is the prescribed-frame countercontrol, not a
priority claim for the underlying Lie-algebra classification.
