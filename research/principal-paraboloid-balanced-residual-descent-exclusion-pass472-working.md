# Balanced residual descent excludes every higher balanced profile

Working scoped exclusion: retain every actual hypothesis of264 and
assume \(k=s+f\), \(f_s=0\), \(n_{ss}f'''\ne0\). There is no
balanced profile
\[
\deg_v\sigma=2c,\qquad \deg_v y=\deg_v\theta=3c,\qquad c>1.
\tag{1}
\]
The proof uses the complete cubic reduction471 and eliminates \(q\)
between its genuine fourth level and tangent minor. Two exact
residual subtractions then force the entire intrinsic polynomial
line to be generated by a degree-\(c\) polynomial, contradicting
\(c>1\).

Together with the separate parity exclusion470 for \(c=1\), this
removes the balanced branch under these retained actual hypotheses.
It is not an arbitrary chart-entry result, a recognition theorem,
or a solution of general cancellation.

## 2. Three more exact coefficient gates from the full fourth level

Substitute \(R=\rho P+S\) in \(\Psi\). Since
\(3t_3+2e\rho/\eta=0\), its possible \(P^2S\) term cancels
identically. Its entire expansion is
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Psi={}&\{(2\eta t_2+j_1+d\rho)P+B_0+dS\}q\\
&+(F_3+3\rho t_3+e\rho^2/\eta)P^3\\
&+(eP/\eta-\ell_*)S^2
 +\{2(t_2-\ell_*\rho)P+t_1\}S\\
&+C_2P^2+C_1P+F_0 ,
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]
where
\[
B_0=j_0+\eta t_1,\qquad
C_2=F_2+2\rho t_2-\ell_*\rho^2,\qquad
C_1=F_1+\rho t_1.
\tag{10}
\]
Every coefficient of (9) is retained.

The \(P^3\) term is the only possible term of intrinsic degree
\(6c\) in (9). Its coefficient therefore vanishes. After that
vanishing, the \(Pq\) term is the only possible term of degree
\(5c\). The other terms have degree at most \(4c\).
Thus
\[
\boxed{
F_3=\frac{9\eta t_3^2}{4e},\qquad
j_1=-2\eta t_2-d\rho.}
\tag{11}
\]
Neither \(B_0,C_1,F_0\) nor any actual level is presumed zero.

For precision about the next coefficient, let \(P_*,Q_*,S_*\)
be the nonzero leading coefficients of \(P,q,S\).
The full third level and the exact leading minor from471 give
\[
\gamma Q_*^2+t_3P_*^3=0,\qquad
b_1P_*S_*Q_*+2dt_3P_*^3=0,\qquad
b_1=-4e\gamma/\eta\ne0.

\[
S_*Q_*=\frac{\eta d t_3}{2e\gamma}P_*^2,\qquad
S_*^2=-\frac{\eta^2d^2t_3}{4e^2\gamma}P_* .
\tag{13}
\]
The second equality follows by squaring the first and using the
first identity in (12); all cancelled coefficient factors are
nonzero.

The degree-\(4c\) part of (9), after (11), is
\(dS_*Q_*+(e/\eta)P_*S_*^2+C_2P_*^2=0\).

\boxed{C_2=-\frac{\eta d^2t_3}{4e\gamma}\ne0.}

## 4. The entire high-weight support and first residual

For this section assign formal weights \(2\) to \(P\) and \(1\)
to \(S\). They are bookkeeping weights for polynomials over \(L\).
They become intrinsic degree bounds after multiplication by \(c\);
no independence of the evaluated \(P,S\) is assumed.

Put
\[
C_*=\frac{4e^2\gamma}{\eta^2}\ne0,\qquad
\delta=\frac{d^2t_3}{C_*}
       =\frac{\eta^2d^2t_3}{4e^2\gamma}\ne0,
\tag{24}
\]
\[
k_{30}=2B_0dt_3-a_1C_2,\qquad
k_{22}=6d\eta t_3-2b_1k-\frac e\eta a_1.
\tag{25}
\]
Direct multiplication of the complete (16),(18)--(21) gives
\[
\boxed{
K=C_*P^2S(S^2+\delta P)
                       +k_{30}P^3+k_{22}P^2S^2+K_{\le5}(P,S),}
\tag{26}
\]
where every monomial of \(K_{\le5}\) has weight at most five.
For an explicit coefficient check, all terms of weight at least
six are precisely
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{monomial}&\text{coefficient}\\ \hline
P^3S&2d^2t_3-b_1C_2=d^2t_3=C_*\delta\\
P^2S^3&-b_1e/\eta=C_*\\
P^3&2B_0dt_3-a_1C_2=k_{30}\\
P^2S^2&6d\eta t_3-2b_1k-(e/\eta)a_1=k_{22}.
\end{array}
\tag{27}
\]
For example all products involving \(Q_2\), other than those already
recorded in (27), have weight at most five. The remaining products
of the displayed \(\mathcal A,\mathcal N\) have the same bound.
The complete possible support of the lower polynomial is contained in
\[
P^2S,\ P^2,\ PS^3,\ PS^2,\ PS,\ P,\ S^3,\ S^2,\ S,\ 1 .
\tag{28}
\]
In (26), \(K_{\le5}\) is defined by subtracting the displayed four
terms from the exact polynomial (22), so every lower coefficient
is specified by (16),(18)--(21), not left as an unspecified error.

The two weight-seven terms must cancel at their intrinsic degree
\(7c\), since (23) has degree \(3c-1\). This agrees with the second
identity in (13) and gives the residual
\[
U=S^2+\delta P,\qquad \deg_vU<2c
\quad\hbox{or }U=0 .
\tag{29}
\]
Now (26) becomes
\[
K=C_*P^2SU+k_{30}P^3+k_{22}P^2S^2+K_{\le5}(P,S).
\tag{30}
\]
If \(\deg_vU>c\), its first term has degree \(5c+\deg_vU>6c\).
The next two terms have degree at most \(6c\), and every term of
the lower polynomial has degree at most \(5c\). The leading term
would therefore be unique and nonzero, contradicting (23).
Thus
\[
\boxed{\deg_vU\le c\quad\hbox{or }U=0.}
\tag{31}
\]
Zero or constant \(U\) is included.

## 5. The second residual must be constant

Define the coefficient, with no nonzero assumption,
\[
\mu=\frac{k_{30}/\delta-k_{22}}{C_*},\qquad V=U-\mu S .
\tag{32}
\]
Substitute \(P=(U-S^2)/\delta\) in (26). The exact identity in
independent formal symbols \(S,U\) is
\[
\boxed{
K\!\left(\frac{U-S^2}{\delta},S\right)
=\frac{C_*}{\delta^2}S^5(U-\mu S)+\mathcal R_5(S,U),}
\tag{33}
\]
with the entire remainder
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal R_5={}&
\frac{C_*}{\delta^2}(SU^3-2S^3U^2)\\
&+\frac{k_{30}}{\delta^3}(U^3-3S^2U^2+3S^4U)\\
&+\frac{k_{22}}{\delta^2}(S^2U^2-2S^4U)\\
&+K_{\le5}\!\left(\frac{U-S^2}{\delta},S\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{34}
\]
Every monomial of \(\mathcal R_5\) has ordinary total degree at
most five. For its last line this follows from the weight bound
in (28): replacing \(P\) by \((U-S^2)/\delta\) gives total degree
at most \(2i+j\) for a term \(P^iS^j\).
No lower coefficient has been dropped.

Since both input degrees in (34) are at most \(c\), its evaluation
has degree at most \(5c\). If \(\deg_vU=c\), the coefficient of
degree \(6c\) in (33) forces
\(\operatorname{lc}_vU=\mu\,\operatorname{lc}_vS\).
If \(\deg_vU<c\), including zero or constant \(U\), the same
comparison forces \(\mu=0\). Otherwise the unique degree-\(6c\)
term would be \(-C_*\mu S^6/\delta^2\).
Therefore
\[
\deg_vV<c\quad\hbox{or }V=0 .
\tag{35}
\]

Substitute \(U=\mu S+V\) in (33). Its complete form is
\[
K\!\left(\frac{\mu S+V-S^2}{\delta},S\right)
=\frac{C_*}{\delta^2}S^5V+\mathcal R_5(S,\mu S+V),
\tag{36}
\]
where the second term still has total degree at most five.
If \(V\) had positive intrinsic degree \(h<c\), the first term
would have degree \(5c+h>5c\), with nonzero leading coefficient.
The entire second term has degree at most \(5c\), so cannot
cancel it. This contradicts (23).
We conclude
\[
\boxed{V=v_*\in L,}
\tag{37}
\]
allowing \(v_*=0\). Neither \(U,V,\mu\), nor any lower coefficient
was divided by in this argument.

## 6. Polynomial return yields the contradiction

Equations (29),(32),(37) give
\[
P=\frac{\mu S+v_*-S^2}{\delta}\in L[S],\qquad
R=\rho P+S\in L[S].
\tag{38}
\]
The full fourth level (15) now gives
\[
q=-\frac{\mathcal N(P,S)}{dS+B_0}\in L(S).
\tag{39}
\]
The denominator is a nonzero polynomial of degree \(c\). This
step is only in the fraction field; it is not declared a unit of
\(O\). But \(q\) already belongs to \(L[v]\), and
\[
L(S)\cap L[v]=L[S]\qquad\hbox{for nonconstant }S\in L[v].
\tag{40}
\]
To justify the intersection, write an element as
\(A_*(S)/B_*(S)\), with coprime \(A_*,B_*\in L[T]\).
Bezout polynomials over \(L[T]\) give \(I_*A_*+J_*B_*=1\).
If the quotient is in \(L[v]\), then \(B_*(S)\) divides \(A_*(S)\),
hence one, in \(L[v]\). It is therefore a polynomial unit.
Since \(S\) is nonconstant, this forces \(B_*\) constant.
This proves (40) over the exact field \(L\), without rationality
or an unproved normality assertion for a projected ring.

It follows that \(q\in L[S]\). Using (4),(38),
\[
O=L[P,S,q]=L[S]=L[v].
\tag{41}
\]
A polynomial expression for \(v\) in \(S\) is nonconstant, so
its degree is \(c\) times a positive integer. As \(v\) has degree
one, this forces \(c=1\), contrary to (1).
Thus no higher balanced profile occurs.
