# The arbitrary slice-frame gap and an exact linear-frame obstruction

The universal reduction to an invariant-coefficient extension is not
proved. This audit separates two algebraic requirements that must not
be merged: finding an invariant coordinate three-ring, and making the
last coefficient invariant while retaining that three-ring. The first
has a useful exact normalizer formulation. The second is a concrete
additive quotient condition and can fail even for a primitive base and
an actual slice.

There is also a fully explicit negative control: a coordinate translation
on four-space, with a quadratic slice and a known polynomial kernel,
preserves no coordinate three-ring generated by affine-linear forms.
Thus linear-frame extraction is not a consequence of the slice, even
in a case where a nonlinear frame is already known.

These are reductions and obstructions to inference rules, not a
cancellation counterexample or a novelty claim. This note does not
assert that nonlinear invariant-extension frames fail to exist.

## 1. Which proposed conclusions already recognize the kernel

Let
\[
B=\mathbb C[x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4],\qquad E\in\operatorname{LND}(B),
\qquad E(s)=1,\qquad A=\ker E.
\]
Then \(B=A[s]\). The kernel is finitely generated and regular;
regularity descends from its polynomial extension. For this fixed pair
\((E,s)\), the following conditions are equivalent:

1. \(A\cong\mathbb C^{[3]}\).
2. The given slice \(s\) is a polynomial coordinate of \(B\).
3. \(A\) contains an ambient polynomial coordinate of \(B\).
4. \(E\) has rank at most three, in the usual LND rank convention.

The first two are equivalent because \(B/(s)=A\), and if
\(A=\mathbb C[a_1,a_2,a_3]\), then
\((a_1,a_2,a_3,s)\) is an ambient coordinate system. The first
implies the third, and the third implies the first by
Bhatwadekar--Gupta--Lokhande Theorem 3.5: a nonzero LND of a polynomial
four-ring fixing an ambient coordinate and having regular kernel has
polynomial kernel over that coordinate.[^1] The equivalence of the
third and fourth is the definition of rank in four variables.

Thus proving that every slice action has rank at most three is already
the global recognition step. It is not a free preliminary reduction to
the solved families. The [earlier stable-coordinate note](stable-coordinate-pass.md)
also records why exchanging \(s\) with an added indeterminate proves
one-stable coordinateness but not unstabilized coordinateness.

An abstract polynomial subalgebra is much weaker than an ambient
coordinate subalgebra. Even for \(A=\mathbb C[a,b,c]\) and
\(B=A[s]\), the invariant subalgebra
\[
R_0=\mathbb C[a^2,b,c]
\tag{1}
\]
is a smooth polynomial three-ring, and \(A\) is finite over it,
but \(B\ne R_0[w]\) for every \(w\in B\). If that equality
held, polynomial degree in \(w\) would make \(R_0\) factorially
closed in \(B\). The inclusion \(a^2\in R_0\) would force
\(a\in R_0\), which is false. Choosing three algebraically
independent invariants does not construct the required complement.

## 2. General stable-base lemma

**Lemma.** Let \(R\) be any characteristic-zero domain, let
\(B=R[w]\), and let \(D_1\in\operatorname{LND}(B)\) satisfy
\(D_1(R)\subseteq R\). Then
\[
D_1=D+b\partial_w,\qquad
D=D_1|_R\in\operatorname{LND}(R),\quad b\in R.
\tag{2}
\]
In particular \([D_1,\partial_w]=0\). No slice or regularity
hypothesis is needed.

**Proof.** First suppose that \(R\) contains \(\mathbb Q\).
Introduce an indeterminate \(u\). The polynomial
automorphism \(\exp(uD_1)\) of \(R[u,w]\) restricts to
\(\exp(uD)\) on \(R[u]\). Compose it with the coefficientwise
automorphism \(\exp(-uD)\), fixing \(w\). The composite is
an \(R[u]\)-automorphism of a one-variable polynomial ring.
Over a domain every such automorphism has the form
\(w\mapsto a(u)w+c(u)\), with \(a(u)\in R[u]^*=R^*\).
Evaluation at \(u=0\) makes \(a(u)=1\). Undoing the coefficient
automorphism shows
\(\exp(uD_1)(w)-w\in R[u]\); its coefficient of \(u\) is
\(D_1(w)\in R\), as claimed. For a general characteristic-zero
domain, apply the argument after localizing at the nonzero integers.
The absence of positive powers of \(w\) then descends to the
original element \(D_1(w)\in R[w]\), proving the same conclusion.

Conversely every derivation in (2) is locally nilpotent: its iterates
on \(R\) are those of \(D\), and
\(D_1^{j+1}(w)=D^j(b)\). This converse and commutation are
the general-extension statement in Freudenburg Lemma 2.2; it allows
arbitrary \(b\in R\), not only invariant \(b\).[^2]
For polynomial algebras, the forward statement is already the
restriction/extension principle attributed to Freudenburg and restated
as [Dasgupta--Gaifullin Proposition 2.16](https://www.mathnet.ru/php/archive.phtml?jrnid=sm&option_lang=eng&paperid=10094&wshow=paper).
The argument above reconstructs the principle over the stated general
domain; it is not a priority claim for the polynomial-ring assertion.

For \(B=\mathbb C^{[4]}\), an **ambient coordinate three-ring**
means an embedded \(R\cong\mathbb C^{[3]}\) with an actual
polynomial complement \(B=R[w]\). The lemma gives the equivalence
\[
\begin{split}
&\text{there is an }E\text{-stable ambient coordinate three-ring}\
&\qquad\Longleftrightarrow
\text{there is a coordinate partial }T\text{ with }[E,T]=0.
\end{split}
\tag{3}
\]
For the reverse implication take \(R=\ker T\); commutation
makes it \(E\)-stable. A coordinate partial here means the
partial derivative in a genuine polynomial coordinate frame, not
merely an arbitrary LND possessing a slice.

## 3. Normalizing the slice foliation does not weaken this requirement

There is a related exact intrinsic statement. Write \(B=A[s]\)
and \(E=\partial_s\). For any \(T\in\operatorname{LND}(B)\),
the following are equivalent:
\[
T(A)\subseteq A,
\qquad [T,E]\in B E,
\qquad [T,E]=0.
\tag{4}
\]
The first condition and the lemma imply
\(T=d+q\partial_s\), with \(d\in\operatorname{LND}(A)\)
and \(q\in A\), giving the third condition. The third implies
the other two. For the remaining direction, apply
\([T,E]\in BE\) to \(a\in A\): it gives \(E(Ta)=0\),
so \(T\) preserves \(A\).

In particular, replacing commutation by preservation of the slice
foliation, while retaining local nilpotence, supplies no extra class
of candidate coordinate partials. The ordinary centralizer formula is
\[
Z_{\operatorname{Der}(B)}(E)
=\{d+q\partial_s:d\in\operatorname{Der}(A),\ q\in A\}.
\tag{5}
\]
The [intrinsic-action argument](intrinsic-actions-pass.md) already
proves that some nonzero \(d\in\operatorname{LND}(A)\) exists.
It does not prove that any resulting element of (5) is a coordinate
partial. For example adding \(\partial_s\) to the extension of
\(d\) produces a commuting LND with a slice, but its kernel is
isomorphic to \(A\) by the finite variable-time exponential.
Calling it a coordinate partial would assume the unknown conclusion.

## 4. The second requirement in a fixed coordinate three-ring

Suppose the first requirement has actually been met:
\[
B=R[w],\qquad E=D+b\partial_w,
\quad R\cong\mathbb C^{[3]},\quad b\in R.
\tag{6}
\]
Put \(C=\ker D\). An \(R\)-automorphism of \(R[w]\) is
\(w\mapsto c w+H\), with \(c\in\mathbb C^*\), \(H\in R\).
In that new coordinate the last coefficient is
\(cb+D(H)\). Thus it can be made invariant exactly when
\[
b\in C+D(R).
\tag{7}
\]
Equivalently the class of \(b\) vanishes in the additive quotient
\(R/(C+D(R))\). This is a quotient of vector spaces, not a
claim that \(D(R)\) is an ideal. Allowing an automorphism of
\(R\) itself does not change the vanishing of this class: it
transports both \(C\) and \(D(R)\) isomorphically.

In this fixed frame, (7) is also equivalent to the existence of a
\(\partial_w\)-slice \(w'=w+H\) satisfying \(E^2(w')=0\).
The project theorem for \(A_0D_0+B_0\partial_{w'}\), with both
coefficients invariant under \(D_0\), falls inside (7), since
one can absorb the invariant replica coefficient into \(D\).

Condition (7) is not forced by an actual slice, even for primitive
\(D\). The separate pass-21 primitive nongauge calculation uses
\[
D=x^2\partial_y+y^m\partial_z,\quad m\ge2,
\qquad b=1+xy.
\tag{8}
\]
For \(E=D+b\partial_w\), a polynomial slice is
\[
s=w-xyw-\frac{x^2y^2w}{2}+\frac{x^3w^2}{2}
  +\frac{y^3}{2}+\frac{xy^4}{8}.
\]
The equality \(E(s)=1\) only uses \(D(y)=x^2\), so it
holds for every \(m\). It was independently checked in this
audit for \(m=2,3,4,7\). The all-parameter obstruction is immediate
once the exact kernel is recorded:
\[
C=\mathbb C[x,(m+1)x^2z-y^{m+1}],\quad
D(R)\subseteq(x^2,y^m).
\]
Modulo this ideal, \(C\) maps into \(\mathbb C[x]/(x^2)\),
but the term \(xy\) in \(b\) survives. Hence (7) fails.
Primitivity is \(\gcd(x^2,y^m)=1\). This shows that even a
successful first frame does not automatically produce an invariant
last coefficient while preserving that frame. It does not exclude
changing the embedded three-ring nonlinearly.

The [primitive rank-three construction](primitive-noninvariant-bridge-pass21.md)
strengthens this control: its added coefficient also fails (7), every
slice has degree at least two in the original added variable, and its
kernel nevertheless has an explicit polynomial coordinate presentation.
The [independent audit](primitive-bridge-audit-pass21.md) checks the
second-image obstruction and the nonlinear physical inverse. Thus the
failure is not removed by restricting the base to primitive rank three.

## 5. A coordinate translation with no stable affine-linear three-ring

Begin with polynomial coordinates \((a,b,c,s)\), and perform four
elementary changes:
\[
u=a+s^2,\qquad v=b+u^2,\qquad w=c+v^2,\qquad z=s+w^2.
\tag{9}
\]
The inverse is explicit:
\[
s=z-w^2,\quad a=u-s^2,\quad b=v-u^2,\quad c=w-v^2.
\tag{10}
\]
Regard \(E=\partial_s\) in the frame \((u,v,w,z)\). It is
\[
E=2s\partial_u+4us\partial_v+8uvs\partial_w
 +(1+16uvws)\partial_z,\qquad s=z-w^2.
\tag{11}
\]
Its slice is quadratic, and its kernel is exactly
\(\mathbb C[a,b,c]\), with the generators (10).

Nevertheless no nonzero constant vector field commutes with it.
Let
\(V=\lambda_u\partial_u+\lambda_v\partial_v+
\lambda_w\partial_w+\lambda_z\partial_z\), with constant
coefficients. The \(u\)-component of \([V,E]=0\) gives
\[
2\lambda_z-4w\lambda_w=0,
\]
so \(\lambda_w=\lambda_z=0\). The \(v\)-component then
gives \(4s\lambda_u=0\), hence \(\lambda_u=0\).
The \(w\)-component gives \(8us\lambda_v=0\), hence
\(\lambda_v=0\).

If an algebra generated by three independent affine-linear forms
in \((u,v,w,z)\) were \(E\)-stable, its complementary
constant coordinate partial would commute with \(E\), by (2).
The preceding computation excludes it. Therefore
\[
\text{no affine-linear change of frame supplies an }E\text{-stable
coordinate three-ring for (11)}.
\tag{12}
\]
This is stronger than failure of one chosen coordinate projection.
It rules out every linear frame for this example. It does not rule
out nonlinear frames: (10) provides one explicitly. The symbolic
control verified \(E(s)=1\), the three invariant identities,
the inverse Jacobian determinant \(1\), and rank four of the
constant-vector centralizer equations. These supplement the displayed
all-coefficient proof, rather than substituting for it.

## 6. Exact residual problem

The invariant-extension route still requires a proof that every slice
LND admits some appropriate **nonlinear** coordinate three-ring,
followed by an invariant-coefficient frame or a more general treatment
of the last coefficient. None of the following supplies that proof:

- abstract polynomial invariant subalgebras, as (1) shows;
- linear-frame minimization, as (9)--(12) show;
- an arbitrary intrinsic additive action, which need not be coordinate;
- preservation of the slice foliation, which is exactly (4); or
- one commuting coordinate partial, if the next step silently retains
  its three-ring and assumes (7).

The [filtration controls](filtration-obstruction-pass.md) already show
why lower coefficient extraction and slice projection need not preserve
local nilpotence. The [height bounds](coordinate-frame-minimization-pass.md)
give genuine sufficient conditions, but not a universal height-decreasing
operation. The tangent/projective-module investigation is separate:
this note has not replaced a polynomial complement by a module splitting.

A proof that every slice LND fixes an ambient coordinate would settle
the recognition question by Section 1. A proof of (3) for every slice
would be a weaker, genuinely useful structural reduction, but it has not
been established here. The fixed-three-ring counterexample does not
disprove that existential nonlinear reduction.
