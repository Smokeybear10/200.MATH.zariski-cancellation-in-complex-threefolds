# All specializations and the affine birational projected-line profile

Working result: retain the complete actual nonzero-coupling principal
chart264. Every specialization of the actual X coordinate over the
exact coefficient field gives a polynomial embedding of the generic
line. Consequently the intrinsic profile
\[
(\deg_v y,\deg_v\sigma,\deg_v\theta)=(2,1,\leq1)
\tag{1}
\]
is impossible. If the projected plane curve is a line and its projection
has function-field degree one, all three functions y,sigma,theta are
affine in v. Neither conclusion assumes that the cubic surface level
is reducible.

The last section records an exact two-point collision obstruction for
general degrees. It does not assert that the prohibited two-point
configuration must exist, or recognize the remaining affine profile.

## 1. Complete actual assumptions and notation

Retain the actual hypotheses of the fully read
[264](principal-one-affine-paraboloid-chart-pass264-working.md).
In particular,
\[
\begin{gathered}
\mathcal B=\mathbb C[X,Y,Z,W]=A[u],\qquad E=\partial_u,\qquad
a=EX\ne0,\quad Ea=0,\\
\deg_E(X,Y,Z,W)=(1,2,2,2),\qquad
I_{\mathrm{initial}}=(X^2Z-Y^2),\\
L=\operatorname{Frac}\ker_A N,\qquad
[L:\mathbb C(t,s)]<\infty,\qquad
Na=Nt=Ns=0,\qquad Nv=1,\\
K=\operatorname{Frac}A=L(v),\qquad a\in L^*,\qquad
O=L[v]=L[y,\sigma,\theta].
\end{gathered}
\tag{2}
\]
The selected N is the actual nonzero shift-four leading derivation of
the literal Z-partial. The coefficient partials in t,s commute and fix
v. The field L and all intercept functions are killed by E. The last
equality in (2) is the three-generator return from the nonzero-coupling
case, not a presumption that sigma and theta alone generate O.

Write m=k_s and assume m is nonzero. The full actual expressions,
their first two potentials, and their calibration are
\[
\begin{aligned}
Y&=y+\sigma X+tX^2,\\
Z&=z+(2t\sigma+k)X+t^2X^2,\\
W&=w+\theta X+sX^2,\\
z&=2ty+\sigma^2+k_t\sigma+m\theta+n,\\
mw&=\ell-(2\sigma+k_t)y-F(\sigma,\theta),\\
F(P,V)&=\frac{k_{tt}}2P^2+m_tPV+\frac{m_s}2V^2+n_tP+n_sV,\\
\det\frac{\partial(Y,Z,W)}{\partial(t,s,v)}&=-a^4\in L^* .
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]
Here k,n,ell belong to L. The determinant differentiates at fixed
actual X. The conclusions below use the original ring and its
derivation, not merely an abstract family satisfying some displayed
potential equations.

The retained monic surface level has the shape
\[
H_0(U,P,V)=U^2+A_1(P,V)U+A_0(P,V),\qquad
H_0(y,\sigma,\theta)=0,
\tag{4}
\]
where
\[
\deg A_1\le2,\quad \deg A_0\le3,\qquad
A_1=F_P-\frac{(2P+k_t)F_V}{m}.
\tag{5}
\]
These are formal degrees over L. Reducibility of H0 is not assumed
in any section of this note.

## 2. The exact polynomial ring survives every X-specialization

All coefficients in (3) lie in O, so the subring of the original
fraction field generated by L and the actual polynomial ring satisfies
\[
L[\mathcal B]\subseteq L[X,v].
\tag{6}
\]
The reverse inclusion follows from actual derivatives. Since E
preserves the original ring and kills its adjoined field L, the
ring L[B] is E-stable. Since a is invertible in L, literal
differentiation of the first and third rows of (3) gives
\[
\sigma=\frac{EY}{a}-2tX\in L[\mathcal B],\qquad
\theta=\frac{EW}{a}-2sX\in L[\mathcal B],\qquad
y=Y-\sigma X-tX^2\in L[\mathcal B].
\tag{7}
\]
The whole return O=L[y,sigma,theta] now puts v in L[B]. Thus
\[
\boxed{L[X,Y,Z,W]=L[\mathcal B]=L[X,v].}
\tag{8}
\]
No derivative is claimed to belong to the algebra generated by an
arbitrary formal map: EY and EW lie in the original polynomial ring
because E is its actual derivation. This is the essential input.

The variables X,v are algebraically independent over L. Indeed
K=L(v), whereas X is transcendental over K since X=au+c_X with
a nonzero and c_X in A. Consequently for any field extension L'/L
and any x0 in L', base extension of (8) and then evaluation at X=x0
give
\[
L'[Y(x_0,v),Z(x_0,v),W(x_0,v)]=L'[v].
\tag{9}
\]
The same three polynomial expressions and the same coefficients
are used on both sides. In particular this is a closed immersion of
the line into affine three-space over L'. It is not specialization
of t or s, an original complex coordinate change, or descent of an
L-coordinate to the original ring. Even when x0 is a coefficient
function, it is held fixed in this evaluation and is not differentiated.

## 3. Exclude a quadratic first function and affine projected functions

Suppose (1) holds. Put
\[
y=\alpha v^2+\alpha_1v+\alpha_0,\qquad
\sigma=\beta v+\beta_0,\qquad
\theta=\gamma v+\gamma_0,\qquad
\alpha\beta\ne0,
\tag{10}
\]
with all coefficients in L. The coefficient gamma may vanish.
Because F is quadratic in its two arguments, F(sigma,theta) has
v-degree at most two. Equations (3) therefore give
\[
\begin{aligned}
[v^2]Y&=\alpha,& [v]Y&=\alpha_1+\beta X,\\
[v^2]Z&=2t\alpha+\beta^2,&
[v]Z&=z_1+2t\beta X,\\
[v^3]W&=-\frac{2\alpha\beta}{m}\ne0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]
Here z1 is a coefficient in L. No X-dependent term changes the
cubic coefficient of W. The quadratic coefficient of Z is allowed
to vanish.

Apply the fully read
[323, Section1 generation lemma](principal-paraboloid-all-conic-polynomial-specialization-exclusion-pass323-working.md)
with its ordered generators (F,G,H) equal to (Z,Y,W), its variables
(x,z) equal to (X,v), and its coefficient field equal to L. In that
notation its three required nonvanishing quantities are
\[
g_2=\alpha,\qquad h_3=-2\alpha\beta/m,\qquad
r=\alpha(2t\beta)-(2t\alpha+\beta^2)\beta=-\beta^3.
\tag{12}
\]
They are all nonzero, and its full polynomial generation hypothesis
is precisely (8). This is a contradiction.

Equivalently choose the unique X0 in L that kills the linear
v-coefficient of
\(\alpha Z-(2t\alpha+\beta^2)Y\).
That combination is then constant in v. At X0, Y remains exactly
quadratic and W exactly cubic. Equality (9) says these two
polynomials generate L[v], which is impossible.

For clarity the last obstruction is elementary over L itself.
After translating v, rescaling the quadratic, and subtracting its
multiples and a constant from the cubic, the generated algebra is
\[
L[\xi^2]\oplus \xi(A\xi^2+B)L[\xi^2],\qquad A\ne0.
\tag{13}
\]
Its odd part cannot contain xi because A xi^2+B is nonconstant.
There is no need for a classification theorem for plane embeddings
or an algebraically closed coefficient field.

## 4. Consequence for a birational projected line

Let C=L[sigma,theta] and assume the full projected plane equation
has degree one and
\[
[L(v):\operatorname{Frac}C]=1.
\tag{14}
\]
A linear relation expresses one projected function as an affine
function of the other, or makes one constant. Since at least one
is nonconstant, C=L[q] for a nonconstant polynomial q in v.
For such a polynomial the degree of L(v)/L(q) is deg_v q:
division gives a spanning basis \(1,v,\ldots,v^{\deg q-1}\),
and their independence follows by comparing the distinct leading
degrees of polynomial multiples of these basis elements.
Thus (14) forces deg_v q=1. In particular
\[
C=O,\qquad \deg_v\sigma,\deg_v\theta\le1 .
\tag{15}
\]
This polynomial return follows from the linear plane equation and
the specified degree-one projection. It is not inferred from
birationality of an arbitrary plane curve.

The monic relation (4), together with (5),(15), bounds deg_v y
by two. If it were r>2, the term y^2 would have degree 2r,
strictly exceeding both r+2 and 3, so could not cancel.
If sigma is nonconstant, Section3 excludes degree two.
If sigma is constant, formula (5) shows that A1(sigma,theta)
has degree at most one: both F_P and F_V are affine in theta
and 2sigma+k_t is a coefficient. Then a quadratic y would give
degree four in y^2 while all other terms of (4) have degree at
most three. That too is impossible. Hence
\[
\boxed{\deg_v y,\deg_v\sigma,\deg_v\theta\le1.}
\tag{16}
\]
No factorization of H0 was used. Thus this also constrains the
irreducible-surface alternative of300.

The degree-two projection is different: a plane line there can
have sigma,theta quadratic in the original line parameter, so
neither (15) nor Section3 applies. The retained degree-two work
[303](principal-paraboloid-double-cover-line-obstruction-pass303-working.md)
and [307](principal-paraboloid-constant-sigma-double-cover-recognition-pass307-working.md)
is separate and is not invoked in this proof. This note supplies
no new proof of that branch and does not identify its parameter
with the birational parameter used here.

## 5. An exact necessary two-point condition in arbitrary degrees

Work over an algebraic closure of L, holding all coefficient
functions fixed. Put
\[
S(v)=\sigma(v)^2+k_t\sigma(v)+m\theta(v)
     =z(v)-2t\,y(v)-n.
\tag{17}
\]
For two distinct parameter values v1,v2, write
Delta f=f(v1)-f(v2), and use bars for the arithmetic means.
Suppose S(v1)=S(v2) and Delta sigma is nonzero. Then
\[
\Delta\theta
 =-\frac{\sigma(v_1)+\sigma(v_2)+k_t}{m}\Delta\sigma,\qquad
X_0=-\frac{\Delta y}{\Delta\sigma}.
\tag{18}
\]
At this X0 the two values of Y agree. The identity
Z-2tY=S+n+kX-t^2X^2 shows that the two values of Z also agree.
Taking the difference of the second potential in (3) gives the
exact remaining difference
\[
m\bigl(\Delta w+X_0\Delta\theta\bigr)
 =-\bigl((y(v_1)+y(v_2))\Delta\sigma+\Delta F\bigr).
\tag{19}
\]
Because F is quadratic, its finite difference polarizes exactly:
Delta F=F_P(bar sigma,bar theta)Delta sigma
       +F_V(bar sigma,bar theta)Delta theta.
Consequently W also agrees if and only if
\[
\begin{aligned}
0=\mathcal C:={}&y(v_1)+y(v_2)
 +\frac{k_{tt}}2\bigl(\sigma(v_1)+\sigma(v_2)\bigr)
 +\frac{m_t}2\bigl(\theta(v_1)+\theta(v_2)\bigr)+n_t\\
&-\frac{
 \bigl(m_t(\sigma(v_1)+\sigma(v_2))
       +m_s(\theta(v_1)+\theta(v_2))+2n_s\bigr)
 \bigl(\sigma(v_1)+\sigma(v_2)+k_t\bigr)}
 {2m}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{20}
\]
This is the formal H0_U evaluated at
(bar y,bar sigma,bar theta), not a coefficient derivative or
a derivative of the chosen X0.

Equality (9) prohibits equality of all three coordinates at
distinct parameter values. Thus every pair satisfying the
hypotheses of (18) must satisfy C nonzero.
If Delta sigma=0, then equality of S forces Delta theta=0
because m is nonzero. Equality of Y would then force Delta y=0,
contradicting O=L[y,sigma,theta] at distinct points. That case
therefore cannot produce a collision either.

These are necessary no-collision conditions. They do not assert
that the equations S(v1)=S(v2) and C=0 have an off-diagonal
solution for every remaining degree profile. In particular no
new universal exclusion follows solely from writing (20).
