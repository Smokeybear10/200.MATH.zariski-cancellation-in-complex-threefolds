# Intrinsic invariance of the whole-fiber good velocities

At an actual second-image obstruction point, the residues of the two
good coordinate velocities are killed by the intrinsic derivation on
the entire scheme-theoretic fiber. This does not require that the fiber
be reduced or that either velocity be a global unit.

At a reduced obstruction with rank-zero first jet, adjoining these
velocities to the degree-two image gives an algebra of complex
dimension at most six. This is a bound on a particular subalgebra,
not on the full one-dimensional fiber ring.

## 1. Actual inputs

Use the actual setup of
[the fiber-open note](fiber-invariant-opens-pass50.md), with
\(R=C_{\mathfrak m}\), \(A_0=A_R\), and
\(B_0=A_0[s_*]\). The original sliced derivation satisfies
\(Es_*=1\); the primitive intrinsic LND \(D\) kills \(R\).
Let \(\overline A=A_0/\mathfrak mA_0\), and let
\(\overline D\) be the induced derivation. This is a well-defined
LND, since the ideal is generated by invariants.

Assume the point is an actual second-image basepoint. The
[actual frame theorem](frame-filtration-generation-pass49.md)
puts it in \(V(f,g,h_1,h_2)\). Put \(P_i=Ex_i\) for the
two good coordinates and \(p_i=\overline{P_i}\). They belong
to \(\overline A\), through the actual decomposition
\(\overline B=\overline A[s_*]\).

The fiber-open proof supplies
\[
(p_1,p_2)\overline A=\overline A,
\quad I_1,I_{12},I_2\in F_2(A_0),
\]
\[
\overline I_1=p_1^2,
\quad\overline I_{12}=p_1p_2,
\quad\overline I_2=p_2^2.
\tag{1}
\]
The earlier [flat-conic gate](flat-conic-topology-gate-pass47.md)
gives \(D(F_2)\subset\mathfrak mF_2\). Thus \(\overline D\)
kills all three elements on the second line of (1).

## 2. A unimodular pair removes the zero-divisor problem

On the localization \(\overline A[p_1^{-1}]\), differentiation
of \(p_1^2\) gives
\[
0=2p_1\overline Dp_1,
\quad\text{hence}\quad\overline Dp_1=0.
\]
Differentiating \(p_1p_2\) then gives \(\overline Dp_2=0\).
The same argument works on \(\overline A[p_2^{-1}]\), with
the roles swapped. These principal opens cover by (1), so
\[
\boxed{\overline Dp_1=\overline Dp_2=0
\quad\text{in the full }\overline A.}
\tag{2}
\]
The step from the two localizations to the ring can also be checked
elementwise: each value is annihilated by powers of both \(p_i\),
and those powers generate the unit ideal. No cancellation by a
possible zero divisor is used before it is made a unit.

For an arbitrary fixed supplied slice, the actual invariants
\(a_i=P_i-h_i s_*\in A_0\) reduce to \(p_i\). Consequently
\(Da_i\in\mathfrak mA_0\). This does not bound the intrinsic
degree of \(a_i\), or make it a member of \(F_2\).

## 3. Rank-zero finite-image strengthening

Suppose additionally \(JR=\mathfrak mR\) and the coefficient
first-jet matrix has rank zero. The
[initial-conic theorem](initial-conic-fiber-pass48.md) gives
\[
S=\mathbb C[\bar z,\bar w]\subset\overline A,
\qquad 1\le\dim_{\mathbb C}S\le3.
\tag{3}
\]
This actual image algebra retains its nilpotents. Since
\(F_2=R+Rz+Rw\), the three residues in (1) lie in \(S\).
Put \(T=S[p_1,p_2]\subset\overline A\).

First, \((\overline I_1,\overline I_2)S=S\). To prove this
without assuming faithful flatness of \(S\subset\overline A\),
decompose the finite-dimensional ring \(S\) into its Artinian
local factors. If the two elements were both nonunits on one
factor, they would be nilpotent there. Its nonzero idempotent
stays nonzero in the containing ring \(\overline A\). On that
nonzero ring factor both elements would still be nilpotent and
could not generate the unit ideal, contradicting (1).

The relations \(p_1^2, p_1p_2,p_2^2\in S\) already make
\(T\) a finite \(S\)-module spanned by \(1,p_1,p_2\).
On a local factor where \(\overline I_1\) is a unit,
\[
p_2=\frac{\overline I_{12}}{\overline I_1}p_1,
\]
so that factor of \(T\) is generated by \(1,p_1\) over
the corresponding factor of \(S\). On a factor where
\(\overline I_2\) is a unit, use \(1,p_2\) instead.
Every local factor is of one of these types. Summing the
dimension bounds from the resulting surjections \(S_i^2\to T_i\)
gives
\[
\boxed{\dim_{\mathbb C}T\le2\dim_{\mathbb C}S\le6.}
\tag{4}
\]
The derivation \(\overline D\) kills \(S\) and both \(p_i\),
so it vanishes on this entire finite-dimensional subalgebra.
Its reduction has at most six points. This bounds the distinct
joint values of these functions, not the number of connected
components of the whole fiber.

## 4. Exact limits

The actual fiber ring \(\overline A\) is one-dimensional and
is not asserted finite over \(T\). The derivative of another
higher-degree invariant can still be nonzero there. Equation (2)
does not prove \(\overline D=0\), nor exclude a fixed fiber.
No generation by \(F_3\), normalization, connectedness or
reducedness of the full fiber is inferred.

The new argument uses the actual slice identity, the already
proved coefficient vanishing, and elementary localizations and
Artinian decomposition. No numerical checker is attached to
these universal module statements. No cancellation solution,
historical novelty, external expert review or formal verification
is claimed.
