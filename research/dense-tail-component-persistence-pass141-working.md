# Formal persistence on a first-tail component

A component of the first-tail equation supports the complete formal
dense Jacobian and Taylor system whenever the second tail is a
nonconstant function on that component. The proof uses the universal
cofactor derivation and just the first two negative coefficients.
All later Laurent identities then follow because the last polynomial
has a polynomial derivative in \(X\).

This is a function-field persistence statement. It does not turn the
component into an inverse branch of a given polynomial automorphism.
The component's function field can be a proper finite extension of
the field generated by its second tail, and none of the ambient
polynomial-return or field-generation conditions is inferred.

## 1. Exact data and the component hypothesis

Let \(L/\mathbb C(\tau,\rho)\) be finite separable, with
\(\tau,\rho\) independent. Extend the two base partials uniquely
to \(L\); they commute. For independent variables \(U,V\), use
\[
\begin{gathered}
H_d(f;U,V)=\sum_{a+b=d}
 \frac{\partial_\tau^a\partial_\rho^b f}{a!\,b!}U^aV^b,\\
f_0,\ldots,f_m,f_{-1}\in L,\qquad m\ge0,\quad f_m\ne0,\\
\Phi=-\sum_{j=0}^{m}H_{j+1}(f_j;U,V)-f_{-1}\ne0,\\
\Psi=-\sum_{j=0}^{m}H_{j+2}(f_j;U,V)-H_1(f_{-1};U,V).
\end{gathered}
\tag{1}
\]
These are the exact signed polynomials of the dense Taylor
construction139.[^1] The lemma itself only requires the algebraic
data in (1); it does not assume that every factor below is realized
by original coordinates.

Choose an irreducible factor \(h\) of \(\Phi\) in \(L[U,V]\).
Put
\[
R_h=L[U,V]/(h),\qquad F_h=\operatorname{Frac}R_h,\qquad
y=\overline U,\quad z=\overline V,\quad v_h=\Psi(y,z),
\qquad v_h\text{ transcendental over }L .
\tag{2}
\]
The last condition is essential to the statement proved here.
It means that \(\Psi\) is a nonconstant function on the curve.
Merely requiring \(v_h\notin L\) would be weaker and insufficient:
the algebraic constant field of \(F_h\) can be larger than \(L\).

The field \(F_h\) is finitely generated of transcendence degree one
over \(L\). Consequently \(F_h/L(v_h)\) is finite and separable.
Extend \(\partial_\tau,\partial_\rho\) from \(L\) to \(L(v_h)\)
by fixing \(v_h\), and let \(\partial_v\) be the \(L\)-derivation
with \(\partial_vv_h=1\). Extend all three uniquely through the
finite separable extension to \(F_h\). They commute because their
commutators vanish on \(L(v_h)\), and a derivation vanishing on
that base also vanishes on its algebraic extension.

In general \(F_h\ne L(v_h)\). Even if these fields happen to be
equal, no assertion \(R_h=L[v_h]\) has been made.

## 2. A cofactor derivation independent of the last row

Adjoin an indeterminate \(X\) over \(F_h\), and set
\(Y_h=y+\tau X,\ Z_h=z+\rho X\). Use subscripts for the three
extended partials, and define
\[
\begin{gathered}
\eta=y_\tau+z_\rho,\qquad M=y_\tau z_\rho-y_\rho z_\tau,\\
C_\tau=y_\rho z_v-y_vz_\rho,\qquad
C_\rho=y_vz_\tau-y_\tau z_v,\\
T_hX=0,\qquad
T_h=(C_\tau-Xy_v)\partial_\tau+
    (C_\rho-Xz_v)\partial_\rho+
    (X^2+\eta X+M)\partial_v,\\
T_hY_h=T_hZ_h=0,\qquad
[X^2]T_h|_{F_h}=\partial_v .
\end{gathered}
\tag{3}
\]
The vector of coefficients in the third line is exactly the cross
product of the two rows
\((y_\tau+X,y_\rho,y_v)\) and
\((z_\tau,z_\rho+X,z_v)\). Its scalar product with either row
is zero, proving the fourth line explicitly. No last row or
Jacobian-unit hypothesis is used for these two annihilations.

The coefficients in (3) belong to \(F_h[X]\), possibly with poles
on the curve. Thus \(T_h\) maps \(F_h[X]\) to itself. It has the form
\(D_0+XD_1+X^2\partial_v\) on \(F_h\), so it extends continuously
to \(F_h((X^{-1}))\). Each output Laurent coefficient uses at most
three input coefficients, and the order can drop by at most two.
Local nilpotence, preservation of \(R_h[X]\), and preservation of
a chosen ambient polynomial ring are not asserted.

## 3. The algebraic Taylor field is still annihilated

The commuting coefficient partials define
\[
\begin{gathered}
\Theta_h(f)=\sum_{d\ge0}H_d(f;y,z)X^{-d},\qquad
\Theta_h:L\hookrightarrow F_h((X^{-1})),\\
\Theta_h(\tau)=Y_h/X,\qquad
\Theta_h(\rho)=Z_h/X,\qquad
T_h\Theta_h(f)=0\quad(f\in L).
\end{gathered}
\tag{4}
\]
For completeness, first take the Taylor homomorphism in two
independent formal increments over \(L\), then substitute
\(y/X,z/X\). Every Laurent coefficient involves finitely many
terms. The constant term is \(f\), so the resulting map is
injective and every nonzero image is a unit.

By (3), \(T_h\) kills the images of the separating base parameters.
Transport the monic minimal polynomial of \(f\in L\) over
\(\mathbb C(\tau,\rho)\) through \(\Theta_h\), and differentiate
at \(\Theta_h(f)\). Its coefficients are annihilated. The derivative
of that polynomial at the root is nonzero, since its constant term
is the original separable minimal-polynomial derivative at \(f\).
Hence \(T_h\Theta_h(f)=0\).
This repeats the exact-field argument139 on \(F_h\), without
assuming \(T_h(L)\subset L\).[^2]

## 4. The first two coefficients force the full determinant identity

Define a polynomial by the nonnegative part of the prescribed
Taylor series:
\[
\begin{gathered}
\mathscr S_h=\sum_{j=0}^{m}X^j\Theta_h(f_j),\qquad
W_h=(\mathscr S_h)_{\ge0}\in R_h[X],\qquad \deg_X W_h=m,\\
\mathscr R_h=W_h-\mathscr S_h\in X^{-1}F_h[[X^{-1}]],\\
[X^{-1}]\mathscr R_h
=-\sum_{j=0}^{m}H_{j+1}(f_j;y,z)=f_{-1}.
\end{gathered}
\tag{5}
\]
Polynomial membership holds because the finitely many retained
coefficients are polynomials in \(y,z\) over \(L\).
The highest coefficient is \(f_m\ne0\).
The last equality uses \(h\mid\Phi\); it is exactly the point
where the first-tail component is imposed.

Subtract the full annihilated series of that first coefficient:
\[
\begin{gathered}
\mathscr R_h^{(2)}
=\mathscr R_h-X^{-1}\Theta_h(f_{-1})
\in X^{-2}F_h[[X^{-1}]],\\
[X^{-2}]\mathscr R_h^{(2)}
=-\sum_{j=0}^{m}H_{j+2}(f_j;y,z)-H_1(f_{-1};y,z)
=\Psi(y,z)=v_h,\\
T_h\mathscr R_h^{(2)}=T_hW_h,\qquad T_hW_h=1.
\end{gathered}
\tag{6}
\]
To prove the last assertion, the left side has no positive powers
of \(X\), since its input begins at \(X^{-2}\) and the derivation
raises exponents by at most two. Its constant coefficient is
\(\partial_vv_h=1\).
On the other hand \(T_hW_h\in F_h[X]\), by (3) and (5).
A polynomial that is also a Laurent series with no positive powers
is constant. Its constant coefficient gives \(T_hW_h=1\).
In particular all negative coefficients of
\(T_h\mathscr R_h^{(2)}\) vanish automatically.

The cross-product calculation in Section 2 now gives the exact
relative Jacobian identity
\[
\det
\begin{pmatrix}
y_\tau+X&y_\rho&y_v\\
z_\tau&z_\rho+X&z_v\\
(W_h)_\tau&(W_h)_\rho&(W_h)_v
\end{pmatrix}
=T_hW_h=1.
\tag{7}
\]
Thus this component supports all the dense formal cofactor and
Taylor identities, not only the first two equations checked in
isolation. The argument also covers \(m=0\). It uses no division
by \(m\), \(y_v\), or \(z_v\).

## 5. Simplicity and the precise remaining bridge

The definitions in (1) satisfy
\[
\Psi_U=\Phi_\tau,\qquad \Psi_V=\Phi_\rho .
\tag{8}
\]
This follows by differentiating homogeneous Taylor polynomials;
the coefficient partials on the right fix \(U,V\).
If \(h^2\mid\Phi\), both right sides vanish modulo \(h\).
Then the \(L\)-derivation \(\partial_v\) on \(F_h\) gives
\[
1=\partial_v\Psi(y,z)
=\Psi_U(y,z)y_v+\Psi_V(y,z)z_v=0,
\]
a contradiction. Hence every component satisfying (2) is simple
in \(\Phi\). The converse is not proved: simplicity alone has not
been shown to make \(\Psi\) transcendental over \(L\).

This lemma establishes formal persistence, not polynomial inverse
persistence. In particular:

- \(F_h/L(v_h)\) can have degree greater than one, and the projected
  functions \(y,z\) need not be polynomials in \(v_h\).
- Even an equality \(R_h=L[v_h]\) would not identify a complex
  polynomial model before localization or remove invariant
  denominators and algebraic coefficient extensions.
- No equality
  \(F_h(X)=\mathbb C(X,Y_h,Z_h,W_h)\) has been proved. Nor is the
  original action \(E\), with its required polynomial values and
  global slice, shown to descend to the displayed tuple.

The actual selected component in139 has the stronger whole-line
and actual inverse properties. The formal inverse-branch family141
shows that even full relative determinant and Taylor identities
can coexist with a proper inverse-field extension.[^3]
Therefore counting the components furnished by this lemma as
branches of a given actual inverse would still require a separate
argument establishing that missing field and polynomial structure.
