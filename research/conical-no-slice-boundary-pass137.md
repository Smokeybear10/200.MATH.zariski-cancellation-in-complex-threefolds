# A conical quadratic action without a slice

The global-slice hypothesis in the conical nonexistence theorem cannot
be omitted. The following polynomial action has the nonlinear rank-two
cone, three good coordinates and a quadratic fourth coordinate. Its
nonconstant common speed factor prevents a slice.

## 1. Two actual polynomial coordinate systems

Start with \(B=\mathbb C[x,\tau,\xi,v]\) and set
\[
\begin{gathered}
X=x,\qquad Y=2v+\tau x,\qquad
Z=4\tau v+\xi+\tau^2x,\\
W=-\tfrac12\tau+vx+\tfrac12\tau x^2 .
\end{gathered}
\tag{1}
\]
These are actual polynomial coordinates. The inverse is
\[
x=X,\qquad
\tau=XY-2W,\qquad
v=\tfrac12(Y-\tau X),\qquad
\xi=Z-2\tau Y+\tau^2X .
\tag{2}
\]
Substitution of (1) into (2) returns \(x,\tau,\xi,v\).
Conversely substitution of (2) into (1) returns \(X,Y,Z,W\):
the first two coordinates are immediate, the third uses
\(4\tau v=2\tau Y-2\tau^2X\), and the fourth uses
\(vx+\tau x^2/2=XY/2\). Thus
\(B=\mathbb C[X,Y,Z,W]\) in the original ring, not just after
localization or in its fraction field.

## 2. The action and its nonlinear cone

Let \(E=\xi\partial_x\), holding \(\tau,\xi,v\) fixed.
This is locally nilpotent: each application lowers the \(x\)-degree
and the multiplier \(\xi\) is invariant. Its values are
\[
\begin{gathered}
EX=\xi,\quad EY=\xi\tau,\quad EZ=\xi\tau^2,\quad
EW=\xi(v+\tau x),\\
E^2X=E^2Y=E^2Z=0,\qquad
E^2W=\xi^2\tau,\qquad E^3W=0 .
\end{gathered}
\tag{3}
\]
The speed field is
\(\mathbb C(\xi,\xi\tau,\xi\tau^2)=\mathbb C(\xi,\tau)\),
so it has transcendence degree two.
Its prime relation is the irreducible homogeneous quadratic
\(S R-T^2\). It has no nonzero homogeneous linear relation.

The radial derivation on this field is
\(\mathcal R=\xi\partial_\xi\), holding \(\tau\) fixed.
For \(q=E^2W=\xi^2\tau\), one has
\(\mathcal R(q)=2q\).
Thus this is an actual example in the resonant branch once the
global-slice hypothesis is dropped.

## 3. Exactly why there is no slice

Every \(E\)-derivative is divisible by the nonunit coordinate \(\xi\).
Since \(EX=\xi\), the ideal generated by all derivative values is
\[
(E(B))B=(\xi)\ne B .
\tag{4}
\]
Hence \(Eu=1\) is impossible for any polynomial \(u\in B\).
The kernel is nevertheless the ordinary polynomial ring
\(\mathbb C[\tau,\xi,v]\), since \(E(f)=0\) is equivalent to
\(\partial_xf=0\) in this domain.
This example is not a cancellation counterexample.

There is even an invariant elementary bad-coordinate correction:
\[
I=W-\tfrac12XY=-\tfrac12\tau,\qquad
B=\mathbb C[I,X,Y,Z],\qquad
(EX,EY,EZ)=\xi(1,-2I,4I^2).
\tag{5}
\]
This is exactly where the last step of the nonexistence theorem
needs the slice. The row over \(\mathbb C[I]\) is primitive, but
its common factor \(\xi\) is not a unit.
The rank-two speed field therefore need not collapse to
\(\mathbb C(I)\).

## 4. The actual selected partial and the coefficient field

The original partial \(T=\partial_W\), holding \(X,Y,Z\) fixed,
has the following expression in the adapted coordinates:
\[
T=-2\partial_\tau+8v\partial_\xi+x\partial_v,\qquad T(x)=0 .
\tag{6}
\]
Applying it to (1) gives \(T(X)=T(Y)=T(Z)=0\) and \(T(W)=1\).
Its coefficient of \(x\) on \(\mathbb C[\tau,\xi,v]\) is
\(\partial_v\). Thus its coefficient field is
\(L=\mathbb C(\tau,\xi)\), but \(T(\xi)=8v\notin L\).
This gives an actual omitted-slice boundary for coefficient-field
preservation, unlike hypothetical formal jets in an excluded sliced
branch.

The normalized determinant of \((Y,Z,W)\) with respect to
\((\tau,\xi,v)\), holding \(x\) fixed, is one. Here \(y=2v\),
\(\rho=\tau^2\), \(\sigma=\tau\), and \(k=\xi\), so
\(y_v=2\) and \(\sigma_\xi=0\). The speed-cofactor tangent
identity still works. Using the \(x^3\) determinant coefficient alone
would not detect tangency because it is multiplied by \(\sigma_\xi\).
