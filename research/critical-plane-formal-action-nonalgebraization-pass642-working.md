# The critical-plane formal action does not algebraize

September 18, 2026

The whole critical-plane formal neighborhood of a Koras–Russell
cylinder, together with its distinguished function and free sliced
action, agrees with the corresponding neighborhood of an explicit
polynomial on affine four-space. It also admits a compatible sliced
additive action on every finite critical-plane thickening. No global
derivation of the cylinder induces that chosen system of actions.

This is a local-to-global boundary, not an obstruction to the cylinder
being affine four-space. The polynomial comparator has different
generic fibers. Compatible finite actions failing to give a global
LND are already retained in [finite-thickening31](finite-thickening-algebraization-pass31.md)
and [projected-translation103](quadratic-adic-projected-translation-pass103.md).
The result here identifies the exact critical-plane model for this
candidate and rules out even a global derivation inducing the selected
system. No novelty or cancellation conclusion is claimed.

## 1. The cylinder, function, and reduced critical plane

Fix integers
\[
m\ge2,\qquad p,q\ge2,\qquad \gcd(p,q)=1,
\tag{1}
\]
and put
\[
\begin{gathered}
R_0=\mathbb C[x,y,z,t]/(x+x^m y+z^p+t^q),\qquad B=R_0[w],\\
I=(x,z,t)B,\qquad V=1+x^{m-1}y,\qquad E=\partial_w.
\end{gathered}
\tag{2}
\]
The cubic case is \((m,p,q)=(2,2,3)\). Its established provenance,
generic plane fibers, and cuspidal zero fiber are explicitly recorded
in Dubouloz's primary paper.[^kr] We use the displayed equations, not
a claim that this cylinder is polynomial.

The defining polynomial is primitive and linear in \(y\) over the
UFD \(\mathbb C[x,z,t,w]\): its coefficients \(x^m\) and
\(x+z^p+t^q\) are coprime. It is irreducible over the fraction field,
so Gauss's lemma shows that \(B\) is a domain. It is smooth. Indeed,
a zero of all partial derivatives of its equation would have
\(x=z=t=0\), where the \(x\)-derivative is one.

The reduced critical locus of the function \(x\) on this smooth
fourfold is precisely \(V(I)\). The Jacobian criterion for the map
\(x\) requires the other equation derivatives
\(x^m,pz^{p-1},qt^{q-1}\) to vanish, which set-theoretically gives
\(x=z=t=0\). Thus
\[
B/I=\mathbb C[y,w],\qquad
B/(x)=\bigl(\mathbb C[z,t]/(z^p+t^q)\bigr)[y,w].
\tag{3}
\]
We complete along the reduced ideal \(I\), not along the possibly
nonreduced Jacobian scheme. The latter distinction does not affect
the description of the entire reduced critical plane.

The action generated by \(E\) is free and sliced by \(w\), and it
annihilates \(x\). For every nonzero scalar \(c\), elimination of
\(y\) gives
\[
B/(x-c)=\mathbb C[z,t,w],\qquad
B_x=\mathbb C[x,x^{-1},z,t,w].
\tag{4}
\]
The zero fiber in (3) is nonnormal. Its irreducible cusp curve is
singular at the origin, and the singular plane in its three-dimensional
product is a codimension-one nonregular locus. None of these facts
asserts that \(B\cong\mathbb C^{[4]}\).

## 2. The complete pair and both inverse maps

Write \(\widehat B=\varprojlim_N B/I^N\). Since \(V\in1+I\), every
rational binomial power of \(V\) is defined in this completion by
the series with constant term one. Set
\[
Z=zV^{-1/p},\qquad T=tV^{-1/q}.
\tag{5}
\]
The relation \(xV+z^p+t^q=0\) gives the exact identity
\[
x=-(Z^p+T^q).
\tag{6}
\]
There is a continuous homomorphism
\[
\alpha:\mathbb C[y,w][[Z,T]]\longrightarrow\widehat B
\tag{7}
\]
fixing \(y,w\) and defined by (5).

For the inverse, in \(S=\mathbb C[y,w][[Z,T]]\) put
\[
x_* =-(Z^p+T^q),\qquad V_*=1+x_*^{m-1}y,
\tag{8}
\]
and send
\[
x\longmapsto x_*,\quad y\longmapsto y,\quad w\longmapsto w,
\quad z\longmapsto ZV_*^{1/p},\quad
t\longmapsto TV_*^{1/q}.
\tag{9}
\]
These images satisfy the whole defining equation, since its image is
\(V_*(x_*+Z^p+T^q)=0\). They send \(I\) into \((Z,T)\), and hence
extend continuously to \(\beta:\widehat B\to S\).

The map \(\alpha\) sends \(x_*\) to \(x\) by (6), and then sends
\(V_*\) to \(V\). Thus \(\alpha\beta\) fixes every original
generator. Conversely, \(\beta(V)=V_*\), so \(\beta\alpha\) fixes
\(Z,T,y,w\). The binomial powers cancel because they are the
unique indicated roots with constant term one. Both compositions
are identity maps by continuity. Moreover the image of \(I\) under
\(\beta\) generates exactly \((Z,T)\): the images of \(z,t\) are
unit multiples of \(Z,T\), and \(x_*\in(Z,T)^2\).

Consequently we have an isomorphism of complete rings, distinguished
functions, and continuous derivations
\[
\boxed{
(\widehat B,x,E)
\cong
\bigl(\mathbb C[y,w][[Z,T]],-(Z^p+T^q),\partial_w\bigr).
}
\tag{10}
\]
It is defined along the entire plane \(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb C[y,w]\),
not just after completion at one point or passage to its generic point.

## 3. Compatible genuine actions on every finite thickening

Transport the continuous derivation \(\partial_y\), fixing
\(Z,T,w\), through (10), and call it \(\widehat\delta\). For each
positive integer \(N\), the same isomorphism gives
\[
B/I^N\cong
\bigl(\mathbb C[Z,T]/(Z,T)^N\bigr)[y,w].
\tag{11}
\]
Thus \(\widehat\delta\) induces an actual LND \(\delta_N\) on
\(B/I^N\), with slice \(y\). In particular its exponential is a
polynomial additive-group action even though the ring can be
nonreduced. The actions commute with the action of \(E\), fix
\(x\), and are compatible under all transition maps.

For an additive parameter \(\lambda\), their full formulas are
\[
\begin{gathered}
x\longmapsto x,\quad w\longmapsto w,\quad y\longmapsto y+\lambda,\\
z\longmapsto z\left(1+\frac{\lambda x^{m-1}}{V}\right)^{1/p},
\qquad
t\longmapsto t\left(1+\frac{\lambda x^{m-1}}{V}\right)^{1/q}
\qquad\text{in }(B/I^N)[\lambda].
\end{gathered}
\tag{12}
\]
Here \(V^{-1}\) is a finite geometric series in the nilpotent
element \(V-1\), and each displayed binomial series truncates because
\(x^{m-1}\) is nilpotent modulo \(I^N\). Formula (11) proves the
whole group law and inverse, since (12) is just translation in
\(y\) conjugated by an actual finite-quotient isomorphism.

The nilpotence indices are unbounded, with an exact formula. Put
\(d=(m-1)\min(p,q)\). For \(N\ge2\), the least positive integer
\(k\) such that \(\delta_N^k(\bar z)=0\), and separately the least
one for \(\bar t\), is
\[
\boxed{\quad 1+\left\lfloor\frac{N-2}{d}\right\rfloor.\quad}
\tag{13}
\]
Indeed (9) expands as
\[
z=Z\sum_{j\ge0}\binom{1/p}{j}
 y^j[-(Z^p+T^q)]^{(m-1)j}
\tag{14}
\]
in the complete coordinates. Its coefficient of \(y^j\) has
\((Z,T)\)-order exactly \(1+dj\). Every binomial coefficient is
nonzero, and the lowest homogeneous term is nonzero. Therefore the
largest surviving \(y\)-degree modulo \((Z,T)^N\) is exactly
\(\lfloor(N-2)/d\rfloor\). Characteristic zero makes repeated
\(y\)-differentiation have the claimed index. The proof for \(t\)
uses \(1/q\) instead. For \(N=1\), both residues are already zero.

The compatible actions take values in the inverse limit of the
polynomial action rings \((B/I^N)[\lambda]\). They are not asserted
to define an ordinary algebraic action with values in
\(\widehat B[\lambda]\), nor is \(\widehat\delta\) locally nilpotent.

## 4. The selected system has no global derivation lift

Differentiating (8)--(9) at fixed \(Z,T,w\) identifies the formal
derivation with the following derivation of the rational function
field of \(B\):
\[
\delta(y)=1,\quad \delta(x)=\delta(w)=0,\quad
\delta(z)=\frac{x^{m-1}z}{pV},\quad
\delta(t)=\frac{x^{m-1}t}{qV}.
\tag{15}
\]
It preserves the defining relation: its derivative is
\(x^m+x^{m-1}(z^p+t^q)/V=0\). Thus it is a genuine derivation
on \(B_V\) and agrees with \(\widehat\delta\) in the completion.

Suppose a derivation \(\Delta:B\to B\) induced \(\delta_N\) on
every \(B/I^N\). Agreement on \(z\), followed by multiplication
by \(pV\), would give
\[
pV\Delta(z)-x^{m-1}z\in\bigcap_{N\ge1}I^N=0.
\tag{16}
\]
For the separation used here, localize at any maximal ideal
containing \(I\). Krull's intersection theorem in that Noetherian
local ring makes the image of the intersection zero. The localization
map from \(B\) is injective because \(B\) is a domain, proving the
last equality in (16).[^krull]

Now evaluate (16) at the actual point
\[
x=1,\quad y=-1,\quad z=1,\quad t=\tau,
\quad \tau^q=-1,\quad w=0.
\tag{17}
\]
It satisfies the defining equation. Here \(V=0\), whereas
\(x^{m-1}z=1\), a contradiction. Hence no global derivation,
and therefore no global LND, induces this particular compatible
system on all finite thickenings.

Even on \(B_V\), (15) is not locally nilpotent. The unit \(V\)
has \(\delta(V)=x^{m-1}\ne0\). An LND on a characteristic-zero
domain kills every unit: its exponential sends a unit to a unit
of the polynomial action ring, whose units lie in the coefficient
ring. Differentiation at zero then gives zero.

For completeness, nonnilpotence is also visible term by term:
\[
\delta^k(z)=
\left[\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}\left(\frac1p-j\right)\right]
\frac{x^{(m-1)k}z}{V^k}\ne0\qquad(k\ge1).
\tag{18}
\]
Induction uses \(\delta(x)=0\) and \(\delta(V)=x^{m-1}\).
The same nonzero expressions occur in the separated completion.
This is compatible with finite nilpotence in (13), not a contradiction
to it.

## 5. What the affine-four-space comparator does and does not show

Let
\[
B_0=\mathbb C[y,w,Z,T],\quad F_0=-(Z^p+T^q),\quad
J=(Z,T),\quad E_0=\partial_w.
\tag{19}
\]
Its reduced critical plane is \(V(J)\), and completing along \(J\)
gives exactly the right side of (10). The comparator has the global
translation \(\partial_y\). Its finite actions correspond to (12),
even though that system has no global derivation lift on \(B\).
The zero fibers of \(x\) and \(F_0\) also have the same cusp-product
coordinate ring.

Their nonzero fibers are different. Equation (4) gives affine
three-space for \(B\), whereas the comparator gives
\[
B_0/(F_0-c)=
\bigl(\mathbb C[Z,T]/(Z^p+T^q+c)\bigr)[y,w]
\qquad(c\ne0).
\tag{20}
\]
This curve product is not affine three-space. The smooth projective
model of the curve is a degree-\(p\) cover of the \(T\)-line, totally
ramified at the \(q\) simple finite roots and at infinity. Coprimality
gives one point at infinity. Riemann--Hurwitz gives genus
\((p-1)(q-1)/2>0\), so the affine curve and its product with
\(\mathbb A^2\) have nonzero first homology.

Thus the completed pair, its free sliced \(E\)-action, and this
whole compatible system of finite-order translations do not detect
the generic-fiber distinction. A recognition or normality argument
must use additional global input. This note neither supplies that
input nor rules out some different global action on \(B\).

## 6. Provenance and verification boundary

The general failure of all-order finite nilpotence to imply global
nilpotence is already proved in pass31. Pass103 also supplies exact
formal translation coordinates and explains why an inverse limit
of polynomial slice presentations need not itself be polynomial in
the slice variable. The present proof does not replace those results
or label the underlying principle new.

The [retained cylinder route](cylinder-route.md) already states the
one-bad-fiber target for the cubic. Here the additional explicit data
are (10), the entire-plane finite actions (12), and their impossibility
of being induced by any global derivation of this \(B\). They concern
this selected system, not the existence of every possible action.

All binomial, inverse, group-law, and nonextension assertions have
the uniform written proofs above. The coprime assumptions are used
for the irreducible cusp and the generic-curve comparison; the formal
calculations themselves require only \(m,p,q\ge2\). Saved checker
scripts were not executed, imported, or compiled. Independent review,
if completed, belongs in the separate review record. Novelty remains
unassessed, and the polynomial-cylinder and general cancellation
questions remain unresolved.
