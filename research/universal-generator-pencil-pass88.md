# An actual generator pencil and its tensor-diagonal constraint

The completed relative generators in the
[invariant-plane lemma](constant-acceleration-invariant-plane-pass88.md)
satisfy an identity over a polynomial parameter, not merely a
collection of identities at closed parameter values. That identity
forces the slice difference into the fraction field of the smaller
generator ring and makes its tensor difference nilpotent. Neither
conclusion alone descends the slice difference into that ring.

## 1. The pencil comes from the original algebra equality

Use the actual notation of equations (9)--(10) of the cited lemma.
In particular S is the actual polynomial invariant plane, sigma is
the completed slice, and A_1,A_2,c are actual elements of A. Put
\[
R=S[A_1,A_2]\subset A,\qquad A=R[c],\qquad
h=u-\sigma\in A,\qquad
z=c+q\sigma h+\frac q2\sigma^2,\qquad q\in\mathbb C^*.
\tag{1}
\]
The inverse S-matrix supplies the equality of subalgebras
B=R[sigma,z]. The original slice presentation is B=A[sigma],
and sigma is transcendental over A. Subtract the last term in (1)
and rescale the polynomial parameter by tau=q sigma. This gives
\[
A[\tau]=R[\tau,c+\tau h]
\quad\text{inside }A[\tau].
\tag{2}
\]
The identification of tau with q sigma uses the actual slice
presentation. It is not an assertion that a formal S-matrix is an
ambient coordinate change. In particular (2) is an equality over
R[tau], before any specialization of tau.

For Sections 2--3, it is enough to assume that R is a
characteristic-zero domain, A=R[c] is a domain, h belongs to A,
and (2) holds. No smoothness, finite extension, normality of R,
or actual additive action is needed for those algebraic statements.

## 2. The slice difference lies in the fraction field of R

Let K=Frac(R). Suppose first that c is algebraic over K. Then
L=K[c] is a finite separable field extension. Localization of (2)
and extension to an algebraic closure of K give
\[
L[\tau]=K[\tau,c+\tau h],\qquad
L\otimes_K\overline K\simeq\prod_{i=1}^{d}\overline K,
\qquad d_i=c_i+\tau h_i.
\tag{3}
\]
Here c_i and h_i are the images under all K-embeddings of L.
The product ring over the algebraic closure must be generated by
the single tuple (d_i) over its diagonal polynomial coefficient
ring. If h_i and h_j were different, specializing
tau=(c_j-c_i)/(h_i-h_j) would make its i-th and j-th entries equal.
Every polynomial in that tuple would then have those two entries
equal, whereas the full product ring contains the idempotent
separating them. Specialization preserves the asserted algebra
equality, so this is impossible. Consequently all conjugates of h
are equal. Separability implies h lies in K. This also covers the
degree-one extension, where the conclusion is immediate.

If c is transcendental over K, write h=H(c) with H in R[T].
After passage to K(tau), the equality of polynomial rings in (2)
implies that c+tau H(c) has c-degree one. Indeed c is a polynomial
in that element, so multiplication of degrees forces degree one.
Thus H(c)=a c+b for a,b in K. If a were nonzero, specialize the
localized equality over K[tau] at tau=-1/a: the proposed generator
becomes the constant -b/a and cannot generate K[c]. Hence a=0.
Since H originally has coefficients in R and c is transcendental,
in this case h in fact belongs to R. In both cases,
\[
h\in A\cap\operatorname{Frac}(R).
\tag{4}
\]
The algebraic case does not say that A is finite over R or that
h is integral over R. It also does not justify h in R.

There is a complete branch conclusion in the original setting (1).
If c is transcendental over Frac(R), then trdeg R=2 because
trdeg A=3 and A=R[c]. The inclusion S in R has the same
transcendence degree. Every element of R is therefore algebraic over
Frac(S). The original intrinsic derivation W kills S, so its
extension to fraction fields kills such an element: differentiate
its minimal polynomial, whose derivative is nonzero in
characteristic zero. Thus R is contained in ker(W|A)=S. It follows
that R=S and A=S[c] is C cubed. The remaining branch in this
specific application has c algebraic over Frac(R). This branch
conclusion uses the actual invariant plane and its actual W, not
the abstract pencil identity alone.

## 3. A nilpotent difference on the whole tensor product

Set D=A tensor_R A, which is allowed to be nonreduced and to have
zero divisors. Use consistent left-minus-right differences:
\[
c_1=c\otimes1,\quad c_2=1\otimes c,\quad
\delta c=c_1-c_2,\qquad
\delta h=h\otimes1-1\otimes h=g\,\delta c
\quad(g\in D).
\tag{5}
\]
The last factorization follows by writing h as a polynomial in c
over R and taking its polynomial divided difference. No division
by delta c is used.

From (2) choose a polynomial F(tau,T) over R with
c=F(tau,c+tau h). Apply the two tensor maps and subtract.
The polynomial divided difference of F in its second argument
gives some k(tau) in D[tau] with
\[
\delta c=(\delta c+\tau\delta h)k(\tau)
         =\delta c(1+\tau g)k(\tau).
\tag{6}
\]
If delta c is zero, so is delta h and the conclusion below is
immediate. Otherwise set Dbar=D/Ann_D(delta c). The annihilator
of delta c in D[tau] is exactly Ann_D(delta c)[tau], by taking
coefficients. Thus (6), with no illicit cancellation in D, gives
\[
1=(1+\tau\bar g)\bar k(\tau)\quad\text{in }\bar D[\tau],
\qquad g^N\delta c=0,\qquad (\delta h)^N=0
\quad\text{for some }N\ge1.
\tag{7}
\]
For completeness, let the inverse polynomial have degree at most m.
Comparison of coefficients gives its constant coefficient 1 and
successive coefficients (-gbar)^j. The final coefficient equation
forces gbar to the m+1 to vanish. Take N=m+1. Then g to the N
lies in Ann_D(delta c), and
(delta h) to the N=(delta c) to the N-1 times (g to the N delta c)
is zero. This argument retains the entire nonreduced tensor ring.

Thus the two images of h agree in the reduced tensor product. They
need not agree in D itself. If D happened to be reduced, they would
agree in D, but no reducedness hypothesis is asserted here. Nor
is effective descent along R to A being assumed: the relevant
faithful flatness in the prior lemma is S to A, not R to A.

## 4. A cusp control prevents stronger ring descent

Take the algebra-only example
\[
R=\mathbb C[v^2,v^5]\subset A=\mathbb C[v],\qquad
c=v,\qquad h=v^3,\qquad
v=(1-\tau v^2)(v+\tau v^3)+\tau^2v^5.
\tag{8}
\]
This proves (2) for all polynomial tau. Nevertheless h is not in R:
the exponent 3 is not in the semigroup generated by 2 and 5.
Also Frac(R)=C(v), since v=v^5/(v^2)^2, so (4) is consistent.

Here the tensor ring and its differences are explicit:
\[
D=\mathbb C[X,Y]/(X^2-Y^2,X^5-Y^5),\qquad
\delta c=X-Y,\quad \delta h=X^3-Y^3\ne0,\quad
(\delta h)^2=0,\quad g=X^2+XY+Y^2.
\tag{9}
\]
Indeed the two defining relations imply Y^4(X-Y)=0. Reduction
modulo X squared minus Y squared gives
delta h=Y squared times (X-Y), so its square is zero. Its
nonvanishing follows already by homogeneous degree: a degree-three
element of the displayed ideal must be a linear multiple of
X squared minus Y squared, whereas X cubed minus Y cubed is not
such a multiple, as evaluation at X=-Y shows. The accompanying
exact control also computes its nonzero normal form. Further,
g squared times delta c is zero. Equivalently the inverse in (7)
can be represented here by 1-tau g modulo Ann_D(delta c).

The map v to v+tau v cubed is not an automorphism of C[tau,v]
fixing C[tau]: it has v-degree three. The relative algebra equality
instead uses the embedded coefficients v squared and v to the fifth.
It must not be confused with an R-endomorphism of A that changes
c while preserving its relations over R. This control is not a
smooth threefold, a polynomial invariant-plane construction, or an
actual three-good-coordinate action.
