# Classification of irreducible actions preserving a normalized cubic suspension

An irreducible locally nilpotent derivation preserving the normalized
cubic suspension below has an explicit two-polynomial description.
Its fixed-point-free members are precisely those with constant nonzero
speed in the distinguished coordinate. For these members the invariant
ring and a polynomial straightening are explicit.

This is a classification under a specified invariant presentation and
derivative-degree bounds. It neither supplies that presentation for an
arbitrary action nor proves the general cancellation conjecture.
The generic-field step uses Wang's classification of two-quasi-nice
derivations in the precise form of Dasgupta--Gupta, Theorem 4.1.[^1]
The same classification appears in Wang's doctoral thesis,
Theorem 2.4.5 and Remark 9, over a characteristic-zero field.[^5]

## 1. Statement

Let the displayed generators be actual polynomial coordinates, and assume
\[
\begin{gathered}
B=\mathbb C[X,Y,Z,W],\qquad E\in\operatorname{LND}_{\mathbb C}(B),\\
E^2X=E^2Y=E^2Z=0,\qquad E^3W=0,\qquad s=EX\ne0,\\
I=XW-F(Y)-\delta Z\in\ker E,\qquad
F(T)=\gamma T^3+\beta T^2+\alpha T+\epsilon,\quad
\gamma\delta\ne0.
\end{gathered}
\tag{1}
\]
Assume that \(E\) is irreducible: no nonunit of \(B\) divides
every element of \(E(B)\). Write \(t=EY\).

If \(t=0\), then \(s\in\mathbb C^*\) and \(EW=0\).
If \(t\ne0\), there are \(c\in\mathbb C^*\) and nonzero
polynomials \(h,g\in\mathbb C[I]\), with \(h\) monic and
\(\gcd(h,g)=1\), such that
\[
\begin{gathered}
EI=0,\qquad EX=ch^3,\qquad EY=h^2g,\\
EW=\frac{h g^2}{c}(3\gamma Y+\beta)
       -\frac{g^3}{c^2}\gamma X.
\end{gathered}
\tag{2}
\]
Conversely, every choice in (2) defines an irreducible locally
nilpotent derivation satisfying (1). Here \(Z\) is recovered by
\(Z=(XW-F(Y)-I)/\delta\).

For \(t\ne0\), the fixed locus as a reduced set is
\[
\{h(I)=0,\ X=0\}\subset\operatorname{Spec}B.
\tag{3}
\]
It is empty exactly when \(h=1\). In particular, under (1),
fixed-point freeness implies \(EX\in\mathbb C^*\).
An initial slice and a unit ideal generated by \(EX,EY,EZ\)
are not required. Fixed-point freeness here means the exact
algebraic condition \(E(B)B=B\), which already implies irreducibility.

## 2. Coordinate reduction and derivative identities

Since \(\delta\ne0\), there is an actual equality
\(B=\mathbb C[I,X,Y,W]\), with the polynomial inverse for
\(Z\) displayed above. Thus \(I\) is an indeterminate in this
presentation. Put \(K=\mathbb C(I)\) and
\(B_K=K[X,Y,W]\). The derivation fixes \(K\), remains nonzero
and locally nilpotent, and is irreducible over \(K\).

For the last assertion, the coefficient ideal in these coordinates
is generated by \(s,t,EW\). A nonconstant common factor over
\(K[X,Y,W]\) would have a primitive irreducible representative
in \(\mathbb C[I][X,Y,W]\). Gauss's lemma would make that
representative divide all three original coefficients, contradicting
irreducibility in \(B\). Factors belonging to \(\mathbb C[I]\)
become units upon localization and cause no problem.

Set \(q=E^2W\). Differentiating \(EI=0\) twice further gives
\[
2sEW+Xq=F''(Y)t^2,\qquad sq=2\gamma t^3.
\tag{4}
\]
Indeed \(E^2Z=0\) gives the first identity, and \(E^3W=0\)
gives \(0=E^3I=3sq-6\gamma t^3\).

## 3. The two generic alternatives

Theorem 4.1 of Dasgupta--Gupta applies to the nonzero irreducible
derivation on \(K[X,Y,W]\), since \(E^2X=E^2Y=0\).
Its first alternative supplies a nonzero invariant \(K\)-linear
form in \(X,Y\). Its second supplies an invariant coordinate \(V_0\)
while retaining \(X,Y\), with \(s,t\in K[V_0]\). These are the
only portions of that theorem needed here.[^1]

In the first alternative the coefficient of \(Y\) in the invariant
linear form is nonzero, because \(s\ne0\). Consequently \(t=ks\)
for some \(k\in K\). Equations (4) give
\[
q=2\gamma k^3s^2,\qquad
EW=s\bigl(k^2F''(Y)/2-\gamma k^3X\bigr).
\tag{5}
\]
Thus \(s\) divides every derivation coefficient in \(B_K\).
Irreducibility implies \(s\in K^*\), and hence \(t\in K\).

In the second alternative, the equality
\(K[X,Y,V_0]=K[X,Y,W]\) forces
\(V_0=\lambda W+P(X,Y)\), where \(\lambda\in K^*\)
and \(P\in K[X,Y]\). This follows by comparing degrees of
mutually inverse polynomials in the single variable \(W\) over
\(K[X,Y]\); their leading coefficients are units, hence elements
of \(K^*\). In coordinates \(V_0,X,Y\), irreducibility gives
\(\gcd_{K[V_0]}(s,t)=1\). The divisibility \(s\mid t^3\)
from (4) therefore implies \(s\in K^*\).

If \(t\notin K\), set \(d=\deg_{V_0}t>0\). From \(E^2V_0=0\),
\[
\lambda q=-s^2P_{XX}-2stP_{XY}-t^2P_{YY}.
\tag{6}
\]
In the independent variables \(X,Y,V_0\), the right side has
\(V_0\)-degree at most \(2d\). But \(q=2\gamma t^3/s\)
has degree \(3d\). This is impossible. Hence \(t\in K\)
in this alternative as well.

Since \(B\cap K=\mathbb C[I]\), both alternatives prove
\(s,t\in\mathbb C[I]\). This intersection is taken in the
actual coordinate presentation, not in an unspecified invariant field.

## 4. Descent by coefficient comparison and valuations

If \(t=0\), (4) gives \(q=EW=0\). The only nonzero
coefficient in coordinates \(I,X,Y,W\) is \(s\), so
irreducibility makes \(s\) a nonzero constant.

Suppose now that \(t\ne0\). Equations (4) give
\[
EW=(3\gamma Y+\beta)A_0-\gamma XC_0,
\qquad A_0=\frac{t^2}{s},\quad C_0=\frac{t^3}{s^2}.
\tag{7}
\]
Since \(EW\in\mathbb C[I,X,Y,W]\), coefficient comparison
in \(X,Y,W\) shows \(A_0,C_0\in\mathbb C[I]\).
For an irreducible \(p\in\mathbb C[I]\) dividing \(s\), write
\(e=v_p(s)>0\) and \(f=v_p(t)\). Polynomiality gives
\(2f-e\ge0\) and \(3f-2e\ge0\), hence \(f>0\) and
\(2f-e\ge e/3>0\).

The common coefficient valuation is
\[
\min\{v_p(s),v_p(t),v_p(EW)\}
=\min\{e,f,2f-e,3f-2e\}.
\tag{8}
\]
The \(Y\) and \(X\) coefficients in (7) are distinct and
nonzero, so cancellation cannot change this minimum.
Irreducibility forces it to be zero. The first three entries
are positive, so \(3f=2e\). Thus \(e=3k,f=2k\) for a
positive integer \(k\).

Collect these powers over the factors of \(s\) into a monic
polynomial \(h\). Then \(s=ch^3,t=h^2g\) with
\(\gcd(h,g)=1\), and (7) is precisely (2).
When \(s\) is already constant, take \(h=1\).

Conversely, (2) is triangular over \(\mathbb C[I]\): the
derivatives of \(X,Y\) are fixed and that of \(W\) is linear
in \(X,Y\). It is locally nilpotent, and
\(q=2\gamma h^3g^3/c\). Substitution into the first identity
of (4) proves \(E^2Z=0\); all other bounds in (1) follow
directly. A common prime factor of the coefficients must
divide \(h\). Modulo such a prime, the \(X\) coefficient of
\(EW\) is nonzero because \(g\) is coprime to \(h\).
Hence the derivation is irreducible.

At a point, \(EX=0\) forces \(h(I)=0\), and then \(EY=0\).
Coprimality ensures \(g(I)\ne0\) there, so \(EW=0\) is
equivalent to \(X=0\). This proves (3). Multiplicities of
the fixed-point scheme are not being identified with the reduced set.

## 5. Explicit straightening in the fixed-point-free case

Fixed-point freeness forces \(h=1\), so put \(s=c\in\mathbb C^*\),
\(t\in\mathbb C[I]\), and \(\eta=t/c\). This includes \(t=0\).
Define
\[
\begin{gathered}
V=Y-\eta X,\\
J=W-\eta^2(3\gamma V+\beta)X-\gamma\eta^3X^2.
\end{gathered}
\tag{9}
\]
One has \(EV=EJ=0\). The inverse polynomial expressions are
\[
\begin{gathered}
Y=V+\eta(I)X,\qquad
W=J+\eta(I)^2(3\gamma V+\beta)X+\gamma\eta(I)^3X^2,\\
Z=(XW-F(Y)-I)/\delta,\qquad
B=\mathbb C[I,V,J,X],\quad E=c\partial_X,\quad
\ker E=\mathbb C[I,V,J].
\end{gathered}
\tag{10}
\]
Both inverse compositions follow successively from recovering \(I,V,J\)
and substituting the displayed triangular formulas. No inversion of
\(X\), \(t\), or a polynomial in \(I\) occurs. In these coordinates
\(\delta EZ=c(J-\eta F'(V))\), so one may also write
\(\ker E=\mathbb C[I,V,EZ]\).

The necessity of fixed-point freeness for this conclusion is concrete.
With \(F(Y)=Y^3/2\), \(\delta=1\), \(h(I)=I\), \(g=1\), \(c=1\),
equation (2) gives
\[
EX=I^3,\qquad EY=I^2,\qquad EW=3IY/2-X/2,
\qquad Z=XW-Y^3/2-I.
\tag{11}
\]
This is an actual irreducible locally nilpotent derivation with the
required degree bounds and nonconstant \(EX\), but it has fixed
points at \(I=X=0\). It has no slice. It is not a cancellation
counterexample.

## 6. Consequence for successful primitive corrections

Consider an actual sliced frame \(B=\mathbb C[x_1,x_2,x_3,z]=A[u]\)
with \(E=\partial_u\), \(E^2x_i=0\), \(E^3z=0\), and a dense
homogeneous cone of good speeds \(a_i=Ex_i\). Suppose there is a
primitive relation \(L(a)E^2z=2F_3(a)\), where \(L\) is nonzero
homogeneous linear, \(F_3\) is homogeneous cubic and \(\gcd(L,F_3)=1\).
If both correction conditions give
\[
I=(L(x)+\rho)z-F_3(x)-G_2(x)-G_1(x)-G_0\in A,
\quad\text{then}\quad L(a)\in\mathbb C^*.
\tag{12}
\]
Here \(\rho,G_0\in\mathbb C\), while \(G_2,G_1\) are respectively
homogeneous quadratic and linear polynomials in the good coordinates.

Here is the precise dependency on the retained recognition result.[^2]
Take \(X=L(x)+\rho\) and two affine-linear complementary goods.
Divide the corrected polynomial by \(X\), absorb its quotient of
degree at most two into the bad coordinate, and apply the
invariant-suspension theorem. Its conclusion makes the residual
plane polynomial a coordinate. Primitivity gives exact degree three.
The cubic plane-coordinate criterion then makes the residual polynomial
\(F(Y)+\delta Z\), after an affine-linear plane change, with
\(\gamma\delta\ne0\).[^3] All three goods remain \(E\)-affine
and the new bad coordinate satisfies \(E^3W=0\). Density gives
\(EX=L(a)\ne0\). The existing slice implies fixed-point freeness,
so Sections 1--5 apply.

Thus no unit-good-row assumption is needed in (12). In fact success
forces that row to generate the unit ideal, since its constant linear
combination \(L(a)\) is a nonzero constant. The earlier elementary
unit-row argument remains a separate proof under stronger hypotheses.[^4]
Its nonprimitive example shows that primitivity cannot be deleted
from this application.

This closes a limitation of the correction route: whenever a primitive
lift succeeds in the stated sliced profile, an affine combination of
the original good coordinates was already a coordinate slice. It does
not prove that any proposed primitive relation succeeds. An actual
primitive frame with nonconstant \(L(a)\) would force a correction
failure, but no such frame is constructed here. Neither arbitrary
coordinate-frame entry nor existence of an appropriate primitive
relation is established.

## 7. The dense successful normal forms

There is also an exact parametrization of the normalized successful
dense frames. It is a parametrization, not a uniqueness assertion.
Choose \(c,\delta,\gamma\in\mathbb C^*\), the cubic \(F\) in (1),
and a nonconstant polynomial \(p\in\mathbb C[I]\). In actual
translation coordinates \(I,V,R,u\), with \(E=\partial_u\), set
\[
\begin{gathered}
\Delta_F(p,V,u)=
\gamma(3V^2p+3Vp^2u+p^3u^2)+\beta(2Vp+p^2u)+\alpha p,\\
X=cu,\qquad Y=V+p(I)u,\qquad
Z=-\frac{I+F(V)}{\delta}+Ru,\qquad
W=\frac{\delta R+\Delta_F(p(I),V,u)}{c}.
\end{gathered}
\tag{13}
\]
The divided difference is polynomial even at \(u=0\).
The inverse is successive and polynomial:
\(u=X/c\), \(I=XW-F(Y)-\delta Z\),
\(V=Y-p(I)u\), and
\(R=(cW-\Delta_F(p(I),V,u))/\delta\).
The identity \(u\Delta_F=F(V+pu)-F(V)\) verifies both
inverse compositions, including the hyperplane \(u=0\).

Its good speeds are \(c,p(I),R\), and \(q=2\gamma p(I)^3/c\).
Because \(p\) is nonconstant, \(p(I)\) and \(R\) are algebraically
independent. The homogeneous speed cone is dense, its field has
transcendence degree two, and \(q\) lies outside the quadratic
span of the good speeds. The primitive pair \(L=X,F_3=\gamma Y^3\)
passes the two corrections, with corrected invariant \(I\).

Conversely Sections 1--6 put every successful primitive dense frame
into (13) after the stated affine good changes and quadratic bad
shear. Indeed put \(u=X/c\), \(p(I)=EY\),
\(V=Y-p(I)u\), and \(R=EZ\). Equation (10) and
\(\delta R=c(J-(p/c)F'(V))\) show that \(I,V,R,u\)
are actual coordinates. Integrating the good speeds gives the
displayed \(Y,Z\), and \(EI=0\) gives the displayed polynomial
expression for \(W\). Density excludes constant \(p\), since
otherwise \(EY-(p/c)EX=0\) is a homogeneous linear relation.
This characterizes the successful side without proving that every
candidate frame belongs to it.

## 8. The adjoint degree of a successful primitive frame

Let \(A=\ker E=\mathbb C[I,V,R]\) and take the bad coordinate
derivation \(T=\partial_W\), with \(X,Y,Z\) held fixed. In (13),
direct differentiation gives
\[
\begin{gathered}
TI=cu,\qquad Tu=0,\qquad TV=-cp'(I)u^2,\\
TR=\frac c\delta-\frac c\delta p'(I)F'(V)u,\\
N=[u^2]T|_A=-cp'(I)\partial_V,\qquad
\ker_A N=\mathbb C[I,R].
\end{gathered}
\tag{14}
\]
For example \(TV\) follows from \(V=Y-p(I)u\). For \(TR\),
differentiate \(cW=\delta R+\Delta_F\). The divided-difference
identity gives \(T\Delta_F=cp'(I)F'(V)u\); it is a polynomial
identity, not a division valid only away from \(u=0\).

The derivative \(p'\) is nonzero because density forces \(p\)
nonconstant and the characteristic is zero. On the polynomial
generators of \(A\), \(T\) has coefficients of \(u\)-degree at most
two, and the coefficient on \(V\) has exact degree two. The Leibniz
rule therefore gives \(T(A)\subset A[u]_{\le2}\), with exact
degree bound two. As multiplication by the nonzero element
\(-cp'(I)\) does not affect the kernel of \(\partial_V\) in a
domain, the last equality of (14) follows.

This identifies the degree and intrinsic plane used in the retained
adjoint construction.[^3] The normalization in Section 6 only changes
the bad derivative by a nonzero constant factor: affine good changes
and bad shears by good polynomials preserve it. Replacing the original
global slice by \(X/c\) shifts that slice by an invariant; translation
in the slice variable preserves the exact polynomial degree on \(A\).
Consequently a successful primitive dense frame has adjoint degree
\(d=2\), never \(d=1\). In the normalized presentation its intrinsic
plane is exactly \(\mathbb C[I,EZ]\).

The earlier \(d=1\) alternative was a conditional identity, not an
existence theorem. This calculation excludes it on the successful
primitive side. The separate
[dense three-good cofactor argument](dense-three-good-adjoint-degree-pass132.md)
proves the broader exclusion under rank two and cone density alone,
without assuming a mixed relation or a successful correction. Thus
constructing a dense rank-two \(d=1\) frame is not a remaining target.
