# Quadratic sliced extensions: whole normal form and the repair gap

## 1. Scope and exact question

Work over C. Let R=C[x,y,z], let D be a locally nilpotent derivation
(LND) of R, and suppose that a,b are nonzero elements of ker D and
r belongs to R with Dr=a^2 b. Set B=R[t] and

    c=ab, d=a^2 b, E=D+(1+cr) partial_t,
    v=at-r^2/2, q=cv-r,
    u=t-brv+(ab^2/2)v^2.

The question here is whether this PARTICULAR u is always a coordinate
of B. A coordinate means an element in a full polynomial coordinate
system, not merely a slice or a smooth function.

This note proves a whole quadratic presentation of ker E, identifies
its inherited action and fixed scheme, and gives an exact criterion
for one proposed globalizing map. It also proves that globalizing
while fixing the whole old invariant ring can be impossible even
when u is a coordinate. It does NOT settle the question for arbitrary
rank-three D, let alone arbitrary cancellation. Novelty is UNKNOWN.

The actual ambient ring in this note is C^[4]. No polynomiality
assumption about the separate two-stage test cylinder671 is used.
The nonzero a,b assumptions exclude degenerate data. If b=0, then
Dr=0, E=D+partial_t and u=t, so recognition is immediate.

## 2. Whole slice and quotient maps

E is locally nilpotent: it restricts to D on R, and

    Et=1+cr, E^2t=a^3 b^2, E^3t=0.

Nilpotence on the finite algebra generators suffices by Leibniz.
Direct polynomial identities give

    Ev=a, Eq=0, v+q^2/2=au, Eu=1,
    r=du-q-cq^2/2.                                      (1)

The finite Taylor map pi(h)=sum_j (-u)^j E^j(h)/j! gives
K=ker E isomorphic to B/(u), and B=K[u]. In particular K is
finitely generated, a domain, and regular, since K[u] is regular.

There is a whole R-algebra isomorphism

    A := R[Q]/(F)  -->  B/(u),
    F=r+Q+cQ^2/2,       Q |--> q mod u.                  (2)

Its inverse fixes R and sends

    t |--> t0(Q)=bQ^3/2+ab^2 Q^4/8.                     (3)

Here are certificates of both directions, with no inversion of a,b,c.
Equation (1) gives F(q)=du, so (2) is well-defined. In A, substitute
r=-Q-cQ^2/2 and t=t0(Q). The resulting values are

    v=-Q^2/2, q=Q, u=0.

Conversely, the identity in B

    t-t0(q)=(1-cq-c^2 q^2/2)u+(a^3 b^2/2)u^2           (4)

shows that the other composition fixes t modulo u. Both maps fix R.
Thus they are mutual inverse maps on all algebra generators.

Under K isomorphic to A, the natural inclusion of R into A corresponds
to pi|R, not to the original non-invariant R inside B. Elements of
ker D are fixed by pi, so their identification is unchanged.

This is an exact stable-polynomial presentation: A[U] is C^[4].
It does not yet prove A=C^[3]. Even a separate proof A=C^[3] must
not silently be called a proof that F is a coordinate of R[Q].

## 3. The inherited action, kernel, and nonreduced fixed scheme

Write W=1+cQ. On A the formulas

    delta(h)=W D(h) for h in R,    delta(Q)=-d           (5)

define an LND. First delta(F)=Wd-dW=0. For the LND license, ambient
partial_t commutes with E and thus restricts to K. Its representative
on B/(u) is V=partial_t-(1+cq)E: indeed partial_t(u)=1+cq and Vu=0.
One has V(h)=-(1+cq)D(h) and V(q)=d. Therefore (5) is NEGATIVE
that induced LND, rather than an arbitrary multiple of D asserted
to be nilpotent. This proves local nilpotence without a degree guess.

The kernel is exactly the old invariant ring:

    ker_A delta = ker_R D.                              (6)

In fact ker(partial_t|K)=K intersect R=ker D in B, and the quotient
identification fixes each element of ker D.

Let J=(Dx,Dy,Dz) be the full ideal generated by D(R) in R. The ideal
of the fixed scheme of delta is

    J_delta=(WJ,d)A=JA.                                 (7)

The reverse inclusion in (7) follows from d=Dr in J and the exact
Bezout identity

    W(1-cQ)+bQ^2 d=1.                                  (8)

For every j in J, multiply (8) by j. The forward inclusion is immediate.
This uses ideals, not just common zeros.

More precisely, there is a natural R-algebra isomorphism

    A/J_delta isomorphic to R/J.                        (9)

In T=R/J one has d=0 and c^2=bd=0. Set Q0=-r-cr^2/2 in T.
Then F(Q0)=0 and

    F=(Q-Q0)(1+c(Q+Q0)/2) in T[Q].

The second factor is a unit, since its nonconstant summand is
square-zero. Evaluation at Q0 proves (9), including nilpotents.
Consequently this inherited action is fixed-point-free exactly when
D is. It does not manufacture a free action or a second invariant
direction from arbitrary rank-three input.

There is also a direct smoothness check: F_Q=W, D_R(F)=d, and (8)
puts 1 in the Jacobian ideal of F. D_R differentiates only R here.

## 4. Exact globality criterion for the uncorrected exponential

In B_a define

    L=D/a+br partial_t,  N=-vL,  Phi=exp(N).

L is locally nilpotent: D/a is locally nilpotent on R_a, and
L(t)=br, L^2(t)=ab^2, L^3(t)=0. Also Lv=0, so N is an LND.
Its finite exponential is an automorphism of B_a and Phi(t)=u.

The following conditions are equivalent:

    (i)   Phi(B) is contained in B;
    (ii)  Phi restricts to an automorphism of B;
    (iii) a divides r^2 D(x), r^2 D(y), and r^2 D(z) in R.

For (iii), note that

    N(h)=-tD(h)+(r^2/(2a))D(h) for h=x,y,z,
    N(t)=-brv.

Thus (iii) is precisely N(B) contained in B. Nilpotence descends
from B_a, and both exp(N) and exp(-N) then preserve B, proving (ii).
Condition (ii) implies (i).

For the remaining implication assume (i). For each generator h,
exp(lambda N)(h) is a polynomial of some finite degree n in lambda
with coefficients in B_a. Its values at the integers 0,...,n lie in
B, since exp(jN)=Phi^j preserves B for every nonnegative integer j.
The rational Vandermonde inverse recovers every coefficient in B.
In particular N(h) belongs to B. The displayed generator formulas
force (iii). No inverse regularity was assumed in this implication.

More generally put gamma=gcd(Dx,Dy,Dz), up to a nonzero constant.
Since D is nonzero, gamma is nonzero. Valuations show that (iii)
is equivalent to a dividing r^2 gamma, with all multiplicities retained.
If D is primitive, meaning gcd(Dx,Dy,Dz)=1, (iii) is equivalent to
a dividing r^2, by taking valuations at every irreducible factor of a.
In that case H=r^2/(2a) is already polynomial and E(t-H)=1 with
t-H an ambient coordinate. Thus this easy positive primitive case
also has a direct elementary recognition. Failure of (iii) excludes
ONLY the uncorrected Phi, not every possible completion of u.

A harmless normalization preserves the entire construction. If
e is a nonzero invariant with e^2 dividing a and e dividing r,
replace a,r,b by a/e^2,r/e,e^3 b. The quotients are invariant where
required, the same E and u result, v is divided by e^2, q by e,
and N is unchanged. The divisibility criterion is unchanged as well:
both a and r^2 are divided by e^2. Thus this normalization cannot
turn a genuine failure of (iii) into a success.

## 5. A whole obstruction to fixing the old invariant ring

For every integer m>=3 take

    R=C[x,y,z], D=x^2 partial_y+m y^(m-1) partial_z,
    a=x, b=1, r=y, g=x^2 z-y^m, C0=C[x,g].

D is a triangular LND and ker D=C0. To see the latter assertion,
invert x: R_x=C[x,x^-1,g,y] and D=x^2 partial_y. If an invariant
P(x,g)/x^n has a pole with P not divisible by x in C[x,g], its
residue P(0,-y^m) is nonzero. It cannot belong to R. This proves
the required x-saturation and the claimed kernel.

Here u IS a coordinate. In fact (x,q,z,u) are coordinates. For new
values (x,Q,z,U) put V'=xU-Q^2/2. The whole inverse is

    y=xV'-Q,  t=U-QV'+x(V')^2/2,

with x,z unchanged. Substitution in both directions verifies it.

Nevertheless NO automorphism of B fixing C0 pointwise can send t to u.
Such an automorphism would induce a C0-algebra isomorphism R to B/(u).
Using (2) and setting W0=-Q, the latter ring is C[x,W0,Z] with

    y=W0-xW0^2/2,  g=x^2 Z-(W0-xW0^2/2)^m.             (10)

Suppose a C0-isomorphism exists. After inverting x it is an
automorphism between one-variable polynomial rings over
C0_x=C[x,x^-1,g]. Hence the image of y is

    alpha W0+beta, alpha=lambda x^k,
    lambda in C*, k an integer, beta in C0_x.           (11)

Regularity forces k>=0 and beta in C0. Indeed a negative minimum
x-order cannot cancel: at x=0 its coefficient is either a nonzero
polynomial in -W0^m, a nonzero multiple of W0, or their sum.
The sum cannot vanish because W0 is not in C[W0^m]. Applying this
same argument to the inverse, whose linear coefficient is alpha^-1,
forces k<=0. Thus k=0.

Preserving g and reducing modulo x now gives

    (lambda W0+beta(0,-W0^m))^m=W0^m.

Every mth root of W0^m in C[W0] is a constant mth root of unity
times W0. Since beta(0,-W0^m) has no linear term, lambda^m=1 and
beta=x beta1 for a beta1 in C0. Preservation of g requires
(lambda W0+x beta1)^m-(W0-xW0^2/2)^m to be divisible by x^2.
Its first x-coefficient forces

    beta1(0,-W0^m)=-lambda W0^2/2.                     (12)

This is impossible for m>=3: the left side belongs to C[W0^m].
This contradiction proves the claimed all-degree obstruction.

In particular, a successful universal repair cannot consist only of
flows generated by D, partial_t, and their replicas or compositions
which fix C0, even if intermediate maps use invariant localizations.
A whole endpoint of that kind would fix C0 and contradict (12).
Allowing a change of old invariants is essential in these controls.
This does NOT obstruct unrestricted completion or cancellation.

## 6. Existing positive recognition and the remaining gap

If D fixes an actual polynomial coordinate h of R, E fixes h in
B=C[h]^[3]. K is regular by Section2. The published
Bhatwadekar--Gupta--Lokhande Theorem3.5 gives K=C[h]^[2]. Thus the
PARTICULAR u, together with h and two generators of K over C[h],
forms a whole coordinate system. This covers every rank<=2 base D;
it is an established theorem application, not a new result.[^BGL]

Another sufficient condition is ker D=C[h,k] and Dr in C[h]\{0}.
Daigle's coordinate-upgrade lemma makes h an actual R-coordinate,
so the preceding recognition applies. Merely having a,b algebraically
dependent does not supply this kernel-coordinate hypothesis.[^DG]

The rank-three DG family already recognized in595 remains closed.
Its pole repair uses an extra triangular action and changes an old
invariant. The present normal form does not reproduce that repair
for arbitrary D. The missing step is a whole global construction,
or another applicable recognition theorem, for the arbitrary rank-three
data outside the positive classes above. No existence, termination,
or universal invariant-coordinate claim is asserted.
