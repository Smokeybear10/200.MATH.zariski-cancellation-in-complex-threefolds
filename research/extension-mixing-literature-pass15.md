# Vertical extensions, invariant mixing, and the elementary-row boundary

This bounded primary-source audit did not locate a theorem recognizing the
kernel of every slice extension
\[
E=D+f\partial_t\quad\text{on }\mathbb C^{[3]}[t]
\]
when the base LND has rank three and \(f\) is arbitrary. This is a search
outcome, not a claim that the exact class is a published open problem.
The useful new boundary is exact: Cohn's original non-elementary matrix
obstructs extending a particular one-parameter Euclidean method to arbitrary
two-parameter coefficients. It does not obstruct general nonlinear conjugacy:
the direct matrix lift in Section 6 explicitly bypasses it in a substantial
congruence class, including the displayed Cohn-type example.

The comparison starts from
[extension-literature.md](extension-literature.md) and the updated
[commuting-coordinate-partial-pass.md](commuting-coordinate-partial-pass.md).
The latter already disproves a general original-frame gauge necessity
\(f\in\mathbb C^*+D(R)\), including for rank-three replicas.

## 1. The closest general extension theorem has extra hypotheses

In [Freudenburg, *Actions of SL2(k) on Affine k-Domains and Fundamental
Pairs*, Sections 7.1 and 7.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.12021), work over
characteristic zero. For a domain \(B\), nonzero LND \(D\),
\(A=\ker D\), put \(F_n=\ker D^{n+1}\), \(I_n=D^nF_n\).
Choose \(a\in A\setminus\{0\}\) and extend by \(D'u=a\).
Proposition 7.1 assumes
\[
aA\cap I_n=aI_n\qquad(n\ge0).
\]
It identifies \(\ker D'\) with the subalgebra generated by
\(\sum_{n\ge0}a^nF_n\), via the localized exponential
\(\exp(-uD/a)\). Polynomiality is not its conclusion.

Theorem 7.6 additionally assumes a fundamental pair \((D,U)\) and
\(a\in A_0\setminus\{0\}\), where the subscript is the induced grading.
It describes this kernel, makes the restricted \(\partial_u\) fundamental,
and, over an algebraically closed field, proves quotient surjectivity exactly
when \(a\in k^*\). Thus a slice forces a constant coefficient in this
specific setting. Arbitrary rank-three \(D\) and arbitrary \(f\) do not
satisfy these premises merely because \([E,\partial_t]=0\).

Access: full theorem and surrounding setup in the original arXiv v2 PDF,
printed pages 23 and 25. Publication: *Transformation Groups* 29 (2024),
959–1003.

## 2. Proper triangular actions: an actual triangular frame is required

[Dubouloz, Finston and Jaradat, *Proper triangular additive-group actions
on affine four-space are translations*](https://msp.org/ant/2014/8-8/ant-v8-n8-p06-p.pdf),
*Algebra & Number Theory* 8 (2014), 1959–1984, has the precise corollary:
over any characteristic-zero field, every proper triangular
\(\mathbb G_a\)-action on \(\mathbb A^4\) is a translation.
Its preceding corollary treats proper triangular actions on
\(\mathbb A^3_S\) for an affine Dedekind base \(S\).

A polynomial slice supplies properness: the action becomes translation on
\(\operatorname{Spec}(\ker E)\times\mathbb A^1\). It does not supply an
ambient triangular polynomial frame. In particular, the extension presentation
with a commuting coordinate partial does not turn a rank-three base derivation
into a triangular one. The source's broader 2014 status discussion is not a
current-status theorem for the extension subclass.

Access: original publisher PDF, main theorem and corollaries on printed
pages 1962–1963.

## 3. What the plinth ideal does, and does not, recognize

[Shakhmatov, *Cylinders and the zero locus of the plinth ideal*,
Theorem 2.6](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.00138v1), arXiv:2605.00138v1,
submitted April 30, 2026, works over an algebraically closed field of
characteristic zero. For an affine variety \(X\), an additive action with
LND \(\partial\), and nonzero \(h\in\mathbb C[X]\), the principal open
\(D_X(h)\) is an equivariant cylinder exactly when
\(h^n\in\operatorname{pl}(\partial)\) for some \(n\).
Corollary 2.7 identifies the complement of all principal equivariant cylinders
with the zero locus of the plinth ideal.

For a slice extension, \(\operatorname{pl}(E)=\ker E\), so this recovers
the known global cylinder, not the identification of its quotient with
\(\mathbb A^3\). Nor does it force the induced LND
\(\partial_t|_{\ker E}\) to have a slice. An abstract affine-plane common
kernel is not the same as a polynomial ambient coordinate pair.

Access: original arXiv HTML, Definitions 2.2–2.5, Theorem 2.6 and its proof,
and Corollaries 2.7–2.8.

## 4. One-parameter mixing is an elementary algorithm, not a sourced new theorem

Let \(D_0b=g\ne0\), \(D_0g=0\), with \(g\) nonconstant, and take
\[
E=A(g)D_0+B(g)\partial_t.
\]
The proposed polynomial transformations implement
\[
(A,B)\longmapsto(A-HB,B),\qquad
(A,B)\longmapsto(A,B-gKA),
\tag{1}
\]
with \(H,K\in\mathbb C[g]\). The first is conjugation by
\(\exp(tH(g)D_0)\). For the second use
\(\Psi=\exp(bK(g)\partial_t)\): since
\(\Psi D_0\Psi^{-1}=D_0-gK(g)\partial_t\), it gives exactly (1).
Both fix \(g\).

There is a direct degree descent. If \(\deg A\ge\deg B\), divide
\(A\) by \(B\) using the first operation. If \(\deg B>\deg A\),
divide \(B\) by \(gA\) using the second. Its remainder has degree at
most \(\deg A\); an equality is then handled by the first operation.
Thus the sum of degrees decreases until one coefficient is constant or zero.
For coprime \(A,B\), this gives:
\[
B(0)\ne0\ \Longrightarrow\ E\sim B(0)\partial_t,
\qquad
B(0)=0\ \Longrightarrow\ E\sim A(0)D_0.
\tag{2}
\]
Indeed, (1) preserves \(B(0)\), and in the second branch also \(A(0)\).
Constant terminal coefficients finish the reduction. If a common nonconstant
factor existed, it would divide every value of \(E\), excluding a slice.
If \(D_0\) has no slice on the base, its extension killing \(t\) has no
slice either, by evaluation at \(t=0\). Consequently (2) yields the stated
slice criterion for nonslice \(D_0\).

No directly matching published LND normal-form statement was verified in this
bounded search. These are explicit conjugations and a relative Euclidean
argument. Their elementary character is not evidence of originality.

## 5. Cohn's exact two-parameter obstruction

[P. M. Cohn, *On the structure of the GL2 of a ring*, Proposition 7.3
and the following example](https://www.numdam.org/item/PMIHES_1966__30__5_0.pdf),
*Publications Mathématiques de l'IHÉS* 30 (1966), 5–53,
[DOI 10.1007/BF02684355](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02684355), proves that
\[
M=\begin{pmatrix}1+xy&x^2\\-y^2&1-xy\end{pmatrix}
\in\mathrm{SL}_2(k[x,y])
\]
is not a product of elementary matrices. Its first row is unimodular but
not elementarily reducible to \((1,0)\).

For algebraically independent invariants \(g,h\), this applies in the
identified subring \(\mathbb C[g,h]\). Swapping and changing signs with
constant elementary matrices gives the same obstruction for
\((g^2,1+gh)\). The connection with the extension problem is concrete:
\[
E=g^2D_0+(1+gh)\partial_t,
\qquad
s=(1-gh+g^2h^2)t-h^3b
\]
satisfies \(E(s)=1\), because
\((1-gh+g^2h^2)(1+gh)-g^3h^3=1\).

This blocks elementary coefficient-row reduction over \(\mathbb C[g,h]\),
including the restricted operations (1). It does not establish a conjugacy
obstruction for the derivation. In particular, elementary reducibility can
change after passing to the larger invariant ring \(\ker D_0\), and general
polynomial automorphisms need not act by these coefficient-row operations.
No nontranslation or cancellation counterexample follows.

Access: original Numdam journal PDF, printed page 26. The matrix and the
non-elementarity conclusion are explicit there, not inferred from a later
survey. The row consequence also follows directly: if \(MP\) had first row
\((1,0)\) for elementary \(P\), its determinant would make \(MP\)
elementary lower triangular, contradicting Cohn's conclusion.

## 6. The direct matrix lift bypasses the elementary-word obstruction

The concurrent constructive argument in
[extension-mixing-pass15.md](extension-mixing-pass15.md) gives a stronger
method than elementary words. Here is an independent algebraic check, not an
attribution to Cohn or the extension papers.

Write \(R=\mathbb C^{[3]}\), \(C=\ker D_0\), \(D_0b=g\ne0\), and
take \(A,B,L,M\in C\) with
\[
B\equiv1\pmod{gC},\qquad BM+gAL=1.
\]
Then \(M\equiv1\pmod{gC}\). Define finite flow evaluation by
\(\mathcal F_W(r)=\sum_{j\ge0}W^jD_0^j(r)/j!\), and set
\[
\begin{aligned}
W&=\frac{B-1}{g}b-At,&
\Phi(r)&=\mathcal F_W(r),&\Phi(t)&=Lb+Mt,\\
W'&=\frac{M-1}{g}b+At,&
\Psi(r)&=\mathcal F_{W'}(r),&\Psi(t)&=-Lb+Bt.
\end{aligned}
\]
These are polynomial homomorphisms. In the local-slice presentation
\(R_g[t]=C_g[b,t]\), their matrices on \((b,t)\) are
\[
\begin{pmatrix}B&-gA\\L&M\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\begin{pmatrix}M&gA\\-L&B\end{pmatrix}.
\]
They are inverse matrices of determinant one. Injectivity of localization
therefore proves \(\Phi\Psi=\Psi\Phi=\mathrm{id}\) already on \(R[t]\).
For \(E=AD_0+B\partial_t\), one has \(EW=-A\), so the chain rule gives
\(E\Phi(r)=0\), while \(E\Phi(t)=1\). Hence \(\Phi\) supplies
polynomial invariant coordinates and a coordinate slice.

The distinction between flow evaluation and an exponential is essential:
\(\mathcal F_W\) is generally not \(\exp(WD_0)\), because
\(D_0W=B-1\) need not vanish.

Under \(B\equiv1\pmod{gC}\), a slice also forces \((A,B)=C\).
Modulo \(B\), the element \(g\) is invertible, so the localized-slice
presentation identifies \(R[t]/(B)\) with
\((C/(B))[b,t]\). There \(E=Ag\partial_b\), and a slice forces
\(Ag\), hence \(A\), to be a unit in \(C/(B)\). Conversely this
unimodularity gives \(BM+gAL=1\), hence the lift. If \(B\) is a unit,
the converse construction is immediate without taking its quotient.

For the example in Section 5 choose
\(A=g^2\), \(B=1+gh\), \(M=1-gh+g^2h^2\), \(L=-h^3\).
Thus its coordinate-slice conclusion is now explicit even though the
coefficient row has no elementary reduction over \(\mathbb C[g,h]\).

A bounded follow-up searched local-slice matrix lifting, invariant-ring
congruences, \(\mathrm{SL}_2\) lifts, and Cohn/Nagata or Vénéreau flow
constructions. No directly matching primary lifting theorem was verified.
Related terminology alone does not establish overlap or originality.

## 7. Coverage and source exclusions

The bounded searches combined vertical extensions, commuting coordinate
partials, rank-three base actions, invariant coefficients, replicas,
stable triangulability, plinth ideals, unimodular rows and elementary mixing.
Publisher abstracts for older triangulability and centralizer papers were
not promoted into precise extension theorems without accessible statements.
The four directly checked primary sources above do not imply the arbitrary
rank-three extension closure.

The survey arXiv:2512.15590v2 is excluded as a mathematical or status authority.
The separate source audit found its Example 2.6 assertion about complex
Russell–Koras cylinders incompatible with its own introduction. No theorem
from that survey is used here. This exclusion and the bounded search limits
do not establish either novelty or a current named open problem.
