# Actual field return forces the quartic weight-four criterion

Status: working proof pending independent review of this saved artifact.
This note closes the quartic graph branch under the retained actual cusp
hypotheses. It does not cover lower graph degrees, other weighted initial
kernels, or unrestricted coordinate entry. No historical novelty claim
is made.

## 1. Actual hypotheses and the normalized coefficient field

Retain the actual four-coordinate cusp setup of
[the generic chart159](cusp-generic-chart-first-reductions-pass159-working.md)
and the quartic graph hypotheses of
[the coefficient-rigidity proof163](cusp-quartic-graph-coefficient-rigidity-pass163-working.md).
Thus \(B=A[u]=\mathbb C[X,Y,Z,W]\), \(E=\partial_u\), the
exact time degrees are \((1,1,2,2)\), and the full weighted initial
kernel is \((XZ-Y^3)\). In the selected chart, \(a=EX\ne0\),
\(N=[u^3](\partial_Z|_A)\), \(S=\ker_A N\), and
\(L=\operatorname{Frac}S\). The projected \(y\) is an affine
parameter in the whole generic line \(A_L=L[v]\), and
\(\vartheta=P(y)\) with \(\deg P=4\).

Use only the unconditional actual constant changes in Section 2 of
[the weight-four criterion165](cusp-quartic-weight-four-sufficiency-pass165-working.md).
They normalize the literal coefficients to
\[
\begin{gathered}
r=1/t,\qquad \lambda=1/t^2,\qquad n_\rho=0,\\
k=\rho/t+k_0,\qquad n=n_0,\qquad
\ell=-\rho/t^2+\ell_0,\qquad k_0,n_0,\ell_0\in K,\\
K:=\ker_L\partial_\rho,
\quad [K:\mathbb C(t)]<\infty,
\quad \ker_K\partial_t=\mathbb C.
\end{gathered}
\tag{1}
\]
These normalizations do not presume the polynomial condition later
proved here. They have actual weighted polynomial inverses, and do not
divide an original-ring element by \(a\).

The exact field-return result from
[the coefficient-field proof164](cusp-coefficient-field-and-contracted-divisors-pass164-working.md)
gives
\[
L=K(\rho),\qquad
\operatorname{Frac}A=\mathbb C(y,z,w)=L(y)=K(\rho)(y).
\tag{2}
\]
In particular \(\rho\) is transcendental over \(K\), and \(y\)
is transcendental over \(L\). No assertion that \(K=\mathbb C(t)\)
is being made. The equality with \(\mathbb C(y,z,w)\) is the
actual polynomial-inverse input. Replacing it by a proper subfield
would invalidate the proof below.

We prove that \(tk_0\in\mathbb C[t]\) has degree at most four.
The criterion165 then supplies an actual invariant coordinate of
original weight four, and its stated regular-kernel theorem gives
\(A\simeq\mathbb C^{[3]}\) in this branch.

## 2. What the lower equations say before polynomiality

A prime denotes \(\partial_t\) on \(K\). Write
\(P=p_4U^4+p_3U^3+p_2U^2+p_1U+p_0\).
The unrestricted normalized equations in163 give
\[
\begin{gathered}
p_4=-t,\qquad
p_3=-t^2k_0'''/6-tk_0''/2,\qquad
p_2=-t^2n_0''/2-tn_0',\\
p_1=\rho/t+b_0,\quad b_0=t\ell_0+t^2\ell_0',\qquad
p_0=h\rho+d_0,\quad h,d_0\in K,\\
p_3'-p_3/t-4th=0,\qquad
p_2'-p_2/t+3p_3h+3t=0.
\end{gathered}
\tag{3}
\]
These are literal coefficients of the actual chart, not free generic
data. Define \(\delta=p_3/(4t)\). The first lower equation gives
\(h=\delta'\). The next gives
\((p_2/t)'=-12\delta\delta'-3\), so
\[
h=\delta',\qquad
p_2/t=-6\delta^2-3t+\Lambda,
\qquad \Lambda\in\mathbb C.
\tag{4}
\]
The integration uses only that a derivative-zero element of \(K\)
is complex. In particular no antiderivative for an arbitrary element
of \(K\) is assumed.

Put \(q=\rho/t\). The two projected original coordinates are
affine in this variable:
\[
\begin{gathered}
z=Z_0+hq,\qquad
w=yZ_0+V_0+(yh-1)q,\\
Z_0=-y^4+4\delta y^3+(-6\delta^2+\Lambda)y^2
       +(k_0'+b_0/t)y+n_0+d_0/t,\\
V_0=t\ell_0-4ty^3-(tk_0''/2+k_0')y^2
                         -(tn_0'+n_0)y.
\end{gathered}
\tag{5}
\]
To check (5), use the actual formulas
\(z=3ty^2+k_ty+P(y)/t+n_0\) and
\(w=t\ell-ty^3-tk_{tt}y^2/2+(y/t)P(y)-tn_0'y\).
The \(\rho\) contributions in \(k_t+tk_{tt}/2\) cancel.
Thus \(w-yz=-q+V_0\), while (3)--(4) give the formula for
\(z\). Every coefficient of \(Z_0,V_0\) lies in \(K\).

The equation of this family of lines is
\[
G:=(1-yh)z+hw=Z_0+hV_0\in K[y],\qquad
[y^4]G=-1,\qquad [y^3]G=4(\delta-th).
\tag{6}
\]
Only the displayed cubic coefficient will be needed. In particular
the unprescribed coefficient \(d_0\) is retained throughout.

## 3. An elementary pencil-degree lemma

Let \(k\) be a field and \(M=k(f,g)\) a field of transcendence
degree one over \(k\), with \(f\) transcendental over \(k\).
Let \(Z\) be transcendental over \(M\), and put \(W=fZ+g\).
If \(R(S,T)\in k[S,T]\) is an irreducible generator of the full
relation ideal of \((f,g)\), then
\[
[M(Z):k(Z,W)]=\deg R,
\tag{7}
\]
where \(\deg R\) is total degree. Hence equality of these two
fields forces \(g\) to be affine in \(f\) over \(k\).

Here is an elementary proof, including the generic projection step.
The relation ideal has height one, so it is generated by such an
irreducible \(R\). The elements \(Z,W\) are algebraically
independent over \(k\): if a nonzero \(Q(Z,W)\) vanished, and
\(Q_d\) were its highest total-degree part, the coefficient of
\(Z^d\) in \(Q(Z,fZ+g)\) would be \(Q_d(1,f)\ne0\), since
\(f\) is transcendental.

The polynomial \(R(S,T)\) stays irreducible on adjoining \(Z\).
The substitution \(T\mapsto W-ZS\) is a polynomial variable
change over \(k\), with inverse \(W\mapsto T+ZS\). Therefore
\(R(S,W-ZS)\) is irreducible in \(k[Z,W,S]\).
If \(R_d\) is its original highest homogeneous part, its leading
coefficient as a polynomial in \(S\) is
\(R_d(1,-Z)\ne0\), and its \(S\)-degree is exactly \(d\).
Gauss's lemma now makes it the irreducible polynomial of \(f\)
over \(k(Z,W)\), up to a field unit. Since
\(g=W-fZ\), one has \(M(Z)=k(Z,W)(f)\). This proves (7).
If \(d=1\), the coefficient of \(T\) in \(R\) is nonzero:
otherwise \(f\) would be algebraic over \(k\). Solving that
linear relation proves the final assertion.

This argument uses a full relation ideal and an exact field degree.
It does not infer degree one from a nonzero Jacobian or from a formal
determinant equation.

## 4. Applying actual field return

Suppose, for contradiction, that \(h\notin\mathbb C\). In
particular \(h\ne0\). Set \(k=\mathbb C(y)\) for this section,
and define elements of \(M:=K(y)\) by
\[
f=y-1/h,\qquad g=G/h.
\tag{8}
\]
There is no conflict with the symbol \(k\) for a literal chart
coefficient in (1); in this section lower-case \(k\) denotes only
the base field \(\mathbb C(y)\).
The element \(h\) is transcendental over \(\mathbb C\), and
\(y\) is transcendental over \(K\). Thus \(f\) is
transcendental over \(k\). The field \(M/k\) has transcendence
degree one.

By (5), \(z\) is transcendental over \(M\), since
\(q\) is and \(h\ne0\). Equations (2), (5), and (6) give
\[
w=fz+g,\qquad
k(z,w)=K(y)(q)=M(z).
\tag{9}
\]
It is also essential to establish the full coefficient field in the
pencil lemma. The inclusions
\(k(z,w)\subset k(f,g)(z)\subset M(z)\) are equalities by
(9). Intersecting with \(M\) gives \(M=k(f,g)\).
For justification, for any subfield \(F\subset M\) and a variable
\(z\) transcendental over \(M\), one has
\(F(z)\cap M=F\): writing an element of the intersection as a
ratio of polynomials in \(z\) and comparing one nonzero coefficient
gives the claim.

The pencil lemma therefore applies to the exact full coefficient
field, and (9) forces its degree to be one. Consequently
\(g\in k+kf\). Multiplying this relation by \(h\) gives
\[
G\in\mathbb C(y)+h\mathbb C(y).
\tag{10}
\]
Since \(G\in K[y]\), every coefficient of this polynomial lies
in the complex span of \(1,h\). To see this without a rationality
assumption on \(K\), take a finite complex basis for the span of
\(1,h\) and the coefficients of \(G\), beginning with \(1,h\).
Its members remain independent over \(\mathbb C(y)\): clear a
common denominator in \(\mathbb C[y]\), then compare powers of
the transcendental \(y\). Comparing in this basis in (10) proves
the stated coefficient assertion.

In particular, (6) gives complex constants \(\alpha,\beta\) with
\[
\delta-th=\alpha+\beta h.
\tag{11}
\]
Differentiate inside \(K\). Since \(\delta'=h\), equation (11)
gives \(-th'=\beta h'\). The element \(t+\beta\) is nonzero,
so \(h'=0\), which implies \(h\in\mathbb C\). This contradicts
the supposition. We conclude that \(h\) is complex, including
the possible value zero.

## 5. The coefficient criterion and quartic recognition

Write \(h\in\mathbb C\). Since \(\delta'=h\), there is
\(j\in\mathbb C\) with \(\delta=ht+j\). Its definition in
(3) is now the exact Euler equation
\[
tk_0'''+3k_0''=-24ht-24j,
\qquad
\bigl(t^3(k_0''+6ht+8j)\bigr)'=0.
\tag{12}
\]
Thus \(k_0''=-6ht-8j+c_*/t^3\), with \(c_*\in\mathbb C\).
Subtract the explicit second primitive
\(-ht^3-4jt^2+c_*/(2t)\). The remaining second derivative is
zero in \(K\), so the remainder is \(bt+c\), with \(b,c\)
complex. Therefore
\[
k_0=-ht^3-4jt^2+bt+c+q_*/t,
\qquad tk_0\in\mathbb C[t],\quad \deg(tk_0)\le4.
\tag{13}
\]
This uses only exact constants and explicit primitives. It does not
integrate arbitrary algebraic functions or identify \(K\) with
\(\mathbb C(t)\).

The actual inverse changes of165 preserve the coefficient criterion,
so in the original, unnormalized notation
\((A\tau+B)k_0\in\mathbb C[\tau]\) has degree at most four.
The complete sufficient construction165 now applies. It produces an
invariant coordinate \(I\in S\) of original weight four with a
whole-ring polynomial inverse. Its stated application of the retained
Bhatwadekar--Gupta--Lokhande regular-kernel theorem then gives
\(A\simeq\mathbb C[I]^{[2]}\simeq\mathbb C^{[3]}\).

This recognizes every actual quartic graph chart under the assumptions
of Section 1. No additional condition on the locus \(a=0\) is
needed for this branch. This is not a claim that all cusp charts are
quartic graphs, that the lower-degree branches have been recognized,
or that the other \((1,1,2,2)\) initial configurations or unrestricted
four-coordinate entry problem are resolved.

## 6. A separate formal index-eight boundary

The following is not an actual frame. It illustrates why the field
equality (2) is indispensable, even when the normalized lower
determinant equations hold. Take \(K=\mathbb C(t)\), with
independent \(\rho,y\), and put
\[
\begin{gathered}
k_0=-2t^4/5,\quad n_0=2t^5/5+t^2,\quad
\ell_0=4t^6/7+7t^3/5,\quad d_0=0,\quad\Lambda=0,\\
\delta=t^2,\quad h=2t,\quad
p_3=4t^3,\quad p_2=-6t^5-3t^2,\quad
b_0=4t^7+28t^4/5.
\end{gathered}
\tag{14}
\]
These data satisfy all three lower equations in163; their remaining
constant expression is
\(\mathcal S=2\rho+8t^8+66t^5/5+2t^2\ne0\).
Form \(z,w\) by (5). The polynomial from (6) is
\[
G=-y^4-4t^2y^3+2t^4y^2-(4t^6/5+2t^3)y
                 +8t^8/7+16t^5/5+t^2.
\]
For the pencil functions \(f=y-1/(2t)\), \(g=G/(2t)\), set
\(v_0=y-f=1/(2t)\). Then
\[
\begin{gathered}
v_0^7g=P_8(v_0),\\
P_8(V)=-y^4V^8+(1/4-y^3)V^6-(y/4)V^5
 +(y^2/8)V^4+V^3/10-(y/80)V^2+1/224.
\end{gathered}
\tag{15}
\]
Over \(\mathbb C(y)\), the relation
\((y-S)^7T-P_8(y-S)\) is primitive and irreducible: its two
coefficients as a linear polynomial in \(T\) are coprime, since
\(P_8(0)=1/224\). Its total degree is eight, and
\(t=1/[2(y-f)]\) shows that \(f,g\) generate the full coefficient
field \(\mathbb C(y)(t)\). The pencil lemma gives the exact
index
\([\mathbb C(t,\rho,y):\mathbb C(y,z,w)]=8\).
These data therefore fail the actual field-return condition, rather
than supplying an actual counterexample to Section 5.
