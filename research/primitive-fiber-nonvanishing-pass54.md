# A primitive derivation cannot vanish on a whole flat quotient fiber

Let \(A\) be the coordinate ring of a smooth factorial complex
threefold, and let \(D\ne0\) be a primitive locally nilpotent
derivation with polynomial kernel \(C=\mathbb C[u,v]\). Assume
\(C\subset A\) is faithfully flat. Then the derivation induced by
\(D\) on every whole scheme-theoretic fiber is nonzero.

This conclusion does not hold for the reduced fiber. Nilpotent
directions can carry the entire nonzero action. The proof uses the
classical characteristic-zero Jacobian socle theorem on a transverse
Artinian complete intersection, with all local and residue-field
steps retained below. No global canonical trivialization or
recognition of \(A\) as a polynomial ring is required.

In the actual reduced zero-first-jet sector, the result combines with
the pass53 whole-fiber velocity calculation to prove
\(R[F_5]\ne A\otimes_C R\). It does not exclude the obstruction
point or settle cancellation.

## 1. Primitivity means denominator saturation

Choose finite algebra generators \(x_1,\ldots,x_N\) of \(A\).
In the UFD \(A\), primitivity of the LND implies
\[
\gcd\bigl(Dx_1,\ldots,Dx_N\bigr)=1
\quad\text{up to a unit}.
\tag{1}
\]
Here zero values can be omitted. To verify the implication even when
primitivity is defined as the absence of an invariant polynomial
replica factor, let \(\delta\) be that gcd. The Leibniz rule gives
\(D(A)\subseteq\delta A\), hence \(D\delta\in\delta A\).
For an LND in characteristic zero, \(Df\in fA\) implies \(Df=0\):
if \(Df=fq\ne0\), the additive LND degree of the right side is at
least that of \(f\), whereas the left side has degree one less.
Thus \(D\delta=0\), and \(D/\delta\) is again an LND of \(A\).
Primitivity forces \(\delta\) to be a unit.

Consequently, for every prime \(\mathfrak q\) of \(A\), if
\(h\in\operatorname{Frac}A\) and \(hD\) preserves \(A_{\mathfrak q}\),
then
\[
h\in A_{\mathfrak q}.
\tag{2}
\]
Indeed any irreducible denominator in the localized UFD would divide
every \(Dx_i\) there, contrary to (1). Primitivity here is not the
stronger assertion that the coefficient ideal is the unit ideal.

## 2. The precise transverse Jacobian lemma

Let \(K\) be a field of characteristic zero, \(S\) an essentially
smooth local \(K\)-algebra of dimension two, with residue field finite
over \(K\). Let \(a,b\) be a system of parameters. Then
\[
T=S/(a,b)
\]
is a nonzero finite-dimensional local \(K\)-algebra. It is an
Artinian complete intersection. Choose a basis \(dx,dy\) of the
free rank-two module \(\Omega_{S/K}\), and write
\[
da=a_xdx+a_ydy,\qquad db=b_xdx+b_ydy,
\qquad \Delta=a_xb_y-a_yb_x.
\tag{3}
\]
The key assertion is
\[
\Delta\notin(a,b)S.
\tag{4}
\]

The conormal presentation shows that the zeroth Fitting ideal of
\(\Omega_{T/K}\) is generated by the image of \(\Delta\).
Suppose that image were zero. Extend \(K\) to an algebraic closure
\(\bar K\). The nonzero finite algebra \(T\otimes_K\bar K\)
is a product of Artinian local complete intersections. For any factor,
localize the corresponding base-changed smooth surface and complete
its regular local ring. Its completion is
\(\bar K[[X,Y]]\), and its Artinian quotient is unchanged by
completion. Thus the factor is a quotient of that power-series ring
by two parameters.

The classical socle theorem says that its Jacobian determinant
generates its nonzero socle. Fitting ideals commute with base change
and localization, so a zero original Fitting ideal cannot become this
nonzero ideal. This proves (4).

The socle input is Eisenbud--Ulrich, *Duality and socle generators for
residual intersections*, arXiv:1309.2050v4, Appendix Section 8,
Theorem 8.1, printed pp. 40--41. Its complete argument was directly
read. It identifies the Noether different with the socle using the
trace pairing; for a complete intersection the different is the
Jacobian ideal. Characteristic zero makes the trace of the identity
nonzero. The authors explicitly present this as a classical result,
not a new theorem of their paper.[^socle]

The scalar-extension formulation avoids presuming that the original
residue field equals \(K\), or that a chosen coefficient field in a
completion fixes the original \(K\). The power-series step uses the
directly read coefficient-compatible statement in Stacks 0C0S(2);
Fitting base change is Stacks 07ZA(3).[^formal]

## 3. Constructing the transverse surface at an actual fiber component

Fix a closed point \(\mathfrak m=(u-\alpha,v-\beta)\) of
\(\operatorname{Spec}C\), and put
\[
a=u-\alpha,\qquad b=v-\beta.
\]
Take any prime \(\mathfrak q\) minimal over \(\mathfrak mA\).
Flatness preserves the regular sequence \(a,b\). The height theorem
and regularity therefore give
\[
\operatorname{ht}\mathfrak q=2,\qquad
S=A_{\mathfrak q}\text{ regular local of dimension }2,
\quad (a,b)S\text{ is primary to its maximal ideal}.
\tag{5}
\]
The component field \(F=\operatorname{Frac}(A/\mathfrak q)\)
has transcendence degree one over \(\mathbb C\). Choose an actual
\(t\in A\) whose residue is transcendental over \(\mathbb C\).
Then every nonzero polynomial in \(t\) is outside \(\mathfrak q\),
so
\[
K=\mathbb C(t)\subset S,
\qquad [F:K]<\infty.
\tag{6}
\]
The finite extension is separable because the characteristic is zero.

The ring \(S\) is essentially of finite type and regular over the
perfect field \(K\), hence essentially smooth over \(K\).
Equivalently, the residue differential of \(t\) is nonzero in
\(\Omega_{F/\mathbb C}\); the smooth threefold's differential
criterion makes \(t\) a smooth relative parameter at this point.
Thus \(\Omega_{S/K}\) is free of rank two. Choose \(x,y\in S\)
whose differentials give a basis. This is possible by choosing two
members of a finite differential generating set which are independent
modulo the maximal ideal and applying Nakayama's lemma.

The transitivity sequence of differentials then gives a basis
\(dt,dx,dy\) for \(\Omega_{S/\mathbb C}\). In particular
\[
\omega=dt\wedge dx\wedge dy
\tag{7}
\]
is a local volume form. These facts require no rational section of
the component or of the quotient. The relevant smoothness and
differential criterion was directly read in Stacks 29.35.14.[^smooth]

## 4. The Jacobian field is a regular multiple of the primitive field

Contraction with the local generator (7) defines a unique regular
\(\mathbb C\)-derivation \(J\) of \(S\) by
\[
\iota_J\omega=da\wedge db.
\tag{8}
\]
This field kills \(a,b\). Over \(\operatorname{Frac}A\), the
space of derivations killing these two algebraically independent
elements has dimension one. Since \(D\ne0\) also kills them,
\[
J=hD\quad\text{for some }h\in\operatorname{Frac}A.
\]
The regularity of \(J\) and (2) give \(h\in S\).

Wedge (8) with \(dt\). With the orientation in (7),
\[
da\wedge db\wedge dt=J(t)\,\omega,
\qquad J(t)=a_xb_y-a_yb_x=\Delta.
\tag{9}
\]
The coefficients on the right are relative to \(K=\mathbb C(t)\).
The calculation does not assert that \(D\) or \(J\) is
\(K\)-linear. In fact \(J(t)\) is exactly the quantity being tested.
The relative differentials are only a way to compute that element.

Applying (4) to (5)--(6) shows that \(J(t)\notin(a,b)S\).
Since \(h\in S\), it follows that
\[
D(t)\notin(a,b)S.
\tag{10}
\]
Thus the derivation is nonzero on the local scheme of the fiber at
the generic point of **each** component. In particular
\[
\boxed{\quad
\bar D\ne0\text{ on }A/\mathfrak mA
\text{ for every closed }\mathfrak m\subset C.
\quad}
\tag{11}
\]
No reducedness of that ring or its components was used. Nonvanishing
of the socle class is exactly what prevents discarding nilpotents.

For completeness the assertion also holds on the other scheme
fibers of \(\operatorname{Spec}A\to\operatorname{Spec}C\).
On the generic fiber \(D\) remains nonzero. At a height-one base
prime \((\pi)\), localize by \(C\setminus(\pi)\). If the
induced derivation modulo \(\pi\) were zero, every generator value
would be divisible by the nonunit \(\pi\) in this localized UFD,
contrary to (1). Faithful flatness makes this fiber nonempty and
keeps \(\pi\) a nonunit. These three heights exhaust the base plane.

The cylinder assumption is one source of the required smooth
factorial threefold, but is not an extra ingredient of this proof.
Likewise, local nilpotence is used to translate primitive LND into
(1); the subsequent local argument only needs that saturation and
the vertical derivation condition.

## 5. Why the whole scheme fiber is essential

Reuse the classical polynomial example
\[
A_0=\mathbb C[x,y,z],\qquad
D_0=x\partial_y+2y\partial_z,
\qquad C_0=\mathbb C[x,h],\quad h=xz-y^2.
\tag{12}
\]
It is primitive. On the fiber \((x,h)=(0,0)\),
\[
A_0/(x,h)=\mathbb C[y,z]/(y^2),
\qquad \bar D_0(z)=2y\ne0.
\tag{13}
\]
The reduced fiber is \(\mathbb C[z]\), and its induced derivation
is zero. Transversely over \(K=\mathbb C(z)\), the parameters
\(x,xz-y^2\) have Jacobian \(-2y\), the nonzero socle class of
\(K[y]/(y^2)\). This checks the sign and the precise nilpotent
mechanism in (9), without claiming a new example.

Primitivity cannot be omitted. The replica \(xD_0\) has the same
kernel and quotient morphism but induces the zero derivation on the
entire fiber in (13). Smoothness of the total space and faithful
flatness alone would not exclude that replica.

## 6. Consequence for the entire quintic algebra

In the actual independent degree-two frame program, the smooth
factorial threefold, primitive \(D\), polynomial kernel and faithful
flatness are the inherited quotient conclusions, independently audited
in [pass51](intrinsic-quotient-foundation-audit-pass51.md). No
assumption that \(A=\mathbb C^{[3]}\) is added here.

Assume the additional local zero-first-jet sector
\[
R=C_{\mathfrak m},\quad\mathfrak n=\mathfrak mR,
\quad JR=\mathfrak n,\quad B_1,B_2\in\mathfrak n^2,
\quad\mathcal A=A\otimes_C R.
\]
The complete
[pass53 velocity proof](quintic-fiber-velocity-pass53.md), reread in
full for this application, establishes
\[
D(F_5)\subseteq\mathfrak nF_4.
\tag{14}
\]
It follows by the Leibniz rule that \(\bar D\) kills the image of
the whole algebra \(K_5=R[F_5]\) in
\(\mathcal A/\mathfrak n\mathcal A\). Equation (11) shows that it
does not kill the entire actual fiber. Therefore
\[
\boxed{\quad R[F_5]\ne\mathcal A.\quad}
\tag{15}
\]
Every generating set of \(\mathcal A\) over \(R\) needs an element
of intrinsic \(D\)-degree at least six. This conclusion requires
no presentation or fiber-dimension computation for \(K_5\), and
no assumption on the length of the earlier finite algebra \(S_0\).

The [pass53 saturation criterion](quintic-saturation-gate-pass53.md)
then also implies that \((a,b)\) is not a regular sequence on
\(K_5\), although it is regular on the actual \(\mathcal A\).
All these statements retain the zero-first-jet and full actual
filtration hypotheses. They do not construct or exclude such an
obstruction point, prove sixth-degree generation, finish the entire
\((1,1,2,2)\) profile, or supply a universal coordinate-frame bound.

## Sources and verification scope

The characteristic-zero socle theorem is the published input, not a
novelty claim. Its complete Appendix proof, the cited Stacks power
series and Fitting statements and proofs, and the relevant full
smoothness criterion were directly read. The transfer to the actual
fiber is the explicit local argument in Sections 1--4. It does not
invoke an assumed coefficient field, a global volume, a reduced-fiber
substitution, or generic-fiber polynomiality as a global recognition
theorem. No new numerical suite, full regression, metadata change,
human expert review or formal proof certification is claimed here.
All files from pass53 and earlier remain unchanged.
