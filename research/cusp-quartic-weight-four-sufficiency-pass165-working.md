# Quartic cusp charts: an exact weight-four criterion

Status: complete working proof under the retained actual-chart hypotheses,
pending independent review of this saved artifact. This is a conditional
criterion inside the quartic graph branch, not an unrestricted recognition
or entry theorem. No historical novelty claim is made.

## 1. The actual hypotheses and the criterion

Work over \(\mathbb C\). Retain the actual setup of the
[cusp chart159](cusp-generic-chart-first-reductions-pass159-working.md):
\(B=A[u]=\mathbb C[X,Y,Z,W]\), \(E=\partial_u\), with an actual
polynomial inverse, exact \(E\)-time degrees \((1,1,2,2)\), and the
full weighted initial kernel generated by \(XZ-Y^3\). The original
weights throughout this note are \((1,1,2,2)\). They are not the
\(E\)-time degree of an arbitrary polynomial, or a minimum over all
coordinate frames.

Put \(a=EX\ne0\), and use the selected intrinsic derivation
\(N=[u^3](\partial_Z|_A)\), its kernel \(S\), and
\(L=\operatorname{Frac}S\). The actual chart supplies independent
\(\tau,\rho\), a finite extension \(L/\mathbb C(\tau,\rho)\),
commuting coefficient derivatives, and \(A_L=L[v]\). Its joint
coefficient constants are \(\mathbb C\).

Assume the surviving quartic graph case of
[the coefficient-rigidity proof163](cusp-quartic-graph-coefficient-rigidity-pass163-working.md):
the projected \(y\) is an affine parameter over \(L\), and
\(\vartheta=P(y)\) with \(\deg P=4\). The literal coefficients obey
\[
\begin{gathered}
r=k_\rho=\frac1{A\tau+B},\qquad
\lambda=D r^2,\qquad n_\rho=C r^2,\\
A,D\in\mathbb C^*,\quad B,C\in\mathbb C,\qquad
k=r\rho+k_0,\quad k_0\in K:=\ker_L\partial_\rho .
\end{gathered}
\tag{1}
\]
Here \(A\) in (1) is a complex constant, not the invariant ring when
it occurs without a subscript in a coefficient formula. No rationality
of \(K\) is assumed.

The criterion is
\[
\begin{split}
&\text{there is a nonconstant original-weight-at-most-four element
 of }\ker E\\
&\hspace{15mm}\Longleftrightarrow\quad
(A\tau+B)k_0\in\mathbb C[\tau],\qquad
\deg_\tau\bigl((A\tau+B)k_0\bigr)\le4 .
\end{split}
\tag{2}
\]
The zero polynomial is allowed on the right. When (2) holds, the proof
constructs an invariant of exact weight four which is an actual
polynomial coordinate of \(B\). In fact it belongs to \(S\).

Necessity is the coefficient calculation in
[the weight-four coordinate gate164](cusp-weight-four-coordinate-gate-pass164-working.md).
Briefly, there is no nonconstant invariant of weight at most three
because \(r\) is nonconstant, by
[the low-weight obstruction162](cusp-low-weight-invariant-parameter-obstruction-pass162-working.md).
The full initial kernel then makes a weight-four invariant have top
\((\mu X+\nu Y)(XZ-Y^3)\), with complex scalars \(\mu,\nu\).
Its cubic-in-\(X\) coefficient, followed by
\(\partial_\rho\), gives the normalized necessary equation
\((A\tau+B)k_0=(\alpha+\beta\tau)\tau^3+Q_3(1,\tau)\).
This proves the right side of (2). We prove sufficiency below without
using the Hessian or normality endpoint of that gate.

## 2. Normalization by actual weighted polynomial changes

First consider nonzero complex \(\xi,\eta,\omega\), and a complex
\(\theta\). Define
\[
\begin{gathered}
X'=\xi X,\qquad Y'=\eta Y+\theta X,\qquad W'=\omega W,\\
Z'=\frac{\eta^3Z+3\eta^2\theta Y^2+
                 3\eta\theta^2XY+\theta^3X^2}{\xi},\\
X'Z'-(Y')^3=\eta^3(XZ-Y^3).
\end{gathered}
\tag{3}
\]
This is an actual polynomial automorphism preserving the weighted
filtration in both directions. Its inverse first recovers
\(X=X'/\xi\), \(Y=(Y'-\theta X)/\eta\), \(W=W'/\omega\),
and then
\[
Z=\eta^{-3}\{\xi Z'-3\eta^2\theta Y^2
                         -3\eta\theta^2XY-\theta^3X^2\}.
\]
These four displayed recovery identities verify both composition
orders. All divisions are by nonzero complex constants.

The new ratios and literal chart coefficients are
\[
\begin{gathered}
\tau'=(\eta\tau+\theta)/\xi,\quad
\rho'=\omega\rho/\xi^2,\quad y'=\eta y,\quad
\vartheta'=\omega\vartheta/\xi,\\
k'=\eta^3k/\xi^2,\quad r'=\eta^3r/\omega,\quad
n'=\eta^3n/\xi,\quad
\lambda'=\xi^2\eta^3\lambda/\omega .
\end{gathered}
\tag{4}
\]
Primes in (3)--(4) label transformed quantities, not derivatives.
The formula for \(k'\) follows by comparing the \((X')^2\)
coefficient of the last identity in (3); the linear coefficient gives
\(n'\). The graph coefficient of \((y')^4\) is
\(\omega p_4/(\xi\eta^4)\). Since
\(p_4=(r_\tau/r)/\lambda\), applying the same literal formula
in the new chart proves the last equality in (4). Thus no arbitrary
rescaling of the canonical potential is being presumed.

Choose \(\eta\in\mathbb C^*\) with \(\eta^2=A^2/D\), and take
\(\xi=\eta/A\), \(\theta=\eta B/A\), \(\omega=\eta^3\).
Then \(t:=\tau'=A\tau+B\),
\(\rho'=\eta A^2\rho\), \(k'=\eta A^2k\),
\(r'=1/t\), \(\lambda'=1/t^2\), and
\(n'_{\rho'}=(\eta C/A)/t^2\).
The polynomial condition in (2) is preserved, since the new
\(t k'_0\) is \(\eta A^2(A\tau+B)k_0\), with an affine
complex substitution in its variable.

Finally an actual constant translation \(Y\mapsto Y+\varepsilon\)
in the new chart changes
\[
y\mapsto y+\varepsilon,\qquad
k\mapsto k-3t^2\varepsilon,\qquad
n\mapsto n+3t\varepsilon^2-\varepsilon k_t.
\tag{5}
\]
It fixes the ratios, \(r\), and \(\lambda\), and sends
\(n_\rho\) to \(n_\rho-\varepsilon r_t\).
Taking \(\varepsilon=-\eta C/A\) makes \(n_\rho=0\).
It changes \(t k_0\) only by a complex multiple of \(t^3\),
so again preserves (2). These changes preserve the full initial
kernel and the exact time degrees: the new leading linear and cubic
ratios are nonzero because \(\tau\) is transcendental over
\(\mathbb C\).

We now omit transformation labels. All subsequent original-ring
formulas are in this actual normalized frame, where
\(r=1/t\), \(\lambda=1/t^2\), and \(n_\rho=0\).
The weighted inverse changes above will return the result to the
supplied frame.

## 3. Exact lower equations and finite-field integration

Use \(f'=\partial_t f\) in the remainder of the proof. The field
\(K=\ker_L\partial_\rho\) is stable under \(\partial_t\),
and its \(\partial_t\)-constants are exactly \(\mathbb C\).
Write
\[
k=\rho/t+k_0,\qquad n=n_0,\qquad
\ell=-\rho/t^2+\ell_0,
\qquad k_0,n_0,\ell_0\in K.
\]
The literal graph coefficients from163 and its full constant
determinant equation give
\[
\begin{gathered}
P=p_4U^4+p_3U^3+p_2U^2+(\rho/t+b_0)U+p_0,\\
p_4=-t,\quad p_3=-t^2k_0'''/6-tk_0''/2,\quad
p_2=-t^2n_0''/2-tn_0',\quad b_0=t\ell_0+t^2\ell_0',\\
e_0:=(p_0)_\rho\in K,\qquad
p_3'-p_3/t-4te_0=0,\\
p_2'-p_2/t+3p_3e_0+3t=0,\qquad
b_0'-b_0/t+2p_2e_0+tk_0''=0.
\end{gathered}
\tag{6}
\]
The notation \(b_0,e_0\) in this display denotes functions in
\(K\); it will not be used for the complex constants below.
These identities are necessary actual-chart equations. No choice of
free formal data is being substituted for the literal potential.

The condition \(tk_0\in\mathbb C[t]\) of degree at most four
is exactly the existence of complex constants \(h,j,b,c,q\) with
\[
k_0=-ht^3-4jt^2+bt+c+q/t.
\tag{7}
\]
Equation (6) first gives \(p_3=4ht^2+4jt\) and \(e_0=h\).
Consequently \(p_0=h\rho+d_0\), for some \(d_0\in K\).
There is no restriction on \(d_0\) imposed in this proof.

Writing \(p_2=tR\), the next equation of (6) becomes
\(R'=-12h^2t-12hj-3\). Its integration gives a complex
constant \(\Lambda\) such that
\(R=-6(ht+j)^2-3t+\Lambda\). The formula for \(p_2\)
then gives
\[
\begin{gathered}
tn_0''+2n_0'=12h^2t^2+(24hj+6)t+12j^2-2\Lambda,\\
n_0=h^2t^3+(4hj+1)t^2+(6j^2-\Lambda)t+e+f/t,
\qquad e,f\in\mathbb C.
\end{gathered}
\tag{8}
\]
The final equation of (6), using \(b_0=t\ell_0+t^2\ell_0'\),
is
\(t\ell_0''+2\ell_0'=-2hR-k_0''\). Hence
\[
\begin{gathered}
t\ell_0''+2\ell_0'
=12h^3t^2+(24h^2j+12h)t+12hj^2-2h\Lambda+8j-2q/t^3,\\
\ell_0=h^3t^3+(4h^2j+2h)t^2+
       (6hj^2-h\Lambda+4j)t+g+i/t-q/t^2,
\qquad g,i\in\mathbb C.
\end{gathered}
\tag{9}
\]
For precision, these integrations do not assume \(K=\mathbb C(t)\)
or the existence of arbitrary antiderivatives in \(K\). Subtract
the displayed polynomial or Laurent polynomial particular solution.
A remaining first derivative zero gives a complex constant. A
remaining solution \(F\in K\) of \(tF''+2F'=0\) satisfies
\((t^2F')'=0\). Thus \(t^2F'\in\mathbb C\), and subtraction
of its explicit \(-1/t\) primitive leaves another complex constant.
This proves exactly the asserted exhaustive forms (8)--(9), even
when \(K\) is a proper finite algebraic extension of \(\mathbb C(t)\).

## 4. An explicit invariant and all its time coefficients

Put \(H=XZ-Y^3\), and define the complex polynomial of weight
at most three
\[
\begin{split}
Q(X,Y)={}&qX^3+cX^2Y+bXY^2-4jY^3\\
 &+(f+hq)X^2+(e+hc)XY+(6j^2-\Lambda+hb)Y^2\\
 &-(i+c-hf)X-(g+b-he)Y.
\end{split}
\tag{10}
\]
The claimed invariant is
\[
I=-YH+(X+h)W+(1-hY)Z+Q(X,Y).
\tag{11}
\]
It is a polynomial in the actual coordinates and has top
\(-Y(XZ-Y^3)\), hence is nonconstant of exact original weight four.

Here is a direct check of every nonconstant time coefficient, without
assuming that a generic polynomial shear descends. The actual chart is
\[
\begin{gathered}
Y=y+tX,\quad Z=z+(3t^2y+k)X+t^3X^2,
\quad W=w+P(y)X+\rho X^2,\\
z=3ty^2+k_ty+P(y)/t+n_0,\\
H=-y^3+(k_ty+P(y)/t+n_0)X+kX^2,\\
w=t\ell-ty^3-\tfrac12tk_{tt}y^2+(y/t)P(y)-tn_0'y.
\end{gathered}
\tag{12}
\]
All equalities hold in the actual localization, not only on an
independently chosen target frame.

The expression (11) has degree at most three in \(X\). Its cubic
coefficient is
\(-tk+\rho-ht^4+q+ct+bt^2-4jt^3=0\), by (7).
For its quadratic coefficient, the coefficient of \(y\) is
\[-(k+tk_t)-4ht^3-12jt^2+c+2bt=0.\]
Its remaining part is
\[-tn_0+t^3-htk_0+(f+hq)+(e+hc)t+
                          (6j^2-\Lambda+hb)t^2=0,\]
by (7)--(8). The \(\rho\) terms have already cancelled.

For the linear coefficient, substitute the last line of (12) first.
The terms involving \(P(y)\) cancel. The coefficient of \(y^2\) is
\[-k_t-\tfrac12tk_{tt}-6ht^2+b-12jt=0.\]
The coefficient of \(y\) is
\[-n_0-tn_0'+3t^2-h(k+tk_t)+(e+hc)+
                                  2(6j^2-\Lambda+hb)t=0.\]
The constant part is
\[t\ell_0+k_0-htn_0-(i+c-hf)-(g+b-he)t=0.\]
These follow by direct substitution of (7)--(9). This accounts for
every positive power of \(X\).

Since \(E=a\partial_X\) in the actual chart, \(EI=0\) in the
localization, and hence in the original domain \(B\). No assumption
that \(a\) is a unit is used.

There is also a useful exact constant value. Combining (12) with the
graph coefficients in (6) and (8) gives
\[
I=n_0+d_0/t+ht\ell_0\in K\subset L
\tag{13}
\]
after projection to \(X=0\). One can verify this by using
\(w-yz=t\ell-4ty^3-(tk_{tt}/2+k_t)y^2-(tn_0'+n_0)y\).
The \(\rho\) terms cancel because \(p_0=h\rho+d_0\).
In particular the free graph intercept \(d_0\) is not silently set
to zero or constrained by a generic-field parametrization.
The already established invariance puts \(I\) in \(A\), and
(13) then puts it in \(A\cap L=S\).

## 5. A whole-ring inverse and the exact scope

Define \(J=W-YZ\). Equation (11) becomes
\[
\begin{gathered}
I=Z+(X+h)J+Y^4+Q(X,Y),\qquad J=W-YZ,\\
Z=I-(X+h)J-Y^4-Q(X,Y),\qquad W=J+YZ.
\end{gathered}
\tag{14}
\]
Keeping \(X,Y\), the last line recovers \(Z,W\) polynomially
from \(I,J\). Substituting it into the first line returns \(I,J\);
substituting the first line into the last returns \(Z,W\).
Thus \((X,Y,I,J)\) is an actual polynomial coordinate system.
Composing with the explicit inverse changes (3) and (5) proves the
coordinate claim in the originally supplied frame as well.

Both changes preserve the original weighted filtration, so the
constructed invariant still has exact weight four before
normalization. The equality with an element of the intrinsic
coefficient field also survives these constant changes: equivalently,
the original \(\partial_Z I\) has \(E\)-time degree at most two,
so the original selected cubic coefficient \(N(I)\) vanishes.

There is a conditional kernel-recognition corollary using the retained
external theorem of Bhatwadekar, Gupta and Lokhande. Their
[Theorem 3.5](https://arxiv.org/html/1501.01438v1)
states that a nonzero locally nilpotent derivation on a polynomial
four-ring over a characteristic-zero field, fixing one polynomial
coordinate and having regular kernel, has a polynomial two-ring over
that coordinate as its kernel. Its hypotheses hold here: (14) makes
\(E\) a nonzero \(\mathbb C[I]\)-linear locally nilpotent derivation,
and \(A\simeq B/(u)\) is regular. Indeed \(Eu=1\) expresses one
as a polynomial linear combination of the four partial derivatives
of \(u\), so the hypersurface \(u=0\) is smooth. Therefore, when
the equivalent conditions (2) hold,
\[
A\simeq\mathbb C[I]^{[2]}\simeq\mathbb C^{[3]}.
\tag{15}
\]
This last implication invokes that external theorem; it is not a new
proof of the theorem or a recursive audit of its dependencies.

This proves both implications of (2) and supplies a direct coordinate
inverse for the sufficient implication. It does not prove that
\((A\tau+B)k_0\) must be a complex polynomial of degree at most four
in every actual quartic graph chart. In particular no generic
reparameterization has been asserted to be integral, no boundary
\(a=0\) has been removed, and no upper bound for unrestricted
coordinate-entry degree has been obtained. The remaining coefficient
condition is explicit rather than assumed.

This proof is an algebraic extension of the cited retained working
inputs. Independent artifact reviews or finite symbolic controls do
not replace expert verification of those inputs or a historical
literature audit.
