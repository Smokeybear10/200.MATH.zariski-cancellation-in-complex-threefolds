# Origin dilation: an exact family, not a polynomial lifting theorem

For every actual cancellation input, origin dilation produces a smooth
family with central fiber affine three-space and all nonzero fibers equal
to the unknown threefold. Its cylinder is polynomial over the parameter
ring. The family is the extended Rees algebra of a point. Proving it
polynomial over the parameter ring is equivalent to the original problem.

Two natural attempts to supply that implication fail even in an ordinary
translation example: the projected formal coordinates have a nonterminating
inverse, and the natural horizontal derivation is not locally nilpotent.
These are failures of specified constructions, not counterexamples to
cancellation or to the existence of better coordinates.

## 1. The family exists for every actual input

Work over k=C. Suppose

    B=k[z1,z2,z3,z4]=A[s], E in LND_k(B), E(s)=1, A=ker E.

After centering at an ambient point and subtracting a constant from s,
assume s(0)=0. Write E=sum_i P_i(z) partial_zi, and choose N with
E^N(zi)=0 for every i. On S=k[t,z1,z2,z3,z4], define

    E_t(t)=0, E_t(zi)=P_i(tz), sigma=s(tz)/t.

The quotient defining sigma is polynomial because s(0)=0. For j>=1,

    E_t^j(zi)=t^(j-1) (E^j zi)(tz).                    (1)

Indeed E_t(f(tz))=t(Ef)(tz), which proves (1) by induction. Thus E_t
is locally nilpotent on all generators and therefore on S. Directly,

    E_t(sigma)=(Es)(tz)=1.

Let K=ker E_t and R=S/(sigma). The slice theorem, applied as a
k-algebra statement while keeping t invariant, gives

    S=K[sigma], K isomorphic to R over k[t].           (2)

For clarity, the isomorphism sends the class of f in R to
rho_t(f)=sum_j (-sigma)^j E_t^j(f)/j! in K. Expressions in the
hypersurface model R and the embedded kernel K must not be silently
identified when applying derivations.

The identity

    sum_i P_i(tz) partial_zi(sigma)=1                  (3)

makes R smooth over k[t]: the opens where an individual relative
partial derivative is invertible cover Spec R and have standard smooth
hypersurface presentations. This is the usual standard-smooth criterion.
[Stacks 00T6](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00T6),
[Stacks 00T7](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00T7).

Put ell(z)=ds_0(z). Equation (3) at t=0 shows ell is nonzero. Hence

    R/(t)=k[z]/(ell)=k^[3],
    R[t^-1]=A[t,t^-1],
    R/(t-c)=A for every c in k*,
    R[v]=k[t]^[4] as k[t]-algebras.                  (4)

For the second identity substitute x_i=t z_i, so the relation becomes
s(x)=0. The same substitution at t=c proves the third. The last
identity is (2), with v corresponding to sigma, not to a coordinate
already known before applying the slice theorem.

## 2. This is the deformation to the tangent space of a point

Identify A with k[x1,x2,x3,x4]/(s(x)), and let m be its origin maximal
ideal, generated by the classes of the x_i. Then

    R isomorphic to A[t,m/t] inside A[t,t^-1].         (5)

To prove this, map t to t and z_i to x_i/t. Its image is exactly the
displayed algebra. After inverting t its kernel is (sigma). The ideal
(sigma) is already t-saturated: R is a domain by (2), and t is nonzero
in that domain. Therefore no extra relation appears before localization.

This is the usual extended Rees construction. Its special fiber is
gr_m A, which here is the polynomial tangent-space ring because the
point is smooth. The geometric construction is standard, not a proposed
new deformation principle. [Vakil, Sections 3-4](https://math.stanford.edu/~vakil/245/245class14.pdf).

The weights

    wt(t)=1, wt(z_i)=-1

give sigma degree -1 and E_t degree 1. Under (5), the elements x_i=t z_i
have weight zero, and R_0=A. This is a mixed grading, not a positive
grading contracting the whole variety to a point. A positive-grading
recognition theorem cannot be applied by ignoring this distinction.

## 3. The lifting condition is exactly the original missing conclusion

The following conditions are equivalent for this family:

1. A is a polynomial three-ring over k.
2. R is a polynomial three-ring over k[t].
3. There is a k-locally nilpotent derivation delta on R with delta(t)=1.
4. Such a delta can be chosen homogeneous of weight degree -1.

For 1 implies 2, choose polynomial coordinates a1,a2,a3 of A vanishing
at the selected point. Then m=(a1,a2,a3), and (5) becomes

    R=k[t,a1/t,a2/t,a3/t].                            (6)

These are four algebraically independent generators. Differentiation
in t holding the three a_i/t fixed proves 2 and 4. Its degree is -1.
Condition 4 implies 3 trivially. For 3 implies 2, apply the slice theorem
to delta and the slice t:

    R=(ker delta)[t], ker delta isomorphic to R/(t)=k^[3].

Finally 2 implies 1 by specializing at t=1.

Even a polynomial trivialization R_f=k[t]_f^[3] on a neighborhood
f(0)!=0 would suffice: choose a nonzero complex c with f(c)!=0 and
specialize there. Likewise generic polynomiality over k(t) would
suffice, since a polynomial isomorphism and its inverse involve only
finitely many rational coefficients; clearing their denominators and
specializing yields A=k^[3]. None of these trivializations has been
constructed for arbitrary A.

## 4. Formal triviality is available, with a different meaning

Linearly normalize ell=z4. Then sigma=z4+t H(t,z), and the equation
sigma=0 uniquely solves z4 as a formal t-series with coefficients in
k[z1,z2,z3]. Successive substitution, or coefficient recursion, proves
compatible isomorphisms at every finite order and hence

    completion_t(R) isomorphic to k[z1,z2,z3][[t]].    (7)

For precision, substitute the recursively obtained series phi(t,z1,z2,z3)
into sigma. The resulting sigma is zero; also sigma=(z4-phi) times a
unit congruent to 1 modulo t in k[z1,z2,z3,z4][[t]]. Thus the formal
quotient is exactly the ring in (7), not just a set of approximate
solutions.

The right side of (7) allows the z-degree of the t-coefficients to grow
without bound. It is not k[[t]][z1,z2,z3], and (7) does not prove that
R tensor_k[t] k[[t]] is a polynomial algebra over k[[t]]. The general
formal-lifting pitfall, including a separate smooth counterexample
outside the cylinder hypotheses, was already proved in23.

## 5. A finite projector can have a nonterminating formal inverse

Take actual original coordinates x,y,z,w and

    E=partial_x-2x partial_y,
    s=x+(y+x^2)^2.

The two-shear coordinate seed is already in
[654, Section 3](commuting-projected-partials-retraction-recognition-pass654-working.md);
it is reused here, not claimed as a new polynomial automorphism.
Here Es=1 and E^3 kills every ambient generator. In the original input,
the action-coefficient degree is 1, the slice degree is 4, and the
generator nilpotence bound is 3. For its dilation put

    U=y+t x^2, V=sigma=x+t U^2,
    E_t=partial_x-2t x partial_y.

This family really is polynomial. Over k[t], the change (x,y) to (U,V)
has the whole inverse

    x=V-t U^2,
    y=U-t(V-t U^2)^2.                               (8)

Nevertheless the canonical projection of the original transverse
coordinate y is

    Y=rho_t(y)=y+2t x V-t V^2=U-t^3 U^4.             (9)

The variables z,w are unchanged and rho_t(x)=-t U^2. Thus
(Y,z,w,V) agrees with the linear coordinate frame at t=0 and has a
formal inverse at every order. Its inverse must solve

    U=Y+t^3 U^4.

Write

    U=sum_(n>=0) c_n t^(3n) Y^(3n+1),
    c_0=1,
    c_n=sum_(i+j+k+l=n-1) c_i c_j c_k c_l.           (10)

Coefficient comparison proves (10), uniqueness follows recursively,
and every c_n is a positive integer. The inverse therefore never
terminates and has unbounded Y-degree. In particular it does not
belong to k[[t]][Y]. It is not rational over k(t,Y) either: the
polynomial U-t^3 U^4-Y is irreducible over k(t,Y), since as a primitive
polynomial in k(t)[U,Y] it is linear in Y with unit leading coefficient,
and its quotient is k(t)[U]. Gauss's lemma then gives irreducibility
over k(t,Y), of degree four in U.

This fixed, low-complexity sliced input therefore refutes automatic
termination of this projected formal inverse. It does not refute a
better coordinate choice: U,z,w are already successful coordinates of
R. It does not bound the minimum possible coordinate degree for an
unknown kernel. The failure is related to699 but is now realized by
the canonical origin-dilation family itself.

## 6. The natural homogeneous horizontal lift is not always an LND

There is an unconditional polynomial derivation on S:

    D=partial_t-(partial_t sigma) E_t.               (11)

It satisfies D(t)=1 and D(sigma)=0, so descends to the quotient model
R. It is homogeneous of weight degree -1. It need not preserve the
embedded kernel K before transport through (2).

For the example above, E_t(U)=0 and partial_t(U)=x^2. Therefore
D(U)=x^2, and in the quotient V=0, equation (8) gives x=-t U^2. Hence

    D_R=partial_t+t^2 U^4 partial_U

on R=k[t,U,z,w]. The unique highest-U-degree term of D_R^n(U) is

    (product_(j=0)^(n-1) (3j+1)) t^(2n) U^(3n+1).   (12)

Induct on n: differentiating in t does not increase U-degree, whereas
the second summand raises it by three and multiplies its leading
coefficient by the current U-degree. All coefficients in (12) are
nonzero, so D_R is not locally nilpotent.

Even the associated normalized formal horizontal coordinate fails to
be polynomial:

    J=U(1+t^3 U^3)^(-1/3),
    U=J(1-t^3 J^3)^(-1/3).                           (13)

The binomial series are interpreted t-adically. Direct differentiation
gives D_R(J)=0, and cubing verifies that the displayed substitutions
are inverses. Again the actual connection partial_t holding U,z,w
fixed succeeds. The issue is not existence in this recognized example,
but the absence of a general construction of a successful lift.

Thus local nilpotence of E_t, homogeneity, and D(t)=1 do not establish
local nilpotence of D. By Section 3, constructing a different horizontal
LND for every input would itself solve cancellation; it is not a weaker
premise that the family already supplies.
