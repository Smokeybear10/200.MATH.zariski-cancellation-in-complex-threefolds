# The radial-center test cannot cross a primitive plinth divisor

A regular centered radial operator on the actual generic polynomial
plane would produce an algebra retraction to the invariant base.
The entire residue at a genuine primitive plinth divisor contains a
proper finite extension of the base residue field, which forbids that
retraction. Thus no radial center exists at any such divisor.

This is a limitation of the sufficient radial test in
[pass55](radial-slice-descent-pass55.md), not a contradiction to the
actual frame or a cancellation theorem. The obstruction uses the
general residue/support equivalence from pass46, not its degree-two
specialization. An actual polynomial-cylinder control below already
has an affine slice while failing the radial test.

## 1. Degree-zero projection is an actual augmentation

Let \(R\) be a domain containing \(\mathbb Q\), with fraction field
\(L\), and suppose
\[
R[X,Y]\subseteq\mathcal B\subseteq L[X,Y],\qquad
\mathcal B\cap L=R.
\tag{1}
\]
For a center \(\eta=(\eta_X,\eta_Y)\in L^2\), define the
\(L\)-derivation
\[
\Theta_\eta=(X-\eta_X)\partial_X+(Y-\eta_Y)\partial_Y.
\tag{2}
\]
Assume that it preserves the actual ring \(\mathcal B\).
Its values on \(X,Y\) immediately give
\[
\eta_X=X-\Theta_\eta X\in R,\qquad
\eta_Y=Y-\Theta_\eta Y\in R.
\tag{3}
\]
Thus allowing rational centers does not bypass regularity.

For \(b\in\mathcal B\), let \(d\) bound its total polynomial
degree in \(X-\eta_X,Y-\eta_Y\). Its degree-zero component is
\[
\Pi_{0,d}(\Theta_\eta)b
 =\left(\prod_{k=1}^{d}\frac{k-\Theta_\eta}{k}\right)b
 =b(\eta_X,\eta_Y).
\tag{4}
\]
The empty product for \(d=0\) is the identity. The integers in the
denominators are units. Every operator on the left preserves
\(\mathcal B\), while the right side lies in \(L\). Equation (1)
therefore puts it in \(R\).

Although the polynomial operator can depend on \(d\), its output is
literal evaluation. Consequently
\[
\epsilon_\eta:\mathcal B\longrightarrow R,\qquad
b\longmapsto b(\eta_X,\eta_Y)
\tag{5}
\]
is a unital \(R\)-algebra homomorphism and restricts to the identity
on \(R\). This proves a retraction, not merely an \(R\)-linear
constant-term projection. The proof needs neither a quadratic degree
bound nor local nilpotence of \(\Theta_\eta\). The radial operator
is not being asserted to be locally nilpotent.

If \(R\) is a DVR with residue field \(\kappa\), reduction of (5)
would give a unital \(\kappa\)-algebra map
\[
\overline{\mathcal B}\longrightarrow\kappa.
\tag{6}
\]
If the whole residue ring contains a field \(F\supset\kappa\)
with \(1<[F:\kappa]<\infty\), restriction of (6) to \(F\) is
injective. Its image contains \(\kappa\) and lies in \(\kappa\),
so it would identify \(F\) with \(\kappa\) as a
\(\kappa\)-algebra. This is impossible. In fact, this forbids
every \(R\)-algebra retraction in this situation, not only those
obtained from radial operators.

## 2. Application to the actual intrinsic divisor fibers

Retain the actual supplied degree-two setting of pass55:
\[
B=A[s_*]=\mathbb C[x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4],\quad Es_*=1,
\quad C=\ker D\simeq\mathbb C^{[2]},\quad
L=\operatorname{Frac}C,
\]
\[
B_L=L[x_3,x_4],\qquad
\operatorname{pl}(D)=pC,\qquad Dr=p\ne0.
\tag{7}
\]
Here \(D\ne0\) is the primitive intrinsic LND of \(A\), and
the inherited theorem gives factoriality, faithful flatness of
\(C\subset A\), and the full principal plinth. These are inputs
on the unknown \(A\), not an assumption that \(A=\mathbb C^{[3]}\).

For a prime element \(\pi\in C\), put
\[
R=C_{(\pi)},\qquad \kappa=\operatorname{Frac}(C/\pi C).
\]
The complete argument in
[the divisor residue note, Section 2](quadratic-residue-degree-pass46.md)
gives
\[
A_R/\pi A_R=F_\pi[t],\qquad
B_R/\pi B_R=F_\pi[t,s_*],\qquad
[F_\pi:\kappa]=1\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \pi\nmid p.
\tag{8}
\]
These are whole generic divisor fibers, not a subring generated by
selected residue coordinates.

For completeness, the field/support gate uses the following exact
steps. Factorial closure and faithful flatness give a nonempty
one-dimensional domain \(A_R/\pi A_R\). Primitivity ensures that
the induced LND is nonzero. Its invariant ring is algebraic over
\(\kappa\), hence is a field: a minimal polynomial gives the inverse
of each nonzero element. A local slice then becomes a slice over this
field, giving \(F_\pi[t]\). Evaluation at \(t=0\) proves that
\(F_\pi\) is a finite extension. If \(p\) is a unit in \(R\),
the original \(r/p\) is a slice and the residue constants are
\(\kappa\). Conversely, if \(\pi\mid p\) and
\(F_\pi=\kappa\), then \(\bar r\in\kappa\). Subtracting
a lift \(a\in R\) and dividing \(r-a\) by \(\pi\) gives an
actual local slice derivative \(p/\pi\), contradicting the full
plinth \(pR\). No hypothesis \([F_\pi:\kappa]=2\) was used.

The constant-field intersection required in (1) also holds:
\[
B_R\cap L=R.
\tag{9}
\]
An element of this intersection is killed by \(E\), so belongs to
\(A_R\); its intrinsic \(D\)-derivative is zero, so it belongs to
\(C_R=R\). Both kernels commute with localization by base invariants.

Combining (5), (8), and (9) proves
\[
\boxed{\pi\mid p\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{no center }\eta\in L^2\text{ has }
\Theta_\eta(B_{C_{(\pi)}})\subseteq B_{C_{(\pi)}}.}
\tag{10}
\]
The obstruction already occurs modulo \(\pi\), regardless of the
positive multiplicity of \(\pi\) in \(p\).

The same conclusion controls a closed-base localization. If a
radial operator preserves \(B_{C_{\mathfrak m}}\), it also preserves
each further height-one localization, because it kills \(C\).
Therefore
\[
\Theta_\eta\text{ regular on }B_{C_{\mathfrak m}}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad p\notin\mathfrak m.
\tag{11}
\]
This is only a necessary condition. It is not a converse for the
selected generic coordinates. In particular, this note does not
assert that a unit plinth makes those coordinates extend to a
regular centered grading.

In the actual frame \(p\mid\Delta\), by
[the acceleration extension, Section 4](acceleration-divisor-extension-pass45.md).
One must not replace this divisibility by equality. At extra factors
\(\pi\mid\Delta\), \(\pi\nmid p\), the finite-field obstruction
disappears, but the two actual numerator congruences modulo the full
\(\pi^{v_\pi(\Delta)}\) remain to be solved. Nothing here supplies
their solvability or compatible centers on different localizations.

## 3. A sliced, faithfully flat control on actual affine four-space

Use the classical polynomial control already present in
[pass52](universal-frame-reduction-audit-pass52.md), with the invariant
sign convention
\[
A_0=\mathbb C[a,y,z],\quad D_0=a\partial_y+2y\partial_z,
\quad v=y^2-az,\quad C_0=\mathbb C[a,v],
\]
\[
B_0=A_0[s]=\mathbb C^{[4]},\qquad E_0=\partial_s.
\tag{12}
\]
Here \(\ker D_0=C_0\) and its full plinth is \(aC_0\).
One elementary verification localizes at \(a\), where the ring is
\(\mathbb C[a,a^{-1},v,y]\) and \(D_0=a\partial_y\).
Contraction of constants excludes an \(a\)-pole by substituting
\(v=y^2\) modulo \(a\). For the first filtration piece, the same
argument separates the even and odd parts in \(y\), giving exactly
\(F_1=C_0\oplus C_0y\), and hence the stated full plinth.

The presentation
\[
B_0=C_0[s,z,y]/(y^2-az-v)
\tag{13}
\]
is free with basis \(1,y\) over \(C_0[s,z]\). Thus the invariant
inclusion is faithfully flat, not merely generically flat.

Choose the actual polynomial coordinate frame
\[
x_1=a+s^2,\quad x_2=z+s^2,\quad x_3=s,\quad x_4=y+s.
\tag{14}
\]
Its full inverse is
\[
a=x_1-x_3^2,\quad s=x_3,\quad y=x_4-x_3,\quad z=x_2-x_3^2.
\tag{15}
\]
In this frame
\[
E_0(x)=(2x_3,2x_3,1,1),\qquad
E_0^2(x)=(2,2,0,0),\qquad E_0^3(x)=0.
\tag{16}
\]
The exact adjoint heights are \((0,0,1,0)\). In particular the
second adjoints of both displayed bad partials vanish. This is
not an example of the unresolved independent-defect profile
\((1,1,2,2)\), and its bad speeds are both \(1\), not independent.

Now localize at the invariant prime \(a\):
\[
R=(C_0)_{(a)}=\mathbb C(v)[a]_{(a)},\quad
\kappa=\mathbb C(v),\quad L=\mathbb C(a,v).
\]
Writing \(X=x_3=s\), \(Y=x_4=y+s\), the actual generic graph is
\[
(B_0)_L=L[X,Y],\qquad
\mathcal Q_1=a+X^2,\qquad
\mathcal Q_2=\frac{(Y-X)^2-v}{a}+X^2.
\tag{17}
\]
The whole special fiber is
\[
(B_0)_R/a(B_0)_R
 =\bigl(\kappa[y]/(y^2-v)\bigr)[z,s]=F[z,s].
\tag{18}
\]
The element \(v\) is not a square in \(\mathbb C(v)\), as its
valuation at \(v=0\) is odd. Thus \([F:\kappa]=2\). This
relative model is even smooth over \(R\): in its hypersurface
presentation, the relative partials \(a,2y\) generate the unit
ideal, since \(y^2-az=v\) is a unit in \(R\).

There are separately chosen actual commuting LNDs
\[
K_3=-D_0,\qquad K_4=aE_0-D_0,
\qquad K_3=-a\partial_Y,\quad K_4=a\partial_X
\quad\text{generically}.
\tag{19}
\]
Their local nilpotence follows from commuting LNDs and the common
invariant coefficient \(a\). They are determinant-shaped actual
fields with \(\Delta=a\), not the top-frame determinant fields
of the missing independent quadratic defects.

For \(\eta\in R^2\), set \(\eta_u=\eta_Y-\eta_X\). Direct
differentiation of (17) gives
\[
\Theta_\eta\mathcal Q_2
 =2\mathcal Q_2-2\eta_X X
   +\frac{2\bigl(v-\eta_u(Y-X)\bigr)}{a}.
\tag{20}
\]
If this were actual, \(v-\eta_u y\) would vanish in (18).
The elements \(1,y\) are linearly independent over \(\kappa[z,s]\),
while \(v\ne0\). This is impossible. Rational centers outside
\(R^2\) were already excluded by (3).

Nevertheless \(s=X\) is already an actual affine generic slice,
and \(\ker E_0=A_0\) is polynomial. Therefore radial regularity
is not necessary for an affine slice, even with an actual polynomial
four-ring, a smooth faithfully flat invariant DVR quotient, and an
actual quadratic generic graph. This is a scope control, not a
counterexample to the remaining supplied full-frame program.

## 4. Exact primary theorem gates

The theorem checks below are bounded applicability checks. They are
not an exhaustive inventory of proper actions and do not repeat the
[pass55 proper-action audit](proper-quadratic-flow-scope-pass55.md).

Babu and Das, arXiv:2001.07632v5, Theorem 4.4, assume an
\(\mathbb A^2\)-fibration over a Noetherian \(\mathbb Q\)-algebra
and a fixed-point-free base-linear LND. They obtain a slice and an
\(\mathbb A^1\)-fibration kernel; for a seminormal base the kernel
is a rank-one symmetric algebra. Their definition requires polynomial
fibers over every base prime. The complete proof of Lemma 4.2 and
Theorem 4.4 was read: polynomial special fibers are an actual input,
not derived from generic triviality and a slice. At (8) with
\(\pi\mid p\), that input is false. Their theorem therefore
does not supply a radial center here.
[Primary PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2001.07632v5).

Das and Dutta, arXiv:2002.02118v1, Theorem 5.3, consider
\(G=b(X,Y)Z^n-a(X,Y)\) over a DVR, with \(b\ne0\),
\(n\ge2\) invertible in the residue field. They assume the
actual quotient \(R[X,Y,Z]/(G)\) is \(R^{[2]}\), and obtain
the indicated coordinate conclusions. The entire proof and its
Lemmas 5.1 and 5.2 were read. The proof explicitly uses both the
generic and residue polynomial-plane equalities. An actual quadratic
presentation with generic plane, smoothness, faithful flatness and a
slice does not substitute for its polynomial quotient hypothesis.
The residue (18) shows why that substitution fails.
[Primary PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.02118v1).

Sathaye's original 1983 DVR criterion has the familiar generic and
special polynomial-plane premises, as restated in the primary papers
above. The publisher request in this pass returned subscription HTML,
not the original theorem or proof. No fresh full read of Sathaye's
original paper is claimed, and no new theorem here depends on such a
claim. The bibliographic endpoint is
[*Polynomial ring in two variables over a D.V.R.: A criterion*](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01388536).

## 5. Verification, deduplication, and remaining scope

The new algebraic implication is the degree-zero augmentation and
its no-center consequence (10). The residue/support theorem and the
failure of the affine-plane-fibration hypothesis were already proved
in passes 46 and 52. The classical triangular action in (12) is reused,
not presented as a new action family or image-ideal theorem.

An exact inline SymPy check passed both coordinate inverse
compositions, \(D_0(v)=0\), the generic outputs of (19), formula
(20), and all time-iterate identities (16). This is finite arithmetic
verification, not a proof of the general augmentation or source
theorems. No new standalone test suite or full regression is claimed.

The positive radial slice-extraction statement of pass55 remains
valid when its divisibilities hold. The present result shows they
cannot hold across the primitive plinth. It does not decide center
existence at extra determinant factors, force a low-degree slice by
another method, settle higher-generation obstructions, supply an
unrestricted actual frame, or prove complex-threefold cancellation.
