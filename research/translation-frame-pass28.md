# A finite Lie-word criterion for linear triangularization

For the fixed coordinate-frame subproblem, finite nilpotent closure
is exactly linear triangularization. It is not an intermediate condition
we can expect to hold automatically for every coordinate action.

The criterion below has a finite degree bound. A smaller equivalent
matrix test is already published through strong Jacobian nilpotence.
The point is to identify precisely what the proposed Lie-closure search
would recognize, and what it would miss.

## 1. A nilpotent Lie algebra containing translations

Let k be a characteristic-zero field and B=k[x_1,...,x_n].
Let g be a finite-dimensional nilpotent Lie subalgebra of Der_k(B)
containing all n coordinate partials.

Then a single linear coordinate change makes every member of g
strictly triangular:
\[
D(z_i)\in k[z_1,\ldots,z_{i-1}]\quad(D\in\mathfrak g),
\qquad D(z_1)\in k.
\tag{1}
\]

Here is a complete induction proof. A nonzero finite-dimensional
nilpotent Lie algebra has nonzero center. Every central field commutes
with all coordinate partials, so each of its coefficient polynomials
has every partial derivative zero. In characteristic zero those
coefficients are constants. Choose a nonzero central constant field
and a linear change making it partial_{x_n}.

Every field of g commutes with partial_{x_n}, hence preserves its kernel
k[x_1,...,x_{n-1}], and all of its coefficients are independent of x_n.
The restriction image is a finite-dimensional nilpotent Lie algebra
containing the remaining coordinate partials. Apply induction there,
using only a linear change of x_1,...,x_{n-1}. Its lift together with
x_n gives (1): the last coefficient may depend on every earlier variable
but not on x_n.

The base n=1 is included: the central field is a nonzero constant
partial, so all fields commuting with it are constant partials.
No group integration, polynomial inverse or unknown-coordinate
assumption is used in this proof.

If instead g is finite-dimensional and every member is an LND, it is
nilpotent. Over algebraically closed k this is the published
Petravchuk--Sysak theorem.[^1] For polynomial B over any characteristic-zero
field, combine the previously proved local nilpotence of ad(D) on Der(B)
with Engel's theorem to obtain the same conclusion. Being generated by
LNDs does not, by itself, supply the all-members premise.

## 2. The converse and an explicit word-length bound

Let D be any polynomial derivation, not assumed locally nilpotent,
with coefficient total degrees at most d, where d is a nonnegative
integer. Define
\[
\mathfrak g_D=\operatorname{Lie}_k
 \langle D,\partial_{x_1},\ldots,\partial_{x_n}\rangle,
\qquad
W=1+d+\cdots+d^{n-1}.
\tag{2}
\]
For d=0, this sum is one.

The following are equivalent:

1. D is strictly triangular after a linear coordinate change.
2. g_D is finite-dimensional and nilpotent.
3. Every left-normed Lie word of length W+1 in the n+1 specified
   generators vanishes.

Section 1 gives 2 implies 1. For 1 implies 3, a linear change preserves
total-degree bounds and the vector space of constant partials.
In triangular coordinates, choose positive weights
\[
w_1=1,\qquad w_i=d\,w_{i-1}+1.
\tag{3}
\]
They are nondecreasing, and each coefficient D(z_i), being a polynomial
of degree at most d in earlier variables, has weight at most
d*w_{i-1}=w_i-1. The first coefficient is constant.
D and every original constant partial therefore decrease weighted
degree by at least one.

A commutator of two operators decreasing weight by a and b decreases
weight by at least a+b. A Lie word of length W+1 therefore sends every
z_i, whose weight is at most W, to zero. It is the zero derivation.

For 3 implies 2, the Jacobi identity expresses every Lie word as a
linear combination of left-normed words in the generators.
Every longer left-normed word has a vanishing prefix of length W+1.
Thus g_D is spanned by the finitely many words of lengths at most W.
All words of length greater than W vanish, so its lower central series
terminates by step W+1. It is finite-dimensional and nilpotent.

This gives a finite exact certificate for this fixed-frame question.
It is not a practical claim that enumerating all (n+1)^(W+1) words
is a good algorithm; bracket-span compression or the next matrix test
is preferable.

For a finite set of strictly triangular fields without a common
degree bound specified in advance, apply the same argument with their
maximum coefficient degree. It proves finite-dimensional nilpotent
closure. The finite-generation assumption matters: the entire algebra
of triangular polynomial fields need not be finite-dimensional or
nilpotent with one uniform class.

## 3. The published matrix test is smaller

Write D=sum_i F_i partial_{x_i}, F=(F_1,...,F_n), and M=JF.
For n independent tuples of n indeterminates, the condition
\[
M(y^{(n)})\cdots M(y^{(1)})=0
\tag{4}
\]
as a polynomial matrix is equivalent to linear triangularization.

The implication from triangularization is immediate: all matrices
are strictly triangular in one constant basis. Conversely, de Bondt's
Theorem 2.1 and Corollary 2.2(1)-(2) give a constant invertible matrix
T such that T^{-1}M(Tz)T is strictly triangular, and this is the
Jacobian of T^{-1}F(Tz). Characteristic zero makes the corresponding
coefficients depend only on earlier coordinates.[^2]

The theorem explicitly bounds the strong nilpotency index by n.
It credits the earlier strong-nilpotence and linear-triangularization
work of van den Essen--Hubbers and Yu. Therefore this note does not
claim a new linear-triangularization algorithm.

Equation (4) uses independent tuples, not the ordinary matrix power
M(x)^n. Ordinary nilpotence and strong nilpotence must not be interchanged.
The finite Lie-word condition in Section 2 recognizes exactly the
same linear-triangularization subproblem as this established matrix
criterion.

Over C there is another equivalent condition for g_D when D is an
LND: g_D is finite-dimensional and solvable. Indeed, it is generated
by LNDs, so Kraft--Zaidenberg's integration theorem makes it algebraic
and locally finite on B. Their Corollary 3.1.4 then makes every member
an LND; Section 1 applies.[^3] Finite-dimensionality alone is insufficient,
as the exact control below shows.

## 4. A coordinate action with finite but nonsolvable full-frame closure

On k[x,y], put
\[
u=x+y^2,\qquad v=y,\qquad s=v+u^2,
\]
\[
D=(1+4y(x+y^2))\partial_x-2(x+y^2)\partial_y.
\tag{5}
\]
Then
\[
D(u)=1,\qquad D(s)=0,\qquad
y=s-u^2,\quad x=u-(s-u^2)^2.
\tag{6}
\]
Thus D is a genuine coordinate partial in the actual coordinates
u,s. It is locally nilpotent, sliced and primitive.

Use only the intermediate shear coordinates u,v. The original
generators become
\[
\partial_x=P_u,\qquad
\partial_y=P_v+2F,\qquad
D=P_u-2E,
\]
\[
E=uP_v,\qquad F=vP_u,\qquad
H=uP_u-vP_v.
\tag{7}
\]
Their Lie algebra is exactly the five-dimensional span
\[
\langle P_u,P_v,E,F,H\rangle_k
 \simeq \mathfrak{sl}_2(k)\ltimes k^2.
\tag{8}
\]
Indeed E=(P_u-D)/2,
P_v=[P_u,E], F=(partial_y-P_v)/2, and H=[E,F].
These fields are linearly independent.
The nonzero brackets, apart from skew symmetry, are
\[
[P_u,E]=P_v,\quad [P_v,F]=P_u,\quad
[E,F]=H,\quad [H,E]=2E,\quad [H,F]=-2F,
\]
\[
[H,P_u]=-P_u,\qquad [H,P_v]=P_v.
\tag{9}
\]
They prove closure, and the derived algebra equals the full algebra.
In particular it is not solvable or nilpotent.

There is also a direct failed-LND witness:
\[
[\partial_y,D]=(4x+12y^2)\partial_x-4y\partial_y,
\qquad [\partial_y,D]\,(y)=-4y.
\tag{10}
\]
Its repeated values on y never vanish. Thus finite-dimensional closure
of actual LND generators need not consist entirely of LNDs.

No nonconstant affine-linear function ax+by+c has D-value constant:
the cubic coefficient forces a=0 and the remaining quadratic
coefficient forces b=0. A linearly triangular field always has such
a first coordinate. D is therefore not linearly triangularizable,
despite its explicit nonlinear coordinate certificate (6).

Adjoining two unaffected variables embeds this exact example in C^[4].
Its slice kernel is polynomial. Hence rejecting the original frame
does not reject polynomiality; an appropriate nonlinear frame changes
the answer.

## 5. What this does and does not settle for cancellation

If B=A[s]=C^[4] and delta=partial_s has a finite nilpotent closure with
all partials of an actual coordinate frame of B, Section 1 makes
delta linearly triangular in that frame. Its slice already makes
the action proper: the map Ga x B -> B x B is the closed immersion
of pairs with the same A-coordinate, with group parameter equal to
the difference of their s-values.

The published proper-triangular four-space theorem therefore gives
A=C^[3].[^4] This is a sufficient recognition test whose precise
hypotheses can be checked on an explicit input.

It does not prove that the test succeeds in the supplied frame.
Equation (5) shows it can fail even for a coordinate action.
Allowing a search over arbitrary polynomial frames does not give
a termination theorem. If A is already polynomial, a frame consisting
of its three coordinates and s supplies a commuting closure; constructing
such a frame for every unknown A is the original difficulty.

The broader finite transitive-action criterion in the companion
[recognition note](finite-lie-recognition-pass28.md) does not require
the initial standard partials. Its existence is also not inferred
from the cylinder alone.
