# A commuting complementary projection frame with a unit normal component

Date: September 18, 2026. Private working proof. Novelty: UNKNOWN.

For a principal polynomial retraction, it suffices that the projected
coordinate partials complementary to ONE constant nonzero normal
component commute. They then become an actual coordinate frame on the
image algebra. Local nilpotence need not be assumed separately.
In dimension four this uses three projected fields, rather than all
four as in654. The proof works without an initial cylinder presentation.

The unit component is essential to the proof. Mere A-linear independence
does not give a basis or local nilpotence. The controls below distinguish
these properties on actual polynomial cylinders. No successful frame
is supplied for an arbitrary cancellation candidate.

## 1. Exact hypotheses and the inherited relation module

Let k be a field of characteristic zero, n>=1, and let
\[
B=k[X_0,\ldots,X_n],\qquad
\rho:B\longrightarrow A\subseteq B,\qquad
\rho|_A=\mathrm{id},\quad \ker\rho=uB\ne0.
\tag{1}
\]
The map is a k-algebra retraction. Its image is a finite-type domain
isomorphic to B/(u), and u is a nonzero nonunit. Define
\[
r_i=\rho X_i,\quad q_i=(X_i-r_i)/u,\quad e_i=\rho q_i,
\quad h_i=\rho(\partial_{X_i}u),\quad
D_i=\rho\partial_{X_i}|_A.
\tag{2}
\]
Assume that e_0 is a nonzero constant and
\[
[D_i,D_j]=0\qquad(1\le i,j\le n).
\tag{3}
\]
An invertible constant rescaling of X_0 makes e_0=1 without changing
the complementary fields. We use that normalization throughout.

**Conclusion.** There are w_1,...,w_n in A such that
\[
A=k[w_1,\ldots,w_n],\qquad D_i=\partial_{w_i}
\quad(1\le i\le n).
\tag{4}
\]
In particular all the selected fields are LNDs. This conclusion does
not identify the supplied embedded image as a polynomial factor B=A[u].
If that product was an additional input, then of course
B=k[w_1,...,w_n,u], and u is an actual ambient coordinate.

We recall the exact identities proved in654, including their licenses.
Taylor expansion at X=r+u q, division by u, and application of rho give
\[
\sum_{i=0}^n h_i e_i=1,\qquad
D_i(r_j)=\delta_{ij}-h_i e_j.
\tag{5}
\]
The map A^{n+1} to Der_k(A) sending a coefficient vector to the
corresponding combination of the D_i is surjective with kernel Ae.
Surjectivity follows from V=sum V(r_i)D_i, obtained by applying the
polynomial chain rule to the rho-derivation V composed with rho.
Applying a relation to every r_j in (5) proves the kernel assertion.

Since e_0=1, D_1,...,D_n are an A-basis of Der_k(A). More generally
they are A-linearly independent exactly when e_0 is nonzero, and
\[
\operatorname{Der}_k(A)/(A D_1+\cdots+A D_n)\simeq A/(e_0).
\tag{6}
\]
Thus they span exactly when e_0 is a unit. Every unit of A is a
unit of B and therefore belongs to k*. This is why a nonzero
constant, not a merely nonzero polynomial, is assumed. Equivalently,
the theorem assumes that the selected n projected partials form a
commuting A-basis, not just a commuting independent list.

Equation (5) also shows that the differential row of u is unimodular
over B/(u). The hypersurface conormal presentation is explicitly
Omega_{A/k}=A^{n+1}/Ah, and h dot e=1 splits its relation submodule.
Thus Omega_{A/k} is finite projective of rank n. No external
smoothness theorem, product assumption, or unidentified hypothesis
is needed for this cotangent-module license.

## 2. Commutation reduces the coefficient data to a polynomial curve

For i>=1, equation (5) gives D_i(r_0)=-h_i. Commutation applied
first to r_0 and then to r_l, l>=1, yields
\[
D_i(h_j)=D_j(h_i),\qquad
h_jD_i(e_l)=h_iD_j(e_l).
\tag{7}
\]
If r_0 belongs to k, every h_i with i>=1 vanishes. Then
D_i(r_j)=delta_ij for i,j>=1, and A is generated by r_1,...,r_n.
They are algebraically independent, for example by these derivative
identities and a minimal-degree relation. Thus (4) holds with w_i=r_i.

Now assume r_0 is nonconstant. Its differential in Frac(A) is nonzero:
characteristic zero detects transcendence by differentials, and k is
relatively algebraically closed in k(X_0,...,X_n). Since the D_i form
a basis, some h_i is nonzero. Over Frac(A), (7) makes each de_l a
scalar multiple of dr_0. Consequently the differentials of the entire
list r_0,e_1,...,e_n span dimension one. In characteristic zero the
dimension of this differential span is the transcendence degree of
the field they generate. Therefore
\[
\operatorname{trdeg}_k k[r_0,e_1,\ldots,e_n]=1.
\tag{8}
\]

The polynomial curve-factorization theorem supplies a nonconstant
g in B and polynomials R,E_1,...,E_n in k[T] with
\[
r_0=R(g),\qquad e_l=E_l(g),\qquad R\notin k.
\tag{9}
\]
The exact input is de Bondt's Corollary3.2, which is stated over an
arbitrary field and places the parameter in the ORIGINAL polynomial
ring.[^curve] It does not assert that g is an ambient coordinate.

The parameter actually belongs to A, even for the general retraction
(1). Here is the required algebraic-closure argument. Put K=Frac(A)
and localize rho to B_K to K. An element b of B algebraic over K has
an irreducible minimal polynomial over K; applying this localized
retraction gives a root rho(b) in K. The polynomial must be linear,
so b=rho(b) belongs to A. Apply this to g, which is algebraic over K
by the nonconstant equation R(g)=r_0. No cylinder action is needed.

Choose polynomials F_l in k[T] by integration:
\[
F_l'(T)=E_l(T)R'(T),\qquad w_l=r_l-F_l(g).
\tag{10}
\]
Such primitives exist because the characteristic is zero. Since
h_i=-R'(g)D_i(g), direct differentiation gives
\[
D_i(w_l)=\delta_{il}\qquad(1\le i,l\le n).
\tag{11}
\]
The w_l are algebraically independent: a nonzero polynomial relation
of least total degree, differentiated by every D_i, would force all
its partial derivatives to vanish, contradicting characteristic zero.
Crucially, the whole algebra has the presentation
\[
A=k[w_1,\ldots,w_n,g].
\tag{12}
\]
Indeed the right side lies in A; conversely (9)--(10) express every
original generator r_0,...,r_n in that right side.

## 3. The monogenic step closes the coordinate argument

Equation (11) alone would only give an exact coframe, not a general
coordinate theorem. Here (12) supplies the extra algebraic information.
The surjection k[W_1,...,W_n,G] to A has a nonzero height-one prime
kernel, since A has transcendence degree n. The polynomial source is
a UFD, so this kernel is generated by an irreducible polynomial P.
Algebraic independence of the w_i ensures deg_G(P)>=1.

The projective cotangent module Omega_{A/k} is dual to Der_k(A).
By (11), dw_1,...,dw_n are dual to the basis D_1,...,D_n, and hence
form a basis. In particular Omega_{A/k[w_1,...,w_n]}=0. But the
one-generator presentation (12) computes that relative module as
\[
\Omega_{A/k[w_1,\ldots,w_n]}
\simeq \bigl(A/(P_G(w,g))\bigr)\,dg.
\tag{13}
\]
Thus P_G(w,g) is a unit of A, and therefore a scalar c in k*.
In the polynomial source this says P_G-c belongs to (P). Its degree
in G is smaller than deg_G P, whereas every nonzero multiple of P
has at least that degree. Hence P_G=c as a polynomial. In
characteristic zero, P=cG+P_0(W). It follows that g is a polynomial
in w_1,...,w_n. Equations (11)--(12) now prove (4).

This is not an invocation of the Jacobian conjecture. The decisive
additional premise is the actual one-extra-generator equality (12),
obtained from the special projected-field identities.

## 4. A precise coefficient-curve reformulation

Under (1)--(2) and e_0=1, but without initially assuming (3), the
following are equivalent:

* the complementary D_1,...,D_n commute;
* either r_0 is constant, or the joint algebra in (8) has
  transcendence degree one.

The forward direction was proved above. In the nonconstant converse,
(9)--(10) and (5) give (11) without any bracket assumption. Equations
(12)--(13) then prove that the fields are coordinate partials and
therefore commute. The constant case was handled separately.

That separate case is necessary. For rho(X_0)=0 and
rho(X_i)=X_i(1+X_0), i>=1, the kernel is (X_0), r_0=0,
e_0=1 and e_i=-r_i. The complementary fields are the coordinate
partials on A=k[r_1,...,r_n], but the algebra generated by the
e_i has transcendence degree n. Thus the curve criterion must not
be asserted without its constant-r_0 alternative.

## 5. The certificate succeeds where all four projected fields fail

Reuse the actual coordinate control from654 in B=k[x,y,z,t]:
\[
v=y+x^2,\qquad u=x+v^2,\qquad E=\partial_u
\text{ in the coordinates }(u,v,z,t).
\tag{14}
\]
The whole inverse is x=u-v^2, y=v-(u-v^2)^2, with z,t unchanged.
Thus E(x)=1, E(y)=-2x and E(z)=E(t)=0. At the supplied slice u=0,
\[
r_x=-v^2,\quad r_y=v-v^4,\quad
e=(1,2v^2,0,0),
\]
\[
D_x=-2v^2\partial_v,\quad D_y=\partial_v,\quad
D_z=\partial_z,\quad D_t=\partial_t.
\tag{15}
\]
The complementary triple omitting x commutes and meets the theorem.
In (9)--(10) take g=v, R=-g^2, E_y=2g^2, F_y=-g^4. The resulting
w_y is v, and the other two coordinates are z,t.

The fourth projected field is not an LND:
D_x^N(v)=(-2)^N N! v^{N+1} for every N>=1. Also
[D_y,D_x]=-4v partial_v. No retraction with this kernel can make
all four projected fields commute in the original linear frame.
Indeed any constant translation has
\[
T(u)=\alpha+v(4\alpha x+2\beta),
\]
which cannot be one because x,v are algebraically independent.
The necessity theorem654 gives the assertion for EVERY such
retraction. Thus the present criterion is strictly less restrictive
than654 in a fixed frame, even on an already polynomial example.

## 6. Independence without the unit component does not supply LNDs

Reuse the flat-connection control22. In B=k[x,y,z,t] put
\[
E=-\tfrac12\partial_x+(1-x)\partial_t,\qquad
u=t-x^2,\quad a=x+u/2,\quad b=y,\quad c=z.
\tag{16}
\]
The polynomial inverse is x=a-u/2, t=u+(a-u/2)^2, y=b, z=c.
Thus E is the coordinate partial in u. Its projected fields are
\[
D_x=(1-a)\partial_a,\quad D_y=\partial_b,\quad
D_z=\partial_c,\quad D_t=\tfrac12\partial_a,
\qquad e=(-\tfrac12,0,0,1-a).
\tag{17}
\]
The triple D_x,D_y,D_z commutes and is A-linearly independent.
Its omitted component e_t=1-a is nonzero but not a unit, and the
triple fails to span at a=1. Moreover
D_x^N(a)=(-1)^{N-1}(1-a) is nonzero for every N>=1, so D_x is not
locally nilpotent. This is an actual cylinder, not an abstract
commuting-derivation example.

The control refutes automatic local nilpotence from independence
and commutation without the unit hypothesis. It does NOT refute
abstract recognition under some different weaker condition: its
base was polynomial from the start.
