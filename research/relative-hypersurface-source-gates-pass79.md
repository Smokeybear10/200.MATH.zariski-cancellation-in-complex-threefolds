# Recognition gates for the actual relative hypersurface

The two-generator presentation gives a concrete section test, but it does
not itself satisfy a published affine-line-bundle criterion. An actual
solution of the defining equation over the invariant plane would suffice.
The alternative fiber test requires geometric integrality over divisor
residue fields, not merely that those fibers are domains.

This is a bounded primary-source applicability audit. It does not
recognize the remaining action or repeat the already retained
Kaliman--Saveliev free-action endpoint.

## 1. The precise input

Keep the original setting and conclusions of
[the relative presentation](binary-relative-hypersurface-pass78.md):
\[
B=A[s]=\mathbb C[x_1,x_2,x_3,w],\qquad
S=\ker D=\mathbb C[u,v],\qquad
A=S[y,z]\simeq S[Y,Z]/(F).
\tag{1}
\]
Here \(D\ne0\) is the primitive intrinsic LND, \(S\subset A\) is
faithfully flat, and \(F\) generates a height-one prime. The original
nice action, original slice and supplied good partial are retained,
as is the unit transverse-velocity row. They are not hypotheses of
the general source results quoted below.

Write \(K=\operatorname{Frac}S\). A local slice for \(D\) gives
\[
A\otimes_S K=K[t].
\tag{2}
\]
For every irreducible \(\pi\in S\), factorial closure of \(\ker D\)
in the UFD \(A\) makes \(\pi\) irreducible, hence prime, in \(A\).
It remains a nonunit by faithful flatness. Consequently
\[
A\otimes_S\kappa((\pi))
=\kappa((\pi))[Y,Z]/(\overline F_\pi)
\quad\hbox{is a nonzero domain.}
\tag{3}
\]
This does not say that it remains a domain over an algebraic closure
of \(\kappa((\pi))=\operatorname{Frac}(S/\pi S)\).

## 2. A section is an exact sufficient condition

Das--Dutta, Corollary 3.10, applies to a flat algebra over a UFD,
with a retraction having finitely generated kernel, generic fiber a
polynomial line, and integral height-one fibers. It concludes that
the algebra is polynomial in one variable over the base. Their
Theorem 3.9 proves the normal-base version by a DVR argument and
patching; its proof retains the retraction hypothesis.[^dd]

In (1), the three non-section requirements are already verified.
An \(S\)-retraction is exactly an actual pair \((a,b)\in S^2\)
with \(F(a,b)=0\). Its kernel is generated by \(y-a,z-b\).
Thus
\[
\boxed{
\exists\,a,b\in S:\ F(a,b)=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
A\simeq_S S[t].
}
\tag{4}
\]
The converse is evaluation at \(t=0\), followed by the given
presentation. Equation (4) is a test on the actual inclusion
\(S\subset A\), not a choice of abstract coordinates on \(A\).
It implies \(A\simeq_{\mathbb C}\mathbb C^{[3]}\), but the reverse
abstract implication is false for a specified intrinsic quotient,
as the control below demonstrates.

The original evaluation retraction \(A[s]\to A\) does not provide
the different map \(A\to S\). Nor does the determinant-one matrix
over \(S\) in pass78 provide one: its coefficients are actual
elements of \(S\subset B\), and the relation \(F\) remains.

## 3. The divisor-fiber alternative

Dutta's affine-line-bundle theorem, in the precise restatement
Das--Dutta Theorem 1.1(i), assumes a finitely generated faithfully
flat algebra over a Noetherian normal domain, generic polynomial
line, and geometrically integral height-one fibers. Its conclusion
is the symmetric algebra of an invertible ideal. Over \(S=\mathbb
C[u,v]\), that ideal is principal. Therefore its additional
condition here is exactly
\[
\overline F_\pi\text{ absolutely irreducible in }
\kappa((\pi))[Y,Z]\quad
\text{for every irreducible }\pi\in S.
\tag{5}
\]
Condition (5) would imply \(A\simeq_S S[t]\). Conversely that
relative polynomial equality gives (5). This is the published
codimension-one criterion, not a claim that flatness or the unit
transverse velocity proves (5).[^dutta]

An \(\mathbb A^1\)-fibration in this literature has polynomial
fibers over **every** base prime. The quotient by a nonzero LND
with generic fiber a line is not automatically such a fibration
in that terminology. Similarly, the absolute isomorphism
\(A[s]\simeq_{\mathbb C}\mathbb C^{[4]}\) is not the stronger
\(S\)-algebra isomorphism \(A[s]\simeq_S S^{[2]}\).

## 4. A decisive control on both missing conditions

The retained intrinsic Weitzenbock control has
\[
A_0=\mathbb C[u,y,z],\qquad
D_0=u\partial_y+2y\partial_z,\qquad
S_0=\mathbb C[u,v],\quad v=uz-y^2,
\]
\[
A_0\simeq S_0[Y,Z]/(uZ-Y^2-v).
\tag{6}
\]
The total space is smooth, factorial and contractible, and
\(A_0[s]\) is an actual polynomial four-ring. The primitive
derivation has kernel \(S_0\). Every base fiber is a nonempty
curve; flatness follows from the regular-base, Cohen--Macaulay
equidimensional criterion, or directly from the monic relation
in \(Y\).

An \(S_0\)-point would require
\(u b(u,v)-a(u,v)^2-v=0\). At \(u=0\) it gives
\(a(0,v)^2=-v\), impossible in \(\mathbb C[v]\).
Moreover,
\[
A_0\otimes_{S_0}\mathbb C(v)
=\mathbb C(v)[Y,Z]/(Y^2+v)
=\mathbb C(v)(\sqrt{-v})[Z]
\tag{7}
\]
at the divisor \(u=0\). This is a domain but not geometrically
integral. The closed fiber at \((u,v)=(0,0)\) retains the nilpotent
class of \(Y\).

This reused control disproves neither cancellation nor a claim
using all the extra original nice/good/transverse hypotheses.
It shows why the abstract cylinder, the two relative generators,
and intrinsic primitivity cannot replace the missing conditions
in (4) or (5).

## 5. A short equation-level sufficient condition

If the actual \(F\), possibly after an \(S\)-polynomial
automorphism of \(S[Y,Z]\), is jointly affine-linear,
\[
F=aY+bZ+c,\qquad a,b,c\in S,
\tag{8}
\]
then the nonempty one-dimensional fiber hypothesis forces
\((a,b)S=S\). Otherwise a maximal ideal containing both gives
either the empty fiber, if \(c\) is nonzero there, or an affine
plane fiber, if \(c\) is zero there.

Choose \(r,t\in S\) with \(ar+bt=1\). The two rows
\((a,b)\) and \((-t,r)\) have determinant one, so \(F\) and
\(-tY+rZ\) are actual \(S\)-coordinates after translation by
\(c\). Hence \(A=S^{[1]}\). This proof needs neither topology
nor a general embedding theorem.

This joint degree-one condition is stronger than “two relative
generators.” Vénéreau's Main Theorem treats precisely polynomials
jointly affine-linear in the extra variables over two base
variables, under smoothness, irreducibility and integral
acyclicity. Its statement was freshly checked, but no application
of its longer proof is needed for the elementary implication
above.[^ven]

## 6. Excluded shortcuts and source scope

The older residual-coordinate and DVR-plane criteria do not turn
an arbitrary \(F\) in (1) into an \(S\)-coordinate: the polynomial
special-fiber or actual polynomial ambient-base hypotheses must
first be proved. The retained
[DVR scope note](radial-dvr-scope-pass56.md) already distinguishes
these premises.

The withdrawn Gurjar--Koras--Miyanishi--Russell preprint
arXiv:1502.01849v2 is excluded. The current author withdrawal
record was freshly opened; it states that the proof of Theorem
3.1 has an error and that the theorem is not true in that
version. No result from it is invoked.[^withdrawn]

The usable next statements are consequently specific: construct
an \(S\)-point of this \(F\), prove (5), or prove an actual
relative equation simplification such as (8). None has been
established by this source audit. The separate primitive
Hamiltonian calculation being developed in pass79 is not
silently assumed here.
