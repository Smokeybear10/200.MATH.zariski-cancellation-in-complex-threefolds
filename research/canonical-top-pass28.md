# A nonsliced primitive top in the actual nonunit-plinth homotopy

The canonical local-slice homotopy does not force its primitive
highest relative coefficient to have a slice. This already fails
for the explicit input
\[
R=\mathbb C[x,y,z],\quad
D=x^2\partial_y+2y\partial_z,\quad
r=y,\quad p=x^2,\quad d=x.
\tag{1}
\]
Here \(p\) generates the full nonunit plinth, and
\(d\in\sqrt{p\ker D}\) but \(p\nmid d\).
The modification is an actual polynomial four-ring. A nonlinear
horizontal coordinate action with slice \(\lambda\) gives a
primitive highest coefficient with a nonempty fixed locus and
full plinth generated by a nonunit.

The positive boundary is equally explicit: a different horizontal
action on the very same modification, also with slice \(\lambda\),
has a sliced primitive top. Thus the result excludes automatic
success for every horizontal choice, not the existence of a
successful choice or a further repair. It does not address every
rank-three input or resolve cancellation.

## 1. The input and its full plinth

Set \(H_0=x^2z-y^2\) and \(C=\mathbb C[x,H_0]\). Then
\[
\ker D=C,\qquad \ker D^2=C\oplus Cy,
\qquad D(\ker D^2)=x^2C.
\tag{2}
\]
These are full equalities, not bounded-degree checks. After
localizing at \(x\),
\[
R_x=\mathbb C[x,x^{-1},H_0][y],\qquad D=x^2\partial_y.
\]
An element killed by \(D^2\) therefore has the form
\(x^{-N}(Q_0(x,H_0)+yQ_1(x,H_0))\), with \(N\ge0\) minimal
and both \(Q_i\) polynomial. If \(N>0\) and the expression
belongs to \(R\), reduction of its numerator modulo \(x\) gives
\[
Q_0(0,-y^2)+yQ_1(0,-y^2)=0.
\]
The even and odd powers separate, and substitution
\(H_0\mapsto-y^2\) is injective. Both \(Q_i\) would be
divisible by \(x\), contradicting minimality. Thus \(N=0\).
Taking \(Q_1=0\) also proves the kernel assertion. Applying
\(D\) proves the full plinth equality in (2).

The coefficients \(x^2,2y\) are coprime, so \(D\) is
primitive. Its minimal local slice \(r=y\) has \(Dr=p=x^2\).
The modifying coefficient \(d=x\) is in the radical of the
plinth but not the plinth itself.

## 2. The genuine canonical modification and its vertical action

Put \(v=1+\lambda x\). The prescribed modification is
\[
B=R[\lambda,w]/(vw-y)
 =\mathbb C[\lambda,x,w,z],\qquad y=vw.
\tag{3}
\]
The displayed polynomial presentation follows by eliminating
\(y\) with coefficient one. No unknown cancellation step is used.

Its canonical vertical LND is exactly
\[
\Delta\lambda=\Delta x=0,\qquad
\Delta w=x^2,\qquad
\Delta z=2v^2w.
\tag{4}
\]
It restricts to \(vD\) on the original \(R\), since
\(\Delta(vw)=vx^2\). It is triangular and primitive:
\(x\) divides neither \(v\) nor \(w\), so its two nonzero
coefficients have gcd one.

There is also a direct full-plinth calculation on the modification.
Write
\[
H=x^2z-v^2w^2,\qquad C_B=\mathbb C[\lambda,x,H].
\]
Then
\[
\ker\Delta=C_B,\qquad
\ker\Delta^2=C_B\oplus C_Bw,
\qquad \operatorname{pl}(\Delta)=x^2C_B.
\tag{5}
\]
Indeed \(B_x=C_B[x^{-1}][w]\) with
\(\Delta=x^2\partial_w\). Modulo \(x\), one has
\(v=1\) and \(H=-w^2\), so the same even/odd pole-removal
argument proves (5), with \(\lambda\) retained as a coefficient.
Thus the starting vertical action itself has the required
nonunit full plinth. It is not the unit-plinth pair from pass 27
renamed after the fact.

## 3. A nonlinear horizontal action and its complete polynomial inverse

On the physical coordinates of (3), define
\[
F=2xz-w^2,\qquad L_0=x\partial_w+w\partial_z,
\qquad \delta=\partial_\lambda+F L_0.
\tag{6}
\]
Here \(L_0F=0\). Hence \(FL_0\) is a replica of the
triangular LND \(L_0\), and is locally nilpotent:
\((FL_0)^j(q)=F^jL_0^j(q)\). It commutes with
\(\partial_\lambda\). Their sum is an LND by the binomial
formula, and \(\delta\lambda=1\).

The actual invariant coordinates are
\[
a=x,\qquad b=w-\lambda Fx,\qquad
c=z-\lambda Fw+\frac{\lambda^2F^2x}{2}.
\tag{7}
\]
Let
\[
\kappa=2ac-b^2.
\]
Direct substitution gives \(F=\kappa\) and the full inverse
\[
x=a,\qquad w=b+\lambda\kappa a,\qquad
z=c+\lambda\kappa b+\frac{\lambda^2\kappa^2a}{2}.
\tag{8}
\]
Both inverse compositions are polynomial identities. Also
\(\delta a=\delta b=\delta c=0\). Thus
\[
B=\mathcal K[\lambda],\qquad
\mathcal K=\ker\delta=\mathbb C[a,b,c],\qquad
\delta=\partial_\lambda
\]
in these new coordinates. The chosen horizontal slice is still
the original parameter \(\lambda\), not another function.

## 4. The actual highest coefficient is primitive nonsliced after saturation

For compact exact formulas put
\[
V=1+\lambda a,\qquad W=b+\lambda\kappa a,\qquad
T=2aW(2V^2-a).
\tag{9}
\]
The physical identity \(\Delta F=2xw(2v^2-x)\) becomes
\(\Delta\kappa=T\). Differentiating (7) and then using
(8) gives
\[
\begin{split}
\Delta a&=0,\\
\Delta b&=a^2-\lambda aT,\\
\Delta c&=2V^2W-\lambda\kappa a^2-\lambda bT.
\end{split}
\tag{10}
\]
In the last identity, the two terms containing
\(\lambda^2\kappa aT\) cancel. This cancellation is included
in the direct physical-coordinate check.

The highest coefficient of \(T\) is
\([\lambda^3]T=4a^4\kappa\). Therefore the relative degree
of \(\Delta\) is exactly four, with
\[
\Delta|_{\mathcal K}=\sum_{j=0}^4\lambda^j D_j,
\qquad
D_4=-4a^4\kappa L,\qquad
L=a\partial_b+b\partial_c.
\tag{11}
\]
Equivalently, the actual highest commutator is
\[
\frac1{4!}(\operatorname{ad}_\delta)^4\Delta
 =\widetilde D_4,\qquad
(\operatorname{ad}_\delta)^5\Delta=0.
\tag{12}
\]
The tilde denotes extension from \(\mathcal K\) fixing
\(\lambda\). These are coefficients of the actual canonical
action in a fully verified polynomial frame.

The two nonzero coefficients in (11) have gcd
\(a^4\kappa\), up to a nonzero scalar. Since \(\gcd(a,b)=1\),
the primitive part is already
\[
L=a\partial_b+b\partial_c.
\]
It is a genuine LND, but it has no slice: every value lies
in the proper ideal \((a,b)\). More precisely,
\[
\ker L=\mathbb C[a,\kappa],\qquad
\ker L^2=\mathbb C[a,\kappa]\oplus
            b\mathbb C[a,\kappa],\qquad
\operatorname{pl}(L)=a\mathbb C[a,\kappa].
\tag{13}
\]
To verify the full equalities, localize at \(a\), where
\(\mathcal K_a=\mathbb C[a,a^{-1},\kappa][b]\) and
\(L=a\partial_b\). Reduction modulo \(a\), with
\(\kappa=-b^2\), removes all possible poles by the same
even/odd argument as Section 1. In particular this conclusion
is not inferred from finitely many unsuccessful slice searches.
The full plinth of \(D_4\) is
\(a^5\kappa\mathbb C[a,\kappa]\), up to a nonzero scalar.

Nor can a different regular multiple within the rational line
\(\operatorname{Frac}(\mathcal K)L\) remove this obstruction.
A reduced denominator would divide both coefficients \(a,b\),
so every such regular multiple is a polynomial multiple of \(L\).
All still vanish on \(a=b=0\). This is the existing primitive
UFD saturation argument, applied to this explicit top.

## 5. The horizontal action really moves the old plane

The common rational invariants are exactly
\[
\operatorname{Frac}(B)^\delta\cap
\operatorname{Frac}(B)^\Delta=\mathbb C(a),
\qquad \ker\delta\cap\ker\Delta=\mathbb C[a].
\tag{14}
\]
Indeed a \(\delta\)-invariant rational function belongs to
\(\mathbb C(a,b,c)\). If also killed by \(\Delta\), it is
killed by the constant coefficient
\[
D_0=a^2\partial_b+2b\partial_c
\]
and by \(L\), because the nonzero scalar multiplier in
\(D_4\) can be cancelled in the field. Their coefficient
determinant in the \(b,c\) directions is
\[
\det\begin{pmatrix}a^2&2b\\a&b\end{pmatrix}=ab(a-2)\ne0.
\tag{15}
\]
Thus both partial derivatives in \(b,c\) vanish, proving
the rational assertion in characteristic zero. Intersecting
with the polynomial ring proves the ring assertion.

There is a common coordinate \(a=x\), as expected for this
rank-two base, but no common invariant plane or even a joint
rational subfield of transcendence degree two.
The old plane algebra \(C_B=\mathbb C[\lambda,x,H]\) is
not preserved setwise by \(\delta\). For an explicit check,
\[
\left.\Delta(\delta H)\right|_{
 \lambda=0,\,x=1,\,w=0,\,z=1}=-2\ne0.
\tag{16}
\]
Consequently \(\delta H\notin\ker\Delta=C_B\).

## 6. A good horizontal choice on the same modification

Keep exactly the same \(B\) and \(\Delta\), but use
\[
\delta_0=\partial_\lambda
\]
in the original physical coordinates \(\lambda,x,w,z\).
It too is an LND with slice \(\lambda\). In that frame
\[
\Delta=x^2\partial_w+
       (2w+4\lambda xw+2\lambda^2x^2w)\partial_z.
\tag{17}
\]
Its primitive highest coefficient is \(\partial_z\), which
has the polynomial slice \(z\). Its kernel together with
\(\delta_0\) is the coordinate plane \(\mathbb C[x,w]\).

Thus one and the same canonical homotopy admits a successful
and an unsuccessful primitive-top extraction, depending on
the horizontal action. The bad action (6) is the explicit
replica twist \(\delta_0+FL_0\); its failure is not caused
by lack of a horizontal slice, a nonpolynomial inverse, or a
unit-plinth starting action.

The example refutes a universal statement about all such
horizontal choices. It neither refutes a theorem asserting
the existence of some good choice nor gives a way to choose
one on an arbitrary unknown polynomial cylinder. It also
does not exclude a different Lie word or a changed rational
direction yielding a useful action.
