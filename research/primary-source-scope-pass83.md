# Primary inputs to the return-map and socle deductions

The elementary return-map/Taylor argument proves integral dependence
directly. Its main proof does not need a valuative criterion. A
published Briancon--Skoda inclusion then gives cubic membership;
the separate field-valued Jacobian/socle criterion excludes isolated
good-speed zeros. The following record distinguishes those inputs
from alternative proof routes and from fresh proof reconstructions.

## Lipman and Sathaye

Joseph Lipman and Avinash Sathaye, *Jacobian ideals and a theorem of
Briancon--Skoda*, Michigan Mathematical Journal 28 (1981), 199--222.
[Author-hosted original PDF](https://www.math.purdue.edu/~jlipman/papers-older/%5B1981%5D%20Jacobian%20ideals%20and%20a%20theorem%20of%20Briancon-Skoda.pdf).

Theorem 1 on printed page 199 applies with its auxiliary ideal zero
in the regular Noetherian domain C[x_1,x_2,x_3]. With three generators,
d=2 and lambda=1, it gives the required inclusion of the integral
closure of the third power into the ideal. Zero generator entries
can be retained. It does not require the ideal to be primary.

Root and Geometry independently inspected printed pages 199--203
visually. This includes Theorem 1, its complete deduction from
Theorem 1-prime, the core Theorem 2 statement, and the auxiliary
lemma and proof. Neither claims here to have reconstructed the core
Theorem 2 proofs in Sections 2--3. Root used PDF rendering because
text extraction did not preserve this scanned source. Jet separately
inspected printed page 199, including Theorem 1 and its deduction.

## Hickel

M. Hickel, *Une note a propos du Jacobien de n fonctions holomorphes
a l'origine de C^n*, arXiv:0802.0426v1, February 4, 2008.
[Original arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0802.0426).

Theorem 1.1 on printed page 2 is used only over the field C. It
identifies when the Jacobian lies in the generated ideal, and identifies
its nonzero socle class in the isolated case. This is a source theorem,
not a new claim of the investigation. Completion is applied at actual
complex closed points, with faithful flatness used to test membership.

Root and General read the complete field statement, the residue
inputs in Section 2, and the field-specialized proofs in Section 3,
including Theorems 3.1 and 3.3. The field restriction matters: the
printed discussion also includes broader Artinian-base assertions,
which are not imported. This is not a complete read of the second
half of the paper or of Lipman's original residue-theory sources.
Jet independently checked the full displayed field statement; Geometry
read the entire new socle deduction but did not claim that source read.
