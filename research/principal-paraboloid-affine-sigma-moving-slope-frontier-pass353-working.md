# Affine sigma with moving slope: specialization exclusion and two coefficient families

Working necessary conditions in the full actual chart349, under
\(\deg_v\sigma=1\): the centered first intercept, and then \(y,\theta\),
are affine in \(\sigma\). The slope \(y_\sigma\) is a complex constant.
The coefficient \(B\) is either complex affine with nonzero slope, or
a complex constant plus one simple reciprocal pole. The first potential
\(f\) has the corresponding explicit form below.

These are necessary conditions, not recognition or realization of either
remaining family. No coefficient-field translation of an original
coordinate is used. The all-X specialization argument excludes the
quadratic-centered-intercept mode before the remaining scalar analysis.

## 1. Full actual entry and the exact all-X return

Retain all hypotheses of
[349](principal-paraboloid-variable-intercept-slope-quadric-reduction-pass349-working.md),
in particular the full actual chart264 and the full fourth potential296:
\[
\begin{gathered}
\mathcal B=\mathbb C[X,Y,Z,W]=A[u],\quad E=\partial_u,\quad
\deg_E(X,Y,Z,W)=(1,2,2,2),\\
I_{\rm initial}=(X^2Z-Y^2)\text{ is the full initial kernel},\quad
a_0=EX\ne0,\\
O=L[v]=L[y,\sigma,\theta],\quad Nv=1,\quad
K=L(v)=\mathbb C(y,z,w),\quad [L:\mathbb C(t,s)]<\infty,\\
k=s,\quad n=Bs+f,\quad B,f\in F:=\ker_L\partial_s,
\quad b:=B'\ne0.
\end{gathered}
\tag{1}
\]
The field \(F\) is finite over \(\mathbb C(t)\), with derivative
constants \(\mathbb C\). All coefficient derivatives are exact,
initially fixing the original \(v\). Assume in addition
\(\deg_v\sigma=1\), and use \(P=\sigma\) as the polynomial parameter
of \(O=L[P]\). Its speed \(\sigma_v\) belongs to \(L^*\). Therefore
the full literal map has calibration
\[
\begin{aligned}
Y&=y+PX+tX^2,\\
Z&=2ty+P^2+\theta+n+(2tP+s)X+t^2X^2,\\
W&=\ell-2Py-n_tP-B\theta+\theta X+sX^2,\\
\det\frac{\partial(Y,Z,W)}{\partial(t,s,P)}
 &=-a_0^4/\sigma_v\in L^*.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
The new coefficient partials agree with the old partials on \(L\)
and now fix \(P\). The parameter determinant is \(\sigma_v\), not one.

There is an exact original-ring input for every specialization of X:
\[
L[\mathcal B]=L[X,Y,Z,W]=L[X,P].
\tag{3}
\]
Indeed \(E\) preserves \(L[\mathcal B]\), kills \(L\), and
\(EY/a_0-2tX=P\). This gives one inclusion; the polynomial expressions
(2) give the other. After extending the coefficient field and setting
\(X=X_0\), the specialized three polynomials must still generate the
entire polynomial ring in \(P\). This is not an assertion about an
actual complex fiber when \(X_0\notin\mathbb C\).

## 2. Degree reduction to affine centered intercepts

Keep349's centering and coefficient names:
\[
\begin{gathered}
x=y+n_t/2-BP,\quad A_2=n_{tt}/2-B^2,\quad C_2=\ell_s+B^2,
\quad T=\ell_t,\quad \bar h=h-B\ell+n_t^2/4,\\
x^2+A_2P^2-TP+(bP-C_2)\theta=\bar h.
\end{gathered}
\tag{4}
\]
If \(r=\deg_Px\ge2\), polynomiality of \(\theta\) in (4) gives
\(\deg_P\theta=2r-1\), with leading coefficient \(-x_r^2/b\).
The centered fourth polynomial is the complete expression (13),(14)
of349. Its highest relevant terms are
\[
b\theta x-2bP^2x+\frac{b'}2P^2\theta
-\frac{\ell_{ss}}2\theta^2,
\tag{5}
\]
with the other terms of strictly smaller degree than \(\theta^2\).
Thus the constant fourth level first forces \(\ell_{ss}=0\).
If \(r\ge3\), the term \(b\theta x\) then has uniquely highest
degree \(3r-1\), exceeding \(2r+1\), \(r+2\), and the remaining
degrees. Its coefficient is nonzero. Hence \(r\le2\).

We next exclude \(r=2\) using (3), without needing any extra differential
constraint on its coefficients. Write
\[
y=\alpha P^2+jP+k_0,\qquad
\theta=v_3P^3+v_2P^2+v_1P+v_0,\qquad
\alpha\ne0,\quad v_3=-\alpha^2/b\ne0.
\tag{6}
\]
All these coefficients lie in \(L\). Define polynomials over \(L[X]\)
\[
K_X=X-B+2b/\alpha,\qquad T_X=W-K_XZ.
\tag{7}
\]
The cubic terms cancel, so \(\deg_PT_X\le2\). Let \(T_2(X)\) be its
quadratic coefficient. Direct expansion of the full map (2) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
T_2&=-2j-(2b/\alpha)v_2-K_X(1+2t\alpha),\\
[P](\alpha T_X-T_2Y)
 &=-\alpha(2k_0+n_t)-2bv_1
    +\{2j+2bv_2/\alpha+K_X\}(j+X).
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]
The second polynomial is monic quadratic in X. Over an algebraic
closure of \(L\), choose a root \(X_0\). Then
\(\alpha T_{X_0}-T_2(X_0)Y_{X_0}\) is constant in P. Thus the
specialized ring in (3) is generated by \(Y_{X_0}\) of exact degree
two and \(Z_{X_0}\) of exact degree three.

That ring cannot be the whole polynomial ring. Center the quadratic
by a translation of P. After removing the cubic's even part, the ring
has the form
\[
\overline L[\xi^2,\xi(c_3\xi^2+c_1)]
=\overline L[\xi^2]\oplus
 \xi(c_3\xi^2+c_1)\overline L[\xi^2],\qquad c_3\ne0.
\tag{9}
\]
Its odd module does not contain \(\xi\), since the indicated factor
is not a unit. This contradicts (3). We have proved \(\deg_Px\le1\).
Equation (4) then implies \(\deg_P\theta\le1\), and so also
\(\deg_Py\le1\). This includes constant individual intercepts.

## 3. The full scalar's cubic coefficient

Write the remaining functions as
\[
x=aP+d,\quad y=jP+k_0,\quad \theta=cP+e,
\qquad j=a+B,\quad k_0=d-n_t/2,
\tag{10}
\]
with \(a,d,c,e\in L\); no one of them is presumed nonzero. The
coefficients of (4) give
\[
bc=-a^2-A_2,\qquad be=T-2ad+C_2c.
\tag{11}
\]
The cubic coefficient of the complete fourth level is
\[
\frac{n_{ttt}}6-2Bb-2ba+\frac{b'}2c=0.
\tag{12}
\]
These equations involve coefficient derivatives at fixed P. In
particular, differentiating (11),(12) in s gives
\[
bc_s=-2aa_s-b'/2,\qquad
12R a_s=2bb''-3(b')^2,\qquad R=ab'+2b^2.
\tag{13}
\]

Here is the needed scalar coefficient with its holding variables
explicit. Set X=0 in the full calibrated map (2), which retains the
same nonzero scalar. Subtract \(2t\) times the Y differential row from
the Z row. Then add \(2P\) times the first row and B times that second row
to the W row. The rows become, in \(dt,ds,dP\),
\[
\begin{pmatrix}
dy\\
d\theta+2P\,dP+(2y+n_t)dt+Bds\\
-H_Pdt-H_Vds-H_UdP
\end{pmatrix},
\tag{14}
\]
where H is the uncentered third polynomial of349. The level tangent
identity is \(H_P=-H_Uj-H_Vc\), with
\(H_U=2(aP+d)\), \(H_V=bP-C_2\).
All rows here are covectors; there is no differentiation of the
row multipliers \(2P,B\).

Before substituting coefficient constraints, the cubic determinant
coefficient is
\[
2\{4Baa_s+4a^2a_s+aa_sc_t-aa_tc_s-ab c_s
      +a_sbc+a_tb+b^2\}.
\tag{15}
\]
Differentiating (11) in t and using (12) yields
\[
bc_t=-2aa_t-6ba-4Bb+b'c/2.
\tag{16}
\]
Together with (13), this reduces (15) to
\[
[P^3]J_{t,s,P}=\frac{R}{b}(a_t+ca_s+b).
\tag{17}
\]
The scalar calibration (2) forces this expression to vanish.

If \(R\ne0\), we obtain \(a_t+ca_s=-b\). If \(R=0\), then
\(b'\ne0\), \(a=-2b^2/b'\in F\), and hence \(a_s=0\).
Equation (13) gives \(2bb''=3(b')^2\), which directly differentiates
to \(a_t=-b\). Thus the same conclusion holds in this zero-factor
case, without division by R.

## 4. Exact transport constants and the coefficient identity

Define the derivation \(\delta=\partial_t+c\partial_s\) on L.
It preserves L, has \(\delta t=1\), and restricts to the original
t-derivative on F. Equations (13),(16),(17) imply
\[
\delta j=0,\qquad \delta c=-4j.
\tag{18}
\]
Consequently
\[
\kappa=c+4tj,\qquad \rho=s-\kappa t+2jt^2,
\qquad j,\kappa,\rho\in G:=\ker_L\delta,
\quad s=\kappa t-2jt^2+\rho.
\tag{19}
\]
At least one of these three constants is nonconstant over \(\mathbb C\),
since t and s are algebraically independent. The field L is finite over
\(G(t)\): s belongs to that field and L is finite over \(\mathbb C(t,s)\).
Also \(F\cap G=\mathbb C\), since \(\delta|_F=\partial_t\).
These are exact differential-field statements, not ambient coordinates.

Substitute (19), \(a=j-B\), and \(c=\kappa-4tj\) in (11).
The resulting complete coefficient identity is
\[
j^2-(2B+4tb+t^2b')j+(b+tb'/2)\kappa+(b'/2)\rho+f''/2=0.
\tag{20}
\]
We will separate its coefficient fields without presuming either one
is rational. If elements of G are linearly independent over
\(\mathbb C\), they remain independent over F. Indeed, a shortest
nonzero F-linear relation, normalized to make one coefficient one,
can be differentiated by \(\delta\). Minimality makes all the other
normalized coefficients \(\delta\)-constant, hence complex. This
contradicts the original complex independence.

It follows that a complex linear functional on the finite-dimensional
span of \(1,j,j^2,\kappa,\rho\) can legitimately be applied coefficientwise
to (20). In particular a functional taking 1 to 1 shows
\[
f''\in\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb C}
 \{1,\ 2B+4tb+t^2b',\ b+tb'/2,\ b'/2\}.
\tag{21}
\]

## 5. The slope j is complex, including every zero boundary

Suppose first that \(j\notin\mathbb C\). Then \(1,j,j^2\) are
complex-linearly independent. Take a functional sending \(j^2\) to 1
and sending j and 1 to zero. For some \(u_0,v_0\in\mathbb C\),
(20) gives
\[
(u_0t+v_0)b'+2u_0b=-2.
\tag{22}
\]
The constants cannot both vanish. If \(u_0=0\), exact integration in F
gives a complex quadratic B with nonzero quadratic coefficient.
By (21), \(f''\) is then a complex polynomial of degree at most two.
In (20), the coefficient of \(t^2\) forces j to be complex, since
only \(-(2B+4tb+t^2b')j\) and \(f''/2\) have that degree and the
quadratic coefficient of the former parenthesis is nonzero.

If \(u_0\ne0\), put \(T=t+v_0/u_0\). Multiplying (22) by T and
integrating exactly gives
\[
b=-1/u_0+c_0/T^2,\qquad
B=b_1T+b_0+c_{-1}/T,
\quad b_1=-1/u_0\ne0,
\tag{23}
\]
with all constants complex. This integration uses
\((T^2b)'=-2T/u_0\), not a rational ansatz.
Under a complex translation \(T=t-\tau\), merely as notation,
replace \(\kappa,\rho\) by
\(\kappa-4\tau j,\rho+\tau\kappa-2\tau^2j\). They remain in G,
and (19),(20) retain their form with T in place of t. For (23) the
three coefficient functions in (20) become
\[
2B+4Tb+T^2b'=6b_1T+2b_0,\quad
b+Tb'/2=b_1,\quad b'/2=c_{-1}/T^3.
\tag{24}
\]
Thus (21) puts \(f''\) in the complex span of \(1,T,T^{-3}\).
The coefficient of T in (20) again forces j to be complex because
\(b_1\ne0\). Comparison of these coefficients is legitimate: t is
transcendental over G, as \(\delta t=1\). Both cases contradict the
assumption. Therefore
\[
\boxed{j=y_P\in\mathbb C.}
\tag{25}
\]
The possibility \(j=0\) has not been removed.

## 6. The two remaining exact coefficient families

If \(b'=0\), then \(b\in\mathbb C^*\) and
\(B=bt+B_0\), \(B_0\in\mathbb C\). Equation (20) puts
\(\kappa\) in \(F\cap G=\mathbb C\). Exact integration gives
\[
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
B&=bt+B_0,\qquad b\in\mathbb C^*,\\
f&=2bj\,t^3+(2B_0j-j^2-b\kappa)t^2+f_1t+f_0,\\
c&=\kappa-4tj,\qquad
s=\kappa t-2jt^2+\rho,
\end{aligned}}
\quad
j,B_0,\kappa,f_1,f_0\in\mathbb C,
\quad \rho\in G\setminus\mathbb C.
\tag{26}
\]
The last nonconstancy follows from the independence of t and s.

Suppose instead \(b'\ne0\). Solving (20) for \(\rho\) gives
\[
\rho=-(t+2b/b')\kappa+r_0,
\qquad r_0\in F.
\tag{27}
\]
If \(\kappa\) were complex, \(\rho\) would also belong to
\(F\cap G=\mathbb C\), a contradiction. Thus \(\kappa\) is
nonconstant. Differentiating (27) by \(\delta\) and using the
independence of \(1,\kappa\) over F forces
\(t+2b/b'=\tau\in\mathbb C\). Put \(T=t-\tau\).
Then \((T^2b)'=0\), and exact integration gives
\(B=B_0+c_{-1}/T\), with \(c_{-1}\in\mathbb C^*\).
Use the translated constants specified before (24). Equation (20)
now forces the translated \(\rho\) to be complex and yields
\[
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
B&=B_0+c_{-1}/T,\qquad T=t-\tau,\quad c_{-1}\in\mathbb C^*,\\
f&=(-j^2+2B_0j)T^2-c_{-1}\rho/T+f_1T+f_0,\\
c&=\kappa-4Tj,\qquad s=\kappa T-2jT^2+\rho,
\end{aligned}}
\quad
j,B_0,\tau,\rho,f_1,f_0\in\mathbb C,
\quad\kappa\in G\setminus\mathbb C.
\tag{28}
\]
No logarithmic or algebraic integration term is hidden here: the
displayed primitives have the required second derivative, and the
difference has second derivative zero in F, hence is a complex affine
function. This also treats zero values of j, the integration constants,
or the simple-pole coefficient of f.

## 7. Remaining boundary and verification scope

The result excludes the affine-sigma quadratic-x profile and forces
the affine profile to satisfy (25)--(28). It has not used the remaining
lower scalar coefficients to prove either surviving family impossible
or recognized. The coefficient functions d,e,ell and the original
slice conditions still matter. The strong actual field return in (1)
is retained, not replaced by a formal realization of (26) or (28).

In particular the constants \(\kappa,\rho\) of \(\delta\) are not
declared ambient polynomial coordinates. No nonconstant B(t) has been
used to translate the actual X. The complex translation of t in
Section5 is only a rewrite of identities in L.
