# Actual height-two initial ideals have no uniform relation-weight bound

Status: bounded countercontrol to a proposed next-step lemma.
A globally sliced exact E-time \((1,1,2,2)\) polynomial frame can
have full initial ideal of height two and no nonzero relation of
weight two. A polynomial family strengthens this: the least weight
of a relation outside one fixed principal ideal is unbounded, although
the supplied time profile stays \((1,1,2,2)\).
The construction has an explicit polynomial inverse, and its selected
bad-coordinate shifts are two and one.

Its invariant algebra is already a polynomial three-ring, with a
coordinate slice of unit speed. It is not a cancellation counterexample,
a true minimum-degree-six frame, or an obstruction to finding an
invariant coordinate by other transformations.

## 1. The proposed implication and the actual countercontrol

The [initial-bound theorem157](two-linear-two-quadratic-initial-bound-pass157-working.md)
leaves a height-two possibility for the algebra generated by four
coordinate initials. Its retained height-two fixture has weight-two
relations, so it does not answer the following stronger proposal:

> For every actual globally sliced exact1122 frame with
> nonproportional good speeds and height-two full initial ideal,
> that ideal has a nonzero weight-two element.

Such an element would have the form
\(\alpha\mathsf Z+\beta\mathsf W+Q_2(\mathsf X,\mathsf Y)\),
where \((\alpha,\beta)\ne(0,0)\). The actual inverse in157, equation11,
would turn it into a third E-affine slot, and the supplied three-good
recognition156 would apply. The assertion itself is false.

Start instead with independent polynomial coordinates
\[
B=\mathbb C[u,t,y,z],\qquad E=\partial_u,\qquad
A=\ker E=\mathbb C[t,y,z].
\tag{1}
\]
Define
\[
\begin{aligned}
X&=u,&
Y&=y+tu,\\
Z&=z+(3t^2y+t^4)u+t^3u^2,&
W&=t+XZ-Y^3\\
 &=t-y^3+(z-3ty^2)u+t^4u^2 .
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
All formulas are in the original polynomial ring. In particular
the cancellation in the second expression for \(W\) is exact,
including its cubic time terms.

This is a triangular composition over \(\mathbb C[u]\). First
replace \(y\) by \(Y=y+tu\), retaining \(t,z\). Next replace \(z\)
by \(Z\), whose added polynomial can be written using \(u,t,Y\)
by substituting \(y=Y-tu\). Finally replace the retained variable
\(t\) by \(W=t+uZ-Y^3\), retaining \(u,Y,Z\).
Each step has an elementary polynomial inverse.

## 2. Both full polynomial inverse orders

Given independent \(X,Y,Z,W\), define, in this order,
\[
\widehat u=X,\qquad
\widehat t=W-XZ+Y^3,\qquad
\widehat y=Y-X\widehat t,\qquad
\widehat z=
 Z-(3\widehat t^{\,2}\widehat y+\widehat t^{\,4})X
   -\widehat t^{\,3}X^2 .
\tag{3}
\]
These are polynomials, not rational expressions on an open set.

After substituting (2), the identity \(W=t+XZ-Y^3\) gives
\(\widehat t=t\), then \(\widehat y=y\) and
\(\widehat z=z\); also \(\widehat u=u\).
Thus inverse after forward is the identity.

Conversely start with independent \(X,Y,Z,W\) and apply (3).
The reconstructed forward first coordinate is \(X\).
The reconstructed second coordinate is
\(\widehat y+\widehat tX=Y\).
The definition of \(\widehat z\) gives the reconstructed third
coordinate \(Z\). Therefore the reconstructed fourth coordinate is
\(\widehat t+XZ-Y^3=W\).
This proves the other full composition.

Consequently
\[
B=\mathbb C[X,Y,Z,W]=A[u],\qquad Eu=EX=1.
\tag{4}
\]
The last equality already recognizes \(A\) by the coordinate slice.
It must not be omitted when interpreting the example.

## 3. Exact time degrees and failure of a weight-two relation

The ordinary time-leading coefficients in (2) are
\[
(a,b,e,h)=(1,t,t^3,t^4),\qquad
(\deg_E X,\deg_E Y,\deg_E Z,\deg_E W)=(1,1,2,2).
\tag{5}
\]
Every displayed coefficient is a nonzero polynomial in \(A\).
The good speeds \(1,t\) are not proportional over \(\mathbb C\).
The second derivatives are \(E^2Z=2t^3\) and \(E^2W=2t^4\),
so the coefficients in (5) include the usual factorial division.

Give formal source variables weights \((1,1,2,2)\), and let
\(\eta\) be independent over \(A\). The initial map is
\[
\Theta:
\mathbb C[\mathsf X,\mathsf Y,\mathsf Z,\mathsf W]
\longrightarrow A[\eta],\qquad
(\mathsf X,\mathsf Y,\mathsf Z,\mathsf W)
\longmapsto(\eta,t\eta,t^3\eta^2,t^4\eta^2).
\tag{6}
\]
Its image lies in \(\mathbb C[t,\eta]\). Its fraction field contains
\(\eta\) and \(t=(t\eta)/\eta\), so its transcendence degree is
exactly two. The full prime kernel therefore has height two.

The source weight-two space has basis
\(\mathsf X^2,\mathsf X\mathsf Y,\mathsf Y^2,\mathsf Z,\mathsf W\).
Their images are
\[
\eta^2,\quad t\eta^2,\quad t^2\eta^2,\quad
t^3\eta^2,\quad t^4\eta^2.
\tag{7}
\]
They are complex-linearly independent. Thus
\[
(\ker\Theta)_2=0.
\tag{8}
\]
There is no binary quadratic correction to any nonzero constant
linear combination of the two bad slots that cancels its quadratic
time coefficient.

## 4. The full initial ideal, not just three vanishing relations

In this section only, omit the sans-serif font for the formal
source variables. Put
\[
F_1=XZ-Y^3,\qquad F_2=XW-YZ,\qquad
F_3=Y^2W-Z^2,\qquad I=(F_1,F_2,F_3).
\tag{9}
\]
All three vanish under (6), so \(I\subseteq\ker\Theta\).
The first two have weight three and the third has weight four.

Here is a direct proof of equality. With lexicographic order
\(W>Z>Y>X\), the leading monomials of these displayed generators
are \(XZ,XW,Y^2W\), respectively.
Their replacements
\[
XZ\rightsquigarrow Y^3,\qquad
XW\rightsquigarrow YZ,\qquad
Y^2W\rightsquigarrow Z^2
\tag{10}
\]
strictly decrease that monomial order, so every polynomial reduces
modulo \(I\) to a complex linear combination of monomials in these
three disjoint families:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\mathrm{A}:&X^iY^j,\quad i>0,\ j\ge0,\\
\mathrm{B}:&Y^jZ^k,\quad j,k\ge0,\\
\mathrm{C}:&Y^jZ^kW^\ell,\quad
             \ell>0,\ k\ge0,\ j\in\{0,1\}.
\end{array}
\tag{11}
\]
No uniqueness of this reduction is assumed in advance.

For such a monomial, write its image as \(\eta^A t^B\).
In familyA, \(A=i+j\), \(B=j\), so \(A>B\).
In familyB, \(A=j+2k\), \(B=j+3k\), and
\[
B-A=k\ge0,\qquad 3A-2B=j\ge0.
\tag{12}
\]
These recover \(j,k\) uniquely.
In familyC, \(A=j+2k+2\ell\), \(B=j+3k+4\ell\), so
\(B-A=k+2\ell>0\) and \(3A-2B=j-2\ell<0\).
The parity of \(A\) determines \(j\in\{0,1\}\), then
\[
\ell=(j-3A+2B)/2,\qquad k=(A-j)/2-\ell .
\tag{13}
\]
Thus distinct monomials in (11) have distinct images, including
between the three families. Their images are linearly independent.

If a polynomial lies in \(\ker\Theta\), reduce it to (11).
The reduced expression still has image zero, so all its coefficients
are zero. The original polynomial belongs to \(I\). Hence
\[
\boxed{\ker\Theta=(XZ-Y^3,\ XW-YZ,\ Y^2W-Z^2).}
\tag{14}
\]
This establishes the full ideal and its primality by an actual
injective quotient map, without assuming a determinantal-ideal
classification. The generated initial algebra is not the whole
associated graded algebra \(A[\eta]\).

## 5. Literal selected partials and exact shifts

Differentiate the actual inverse (3), holding the other original
capital coordinates fixed. In base coordinates \((u,t,y,z)\), the
two bad-coordinate partials have columns
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrr}
 &u&t&y&z\\ \hline
\partial_Z&0&-u&u^2&1+(6ty+4t^3)u^2\\
\partial_W&0&1&-u&-(6ty+4t^3)u .
\end{array}
\tag{15}
\]
For instance \(t_Z=-X\), \(y_Z=X^2\); differentiating the last
formula in (3) cancels its two cubic terms and gives the fourth
entry of the first row. The second row follows from
\(t_W=1\), \(y_W=-X\), with the quadratic terms canceling.

These are literal polynomial coordinate partials because both
coordinate inverse orders have already been proved. They are not
formal derivations chosen merely to fit the leading data.

Their exact all-ring E-degree shifts are
\[
k_{\partial_Z}=2,\qquad k_{\partial_W}=1.
\tag{16}
\]
Indeed they kill \(u\), and their coefficients on the algebra
generators \(t,y,z\) have degrees bounded by two and one.
The derivation rule extends the bound to every element of \(A[u]\);
the images of \(y\) attain the respective bounds.

The intrinsic leading derivations are
\[
N_Z=[u^2](\partial_Z|_A)=D,\qquad
N_W=[u](\partial_W|_A)=-D,\qquad
D=\partial_y+(6ty+4t^3)\partial_z .
\tag{17}
\]
They both fix all four leaders in (5).
In fact \(D\) is primitive and has the actual slice \(y\).
If
\[
H=z-3ty^2-4t^3y,
\quad
A=\mathbb C[t,y,H],
\quad
z=H+3ty^2+4t^3y,
\tag{18}
\]
then \(D=\partial_y\) in this actual coordinate system and
\(\ker D=\mathbb C[t,H]\).
Thus the two selected leading fields are dependent, even though
the full initial ideal has height two. Neither their independence
nor a quadratic initial relation follows from that height.

## 6. A whole family with unbounded necessary relation weight

Replace \(t^4\) in (2) by any \(f(t)\in\mathbb C[t]\) of degree
at least four:
\[
\begin{aligned}
X&=u,\qquad Y=y+tu,\\
Z&=z+(3t^2y+f(t))u+t^3u^2,\\
W&=t+XZ-Y^3
 =t-y^3+(z-3ty^2)u+f(t)u^2 .
\end{aligned}
\tag{19}
\]
The same staged inverse is valid, replacing
\(\widehat t^{\,4}\) by \(f(\widehat t)\) in (3).
The verification of both inverse orders in Section2 is unchanged:
first recover \(t=W-XZ+Y^3\), then \(y\), then \(z\).
Thus these are actual polynomial frames, not arbitrary coefficient data.

Their leaders are \((1,t,t^3,f(t))\), and the initial image again
has fraction field \(\mathbb C(t,\eta)\).
The five functions \(1,t,t^2,t^3,f(t)\) are linearly independent,
so the full height-two kernel still has no weight-two element.
The two literal selected columns (15) replace \(4t^3\) by
\(f'(t)\); therefore
\[
k_{\partial_Z}=2,\quad k_{\partial_W}=1,\quad
N_Z=-N_W=\partial_y+(6ty+f'(t))\partial_z,\quad
H_f=z-3ty^2-f'(t)y .
\tag{20}
\]
Again \(A=\mathbb C[t,y,H_f]\), and the selected leading field
is the actual partial in \(y\).

Specialize now to \(f(t)=t^n\), where \(n\ge4\) is an integer.
Write \(I_n\) for the full kernel of the resulting initial map.
It is a graded prime of height two and contains
\[
F_1=XZ-Y^3,\qquad
(X,Y,Z,W)\longmapsto
(\eta,t\eta,t^3\eta^2,t^n\eta^2).
\tag{21}
\]
The kernel of the restriction to \(\mathbb C[X,Y,Z]\) is
exactly \((F_1)\).
Indeed its image has transcendence degree two and \(F_1\) is
irreducible: it is primitive linear in \(Z\), with coefficients
\(X,-Y^3\) coprime in \(\mathbb C[X,Y]\).

Let \(P\in I_n\) be homogeneous of weight \(M\), and expand
\(P=\sum_j W^jP_j(X,Y,Z)\).
Here \(P_j\) has weight \(M-2j\).
A monomial \(X^iY^bZ^c\) of that weight maps to
\(\eta^{M-2j}t^{b+3c}\), where
\[
0\le b+3c\le \left\lfloor\frac{3(M-2j)}2\right\rfloor.
\tag{22}
\]
After multiplying by the image of \(W^j\), the possible
t-exponents lie in the interval
\[
\left[nj,\;nj+\left\lfloor\frac{3(M-2j)}2\right\rfloor\right].
\tag{23}
\]
If \(n>3M/2\), these intervals are pairwise disjoint.
For adjacent indices the upper endpoint of the first is at most
\(nj+3M/2-3j<n(j+1)\).
Since every term has the same \(\eta\)-exponent \(M\), vanishing
of the full image forces the image of each \(P_j\) to vanish.
The exact triple kernel then gives \(P_j\in(F_1)\) for every \(j\),
and hence \(P\in(F_1)\).

This proves that any relation of weight less than \(2n/3\) is
divisible by \(F_1\).
Write \(n=3m+r\), with \(r\in\{0,1,2\}\). The explicit polynomial
\[
X^{\,2m+r-2}W-Y^rZ^m
\quad\text{has weight }2m+r=\left\lceil\frac{2n}{3}\right\rceil
\quad\text{and belongs to }I_n\setminus(F_1).
\tag{24}
\]
The exponent of \(X\) is nonnegative for \(n\ge4\).
Its leading evaluation vanishes because \(3m+r=n\).
It is not divisible by \(F_1\): in the domain
\((\mathbb C[X,Y,Z]/(F_1))[W]\), its W-coefficient is the nonzero
power \(X^{2m+r-2}\).
Consequently the least possible weight of a relation outside
\((F_1)\) is exactly \(\lceil2n/3\rceil\).

In particular, for any fixed integer cutoff \(D\ge3\), choosing
\(n>3D/2\) makes every relation of weight at most \(D\) belong
to \((F_1)\).
The ideal \(I_n\) is graded, so the conclusion also applies to
inhomogeneous elements whose highest weight is at most \(D\),
by applying it to each homogeneous component.
Such elements cannot generate the full height-two ideal.
Thus there is no uniform bound, based only on the exact1122 time
profile, for the weights needed to generate the full initial ideal.
This failure does not occur in the retained height-one theorem,
whose full-principal-kernel assumption is essential.

There is an important separate bound in this same family:
\(t=W-XZ+Y^3\) is an actual invariant ambient coordinate of
weighted degree three, independently of \(n\).
The unbounded weight of a second initial relation therefore does
not imply unbounded coordinate-recognition complexity.
It does not obstruct every bounded-weight invariant lift.

## 7. The surviving entry question

The countercontrol rules out both the proposed weight-two shortcut
and a uniform bounded-weight replacement for generating a height-two
initial ideal in actual globally sliced exact1122 frames.
It does not rule out recognition of every height-two frame.
Here recognition is elementary from \(X=u\), and (3) gives the
invariant coordinates \(t,y,z\) explicitly.

In particular, this frame is not a true global minimizer of the sum
of E-degrees. Its supplied sum is six, while the original frame
\((u,t,y,z)\) has sum one. The example therefore does not refute a
statement restricted to hypothetical nonpolynomial kernels or true
global minimum-six frames. Those restrictions require separate
arguments, not an inference from a failed local shear.

The [three-good synthesis156](three-good-all-degree-recognition-pass156-working.md)
only locates the next possible true minimum at six or higher.
The [positive-weight bound158](arbitrary-positive-weight-initial-bound-pass158-working.md)
bounds a height-one relation by the sum of time degrees minus two;
it gives neither a height-two principal relation nor an upper bound
on that sum. The [retained entry controls124](unrestricted-entry-frontier-pass124.md)
also rule out insisting on strict elementary descent at every step.
These distinct gaps remain after restricted radical recognition211.
