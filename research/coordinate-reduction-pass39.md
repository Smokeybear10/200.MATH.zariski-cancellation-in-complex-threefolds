# Exact coordinate-family tests for adjoint-height reduction

A supplied polynomial family of actual coordinate changes admits an exact,
finite test for adjoint-height reduction. Successful parameter values form
a closed algebraic set. In one parameter, a target which fails initially
can succeed only at finitely many exceptional values. This verifies a
proposed family, rather than producing a successful family.

There is also an all-degree obstruction for one admissible shear family on
an actual polynomial cylinder. Every nonconstant shear in that direction
raises the sum of adjoint heights, although a transverse elementary flow
has one exact lowering parameter and leads to an adapted frame. The
underlying two-shear coordinates are inherited; the entire shear-family
quantifier and exact parameter test are the assertions here.

## 1. The actual frame and the height being measured

Work over \(\mathbb C\), with
\[
B=A[s]=\mathbb C[x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4],\qquad E=\partial_s,
\qquad T_i=\partial_{x_i}.
\tag{1}
\]
No polynomial presentation of \(A\) is assumed. For a nonzero derivation
\(T\), define
\[
h_E(T)=\max\{j\geq0:(\operatorname{ad}E)^jT\ne0\},\qquad
\operatorname{ad}E(T)=[E,T].
\tag{2}
\]
This integer exists: the conjugation by the actual polynomial flow of
\(E\), with independent parameter \(z\), has polynomial coefficients on
the finitely many ambient generators and gives
\[
\exp(zE)T\exp(-zE)
=\sum_{j\geq0}\frac{z^j}{j!}(\operatorname{ad}E)^jT.
\tag{3}
\]
One obtains the displayed coefficients by differentiating at \(z=0\).
The same argument is uniform after adjoining finitely many parameters,
which all derivations fix.

Write \(\mathbf h=(h_E(T_1),\ldots,h_E(T_4))\) and
\(\Lambda=\sum_i h_E(T_i)\). These are not the older shifted
slice-filtration height \(H=\sum_i(q_i+1)\). An adapted frame on a
polynomial \(A\) has \(\mathbf h=(0,0,0,0)\), \(\Lambda=0\), but
\(H=3\), because \(E\) has shifted degree \(-1\).

The [whole-frame note](actual-frame-pass38.md) studies
\(\mathbf h\leq(1,1,1,1)\). The older
[height-eight proof](height-eight-algebra-pass.md) already closes that
condition under the slice hypothesis, and the
[mixed theorem](../deep-pass-13.md) closes the previous height-nine
pattern. Those historical recognition results are not new consequences
claimed here. Existence of a suitable frame remains unproved.

## 2. An exact coefficient ideal for a supplied coordinate family

Let \(\mathbf t=(t_1,\ldots,t_m)\), and suppose that
\[
\Phi_{\mathbf t}\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbb C[\mathbf t]}B[\mathbf t],
\qquad \Phi_{\mathbf0}=\operatorname{id},
\tag{4}
\]
is supplied with its polynomial inverse. Both composition identities must
hold over the full parameter ring. A unit Jacobian alone is not enough.
Set
\[
y_i(\mathbf t)=\Phi_{\mathbf t}(x_i),\qquad
U_i(\mathbf t)=\Phi_{\mathbf t}T_i\Phi_{\mathbf t}^{-1}.
\tag{5}
\]
Thus \(U_i(y_j)=\delta_{ij}\), and every specialized frame is genuine.
The derivation \(E\) is fixed throughout.

For a tuple \(\mathbf r\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^4\), expand
\[
(\operatorname{ad}E)^{r_i+1}U_i(\mathbf t)(x_j)
\quad(1\leq i,j\leq4)
\tag{6}
\]
in monomials of the ambient variables \(x\). Let \(I_{\mathbf r}\)
be the ideal of \(\mathbb C[\mathbf t]\) generated by every resulting
coefficient.

**Coordinate-family criterion.** The complete set of parameter values
whose frame satisfies \(\mathbf h\leq\mathbf r\) is
\[
V(I_{\mathbf r})\subseteq\mathbb A^m.
\tag{7}
\]

**Proof.** A derivation is zero exactly when its values on the four
polynomial generators are zero. Specializing the parameters commutes
with every bracket in (6). The resulting polynomial is zero exactly
when all monomial coefficients vanish. This proves both implications.
The inverse identities ensure that no specialized \(U_i\) is zero.
Formula (3) supplies a uniform finite height bound within the family,
not a universal bound over all coordinate changes.

For one parameter, \(I_{\mathbf r}=(g_{\mathbf r}(t))\), where the
generator is the gcd of its coefficient polynomials, up to a scalar.
If it is zero, every parameter works; if it is one, none works;
otherwise precisely its roots work. If the target fails at \(t=0\),
the ideal is nonzero, so success is a finite exceptional set, possibly
empty. A generic deformation cannot lower a target not already attained
initially. In several parameters, the proper closed success set can have
positive dimension, so this finiteness does not transfer.

There is an exact finite optimization of \(\Lambda\) in one parameter.
Compute the generic heights \(q_i\) over \(\mathbb C(t)\).
For each \(q_i>0\), take the gcd of the coefficient polynomials of
\((\operatorname{ad}E)^{q_i}U_i\). Outside the finite union of their
root sets, every height equals its generic value. At those finitely
many roots, recompute the specialized heights. Comparing these profiles
with the generic profile gives the exact minimum. Height-zero partials
cannot drop below zero.

This is effective algebraic computation for coefficients in an effective
exact field, such as the algebraic numbers. For arbitrary unspecified
complex constants it is a finite algebraic criterion, not a numerical
zero-testing algorithm. It applies, in particular, to
\(\Phi_t=\exp(tW)\) for any supplied LND \(W\) of \(B\), whose
inverse is \(\exp(-tW)\). No local nilpotence of \([E,W]\), finite
Lie closure, or polynomial presentation of \(A\) is required.

## 3. Bounded-degree elementary repair is linear algebra

Fix a pivot \(j\), a nonnegative degree bound \(d\), and every
\[
P\in\mathbb C[x_1,\ldots,\widehat{x_j},\ldots,x_4],
\qquad \deg P\leq d,
\tag{8}
\]
with its coefficients regarded as unknowns. The elementary change
\[
y_j=x_j+P,\qquad y_i=x_i\ (i\ne j)
\tag{9}
\]
has inverse \(x_j=y_j-P(y_1,\ldots,\widehat{y_j},\ldots,y_4)\).
Its partials are
\[
U_j=T_j,\qquad U_i=T_i-(T_iP)T_j\quad(i\ne j).
\tag{10}
\]
For any target profile \(\mathbf r\), substituting (10) in (6)
gives affine-linear coefficient equations in the unknown coefficients
of \(P\). Indeed \(E\), the original partials, and the bracket
operator are fixed and linear, while (10) is affine in \(P\).
Thus a finite linear system decides every repair in this ansatz.

The unchanged pivot must already satisfy \(h_E(T_j)\leq r_j\).
The system includes every other partial, and its rows come from one
polynomial \(P\), rather than incompatible separately chosen row
coefficients. For strict decrease of \(\Lambda\), enumerate the
finitely many nonnegative profiles with sum below the initial sum,
and all four pivots. A solution is an actual lowering shear with its
explicit inverse. Inconsistency of every system certifies that no single
shear of degree at most \(d\) works. Increasing \(d\) is a search,
not a termination theorem.

This is a complete finite-ansatz form of the older
[sufficient symbol-cancellation test](coordinate-frame-minimization-pass.md).
It includes lower commutator coefficients and simultaneous integrability.
It does not assert that a lowering elementary shear always exists, or
that elementary changes generate every automorphism.

## 4. Kernel-preserving changes cannot lower the profile

Suppose \(\Phi\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbb C}B\) satisfies
\(\Phi(A)=A\) setwise. Then
\[
\Phi(s)=u s+h,\qquad u\in A^*=\mathbb C^*,\quad h\in A.
\tag{11}
\]
To prove this, compose with the coefficientwise extension of
\((\Phi|_A)^{-1}\). The resulting automorphism fixes \(A\).
Its degree in \(s\) and that of its inverse multiply to one,
so both have degree one and unit leading coefficients. This also
gives (11) before undoing that coefficient automorphism.

Consequently \(\Phi E\Phi^{-1}=u^{-1}E\), and
\[
(\operatorname{ad}E)^k(\Phi T\Phi^{-1})
=u^k\Phi((\operatorname{ad}E)^kT)\Phi^{-1}.
\tag{12}
\]
Every adjoint height and the entire ordered profile are unchanged.
The setwise hypothesis matters; a one-sided inclusion is not substituted.

This covers extended intrinsic automorphisms, invariant slice
translations, and all their compositions. In particular, intrinsic
LND flows extended to fix \(s\), and vertical replica flows
\(\exp(t hE)\) with \(h\in A\), cannot reduce the height.
A lowering map must move the selected kernel subalgebra inside \(B\).
This necessary condition is not sufficient.

## 5. An entire shear direction fails on an actual polynomial cylinder

Take \(B=\mathbb C[a,b,c,s]\), \(E=\partial_s\), and integers
\(N,M\geq2\). Use the actual ambient coordinates
\[
x=a+s^N,\qquad y=s+x^M,\qquad b,\qquad c.
\tag{13}
\]
Their two-sided inverse is
\[
s=y-x^M,\qquad a=x-(y-x^M)^N,
\tag{14}
\]
with \(b,c\) unchanged. This familiar two-shear construction also
appears in the earlier
[prescribed-frame controls](finite-closure-frame-obstruction-pass30.md).
The height and all-shear assertions below are proved directly.

In the actual \((a,b,c,s)\) coordinates the first two partials are
\[
T_y=\partial_s-Ns^{N-1}\partial_a,\qquad
T_x=\partial_a-Mx^{M-1}T_y.
\tag{15}
\]
Adjoint bracketing by \(E\) differentiates their coefficients in \(s\),
so their exact profile is
\[
(h_E(T_x),h_E(T_y),h_E(\partial_b),h_E(\partial_c))
=(NM-1,N-1,0,0).
\tag{16}
\]
The leading \(\partial_a\) coefficient of \(T_x\) is
\(MN s^{NM-1}\); its \(\partial_s\) coefficient has the lower
degree \(N(M-1)\).

Consider every polynomial shear, with no degree bound,
\[
x'=x+P(y),\qquad y'=y,\qquad P\in\mathbb C[y].
\tag{17}
\]
Its inverse is \(x=x'-P(y')\). Constants leave both partials
unchanged. For \(\deg P=k\geq1\), the new partials are
\[
U_x=T_x,\qquad U_y=T_y-P'(y)T_x.
\tag{18}
\]
Since \(\deg_s y=NM\), one has
\(\deg_s P'(y)=NM(k-1)\). The leading \(\partial_a\)
coefficient of \(P'(y)T_x\) has degree \(NMk-1\) and is nonzero.
This is strictly above \(N-1\), so it cannot cancel against
\(T_y\). The exact new profile is therefore
\[
(NM-1,NMk-1,0,0).
\tag{19}
\]
Every nonconstant shear in (17) strictly raises \(\Lambda\).
For \(k=1\) the maximum height stays unchanged; thus the sum and
maximum are distinct objectives. No shear in this family lowers either.

For the actual additive flow \(x'=x+tP(y)\), the initial profile
occurs at \(t=0\); for every \(t\ne0\) and nonconstant \(P\),
the profile is (19). The exact sum-minimizing parameter set is
\(\{0\}\). This is an all-degree assertion, not a sampled test.

A transverse elementary family nevertheless succeeds:
\[
x'=x,\qquad y'=y-tx^M,
\tag{20}
\]
with inverse \(y=y'+t(x')^M\). Writing
\(y'=s+(1-t)x^M\), its profile is (16) for every \(t\ne1\).
At \(t=1\) its exact profile is
\[
(0,N-1,0,0).
\tag{21}
\]
This is its sole lowering parameter. In the resulting frame
\((x,s,b,c)\), the further shear \(x\mapsto x-s^N\) reaches
\((a,s,b,c)\), whose profile is zero. Both steps have explicit
polynomial inverses.

This is an obstruction to selecting the wrong entire coordinate-change
family, not an obstruction to cancellation or to every elementary
repair. It illustrates why generic parameter selection misses the
useful cancellation in (20).

## 6. Scope, verification, and sources

For a supplied family the success ideal is necessary and sufficient.
For an elementary ansatz of bounded degree it reduces to linear algebra.
For the unbounded-degree family (17) the degree proof excludes every
lowering choice. All statements concern actual coordinates.

None constructs a successful family for arbitrary
\(A[s]\simeq\mathbb C^{[4]}\), classifies globally minimal frames,
or excludes local minima under all elementary moves. No universal
degree bound or terminating coordinate search is proved. The original
cylinder problem remains open in this investigation.

The [standalone checker](coordinate_reduction_checks_pass39.py) imports
only unittest and sympy. Its finite controls check inverse maps, bracket
product rules, success and failure coefficient ideals, the linear
elementary ansatz, and representative shear profiles. They do not
replace the all-degree proof or establish a universal search bound.
All eight methods passed in the initial run in 0.562 seconds.

The algebraic adjoint-action framework is standard. Kraft and
Zaidenberg, Sections 1.1 and 1.2, describe rational locally finite
actions of algebraic groups on vector fields and conjugation.[^kz]
The coefficient criterion is proved directly here. No group-integration
or triangulation theorem is invoked, and no novelty claim is made for
polynomial zero-locus or semicontinuity arguments.

[^kz]: Hanspeter Kraft and Mikhail Zaidenberg,
[*Algebraically generated groups and their Lie algebras*](https://arxiv.org/html/2203.11356v4),
Sections 1.1 and 1.2, directly read; published in the
[*Journal of the London Mathematical Society*](https://doi.org/10.1112/jlms.12866),
2024. The source supplies the established algebraic-action framework,
not existence of a height-lowering coordinate family.
