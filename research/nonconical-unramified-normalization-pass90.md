# A controlled unramified normalization in the nonconical branch

**Status:** the stronger [quadratic-potential proof90](nonconical-quadratic-potential-pass90.md)
now establishes \(A_\Omega=R_\Omega\) under these same actual
hypotheses, hence also \(A_f=R_f\) on the chosen locus below.
The weaker proof is retained to isolate its differential mechanism
and the exact algebra-only limitation of that mechanism.

In the actual three-good-coordinate constant-acceleration setting,
the nonconical determinant gives more than generic birationality.
After inverting specified elements of the actual invariant plane,
the kernel is the finite unramified normalization of the smaller
two-generator ring. This does not identify the smaller ring with its
normalization: the differential condition excludes a common critical
point, not the identification of distinct points.

## 1. Actual hypotheses and the retained normal relation

Retain the full original-coordinate hypotheses and actual constructions
of [invariant-plane88](constant-acceleration-invariant-plane-pass88.md):
\[
\begin{gathered}
B=\mathbb C[x_1,x_2,x_3,z]=A[u],\qquad A=\ker E,\qquad
Eu=1,\quad Ex_i=b_i\ (i\le3),\quad Ez=qu,\quad q\in\mathbb C^*,\\
S=\ker(W|A)=\mathbb C[s_1,s_2],\qquad Wu=0,\qquad
b\in S^3,\quad (b_1,b_2,b_3)S=S,\\
M\in\operatorname{GL}_3(S),\quad Mb=e_1,\qquad
M x_{\rm tr}=(\sigma,A_1,A_2)^t,\qquad
R=S[A_1,A_2],\quad A=R[c],\quad h=u-\sigma\in A.
\end{gathered}
\tag{1}
\]
Here \(E\) and \(W\) are actual LNDs, and the four displayed
coordinates of \(B\) are original polynomial coordinates.
Assume throughout that
\[
\begin{gathered}
L=b_{s_1}\times b_{s_2},\qquad
\Omega=L\cdot b\ne0,\qquad
a=x_{\rm tr}-bu,\quad r=z-\frac q2u^2,\\
L\cdot a=\kappa\in S,\qquad
\Omega h=\alpha A_1+\beta A_2-\kappa,\quad
\alpha,\beta\in S.
\end{gathered}
\tag{2}
\]
The full [actual volume proof88](constant-acceleration-volume-descent-pass88.md)
establishes the last line. The
[generic-degree proof88](constant-acceleration-generic-degree-pass88.md)
establishes \(\operatorname{Frac}(R)=\operatorname{Frac}(A)\)
in this nonconical branch. Neither predecessor asserts normality of
\(R\), or finiteness of \(R\to A\) before further localization.

The generic-degree predecessor first separates the alternative
\(R=S\) with \(c\) transcendental. That alternative cannot occur
here: (2) would put \(h\) in \(\operatorname{Frac}(S)\cap A=S\),
so \(Ma=(-h,A_1,A_2)^t\) would put all of \(a\) in \(S\).
In an intrinsic local-slice presentation, \(a_v=0\) would then make
the actual unit determinant (4) have nonzero \(r_v\) but quadratic
coefficient \(r_v\Omega=0\), a contradiction.
Thus the fraction-field equality is used in its stated
transcendence-degree-three branch.

## 2. A base localization on which the normalization is finite

Choose an actual local slice for \(W|A\). Thus there are
\(g\in S\setminus\{0\}\) and \(v\in A_g\) with
\(A_g=S_g[v]\) and \(Wv=1\). This is the usual local-slice
theorem, not an ambient coordinate claim.
At least one of \(A_1,A_2\), viewed in
\(\operatorname{Frac}(S)[v]\), is nonconstant: otherwise the
fraction field of \(R\) would have transcendence degree two.

Choose one such \(A_i\), and enlarge the localization by its
nonzero leading coefficient and by \(\Omega\). Clearing the
denominator of that coefficient allows all these inversions to be
made by one nonzero element \(f\in S\). Consequently
\[
\begin{gathered}
S_f=S[1/f],\qquad \Omega\in S_f^*,\qquad
A_f=S_f[v],\quad B_f=S_f[v,u],\quad
R_f=S_f[A_1,A_2],\\
A_f\text{ is finite over }R_f,\qquad
\operatorname{Frac}(R_f)=\operatorname{Frac}(A_f).
\end{gathered}
\tag{3}
\]
Indeed the leading coefficient of \(A_i(v)\) is now a unit.
The equation obtained from its expression as a polynomial in \(v\),
after division by that coefficient, is monic in \(v\) with
coefficients in \(R_f\). Since \(A_f=R_f[v]\), this proves
finiteness. This construction does not allow the removed base
divisors to be discarded from the global problem.

The ring \(R_f\) itself has a precise hypersurface presentation.
The kernel of \(S_f[T_1,T_2]\to R_f\) is a height-one prime:
the source has dimension four and \(R_f\) has transcendence degree
three over \(\mathbb C\). The source is a UFD, so that kernel is
generated by one irreducible polynomial. No smoothness, normality,
or flatness over \(S_f\) is inferred from this presentation.

## 3. The original coordinate determinant excludes every common critical point

Take all derivatives below in the actual presentation
\(B_f=S_f[v,u]\), with the other displayed variables held fixed.
Both \(dx_1,dx_2,dx_3,dz\) and \(du,ds_1,ds_2,dv\) are bases
of its module of absolute complex differentials. Therefore
\[
\mathcal J=
\det\begin{pmatrix}
b&u b_{s_1}+a_{s_1}&u b_{s_2}+a_{s_2}&a_v\\
qu&r_{s_1}&r_{s_2}&r_v
\end{pmatrix}
\in B_f^*=S_f^*.
\tag{4}
\]
In particular this determinant is independent of both \(u\) and \(v\).
The assertion uses the original polynomial coordinates and their
actual localized inverse. It is stronger than a nonzero formal
Jacobian.

Suppose the ideal \((A_{1,v},A_{2,v})A_f\) were proper.
The ring \(A_f\) is a finitely generated complex algebra, so there
would be a closed point \((s_1^0,s_2^0,v_0)\) at which both
derivatives vanish. All coefficients in (2) are regular there and
\(\Omega\) is a unit. Differentiating (2) with respect to \(v\)
gives \(h_v=0\) at that point. Since
\(M a=(-h,A_1,A_2)^t\) and \(M\) is over \(S\), this also gives
\(a_v=0\) there.

Specialize the already differentiated matrix (4) at this point,
leaving \(u\) indeterminate. Expansion in its final column yields
\[
\begin{gathered}
\mathcal J(s^0)=
r_v(s^0,v_0)
\det[b(s^0),u b_{s_1}(s^0)+a_{s_1}(s^0,v_0),
                  u b_{s_2}(s^0)+a_{s_2}(s^0,v_0)],\\
r_v(s^0,v_0)\ne0,\qquad
0=[u^2]\mathcal J(s^0)=r_v(s^0,v_0)\Omega(s^0),
\end{gathered}
\tag{5}
\]
a contradiction. The nonzero assertion follows from the unit
\(\mathcal J(s^0)\), and \(\Omega(s^0)\ne0\) by construction.
There is no differentiation of the chosen point, no new
coefficient-field embedding, and no specialization through a pole.
We conclude
\[
(A_{1,v},A_{2,v})A_f=A_f,\qquad
\Omega_{A_f/R_f}
=\frac{A_f\,dv}{(A_{1,v},A_{2,v})A_f\,dv}=0.
\tag{6}
\]
The differential presentation follows because \(R_f\) is generated
over \(S_f\) by \(A_1,A_2\), while \(A_f=S_f[v]\).
Thus \(R_f\to A_f\) is unramified.[^unramified]
The conclusion holds over the whole chosen base locus, not only
on its generic fiber.

## 4. Exact normalization, with a remaining point-identification gate

Since \(A_f=S_f[v]\) is normal, (3) proves that
\[
\boxed{\quad
A_f=\overline{R_f}^{\,\operatorname{Frac}(R_f)}
\text{ is finite, birational and unramified over }R_f.
\quad}
\tag{7}
\]
For completeness, every element of \(A_f\) is integral over \(R_f\).
Conversely, an element of their common fraction field integral over
\(R_f\) is also integral over \(A_f\), because its monic equation
has coefficients in \(R_f\subset A_f\). Normality of \(A_f\)
then places it in \(A_f\).

Equation (7) alone is not a normality theorem for \(R_f\).
Its differential argument excludes a common ramification point of the two relative
generators. It does not exclude different points of the normal
curve having the same values of both generators. Nor does it
make \(R_f\to A_f\) flat or identify a two-generator presentation
of \(A\) over \(S\). The companion quadratic-volume proof supplies
the stronger actual-frame equality by a different coefficient;
the following control is only a boundary for the weaker data.

An elementary algebra-only control makes this distinction exact.
Over any complex polynomial spectator ring \(S_0\), put
\[
\begin{gathered}
A_0=S_0[v],\qquad y=v^2-1,\quad w=v(v^2-1),\qquad
R_0=S_0[y,w]\simeq S_0[Y,W]/(W^2-Y^2(Y+1)),\\
v^2=y+1,\qquad v=w/y\text{ in the fraction field},\qquad
\frac{3v}{2}\,y'-w'=1.
\end{gathered}
\tag{8}
\]
The relation is irreducible, for \(Y+1\) is not a square in
\(\operatorname{Frac}(S_0)(Y)\). Thus this is a finite birational
normalization with zero relative differentials. Nevertheless
\(v\notin R_0\): substitution \(v=1\) and \(v=-1\) gives the same
values \(y=w=0\) on \(R_0\), but different values of \(v\).
Both rings are flat over \(S_0\). Taking \(c=v\) and \(h=0\)
also satisfies the universal generator pencil, so that condition
does not remove this boundary.

This control is not an actual action satisfying (1).
For example, the tempting formal original frame
\(X_1=u,\ X_2=s_1u+y(v),\ X_3=s_2u+w(v)\) and
\(Z=v+qu^2/2\) has Jacobian \(u^2\), not a unit, despite its
parameter speed row \((1,s_1,s_2)\) having \(\Omega=1\).
It therefore cannot serve as an original polynomial coordinate
frame. No node or other point identification is asserted to
occur in the actual target class.

## 5. Deduplication and proof scope

The generic common-critical-point mechanism and the actual
differential-basis determinant are retained from generic-degree88.
The new step is to combine them with the global linear relation
in (2), choose a finite base localization, and exclude common
critical points on every closed fiber there. This yields the
relative normalization and differential conclusions (6)--(7).
The same-pass quadratic-potential proof supersedes this as the
strongest actual-frame conclusion on the chosen open set.
Neither note resolves the nonconical branch globally.

The complete saved invariant-plane88, volume-descent88 and
generic-degree88 proofs were read through EOF for their actual-ring
hypotheses. The only newly accessed primary statement used here
is the standard differential definition/criterion for an unramified
map, cited below. No numerical checker, novelty claim, or edit to
pass89 or an earlier artifact is part of this note.
