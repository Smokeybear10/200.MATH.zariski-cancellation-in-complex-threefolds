# The principal one-affine paraboloid chart

Status: an exact chart and three potential identities for the supplied
full initial kernel \(X^2Z-Y^2\) in an actual globally sliced1222 frame.
The literal \(Z\)-partial has exact shift four, and its calibrated
Jacobian is \(-a^4\). In the nonzero-coupling branch the whole generic
line is generated by three projected functions satisfying a nonzero
cubic polynomial, monic quadratic in the first function.

This is not yet a plane-line presentation or recognition of this
principal sector. In particular a cubic surface relation among three
functions is not a plane curve relation among two. No earlier source,
checker, or frozen packet is changed.

## 1. Actual entry and the full initial relation

Let
\[
\begin{gathered}
B=\mathbb C[X,Y,Z,W]=A[u],\qquad E=\partial_u,\qquad A=\ker E,\\
\deg_E(X,Y,Z,W)=(1,2,2,2),\qquad
a=EX,\quad b=E^2Y/2,\quad c=E^2Z/2,\quad e=E^2W/2 .
\end{gathered}
\tag{1}
\]
All four leaders are nonzero. Assume the full weighted initial kernel,
not just membership of one relation, is
\[
\ker\{\mathbb C[\mathsf X,\mathsf Y,\mathsf Z,\mathsf W]
 \longrightarrow A[T]\}
 =(\mathsf X^2\mathsf Z-\mathsf Y^2),
\quad
(\mathsf X,\mathsf Y,\mathsf Z,\mathsf W)
 \mapsto(aT,bT^2,cT^2,eT^2).
\tag{2}
\]
Then \(a^2c=b^2\). Set \(t=b/a^2\), \(s=e/a^2\).
The initial fraction field is \(\mathbb C(t,s)(aT)\).
Its transcendence degree is three by (2), so \(t,s\) are
algebraically independent. The ratio \(c/a^2\) is \(t^2\).

By the occurring-pivot statement of the complete
[positive-weight bound158](arbitrary-positive-weight-initial-bound-pass158-working.md),
the literal partial \(T_Z=\partial_Z\) has exact horizontal shift
\[
k_Z=(1+2+2)-1=4,\qquad
0\ne N=[u^4]T_Z|_A\in\operatorname{LND}(A).
\tag{3}
\]
It kills all four leaders, hence kills \(a,t,s\).
Let \(L=\operatorname{Frac}\ker_A N\). Its transcendence degree
is two, it is finite over \(\mathbb C(t,s)\), and the local-slice
theorem gives
\[
A\otimes_{\ker N}L=L[v],\qquad Nv=1,\qquad
K:=\operatorname{Frac}A=L(v).
\tag{4}
\]
Finiteness follows because the algebraic closure of
\(\mathbb C(t,s)\) inside the finitely generated field \(K\)
is finite. The coefficient partials \(\partial_t,\partial_s\)
extend uniquely to \(L\), commute, and are extended to fix \(v\).
No rationality of \(L/\mathbb C\) is presumed.

## 2. Actual return and the fourth-power calibration

At fixed actual \(X\), write
\[
Y=y+\sigma X+tX^2,\quad
Z=z+\tau X+t^2X^2,\quad
W=w+\theta X+sX^2 .
\tag{5}
\]
Every coefficient lies in \(A_a\subset L[v]\).
Evaluation at \(X=0\) in \(B_a=A_a[X]\), as fully proved in
[259, Section2](one-affine-three-quadratic-frontier-pass259-working.md),
gives both actual returns
\[
K=\mathbb C(y,z,w),\qquad L[y,z,w]=L[v].
\tag{6}
\]
The second equality uses that \(a\in\ker N\), so its denominator
is already inverted on passing to \(L\).

The horizontal Jacobian
\[
J(X)=\det\frac{\partial(Y,Z,W)}{\partial(t,s,v)}
\tag{7}
\]
is a nonzero scalar in \(K\), independent of \(X\), by the actual
invariant-volume argument of158. Its degree-five coefficient
in the literal map (5) is \(2t\sigma_v-\tau_v\).
Therefore
\[
\tau=2t\sigma+k,\qquad k\in L .
\tag{8}
\]
There is no derivative of a moving root in this calculation.

The cofactor numerator for the inverse entry \(T_Z(v)\) is
\(-Y_tW_s+Y_sW_t\). Its degree-four coefficient is \(-1\):
the relevant top derivatives are \(Y_t=X^2+\cdots\),
\(W_s=X^2+\cdots\), and the other product has smaller degree.
On the other hand the leading coefficient of \(T_Z(v)\) is
\(a^{-4}Nv=a^{-4}\). Consequently
\[
\boxed{J(X)=-a^4\in L^*.}
\tag{9}
\]
Neither the \(-a^2\) of an exact1122 chart nor the \(-a^3\) of
the maximal1123 chart is applicable here.

## 3. Two polynomial potential identities without a coupling assumption

Put \(m=k_s\), \(z_0=z-2ty\), and
\(\gamma=2\sigma+k_t\).
Subtract \(2t\) times the first differential row of (5) from
the second. The middle row becomes
\[
dz_0+2y\,dt+X(\gamma\,dt+m\,ds).
\tag{10}
\]
All differentials in this note hold \(X\) fixed.
The coefficient of \(X^4\) in the resulting determinant is
\[
-(z_0)_v+\gamma\sigma_v+m\theta_v .
\]
Integration in the polynomial variable \(v\) gives
\[
\boxed{z=2ty+\sigma^2+k_t\sigma+m\theta+n,\qquad n\in L.}
\tag{11}
\]
The occurrence of \(2ty\), rather than a coefficient-field change
of the actual coordinate \(Z\), matters.

For formal \(P,V\), define
\[
F(P,V)=\frac{k_{tt}}2P^2+k_{ts}PV+\frac{k_{ss}}2V^2
                  +n_tP+n_sV .
\tag{12}
\]
After (11), the three rows of the determinant can be written as
\[
\begin{aligned}
&dy+X\,d\sigma+X^2dt,\\
&\gamma\,d\sigma+m\,d\theta+B\,dt+C\,ds
                    +X(\gamma\,dt+m\,ds),\\
&dw+X\,d\theta+X^2ds,
\end{aligned}
\quad
B=2y+F_P(\sigma,\theta),\quad C=F_V(\sigma,\theta).
\tag{13}
\]
Its degree-three coefficient is
\[
m w_v+B\sigma_v+C\theta_v+\gamma y_v.
\]
Since \(F_P=k_{tt}P+k_{ts}V+n_t\) and
\(F_V=k_{ts}P+k_{ss}V+n_s\), this is an exact derivative.
Thus
\[
\boxed{m w+(2\sigma+k_t)y+F(\sigma,\theta)=\ell,\qquad \ell\in L.}
\tag{14}
\]
Equations (11),(14) require no division by \(m\), and so remain
valid in the zero-coupling branch \(k_s=0\).

## 4. Nonzero coupling: a monic cubic-surface relation

Assume for this section that \(m\ne0\). Use independent formal
variables \(U,P,V\), and put
\[
g(U,P,V)=\frac{\ell-(2P+k_t)U-F(P,V)}m .
\tag{15}
\]
Then \(w=g(y,\sigma,\theta)\), and (11),(14) prove the exact
three-generator line return
\[
L[y,\sigma,\theta]=L[y,z,w]=L[v].
\tag{16}
\]
The left-to-right inclusion follows since \(\sigma,\theta\in L[v]\);
the reverse inclusion follows by expressing \(z,w\) as polynomials
in \(y,\sigma,\theta\). This does not establish
\(L[\sigma,\theta]=L[v]\).

The formal derivatives needed below are
\[
g_U=-\frac{2P+k_t}{m},\qquad
g_P=-\frac{2U+F_P}{m},\qquad
g_V=-\frac{F_V}{m}.
\tag{17}
\]
Substitute their chain rules into the coefficient of \(X^2\)
of (13). Terms involving \(\sigma_t,\sigma_s,\theta_t,\theta_s\)
and \(y_t,y_s\) cancel in pairs. The result is
\[
\begin{aligned}
0={}&\left(2y+F_P-\frac{(2\sigma+k_t)F_V}{m}\right)y_v\\
&+\left(-m g_t+F_Vg_P\right)\sigma_v
 +\left(-m g_s+F_Vg_V\right)\theta_v ,
\end{aligned}
\tag{18}
\]
with every formal expression evaluated at \((y,\sigma,\theta)\).
The coefficient partials of \(g\) in this formula hold \(U,P,V\)
fixed.

For any coefficient \(f\in L\), use the exact Taylor notation
\[
H_j(f;P,V)=
\sum_{i=0}^j
\frac{\partial_t^{\,j-i}\partial_s^i f}{(j-i)!\,i!}
P^{j-i}V^i .
\tag{19}
\]
Define the polynomial
\[
\mathcal H(U,P,V)=
U^2+F_PU+F_Vg+H_3(k;P,V)+H_2(n;P,V)-\ell_tP-\ell_sV .
\tag{20}
\]
Its three formal derivatives are exactly the three coefficients
of (18):
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal H_U&=2U+F_P-(2P+k_t)F_V/m,\\
\mathcal H_P&=-m g_t+F_Vg_P,\qquad
\mathcal H_V=-m g_s+F_Vg_V .
\end{aligned}
\tag{21}
\]
For example \((H_3k+H_2n)_P=F_t\),
\((H_3k+H_2n)_V=F_s\), \(F_{VP}=m_t\), and \(F_{VV}=m_s\).
These identities verify the coefficient derivatives in (21)
without any assumed formal integrability.
Equation (18) therefore integrates to
\[
\boxed{\mathcal H(y,\sigma,\theta)=h_0,\qquad h_0\in L.}
\tag{22}
\]
This relation is nonzero for a direct reason: \(\mathcal H-h_0\)
is monic quadratic in \(U\). All the other terms in (20) have
\(U\)-degree at most one. Its total degree is at most three,
since \(F\) and \(g\) have total degree at most two and \(F_V\)
has degree at most one. Thus the proved reduction is
\[
\boxed{
L[y,\sigma,\theta]=L[v],\qquad
0\ne\mathcal H-h_0\in L[U,P,V],\qquad
\deg(\mathcal H-h_0)\le3,\quad
\operatorname{lc}_U(\mathcal H-h_0)=1 .
}
\tag{23}
\]

## 5. Scope and the next missing step

The first two potentials are valid for either value of \(m\).
For \(m\ne0\), the cubic relation (23) is additional information
from the full scalar determinant, not an arbitrary parametrized
curve satisfying the leading paraboloid relation.

It is nevertheless a hypersurface equation in three formal variables.
The polynomial line can still have two independent relations there.
Neither a two-generator plane return nor a degree bound on each of
\(y,\sigma,\theta\) follows from (23) alone. The coefficient of
\(X\) and the nonzero constant coefficient of the actual determinant
remain to be exploited. In the branch \(m=0\), solving (14) by
dividing by \(2\sigma+k_t\) is likewise not a polynomial-ring return
argument. Both gaps are left explicit.

The full-kernel assumption (2) and the actual ring returns (6)
are retained throughout. None of the coefficient-field polynomial
expressions is promoted to an ambient coordinate. The actual
translation control in259 has this full initial kernel and \(m=0\),
so the supplied sector itself is not being declared empty.

The sources158 and259 were read for the selected-pivot, localization,
and volume inputs. A fresh unsaved symbolic calculation reconstructed
the determinant coefficients \(X^4,X^3,X^2\), the three primitive
gradients, and the degree bound; it returned zero residuals.
That calculation is not a proof of generic-field return or recognition.
No saved checker was executed, imported or compiled, and no general
1222 recognition or novelty claim is made.
