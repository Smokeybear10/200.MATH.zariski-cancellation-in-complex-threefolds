# Nodal plinth realizability: a stronger obstruction still fails

The divisor \((v^2-u^3+3u)^2-4=0\) is not realized here as a
primitive plinth on \(\mathbb C^3\), nor is it excluded. There
is a substantive refinement: Kaliman's one-intermediate-point
condition over a node does not force a single normalization
branch. An explicit degree-two finite curve map below satisfies
that condition. Thus it cannot supply the missing exclusion by
itself.

## 1. Exact primary-source boundary

In the [final author version of Kaliman's translation paper](https://www.math.miami.edu/~kaliman/library/Ka.invent.2004.c%2B.pdf),
Proposition 3.2(ii), Step 1 proves that a selfintersection point
of the exceptional curve has exactly one inverse image in the
intermediate curve \(Z\). This concerns \(Z\), not its
normalization. Step 2 compares the homology of the normalizations;
Theorem 5.2(i) gives polynomial normalizations for the retained
components. None of these statements asserts that \(Z\) is
normal at that inverse image.

There is also a direct historical boundary. In his
[2007 Oberwolfach problem statement, printed pages 68-69](https://publications.mfo.de/bitstream/handle/mfo/2987/OWR_2007_01.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1),
Kaliman asks whether individual retained components are contractible
or smooth, and whether the whole curve is contractible when it is
connected. The examples listed include disjoint parallel lines.
His [author survey, Section 10, Remark 10.2](https://www.math.miami.edu/~kaliman/library/seattle.paper.pdf)
likewise presents component contractibility as a suspicion, not a
proved input. These are historical statements, not a certification
of the question's present open status. Importing their hoped-for
conclusion would assume the issue being investigated.

## 2. An explicit four-branch intermediate curve

Let \(\Gamma_+\) be the nodal curve
\(v^2-u^3+3u=2\). Its normalization parameter \(t\) gives

\[
u=t^2-2,\qquad v=t(t^2-3).
\tag{1}
\]

The node \((1,0)\) has normalization preimages
\(t=\pm\sqrt3\). Substitute \(t=w^2\), put
\(d=w^4-3\), and define

\[
T=\mathbb C+d\mathbb C[w]
 =\mathbb C[d,dw,dw^2,dw^3],\qquad Z=\operatorname{Spec}T.
\tag{2}
\]

Geometrically, (2) identifies the four distinct roots of \(d\)
to one point. It contains the base coordinate ring through

\[
u=d+1=w^4-2,\qquad v=dw^2.
\tag{3}
\]

These formulas define a finite map \(\theta:Z\to\Gamma_+\)
of generic degree two. Indeed \(w\) is integral over the base
ring by \(w^4=u+2\); \(T\) is a submodule of the resulting
finite base module \(\mathbb C[w]\), hence is finite. Also
\(\operatorname{Frac}T=\mathbb C(w)\), because
\(w=(dw)/d\), whereas the base function field is
\(\mathbb C(t)=\mathbb C(w^2)\). The normalization of
\(Z\) is \(\mathbb A^1_w\).

Above the node, all four normalization points contract to the
same maximal ideal \(d\mathbb C[w]\) of \(T\). Every
point of \(Z\) lifts to its finite normalization, so there are
no other points over that node. Thus

\[
\#\theta^{-1}(1,0)=1,
\qquad
\#\nu_Z^{-1}\bigl(\theta^{-1}(1,0)\bigr)=4.
\tag{4}
\]

Each of these four normalization branches is unramified over its
base normalization branch, since \(w\ne0\) there. There is
ordinary degree-two ramification at \(w=0\), mapping to a
smooth point of \(\Gamma_+\). Consequently neither finiteness,
polynomial normalization, generic degree at least two, nor the
one-point condition forces the contradiction. Replacing
\(u\) by \(-u\) and \(v\) by \(iv\) carries this construction
to \(\Gamma_-\); their disjoint union gives the same curve-level
control for both components of the proposed divisor.

The false shortcut is now precise: one cannot apply
Riemann-Hurwitz as though the unique point in (4) were a unique
point of the normalization over both branches of the base node.
It is four points, and the normalized map is just \(w\mapsto w^2\).

## 3. The elementary puncture-lattice test is also consistent

Label the four puncture loops \(\beta_1,\beta_2\) over the
first base branch and \(\beta_3,\beta_4\) over the second.
The base node contribution in Kaliman's Lemma 3.1 identifies the
sum of the two base loops with zero in the complement quotient.
Accordingly the induced map on this node-local lattice is

\[
\ell:\mathbb Z^4\longrightarrow\mathbb Z,
\qquad \ell(a)=a_1+a_2-a_3-a_4.
\tag{5}
\]

Its integral kernel is generated by

\[
\beta_1+\beta_3,\qquad
\beta_1+\beta_4,\qquad
\beta_2+\beta_4.
\tag{6}
\]

There is no rank or torsion contradiction: these generators have
rank three and a maximal minor of absolute value one. Each pairs
one branch from each side of the node. This is only an abstract
lattice consistency check. It does not construct the exceptional
surface, its fibers, an excision neighborhood, or the actual map
\(\varphi'\) in Kaliman's diagram.

Exact SymPy controls passed for (3)'s nodal equation, squarefreeness
of \(d\), and the integral rank-three kernel in (6). The preceding
ring and finite-map arguments are written proofs, not numerical
inferences from those controls.

## 4. Remaining gap

A genuine exclusion must use additional realizability constraints
on an exceptional surface inside smooth factorial three-space,
its map to \(Z\), and its global excision data. The abstract
finite curve just constructed need not occur in such a quotient.
Conversely, satisfying these curve-level and lattice tests is not
evidence that a primitive LND realizing the nodal plinth exists.

This pass produces a precise failed obstruction and a primary-source
historical boundary. It does not prove connectedness of rank-three
plinth support, construct a counterexample, or advance a novelty
claim.
