# Rank-sensitive cofactors and the rank-one semigroup boundary

**Status: bounded necessary conditions and an abstract numerical
compatibility control.**

For an actual all-positive sliced polynomial four-ring, the rank of
each triple of coordinate initials sharpens the upper bound for the
omitted coordinate partial. Initial rank one also gives a root-free
monomial-curve description. It follows that a globally degree-minimal
all-positive frame cannot have a coordinate degree in the semigroup
generated by the other three.

The numerical tuple \((4,5,6,7)\) nevertheless passes the specific
shift, triple-minor and derivative-order tests proved below. The
control includes three independent formal invariant-top candidates,
not just a finite list of small-weight inequalities. It is **not**
an actual sliced frame, a globally minimizing frame, or a
counterexample to a coordinate-entry theorem.

## 1. Actual hypotheses and retained mechanisms

Throughout the actual statements, assume
\[
\begin{gathered}
B=\mathbb C[X_1,X_2,X_3,X_4],\qquad
E\in\operatorname{LND}_{\mathbb C}(B),\qquad Eu=1,\\
A=\ker E,\qquad B=A[u],\qquad K=\operatorname{Frac}A,\\
d_i=\deg_E X_i\ge1,\qquad S=\sum_i d_i,\qquad
X_i=\sum_{r=0}^{d_i}c_{i,r}u^r,\qquad
c_i=c_{i,d_i}\in A\setminus\{0\}.
\end{gathered}
\tag{1}
\]
The \(X_i\) are actual polynomial coordinates. The slice \(u\) need
not itself be a coordinate. Every degree is the exact E-degree, or
equivalently degree in the unique presentation \(A[u]\).

Use the actual coordinate partial \(T_j=\partial_{X_j}\) and its
exact horizontal shift \(k_j\) from
[158, Sections 2 and 6](arbitrary-positive-weight-initial-bound-pass158-working.md):
\[
\begin{gathered}
k_j=\max\{\deg_E T_j(a):a\in A,\ T_j(a)\ne0\}\ge0,\\
\deg_E T_j(u)\le k_j,\qquad
\deg_E T_j(f)\le\deg_E f+k_j
\quad(T_j(f)\ne0).
\end{gathered}
\tag{2}
\]
The nonzero horizontal action, existence of the maximum, and all-ring
inequality in (2) are retained with their all-positive hypotheses.
They do not follow merely from estimates on four coordinate images.

For independent \(\eta\), define the full coordinate-initial map
\[
\begin{gathered}
\theta:\mathscr P:=\mathbb C[\mathsf X_1,\ldots,\mathsf X_4]
 \longrightarrow A[\eta],\qquad
\mathsf X_i\longmapsto F_i:=c_i\eta^{d_i},\\
I=\ker\theta,\qquad
r_{\mathrm{init}}=\operatorname{trdeg}_{\mathbb C}\mathbb C[F_1,\ldots,F_4],
 \qquad
r_j=\operatorname{trdeg}_{\mathbb C}\mathbb C[F_i:i\ne j].
\end{gathered}
\tag{3}
\]
Here \(I\) is the full homogeneous prime, and
\(1\le r_j\le r_{\mathrm{init}}\le3\) by 158. The algebra generated
by these initials is not the whole associated graded ring \(A[\eta]\).
The numbers \(r_j\) are initial-algebra ranks, not ranks of the
leading locally nilpotent derivations on \(A\).

## 2. Entire cofactors with a rank-sensitive loss

Choose a separating transcendence basis
\(\xi_1,\xi_2,\xi_3\) of \(K/\mathbb C\). Its partial derivatives
extend uniquely through the finite separable field extension.
They need not preserve \(A\). The actual volume argument of 158
gives
\[
M=\frac{\partial(X_1,X_2,X_3,X_4)}
        {\partial(\xi_1,\xi_2,\xi_3,u)},\qquad
\det M=J_0\in K^*.
\tag{4}
\]
For completeness, the original-coordinate flow \(\exp(sE)\) has
Jacobian one: its determinant is a unit of \(B[s]\) and specializes
to one at \(s=0\). This flow fixes \(K\) and translates \(u\);
therefore the nonzero polynomial determinant in (4) is independent
of \(u\). It need not be a complex constant.

Rescale the last column:
\[
\widetilde M=M\,\operatorname{diag}(1,1,1,u).
\tag{5}
\]
Every row \(i\) is a polynomial row of degree at most \(d_i\),
and its coefficient at degree \(d_i\) is
\[
v_i=(\partial_{\xi_1}c_i,\partial_{\xi_2}c_i,
       \partial_{\xi_3}c_i,d_i c_i)\in K^4.
\tag{6}
\]
Its last entry is nonzero, even if \(c_i\) is complex.

The \(K\)-rank of the three rows \(v_i\), \(i\ne j\), is precisely
\(r_j\). Indeed, form the differential matrix of the corresponding
\(F_i\) in \(K(\eta)\). Multiplying its \(\eta\)-column by \(\eta\)
and row \(i\) by \(\eta^{-d_i}\) produces exactly (6).
Both operations are invertible over \(K(\eta)\). Characteristic-zero
separability identifies differential rank with transcendence degree
over \(\mathbb C\), and rank of a matrix with entries in \(K\) is
unchanged on extending to \(K(\eta)\).

Put \(R_j=\sum_{i\ne j}d_i=S-d_j\). Consider any three-by-three
minor of \(\widetilde M\) using these three rows. Expand the full
polynomial rows multilinearly, retaining every lower coefficient.
A term using more than \(r_j\) of the top rows vanishes: those
selected top rows are linearly dependent, also after restriction
to the chosen columns. Any nonzero term must therefore select a
lower coefficient from at least \(3-r_j\) rows. Each such choice
decreases its degree by at least one. Consequently
\[
\deg_u\bigl(\text{each such minor of }\widetilde M\bigr)
\le R_j-(3-r_j).
\tag{7}
\]
Zero minors cause no exception; their degree is \(-\infty\).
Missing lower coefficients can only improve this bound.

For \(T_j(\xi_\ell)\), the relevant cofactor deletes row \(j\)
and horizontal column \(\ell\). It contains the rescaled last
column and is \(u\) times the original cofactor. Also
\(\det\widetilde M=uJ_0\). Division by \(uJ_0\) in the inverse
matrix gives
\[
\deg_u T_j(\xi_\ell)\le R_j-4+r_j.
\tag{8}
\]
The cofactor numerator is divisible by \(u\), so this calculation
does not introduce a Laurent pole. Equivalently one can divide
the rescaled cofactor by \(u\) first and use (4).
The separating-basis chain rule gives the same bound on \(T_j(K)\);
actual membership in \(A[u]\) then gives it on \(T_j(A)\).
Using the exact horizontal definition in (2), we obtain
\[
\boxed{\quad k_j\le S-d_j-4+r_j.\quad}
\tag{9}
\]
For \(r_j=3\) this is the original upper bound \(S-d_j-1\).
For \(r_j\le2\) it includes the loss proved in 158.
If the full initial algebra has rank one, every \(r_j=1\), and
\[
\boxed{\quad k_j\le S-d_j-3\quad(1\le j\le4).\quad}
\tag{10}
\]
The right side is nonnegative because the other three actual
degrees are positive. No statement about the independent rank
of the four leading actions on \(A\) was used.

## 3. Consequence for the full-kernel derivative order

We use the directly proved mechanism in
[560](arbitrary-initial-kernel-derivative-order-bound-pass560-working.md).
For a nonconstant invariant \(f=P(X_1,\ldots,X_4)\in A\),
let \(Q=P_M\) be the nonzero top source part for weights \(d_i\).
Then \(M>0\), \(Q\in I\), and
\[
\ell_I(Q)=\min\{|\alpha|:\partial^\alpha Q\notin I\}\ge1
\tag{11}
\]
is finite. A sufficiently high source derivative is a nonzero
constant. For every escaping derivative, not only a minimizing one,
560 proves
\[
\begin{gathered}
\deg_E T^\alpha f=M-\sum_i d_i\alpha_i,\\
M\le\sum_i(d_i+k_i)\alpha_i
\le\sum_i(S-4+r_i)\alpha_i.
\end{gathered}
\tag{12}
\]
The exact first line comes from nonvanishing under the full initial
map; lower source weights cannot cancel it. The second line uses
the all-ring shifts and (9). Thus at initial rank one,
for any minimizing multi-index in (11),
\[
\boxed{\quad M\le\sum_i(d_i+k_i)\alpha_i
                   \le(S-3)\ell_I(Q).\quad}
\tag{13}
\]
This is a derivative-order inequality, not a bound on the degree
of a chosen generator of the height-three prime.

## 4. Rank one is a complex-scaled monomial curve

Suppose now \(r_{\mathrm{init}}=1\). Let
\[
g=\gcd(d_1,d_2,d_3,d_4),\qquad n_i=d_i/g.
\tag{14}
\]
Choose integers \(e_i\), which may be negative, with
\(\sum_i e_i d_i=g\), and in the fraction field of the initial
algebra put
\[
T=\prod_iF_i^{e_i}=c\eta^g,\qquad
c=\prod_i c_i^{e_i}\in K^*.
\tag{15}
\]
This is only a fraction-field construction with formal initial
forms, not an ambient change of coordinates.

One has
\[
\operatorname{Frac}\mathbb C[F_1,\ldots,F_4]\cap K=\mathbb C.
\tag{16}
\]
To prove this, if an element of the intersection were not complex,
it would be transcendental over \(\mathbb C\). Since \(T=c\eta^g\)
is transcendental over all of \(K\), it and that element would be
algebraically independent over \(\mathbb C\), contradicting initial
rank one. This uses that \(\mathbb C\) is algebraically closed.

For each \(i\), the element \(F_i/T^{n_i}=c_i/c^{n_i}\)
belongs to the intersection in (16) and is nonzero. Hence
\[
F_i=\lambda_iT^{n_i},\qquad
c_i=\lambda_i c^{n_i},\qquad \lambda_i\in\mathbb C^*.
\tag{17}
\]
In fact \(c\in A\). It is integral over \(A\), since
\(c^{n_i}=\lambda_i^{-1}c_i\in A\) for any \(i\).
The ring \(A\) is normal: an element of \(\operatorname{Frac}A\)
integral over \(A\) is integral over the normal ring \(B\), hence
lies in \(B\), and its extended E-derivative is zero, hence it lies
in \(A\). This proves the assertion. Neither \(c\) nor \(T\) is
claimed to be an actual coordinate. No root has been adjoined.

After complex diagonal rescaling \(\mathsf Z_i=\mathsf X_i/\lambda_i\),
the full prime is exactly
\[
\boxed{\quad
\ker\bigl(\mathbb C[\mathsf Z_1,\ldots,\mathsf Z_4]
 \longrightarrow\mathbb C[T],\quad\mathsf Z_i\longmapsto T^{n_i}\bigr).
\quad}
\tag{18}
\]
It is the monomial-curve binomial ideal generated by all
\(\mathsf Z^\alpha-\mathsf Z^\beta\) with
\(\sum_i n_i\alpha_i=\sum_i n_i\beta_i\).
For a direct proof of fullness, group any source polynomial by
its resulting power of \(T\). Within each group the coefficients
sum to zero exactly when it is a linear combination of differences
from one chosen monomial. Different powers of \(T\) are linearly
independent. Thus no additional non-binomial relation is omitted.

## 5. A genuine necessary restriction on a minimizing frame

Suppose a degree has a semigroup representation
\[
d_j=\sum_{i\ne j}\beta_i d_i,\qquad
\beta_i\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}.
\tag{19}
\]
By (17) the leading coefficients satisfy
\[
c_j=\gamma\prod_{i\ne j}c_i^{\beta_i},
\qquad
\gamma=\lambda_j\prod_{i\ne j}\lambda_i^{-\beta_i}\in\mathbb C^*.
\tag{20}
\]
It follows that the actual elementary replacement
\[
X'_j=X_j-\gamma\prod_{i\ne j}X_i^{\beta_i},
\qquad X'_i=X_i\quad(i\ne j)
\tag{21}
\]
has \(\deg_E X'_j<d_j\). The inverse adds the same polynomial
in the other three coordinates, so this is an actual polynomial
automorphism, not a coefficient-field change or an assertion that
an arbitrary low-degree polynomial is a coordinate.

Choose an actual frame minimizing its sorted nonnegative E-degree
tuple lexicographically among all actual frames, and suppose it
has all degrees positive and initial rank one. Replacing one degree
by a strictly smaller one makes the sorted tuple lexicographically
smaller, so (19) cannot occur. Consequently its four degrees must
be distinct and must form a minimal generating set of their
additive semigroup. In particular none is one. Dividing all four
by their gcd gives a minimally four-generated numerical semigroup.
This is a necessary restriction only; it is not sufficient for
global frame minimality.

## 6. An abstract tuple survives the stated numerical tests

Consider solely the abstract data
\[
(d_1,d_2,d_3,d_4)=(4,5,6,7),\qquad S=22,\qquad
(k_1,k_2,k_3,k_4)=(15,14,13,12).
\tag{22}
\]
The gcd of the four degrees is one. None belongs to the semigroup
generated by the other three: all are less than eight, the sum
of two positive generators, and no two are equal. The displayed
candidate shifts are nonnegative and satisfy
\[
k_i=S-d_i-3,\qquad d_i+k_i=19=S-3.
\tag{23}
\]
Here they are **assigned numbers**, not shifts proved to arise
from actual coordinate partials.

Even the strict triple inequalities retained from
[261, Section 3](horizontal-triple-minor-rank-criterion-pass261-working.md)
do not exclude (22). That note proves that an actual frame with
nonpolynomial kernel would need
\(\sum_{i\ne j}k_i\ge d_j\) for each \(j\), since equality to
\(d_j-1\) supplies its recognition endpoint. For (22), the four
left sums are \(39,40,41,42\), respectively, all greater than
the corresponding \(4,5,6,7\). Also \(S=22\le3\sum_i k_i=162\).
This is only a numerical comparison with that retained criterion;
it assumes no nonpolynomial kernel exists.

Take the abstract monomial-curve map
\[
\theta_0:\mathbb C[\mathsf X_1,\ldots,\mathsf X_4]
 \longrightarrow\mathbb C[\eta],\qquad
\mathsf X_i\longmapsto\eta^{d_i},\qquad I_0=\ker\theta_0,
\tag{24}
\]
and its three homogeneous relations
\[
\begin{aligned}
Q_1&=\mathsf X_2^2-\mathsf X_1\mathsf X_3,
&\operatorname{wt}Q_1&=10,\\
Q_2&=\mathsf X_2\mathsf X_3-\mathsf X_1\mathsf X_4,
&\operatorname{wt}Q_2&=11,\\
Q_3&=\mathsf X_3^2-\mathsf X_1^3,
&\operatorname{wt}Q_3&=12.
\end{aligned}
\tag{25}
\]
All lie in the full prime \(I_0\). Their evaluated Jacobian is
\[
\left(\theta_0(\partial_{\mathsf X_i}Q_j)\right)_{j,i}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
-\eta^6&2\eta^5&-\eta^4&0\\
-\eta^7&\eta^6&\eta^5&-\eta^4\\
-3\eta^8&0&2\eta^6&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{26}
\]
Its minor in columns two, three, four is \(4\eta^{15}\ne0\).
Thus the first Taylor forms of these three relations at the
generic monomial-curve point are independent over \(\mathbb C(\eta)\).

Here is a direct all-degree check. For independent variables
\(Z_1,\ldots,Z_4\), substitute
\(\mathsf X_i=\eta^{d_i}+Z_i\) in \(\mathbb C(\eta)[Z_1,\ldots,Z_4]\).
Let \(L_j\) be the linear Taylor form of \(Q_j\), the rows in
(26) applied to \(Z\). The \(L_j\) are independent linear forms,
so substitution of them defines an injective map
\(\mathbb C(\eta)[V_1,V_2,V_3]\to\mathbb C(\eta)[Z_1,\ldots,Z_4]\).

For a nonzero polynomial \(\Phi\in\mathbb C[V_1,V_2,V_3]\), let \(e\) be its
smallest ordinary monomial degree. Since each \(Q_j\) vanishes
at the generic point, the lowest-degree Taylor part of
\(\Phi(Q_1,Q_2,Q_3)\) is \(\Phi_e(L_1,L_2,L_3)\), which is nonzero.
In particular \(Q_1,Q_2,Q_3\) are algebraically independent.
The same Taylor expansion, in characteristic zero, proves
\[
\ell_{I_0}\bigl(\Phi(Q_1,Q_2,Q_3)\bigr)=e
\tag{27}
\]
whenever \(\Phi\) has positive weighted homogeneous degree for
weights \(10,11,12\).
Indeed each Taylor coefficient is the corresponding evaluated
source derivative divided by its multi-index factorial; testing
nonzero in \(\mathbb C(\eta)\) is exactly testing outside \(I_0\).

If the common weight is \(M>0\), choose a monomial of \(\Phi\)
with ordinary degree \(e\). Its weight is \(M\), so
\[
M\le12e<19e=(S-3)\ell_{I_0}\bigl(\Phi(Q_1,Q_2,Q_3)\bigr).
\tag{28}
\]
More strongly, for **every** escaping derivative \(\alpha\),
one has \(|\alpha|\ge e\), and consequently
\[
M<19|\alpha|=\sum_i(d_i+k_i)\alpha_i.
\tag{29}
\]
The comparisons involving \(M\) are asserted only for a nonzero
weighted-homogeneous \(\Phi\). They do not apply to arbitrary
nonhomogeneous sums: \(Q_1+Q_1^2\), for example, has maximum weight
twenty and derivative order one. Actual invariant **tops** are
homogeneous, which is the precise comparison being tested here.
Thus the derivative-order inequalities of 560, including the
rank-one sharpening (13) and every escaping exact-cost comparison,
are compatible with this entire three-variable homogeneous
top algebra in all degrees. There is room for cubic invariant
growth here, unlike a conclusion that every invariant top must
be a scalar power of one relation.

## 7. Exact boundary of the conclusions

Equations (9), (13), (18), and the actual shear (21) are proved
necessary statements for the actual hypotheses (1).
The assigned data (22)--(29) show that these particular numerical
inequalities, even accompanied by the cited strict triple tests,
do not force semigroup redundancy.

No actual ring \(A\), slice, commuting coordinate partials,
lower coordinate coefficients, Jacobian, or all-ring shifts
realizing (22) have been constructed. The three \(Q_j\) are
formal invariant-top **candidates**, not actual invariants.
Their abstract compatibility is not asserted to satisfy every
other necessary condition on a sliced frame. It neither gives
a counterexample to true frame minimality nor rules out a
different degree-reduction argument using more actual data.

The retained direct inputs 158, 560 and 261 were read with their
actual-ring scopes. No saved checker was executed, imported, or
compiled. Fresh inline algebra checked (26), the weights,
semigroup minimality, and the numerical bounds; the complete
arguments are printed above. No prior source or canonical record
is changed. There is no historical novelty, coordinate-entry,
recognition, or completed-cancellation claim.
