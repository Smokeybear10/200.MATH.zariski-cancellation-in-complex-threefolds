# An actual cusp frame with a quartic generic graph

**Status: exact actual boundary with a two-sided polynomial inverse.**

A quartic graph in the surviving affine-\(y\), nonzero-\(r\)
cusp branch does occur in an actual sliced polynomial frame.
The example below has \(r=1/\tau\), selected derivation
\(N=t\partial_y\), and an actual invariant coordinate
\(t=Y^4+(1-XY)Z+XW\). Its generic coefficient poles
do not prevent a polynomial inverse on the whole four-ring.

This is an explicitly polynomial invariant ring, not a
cancellation counterexample. It prevents a blanket exclusion
of actual quartic graphs and does not recognize every such
frame. The preceding generic restrictions163 remain unchanged.

## 1. The polynomial frame

Start with independent polynomial coordinates \(x,y,t,q\),
put \(E=\partial_x\), and define
\[
\begin{gathered}
X=x,\qquad Y=y+tx,\\
Z=t-y^4+(3t^2y+q)x+t^3x^2,\\
W=-q-y^5-4ty^3-(3t^2-t)y\\
\hspace{8mm}+(qy-ty^4-3t^2y^2-t^3+t^2)x+tq x^2 .
\end{gathered}
\tag{1}
\]
All expressions lie in \(\mathbb C[x,y,t,q]\), with no
denominators. The letter \(q\) denotes a source coordinate,
not a rational coefficient or a normalized generic parameter.

Define a polynomial used in the inverse:
\[
\Gamma(x,y,t)=4ty^3+3t^2y+(6t^2y^2+t^3)x
 +4t^3yx^2+t^4x^3 .
\tag{2}
\]

## 2. A triangular proof of both inverse orders

First make the following actual change in the target:
\[
T_0=(1-XY)Z+XW,\qquad Q_0=YZ-W,
\qquad
Z=T_0+XQ_0,\quad W=YT_0+(XY-1)Q_0 .
\tag{3}
\]
For fixed \(X,Y\), its matrix on \((Z,W)\) is
\(\begin{pmatrix}1-XY&X\\Y&-1\end{pmatrix}\),
whose determinant is \(-1\). The displayed inverse
checks both compositions over \(\mathbb C[X,Y]\),
including where \(X\) or \(XY-1\) vanishes.

Direct substitution of (1) gives
\[
T_0=t-Y^4,\qquad Q_0=q+\Gamma(x,y,t).
\tag{4}
\]
For a check of the first cancellation, the difference
\(W-YZ\) is
\[
-q-4ty^3-3t^2y-(6t^2y^2+t^3)x
 -4t^3yx^2-t^4x^3.
\]
Hence \(T_0=Z+x(W-YZ)\) equals
\(t-(y+tx)^4=t-Y^4\). The second equality in (4)
is the negative of the same difference.

After the target change (3), the forward map is therefore
\((x,y,t,q)\mapsto(X,Y,T_0,Q_0)\) with the triangular
inverse
\[
x=X,\qquad t=T_0+Y^4,\qquad
y=Y-Xt,\qquad q=Q_0-\Gamma(X,y,t).
\tag{5}
\]
Composing (5) with (4) recovers each source coordinate.
Conversely, start with independent \(X,Y,T_0,Q_0\) and
use (5). Then \(y+tX=Y\),
\(t-Y^4=T_0\), and
\(q+\Gamma(X,y,t)=Q_0\).
Thus the transformed forward map recovers all four target
coordinates. Applying the inverse in (3) recovers \(Z,W\).
This proves both polynomial inverse orders for (1), not only
field equality or a constant Jacobian.

In particular
\(B=\mathbb C[X,Y,Z,W]=\mathbb C[x,y,t,q]\), and
\(E\) has the actual coordinate slice \(X=x\).

## 3. Exact time degrees and the full cusp initial kernel

With respect to \(E=\partial_x\), the degrees and ordinary
leading coefficients are
\[
\begin{gathered}
(\deg_E X,\deg_E Y,\deg_E Z,\deg_E W)=(1,1,2,2),\\
(a,b,e,d)=(EX,EY,E^2Z/2,E^2W/2)=(1,t,t^3,tq),\\
\ker\{\mathbb C[\mathsf X,\mathsf Y,\mathsf Z,\mathsf W]
 \longrightarrow\mathbb C[y,t,q][\eta]\}
=(\mathsf X\mathsf Z-\mathsf Y^3),
\end{gathered}
\tag{6}
\]
where the map sends the four formal variables to
\(\eta,t\eta,t^3\eta^2,tq\eta^2\), respectively.
All four leading coefficients are nonzero polynomials.

The fraction field generated by the initials contains
\(\eta,t,tq\), hence also \(q\). These three elements
\(\eta,t,q\) are algebraically independent, so the image
has transcendence degree three. The kernel is a height-one
prime. The displayed cusp polynomial vanishes and is
irreducible, being primitive linear in \(\mathsf Z\)
over \(\mathbb C[\mathsf X,\mathsf Y,\mathsf W]\).
It therefore generates the full kernel, not just a selected
leading relation.

## 4. The exact selected derivation

Let \(T=\partial_Z\) in the actual coordinates
\(X,Y,Z,W\), with \(X,Y,W\) held fixed.
Differentiating (3)--(5) gives
\[
\begin{gathered}
Tx=0,\qquad Tt=1-XY=1-xy-tx^2,\\
Ty=-xTt=-x+x^2y+tx^3,\\
Tq=Y-(\Gamma_t-x\Gamma_y)Tt
 =y+tx-(4y^3+6ty)(1-xy-tx^2),\\
N:=[x^3]T|_{\mathbb C[y,t,q]}=t\partial_y .
\end{gathered}
\tag{7}
\]
For the third line, \(TQ_0=Y\) and direct differentiation
of (2) gives \(\Gamma_t-x\Gamma_y=4y^3+6ty\).
Thus the coefficients on \(t,q\) have degree at most two,
while the coefficient on \(y\) has exact degree three.
Equation (7) computes the full top derivation on all three
polynomial generators of the invariant ring, not merely
its value on one candidate parameter.

Consequently
\[
A=\ker E=\mathbb C[y,t,q],\qquad
S=\ker_A N=\mathbb C[t,q],\qquad
L=\mathbb C(t,q),\qquad v=y/t,\qquad Nv=1.
\tag{8}
\]
The generic parameter \(v\) need not belong to \(A\).
Indeed the raw selected plinth is \((t)\subset S\):
\(N(A)=tA\) by polynomial integration in \(y\), and
intersection with \(S\) gives \(tS\).
The primitive quotient \(N/t=\partial_y\) has the
global polynomial slice \(y\). Nothing singular about
the original coordinate map follows from the pole in
the normalized generic parameter.

## 5. The quartic graph over the exact coefficient field

The selected ratios are \(\tau=t\), \(\rho=tq\),
so \(\mathbb C(\tau,\rho)=L\). At \(X=0\), the
projected coordinates and coefficients159 are
\[
\begin{gathered}
y=y,\qquad z=t-y^4,\qquad
w=-q-y^5-4ty^3-(3t^2-t)y,\\
\sigma=3t^2y+q,\qquad
k=\rho/\tau,\qquad r=k_\rho=1/\tau,\qquad
n=\tau^2,\quad \ell=-\rho/\tau^2,\\
\vartheta=P(y),\qquad
P(U)=-\tau U^4-3\tau^2U^2+(\rho/\tau)U-\tau^3+\tau^2,\\
\Phi(U,V)=\tau^{-2}\{V-P(U)\}+m,
\qquad m=1-\tau,\qquad \lambda=\tau^{-2}.
\end{gathered}
\tag{9}
\]
In the first line, the source coordinate \(y\) is already
the projected coordinate, so no renaming or division is
intended. Equations (9) follow from (1) and the literal
potential159, or from the generic formulas in the preceding
163 note after adding \(-\tau^3+\tau^2\) to the graph.
That change also changes the evaluation constant \(m\);
it is not a change of the potential's coefficient data.

Since \(y=\tau v\), the normalized intrinsic degrees
are \(\deg_v y=1\), \(\deg_v\vartheta=4\).
The quartic coefficient is nonzero in \(L\), and
\(L[y,\vartheta]=L[v]\).
For another calibration check, the determinant in
\((\tau,\rho,y)\) is \(-1/\tau\):
\(z_\tau=1\), \(z_\rho=0\),
\(w_\rho=-1/\tau\), and \(y_y=1\).
Changing to \(v=y/\tau\) multiplies that determinant
by \(\tau\), so \(J_v=-1=-a^3\), as required
for \(a=1\) and \(Nv=1\).

The projected field-return condition is stronger here than
in the earlier nonactual generic example: it is a polynomial
ring equality. Namely
\[
t=z+y^4,\qquad
q=-w-y^5-4ty^3-(3t^2-t)y,
\qquad A=\mathbb C[y,z,w]=\mathbb C[y,t,q].
\tag{10}
\]
Both substitutions and their inverse are polynomial on the
whole projected three-ring, including at \(t=0\).
The coefficient \(r=1/t\) only lives in the generic field;
it is not being asserted regular on that divisor.

## 6. The invariant is an actual coordinate, not just a polynomial

The element
\[
I=Y^4+(1-XY)Z+XW=t
\tag{11}
\]
is fixed by \(E\). Moreover \((X,Y,I,Q_0)\) is an
actual polynomial coordinate system. Combine (3) with the
polynomial translation \(I=T_0+Y^4\); its explicit inverse is
\[
Z=I-Y^4+XQ_0,\qquad
W=Y(I-Y^4)+(XY-1)Q_0,
\tag{12}
\]
with \(X,Y\) unchanged. Substitution checks both orders.
Thus this family is recognized already at the level of
an explicit inverse, without invoking a generic coordinate
as an original-ring coordinate.

In the displayed original weights \((1,1,2,2)\), the
invariant \(I\) has weight four, with top
\(-\mathsf Y(\mathsf X\mathsf Z-\mathsf Y^3)\ne0\).
The low-weight obstruction162 also shows that no nonconstant
invariant of weight at most three exists here, because
\(r=1/\tau\) is nonconstant. These are weights in this
fixed frame, not minimum time degrees over all frames.

## Scope and retained sources

This exact example rules out an unconditional exclusion
of the actual affine-\(y\), quartic-\(\vartheta\) cusp
branch. It also illustrates why the inverse and projected
ring-return conditions must be checked for each proposed
generic solution. It does not prove recognition for every
actual quartic graph, other cusp branches, smaller initial
rank, or arbitrary coordinate entry.
