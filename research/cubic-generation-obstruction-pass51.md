# The full cubic algebra cannot generate at a reduced obstruction

At every reduced actual second-image obstruction point, the algebra
generated by the entire intrinsic module \(F_3\) has a
two-dimensional closed fiber. Consequently it is strictly smaller
than the actual intrinsic ring, whose fiber is one-dimensional.

This is not restricted to a generating list obtained by one slice
projection. The rank-one case has an exact two-equation presentation
and a polynomial-plane fiber. In rank zero, an extra determinantal
equation removes the vertical component; its remaining closed fiber
is still a surface, finite free of degree two over a polynomial
plane. The proof does not assert that any such reduced obstruction
point occurs in an unresolved polynomial cylinder.

## 1. Full actual hypotheses and the known cubic module

Use the actual setup of
[the cubic image theorem](third-image-cubic-gate-pass48.md).
At a reduced obstruction point write
\[
R=C_{\mathfrak m},\qquad \mathfrak n=\mathfrak mR=(a,b),
\qquad \mathcal A=A\otimes_C R,\qquad L=\operatorname{Frac}R.
\tag{1}
\]
The ring \(R\) is a regular local surface ring with residue
field \(\mathbb C\); \(a,b\) are regular parameters.
The actual \(\mathcal A\) is a faithfully flat domain over \(R\)
with a nonempty one-dimensional closed fiber.

The supplied full degree-two divisor setup gives
\[
F_2=R\oplus Rz\oplus Rw,\qquad
F_3=F_2\oplus Rt,\qquad r=az+bw,\qquad I_2=p\mathfrak n.
\tag{2}
\]
For invariant coefficients in \(R\), put
\[
B_1=ac+bd,\qquad B_2=ae+bf,\qquad Q=aq+bh,
\qquad \epsilon\in R^*.
\]
The actual identities are
\[
Dz=\epsilon br+B_1,\quad
Dw=-\epsilon ar+B_2,\quad
Dr=p=aB_1+bB_2,
\tag{3}
\]
\[
\epsilon r^2-2B_2z+2B_1w=Q,
\]
\[
bt=\epsilon rz-2ez+2cw-q,\qquad
-at=\epsilon rw-2fz+2dw-h.
\tag{4}
\]
The first-jet determinant theorem has already proved
\[
cf-de\in\mathfrak n,\qquad D(F_3)\subset\mathfrak nF_3.
\tag{5}
\]
In particular the residue matrix below has rank zero or one.

Set
\[
S=R[Z,W,T],\qquad r_S=aZ+bW,
\]
\[
F=bT-\epsilon r_SZ+2eZ-2cW+q,\qquad
G=aT+\epsilon r_SW-2fZ+2dW-h,
\]
\[
I=(F,G),\qquad
K=R[F_3]=R[z,w,t]\subset\mathcal A.
\tag{6}
\]
There is an actual surjection \(S/I\to K\).

The inherited divisor theorem supplies the stronger local statement
\[
(S/I)_{(\pi)}=K_{(\pi)}=\mathcal A_{(\pi)}
\quad\text{for every height-one prime }(\pi)\subset R,
\qquad
(S/I)_L=L[r].
\tag{7}
\]
To see why the first equality is the correct input, one of
\(a,b\) is a unit over \(R_{(\pi)}\). Eliminating \(T\) in
(6) leaves exactly the conic in (4), and the full divisor theorem
identifies that conic with the actual ring there. This is not an
assertion that the conic is the whole ring over \(R\).

## 2. A shared algebraic presentation test

The two polynomials \(F,G\) have no common prime factor in the
UFD \(S\). A common factor would become a unit over \(L\), since
their generic ideal has height two. It would therefore be
associated to a prime element \(\pi\) of \(R\). But (7) is
a domain in which \(\pi\ne0\), incompatible with a nonzero
prime ideal \((F,G)_{(\pi)}\) contained in \(\pi S_{(\pi)}\).
Explicitly, primeness and avoidance of \(\pi\) allow cancellation
of all powers of \(\pi\) from a nonzero member of this ideal,
contradicting containment in \(\pi S_{(\pi)}\).

We will use a depth test twice. Suppose a finite \(S\)-module
\(N\) has a free resolution of length at most two. At any
associated prime \(\mathfrak p\) of \(N\), repeated application
of the depth lemma gives
\[
0=\operatorname{depth}N_{\mathfrak p}
\ge \operatorname{depth}S_{\mathfrak p}-2
=\operatorname{ht}\mathfrak p-2.
\tag{8}
\]
Thus every associated prime has height at most two.
Here \(S\) is a localization of a polynomial ring over
\(\mathbb C\), so all its local rings are regular and
Cohen--Macaulay. The exact depth inequality and its proof were
directly read in [Stacks 00LX](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00LX);
the regular-local input is
[Stacks 00NQ](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00NQ).

Suppose also that \(N_L=L[r]\) and that the localizations in
(7) hold for \(N\). An associated prime cannot contract to a
height-one base prime: localizing there would give an associated
prime of the domain in (7), whose contraction to \(R_{(\pi)}\)
is zero. If all primes over the closed point that contain the
defining ideal have height at least three, (8) excludes them too.

The only remaining associated prime is the contraction of the
unique generic prime. Consequently every nonzero element of \(R\)
is a nonzerodivisor on \(N\), so
\[
N\longrightarrow N_L
\quad\text{is injective.}
\tag{9}
\]
A natural generically injective map \(N\to\mathcal A\) is then
injective, giving the actual presentation of its image. This
test proves a presentation, not flatness over \(R\).

## 3. Rank one: the entire cubic algebra has a plane fiber

Reduce the two equations in (6):
\[
\overline F=2\bar e Z-2\bar c W+\bar q,\qquad
\overline G=-2\bar f Z+2\bar d W-\bar h.
\tag{10}
\]
Suppose their coefficient matrix in \(Z,W\) has rank one.
The two affine equations must be consistent, since
\(S/(I+\mathfrak nS)\) maps to the nonzero actual fiber
\(\mathcal A/\mathfrak n\mathcal A\). Their ideal is therefore
generated by one nonconstant affine-linear polynomial, and
\[
S/(I+\mathfrak nS)\simeq\mathbb C[V,T].
\tag{11}
\]
In particular every prime containing \(I+\mathfrak nS\)
has height at least three in \(S\).

The coprimality established in Section 2 makes the Koszul
complex on \(F,G\) exact. Hence \(S/I\) has a free resolution
of length two and satisfies (8). Applying the presentation
test gives
\[
\boxed{K=S/(F,G),\qquad
K/\mathfrak nK\simeq\mathbb C[V,T].}
\tag{12}
\]
No miracle-flatness inference has been made: this fiber has
dimension two.

Since the actual \(\mathcal A/\mathfrak n\mathcal A\) has
dimension one, (12) proves \(K\ne\mathcal A\) in this rank case.

## 4. Rank zero: the missing determinant must be retained

Now suppose the residue coefficient matrix in (10) has rank zero,
so \(c,d,e,f\in\mathfrak n\). Actual fiber nonemptiness forces
\(\bar q=\bar h=0\) as well. Choose coefficients in \(R\) with
\[
c=ac_a+bc_b,\quad d=ad_a+bd_b,\quad
e=ae_a+be_b,\quad f=af_a+bf_b,
\]
\[
q=aq_a+bq_b,\qquad h=ah_a+bh_b.
\tag{13}
\]
Then the exact two equations in \(S\) are
\[
F=aA_{11}+bA_{12},\qquad
G=aA_{21}+bA_{22},
\tag{14}
\]
where
\[
\begin{split}
A_{11}&=-\epsilon Z^2+2e_aZ-2c_aW+q_a,\\
A_{12}&=T-\epsilon ZW+2e_bZ-2c_bW+q_b,\\
A_{21}&=T+\epsilon ZW-2f_aZ+2d_aW-h_a,\\
A_{22}&=\epsilon W^2-2f_bZ+2d_bW-h_b.
\end{split}
\tag{15}
\]
Define
\[
\Delta=A_{11}A_{22}-A_{12}A_{21},\qquad
I^\sharp=(F,G,\Delta).
\tag{16}
\]
The identities
\[
a\Delta=A_{22}F-A_{12}G,\qquad
b\Delta=-A_{21}F+A_{11}G
\tag{17}
\]
show that \(\Delta\) vanishes in the actual domain
\(\mathcal A\), by cancellation of the nonzero element \(a\).
Thus \(S/I^\sharp\) maps onto \(K\).

The polynomial \(\Delta\) has the exact shape
\[
\Delta=-T^2+\Lambda(Z,W)T+\Omega(Z,W),\qquad
\deg\Lambda\le1,\qquad\deg\Omega\le3.
\tag{18}
\]
The two quadratic \(ZW\) terms cancel in \(\Lambda\); the two
quartic \(Z^2W^2\) terms cancel in \(\Omega\).
In particular \(\overline\Delta\) is nonzero, with coefficient
\(-1\) on \(T^2\). Every prime containing
\(I^\sharp+\mathfrak nS\) has height at least three.

### Exactness of the determinantal complex

Consider the explicit matrix
\[
M=
\begin{pmatrix}
A_{22}&-A_{21}\\
-A_{12}&A_{11}\\
-a&-b
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{19}
\]
Its maximal minors, with signs, are \(\Delta,-G,F\), and
(17) gives
\[
(F,G,\Delta)M=0.
\]
We verify the resulting resolution directly:
\[
0\longrightarrow S^2
 \xrightarrow{M}S^3
 \xrightarrow{(F,G,\Delta)}S
 \longrightarrow S/I^\sharp\longrightarrow0.
\tag{20}
\]
The matrix has rank two over \(\operatorname{Frac}S\), since
one of its minors \(F,G\) is nonzero. Thus the left map is
injective, and any vector in the kernel of the row in (20) is
a unique rational linear combination of the two columns.

For each height-one prime \(\mathfrak q\subset S\), at least
one of \(F,G\) is a unit in \(S_{\mathfrak q}\), by their
coprimality. A corresponding two-by-two submatrix of \(M\)
is invertible there. Solving for the two rational coefficients
therefore puts them in \(S_{\mathfrak q}\).
The UFD intersection
\[
S=\bigcap_{\operatorname{ht}\mathfrak q=1}S_{\mathfrak q}
\]
makes both coefficients global. This proves exactness at \(S^3\),
and hence all of (20). It is the elementary two-column
determinantal resolution, not a claim that arbitrary minors
automatically give a presentation. The general acyclicity
framework is
[Stacks 10.102.9](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00MR),
whose statement and complete proof were directly read; (20)
has been proved here in this particular UFD setting.

At a height-one base prime, one of \(a,b\) is a unit, so (17)
puts \(\Delta\) in \((F,G)\) there. Therefore the divisor
and generic localizations of \(S/I^\sharp\) are exactly (7).
Its resolution (20), its nonzero residual determinant, and
the presentation test now give
\[
\boxed{K=S/(F,G,\Delta).}
\tag{21}
\]
In particular this is the actual generic-component saturation
\[
(F,G,\Delta)=(F,G):(R\setminus\{0\})^\infty.
\tag{22}
\]
Indeed both sides are the kernel of the map into \(L[r]\).
This is the place where the vertical component of \(S/(F,G)\)
is removed. It cannot simply be discarded without the proof.

Reducing the exact presentation (21) gives
\[
\boxed{
K/\mathfrak nK
 =\mathbb C[Z,W,T]/(\overline\Delta),\qquad
\overline\Delta=-T^2+\overline\Lambda T+\overline\Omega.
}
\tag{23}
\]
It is finite free of rank two over \(\mathbb C[Z,W]\),
with basis \(1,T\), and has dimension two.
It can be nonreduced; no reduction of its scheme structure is
taken. Once again \(K\ne\mathcal A\).

## 5. Full \(F_3\) non-generation and the actual next obligation

The rank-two residue case was already excluded by the actual
topological argument in pass 48. Sections 3 and 4 exhaust the
remaining possibilities. Consequently
\[
\boxed{
J R=\mathfrak n
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
R[F_3]\ne\mathcal A,
\qquad
\dim(R[F_3]/\mathfrak nR[F_3])=2.
}
\tag{24}
\]
Every generating set of \(\mathcal A\) over \(R\) must therefore
contain an element of intrinsic \(D\)-degree at least four.

The actual fiber map
\[
K/\mathfrak nK\longrightarrow
\mathcal A/\mathfrak n\mathcal A
\]
has a nonzero kernel, or equivalently
\[
\mathfrak n\mathcal A\cap K\supsetneq\mathfrak nK.
\tag{25}
\]
The missing relations are created by higher-degree elements of
the actual ring. Height-one equality (7) does not remove this
closed-point defect, and the vanishing of \(D\) on \(F_3\)
modulo \(\mathfrak n\) cannot be extended to the whole ring
by a generation argument.

This improves the
[single-slice obstruction](fiber-invariant-opens-pass50.md):
it rules out every possible \(F_3\) generating set at a reduced
actual obstruction point, not only the four projections from
one chosen slice. It does not prove that such a point exists,
exclude it by itself, or establish a degree-four generation
theorem. Any further exclusion must use higher intrinsic degree,
additional true-frame identities, or another property of the
actual quotient. All files from passes 50 and earlier remain frozen.

## Finite verification boundary

The standalone
[cubic_generation_obstruction_checks_pass51.py](cubic_generation_obstruction_checks_pass51.py)
has two exact methods. They check the universal syzygies and minor
signs, the determinant degree cancellations, and independent
elimination-based saturation in three bounded coefficient models.
The rank-zero controls include both a nontrivial cubic residual
surface and a nonreduced fiber with relation \(T^2=0\).
These coefficient models are not asserted to realize the full
actual-cylinder hypotheses.

The completed run passed two methods in 0.083 seconds, exit zero.
The first run stopped at a structural comparison of an unexpanded
matrix identity with the zero matrix; entrywise expansion repaired
that test-harness comparison without changing an identity.
Only unittest and SymPy are imported. No finite computation proves
the general resolution, associated-prime test, or actual generation
obstruction.
