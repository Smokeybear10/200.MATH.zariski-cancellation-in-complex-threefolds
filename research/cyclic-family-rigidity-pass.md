# Cyclic families from an original slice: formal rigidity and the global gap

The original slice supplies a product and a formally constant cyclic
action near every parameter value. It does not, by the arguments checked
here, supply a global or locally uniform topological conjugacy. The missing
step is control at infinity, not vanishing of infinitesimal deformation
classes. In particular, this pass does not establish constancy of the
integral homology orders of the fixed surfaces.

## 1. The actual family supplied by the slice

Let
\[
A=\mathbb C[x,y,z,u],\qquad F=xu^n+G(x,y,z),\qquad n\ge2,
\]
and suppose an original locally nilpotent derivation satisfies
\(D(F)=1\). Put \(K=\ker D\) and \(t=F\). The slice theorem gives
\[
A=K[t],\qquad M=\operatorname{Spec}K,
\qquad\operatorname{Spec}A\simeq M\times\mathbb A^1_t.
\]
Here \(K\) is finitely generated and smooth because
\(K\simeq A/(F)\) and every slice fiber is smooth. The cyclic action
\(u\mapsto\zeta u\) fixes \(t\), so it transports to an algebraic
family \(\rho_t\) of \(\mu_n\)-actions on the fixed affine threefold
\(M\). It need not preserve the displayed subring \(K\subset K[t]\).

The fixed-point family is exactly
\[
\{u=0\}\simeq\mathbb A^3_{x,y,z},
\qquad t|_{\{u=0\}}=G.
\]
Thus the question is whether this particular family of actions has
fixed sets whose integral homology orders cannot change at infinity.

There is an important comparison with stable coordinatehood. If \(F\)
becomes a coordinate after adjoining \(N\) variables, one already has
an algebraic product over \(F\),
\[
\mathbb A^{4+N}\simeq\mathbb A^{3+N}\times\mathbb A^1_F.
\]
Extend the cyclic action trivially to the added variables. Its fixed
fibers are \(G^{-1}(\lambda)\times\mathbb A^N\), with the same
integral homology as \(G^{-1}(\lambda)\). Consequently a
dimension-independent rigidity theorem for finite-group actions on such
products would apply to stable coordinates as well. The original slice
keeps the ambient factor in dimension three; the existence of a product
alone is not an additional dimension-independent input.

## 2. A precise formal conjugacy lemma

**Lemma.** Let \(R\) be a complex algebra and \(\Gamma\) a finite
group. A continuous \(\mathbb C[[\tau]]\)-linear action
\(\rho\) on \(R[[\tau]]\), reducing to an action \(\rho_0\)
on \(R\), is conjugate to the constant extension of \(\rho_0\)
by a continuous automorphism equal to the identity modulo \(\tau\).

The proof is an explicit averaging argument on derivations, not an
assertion that an average of LNDs is locally nilpotent.

Suppose previous conjugations have made the actions agree modulo
\(\tau^m\). Write, modulo \(\tau^{m+1}\),
\[
\rho(g)=(1+\tau^m c_g)\rho_0(g),
\qquad c_g\in\operatorname{Der}_{\mathbb C}(R).
\]
Multiplicativity makes \(c_g\) a derivation. The group law gives
\[
c_{gh}=c_g+g\cdot c_h,
\qquad g\cdot c=\rho_0(g)c\rho_0(g)^{-1}.
\]
Set
\[
b=\frac1{|\Gamma|}\sum_{h\in\Gamma}c_h.
\]
Then \(g\cdot b=b-c_g\). The formal exponential
\(\phi_m=\exp(\tau^m b)\) is a well-defined continuous
automorphism, and
\[
\phi_m^{-1}\rho(g)\phi_m=\rho_0(g)
\pmod{\tau^{m+1}}.
\]
Iterating and taking the \(\tau\)-adic limit proves the lemma.
Every correction fixes the parameter, and convergence is guaranteed
only in the adic topology. No local nilpotence of \(b\) is required.

Apply the lemma with \(R=K\) and \(\tau=t-\lambda\). It identifies
the completed fixed-point family with the constant formal family of
the fixed-point scheme of \(\rho_\lambda\). Here the fixed-point
scheme means the quotient by the ideal generated by
\(\rho(g)(r)-r\), not the invariant ring defining the orbit quotient.

This is a formal statement along the entire special fiber. It does not
bound the degrees of the coefficient polynomials in the successive
corrections, algebraize their infinite product, or give a convergent
automorphism on all of \(M\) over one common analytic parameter disk.

## 3. Why formal conjugacy does not supply the needed topology

Even the formal automorphism
\[
\exp(\tau z^2\partial_z)(z)
=\frac{z}{1-\tau z}
=\sum_{j\ge0}\tau^jz^{j+1}
\]
of \(\mathbb C[z][[\tau]]\) has a pole somewhere on the affine
line for every nonzero value of \(\tau\). Its inverse is the
analogous series \(z/(1+\tau z)\). Adic invertibility therefore
does not give an automorphism of the whole nearby affine fiber. This
example does not say that a different conjugacy cannot exist.

A related elementary family isolates what formal neighborhoods miss:
\[
V=\{(\tau,z)\in\mathbb A^2:1-\tau z\ne0\}
\longrightarrow\mathbb A^1_\tau.
\]
Its fiber at zero is \(\mathbb A^1\); every nonzero fiber is a
punctured affine line. Nevertheless its completion at \(\tau=0\)
is the product \(\operatorname{Spf}\mathbb C[z][[\tau]]\), since
\((1-\tau z)^{-1}\) is already present as a formal series. The
missing point \(z=1/\tau\) escapes to infinity. This is a control
for the implication from formal triviality to topological triviality,
not a counterexample consisting of a finite-group action on a product.

No algebraic family on a product satisfying the original pure-slice
hypotheses and having different fixed-fiber homology orders was
constructed here. Conversely, the formal lemma does not rule one out.

## 4. Averaging can destroy completeness, not only local nilpotence

On the actual product \(\mathbb A^2_{a,b}\times\mathbb A^1_t\),
let the involution exchange \(a,b\) and fix \(t\). Take the LND
\[
D=\partial_t+b^2\partial_a,\qquad D(t)=1.
\]
Its complete flow is
\((t,a,b)\mapsto(t+c,a+cb^2,b)\). The averaged horizontal
derivation is
\[
E=\frac{D+\sigma D\sigma}{2}
=\partial_t+\frac12\bigl(b^2\partial_a+a^2\partial_b\bigr).
\]
It commutes with the involution and satisfies \(E(t)=1\).
But its trajectory through \((0,1,1)\) is
\[
t(c)=c,\qquad a(c)=b(c)=\frac1{1-c/2},
\]
which escapes to infinity at \(c=2\). Thus averaging the given
complete slice field does not even produce a complete smooth real
horizontal field, much less an algebraic additive-group action.

The cyclic family in this example is constant. Another complete
equivariant horizontal field, namely \(\partial_t\), exists.
The example refutes the proposed construction of a global lift by
averaging, not the existence of all possible lifts.

## 5. Exact scope of the located rigidity theorems

Palais's nearby-action theorem concerns a compact Lie group acting on
a **compact** differentiable manifold: sufficiently close actions are
conjugate. It does not give this conclusion for the noncompact
\(M\) above. The compact-manifold hypothesis is explicit in the
paper's opening statement and Theorem A. The author-uploaded text was
checked; the publisher PDF endpoint did not return the document during
this pass. [Palais, *Equivalence of nearby differentiable actions of a
compact group*](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/248744380_Equivalence_of_Nearby_Differentiable_Actions_of_a_Compact_Group),
Bull. Amer. Math. Soc. 67 (1961), 362–364.

Kraft and Russell prove simultaneous linearization for families of
reductive-group actions on \(\mathbb A^2\) parametrized by a
factorial affine curve. Their generic isotriviality theorem separately
assumes linearizability on general fibers. Neither hypothesis is supplied
for the possibly nonpolynomial threefold \(M\), and generic
isotriviality alone would not control omitted parameter values.
Their proof of the two-dimensional family result explicitly uses
the structure of \(\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb A^2)\).
[Kraft–Russell, *Families of Group Actions, Generic Isotriviality, and
Linearization*, Theorems 2 and 4 and Section 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1204.3196).

These are scope checks of the stated theorems, not a claim that no
stronger theorem exists in the literature.

## 6. A sufficient additional condition and the remaining target

Suppose the action family admits local equivariant topological
trivializations over the whole parameter line. Restricting those
trivializations to fixed sets makes all fixed-fiber integral homology
groups locally constant. Since the line is connected, their orders are
constant. The product formula in
[all-fiber-torsion-constraint.md](all-fiber-torsion-constraint.md) then
forces these finite torsion groups to vanish.

A complete equivariant connection with globally defined transport along
parameter paths would suffice for this condition. A compatible proper
equivariant compactification with controlled boundary would be another
route to investigate. Neither is obtained from the original slice by
the formal conjugacy or averaging constructions above.

Even this conditional integral-acyclicity conclusion would leave the
separate algebraic rectification question. For the simplest completion
of \(xu^n+G\), absolute coordinatehood of \(G\) would still not
itself exhibit the relative pair \((x,G)\).
