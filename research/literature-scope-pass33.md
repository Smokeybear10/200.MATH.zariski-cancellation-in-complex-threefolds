## 1. Replica conjugation: definite existing implication

Arzhantsev, Flenner, Kaliman, Kutzschebauch and Zaidenberg,
[*Flexible Varieties and Automorphism Groups*, arXiv:1011.5375v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1011.5375),
Definition 2.1, Remark 2.7(1), Theorem 4.2 and its proof, and
Corollary 4.3 were read directly. Theorem 4.2 assumes an irreducible
affine variety, a group generated by a saturated set of LNDs, and
orbit separation on the specified orbit. Its isotropy representation
on that orbit's tangent space has image the special linear group.
Remark 2.7(1) supplies separation on an open orbit.

Consequently, on an open SAut orbit, take a point where a prescribed
nonzero LND does not vanish. Isotropy conjugates move its value through
all nonzero tangent directions. Finitely many conjugates therefore
span generically. This is a direct source consequence, not a new
flexibility theorem. The replica differential in Lemma 4.1 supplies
the transvections used in the proof.

The distinction needed in our note is precise. Its elementary
fraction-field argument also treats smaller generic orbit rank and
does not assume an open orbit or use a pointwise separation hypothesis.
Its hypothesis is invariance of the chosen family under the **full**
intrinsic SAut group. Self-generated saturation is weaker. We give a
self-contained bracket proof of that formulation, not a claim that
the formulation is absent from the literature.

The finite augmentation statement additionally records which supplied
LND, replica, and interpolation samples enlarge a current rational
span. It still requires the outside LND as input. Neither the cited
theorem nor this reformulation supplies such a direction from
\(A[s]=\mathbb C^{[4]}\), or proves \(\operatorname{ML}(A)=\mathbb C\).

## 2. Rank-two local-slice construction: definite inherited scope

Gene Freudenburg,
[*Local slice constructions in k[X,Y,Z]*, Osaka J. Math. 34 (1997), 757–767](https://ir.library.osaka-u.ac.jp/repo/ouka/all/4752/ojm34_04_02.pdf),
Section 2, Proposition 2 and Theorem 2, and Section 3, Theorem 1 and
the following degree-descent proof, were read directly. Over a
characteristic-zero field, Section 3 treats every irreducible LND
of rank at most two on a three-variable polynomial ring. Up to
coordinate change, each is obtainable from a coordinate partial by
finitely many local-slice constructions. With an invariant coordinate
\(x\), the theorem provides a coordinate pair over \(k(x)\), a kernel
\(k[x,P]\), and a local slice whose derivative lies in \(k[x]\).
Section 2 explains the permitted slice replacement and the new
Jacobian LND from a minimal relation.

This scope includes the rank-two mechanism underlying the pass-33
fields. Explicitly, with \(h=x^2c-b^2\) and
\(F=p(x)b+x^2h^2\), \(p(0)=1\), the pair \((F,h)\) is a
polynomial coordinate pair over \(\mathbb C(x)\):
\[
b=(F-x^2h^2)/p,\qquad c=(h+b^2)/x^2.
\]
The Hamiltonian \(L=J_F\) has \(Lh=-x^2p\).
It would therefore be misleading to present construction of such
rank-two LNDs from rational coordinates as a new general method.

What this published rank-two theorem does **not by its statement**
specify is a horizontal on the fixed four-dimensional canonical
input, preservation of the marked coordinate \(x\) and slice
\(\lambda\), or adjoint degree exactly one for that fixed vertical
action. The pass-33 proof supplies the additional polynomial
identities
\[
H=h-2\lambda xpF^2,\quad
W=b+4\lambda x^3hF^2-4\lambda^2x^4pF^4,\quad
Z=(H+(1+\lambda x)^2W^2)/x^2,
\]
their global inverse, and
\(\widetilde\Delta=D_0-4\lambda xpF L\).
The localized interpretation is a standard replica flow. It alone
does not prove that the two physical quotients by \(x^2\) are
polynomial. That divisibility is checked separately in the proof.
An exact prior-work collision for this marked construction remains
unresolved here.

## 3. Plinth and modification scope

Freudenburg,
[*Canonical factorization of the quotient morphism for a Ga-action on an affine variety*, arXiv:1602.08786](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1602.08786),
Sections 6–8, gives degree-module factorization by equivariant affine
modifications. Section 7 records the principal plinth for actions on
three-space; Section 8.1 computes the familiar triangular quadratic
example. These are inherited structural tools, not an assertion about
minimal horizontal adjoint degree for a separately marked action.

Kayo Masuda,
[*Factorial affine Ga-varieties with principal plinth ideals*, arXiv:2312.05455](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.05455),
Section 2 and Examples 4.1–4.3 were read directly. Example 4.1 is the
quadratic triangular action with full plinth \(x^2\). Example 4.2
allows \(\delta z=f(x)^e\), \(\delta y=h(x,z)\) over a polynomial
coefficient ring, with irreducible \(f\), the stated leading-coefficient
comaximality, and nonempty fixed locus. It proves full plinth \(f^e\).
Thus arbitrary thickness of a bad divisor is already explicit in
the primary literature. These examples do not state the marked
degree-one canonical realization above.

The pass-33 proof of the full ideal \(p\ker L\) uses all-degree
pole contraction of \(\ker L^2\), including repeated roots. Its
reduced fixed support alone would not prove those multiplicities.
The comparison must retain that distinction.
