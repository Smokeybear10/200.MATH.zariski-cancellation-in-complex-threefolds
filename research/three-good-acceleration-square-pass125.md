# Acceleration squared belongs to the three-good speed ideal

For an actual sliced polynomial action with coordinate time degrees at
most \((1,1,1,2)\), the acceleration squared belongs to the ideal of the
three good speeds. The coefficients can be taken in one actual invariant
polynomial plane. This removes the embedded-point exception left in
[the square-support result84](rank-one-square-embedded-support-pass84.md).
It does not establish first-power membership, a coordinate reduction,
or cancellation.

## 1. Statement and the common invariant plane

Assume
\[
 B=\mathbb C[x_1,x_2,x_3,z]=A[u],\qquad E=\partial_u,
 \qquad E^2x_i=0,\quad E^2z=q\in A.
 \tag{1}
\]
All four displayed generators are genuine polynomial coordinates, and
\(u\) is a supplied polynomial slice, not a presumed ambient coordinate.
Set \(a_i=Ex_i\), \(I_A=(a_1,a_2,a_3)A\), and \(I=I_AB\). Then
\[
 \boxed{q^2\in I_A,\qquad q^2\in I.}
 \tag{2}
\]
More precisely, if some \(a_i\ne0\), there is a nonzero intrinsic
LND \(N\) of \(A\) with
\[
 S=\ker N\simeq\mathbb C[s,t],\qquad
 a_1,a_2,a_3,q\in S,\qquad
 S\longrightarrow A\longrightarrow B\text{ faithfully flat},\qquad
 q^2\in J:=(a_1,a_2,a_3)S.
 \tag{3}
\]
The identification of \(S\) is an abstract polynomial-ring statement
about this actual embedded subring. It is not an ambient coordinate
plane and no algebra retraction of its inclusion is asserted.

The [intrinsic-speed proof125](three-good-intrinsic-speed-quotient-pass125.md)
constructs \(N\) from the highest \(u\)-degree part of the actual partial
\(T=\partial_z\). Its homogeneous leading derivation is \(u^dN\),
with \(d\ge0\), and kills \(u\). The top coefficients of \(Tx_i=0\)
give \(N(a_i)=0\). If \(q\ne0\), write
\(z=c_0+c_1u+qu^2/2\). The coefficient of \(u^{d+2}\) in \(Tz=1\)
is \(N(q)/2\), so \(N(q)=0\). For \(q=0\) this membership is
automatic. The polynomial-plane and flatness conclusions are the
Kaliman--Saveliev application in the
[intrinsic quotient proof](intrinsic-quotient-surface-pass.md).[^ks]

If every \(a_i=0\), then \(E=b\partial_z\) in the original coordinates.
The slice equation \(1=b\partial_z u\) makes \(b\in\mathbb C^*\),
and consequently \(q=0\). Thus this degenerate case also satisfies (2).

## 2. The actual return certificate descends to the plane

It remains to treat \(q\ne0\) and proper \(J\). Faithful flatness
makes properness of \(J\) equivalent to properness of \(I=JB\).
The full written [strict return proof84](rank-one-strict-slice-certificate-pass84.md)
uses the actual slice and the rational time \(-2Ez/q\). It gives
\[
 q^L\in I^{L+1},\qquad L=(D-1)^2\ge1,
 \quad D=\deg_{x_1,x_2,x_3}(u)\text{ over }\mathbb C[z].
 \tag{4}
\]
This is the degree of the supplied slice in the good variables, not
the coordinate time degree or a degree minimized by an unknown change
of variables. The proof in84 also excludes \(D\le1\) in this case.

Because \(q\in S\), (4) contracts through the faithfully flat
inclusion \(S\subset B\):
\[
 q^L\in J^{L+1}\subseteq J^L,
 \qquad q\in\overline J,
 \qquad q^2\in\overline{J^2}.
 \tag{5}
\]
The middle assertion follows directly from the monic equation
\(X^L-q^L=0\), whose constant coefficient lies in \(J^L\).
The last can also be checked without a general theorem about powers
of integral closure: \((q^2)^L=q^{2L}\in J^{2L+2}\subseteq(J^2)^L\)
gives the required monic equation. No assumption that integral closure
commutes with arbitrary base change is used.

## 3. The dimension-two Briancon-Skoda step

For each maximal ideal \(\mathfrak m\) containing \(J\), the ring
\(R=S_{\mathfrak m}\) is a two-dimensional regular local ring with
infinite residue field. Its ideal \(JR\) has a reduction \(K\)
generated by at most two elements. Here a reduction means that \(JR\)
is integral over \(K\). The minimal-reduction statement and its
analytic-spread interpretation are stated in Lipman--Sathaye, printed
pages 203--204. The analytic spread is at most \(\dim R=2\).

For precision, this dimension bound can be seen on the blowup of a
nonzero ideal: its closed fiber lies in the effective Cartier divisor
defined by the invertible ideal, and thus has dimension at most one.
The analytic spread is one plus that fiber dimension. The infinite
residue field supplies a reduction with that many generators.

Since \(q\) is integral over \(JR\) and \(JR\) over \(K\), it is
integral over \(K\). Equivalently, use transitivity of integrality on
the Rees rings. Its square is then integral over \(K^2\), by the
same homogeneous Rees-ring argument for powers or the usual power
property of ideal-integrality. Lipman--Sathaye Theorem 1, with auxiliary
ideal zero, two generators, \(d=1\), and \(\lambda=1\), gives
\[
 q^2\in\overline{K^2}\subseteq K\subseteq JR.
 \tag{6}
\]
Zero can be retained as the second generator when \(K\) is principal.
The associated graded ring of the zero ideal is \(R\), so the
regularity hypothesis of that theorem is satisfied. No negative
exponent or three-dimensional bound is substituted here.[^ls]

Membership is automatic at maximal ideals not containing \(J\).
Since ideal membership is detected at maximal localizations, (6)
proves \(q^2\in J\), and hence (3) and (2). This is the familiar
dimension-two Briancon-Skoda consequence, not a new ideal theorem.
The additional argument is locating both the actual speeds and the
acceleration inside the same faithfully flat polynomial plane.

## 4. A separate support-based reconstruction

There is an independent way to combine the common-plane conclusion
with retained84. Let \(M=S\overline{q^2}\subset S/J\). Flatness gives
\[
 M\otimes_S B\simeq B\overline{q^2}\subset B/I.
 \tag{7}
\]
The square-support result84 confines the support of the module on the
right to finitely many lines in the original four-space. In particular
its support has dimension at most one. This conclusion includes the
possibly embedded, nonreduced good-speed scheme.

If \(M\ne0\), choose a closed point of its support in
\(\operatorname{Spec}S\). The fiber of \(\operatorname{Spec}B\to
\operatorname{Spec}S\) is nonempty and purely two-dimensional, by the
intrinsic quotient theorem and the supplied cylinder. A finite module's
support pulls back exactly under a flat morphism. Thus this entire
two-dimensional fiber would lie in the support of (7), a contradiction.
Consequently \(M=0\). This closes precisely the old possible
one-dimensional ambient square defect. It does not show that the
ambient speed scheme has no embedded components.

This alternative inherits the Briancon-Skoda input already used in84;
it is not claimed to be a proof independent of all that theorem's
dependencies. Its use of fiber dimension differs from Section 3's
direct local application on the invariant plane.

## 5. Exact remaining first-power issue

The actual quotient description82 identifies
\(B/I=(A/I_A)[\bar u]=R_0[\bar z]\), with
\(R_0=A/I_A\), including its nilpotents. Write
\[
 \bar z=c_0+c_1\bar u+\frac{\bar q}{2}\bar u^2,
 \qquad c_1\in R_0^*,\qquad \bar q^2=0.
 \tag{8}
\]
The new square conclusion bounds the acceleration's nilpotence order
by two. It does not imply \(\bar q=0\) or reducedness of \(R_0\).
Putting \(v=(\bar z-c_0)/c_1\), the exact inverse now truncates to
\(\bar u=v-\bar q v^2/(2c_1)\). This is a quotient identity, not
a lift to an ambient polynomial coordinate change.

First-power membership is not a formal consequence of (5), even on
a smooth polynomial plane: \(J_0=(s^3,t^3)\) and \(h=s^2t^2\)
satisfy \(h^3\in J_0^4\), \(h^2\in J_0\), and \(h\notin J_0\).
This elementary monomial control is not an actual action or a
counterexample to first-power membership under (1).

The remaining problem is to use additional original-coordinate
identities to prove \(q\in I\), lower the coordinate time degree,
or obtain a recognition condition. None follows from (2) alone.
Even a complete recognition theorem for (1) would still leave the
unrestricted existence of such a frame unproved.
