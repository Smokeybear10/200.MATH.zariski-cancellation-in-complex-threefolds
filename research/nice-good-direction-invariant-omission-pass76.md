# Invariant omission and transverse content after a good direction is supplied

In the nonlinear velocity-cone branch, a published five-variable
theorem gives two restrictions on an actual nice sliced action
with a supplied constant good direction. No nonconstant invariant
can be expressed in three constant linear forms, and the three
velocities transverse to the supplied direction have gcd one.
Neither statement asserts that their ideal is the unit ideal.

The source theorem does not produce the good direction. Its
algebraic-independence hypothesis is verified here using the
[exact apex identification](nice-leading-apex-pass76.md).

## 1. Actual hypotheses and the supplied apex

Let
\[
B=\mathbb C[x_1,x_2,x_3,w],\qquad
E=P\cdot\partial,\qquad E(s)=1,\qquad E(P)=0,\qquad
J=\operatorname{Jac}P,\qquad J^2\ne0.
\tag{1}
\]
Assume that the image-cone polynomial F in the
[leading-boundary theorem](nice-leading-boundary-pass76.md)
is nonlinear and that a nonzero constant good direction is
supplied. Make a genuine constant coordinate change so that this
direction is \(T=\partial_w\). Thus
\(\operatorname{ad}_E^2T=0\).
The apex theorem gives
\[
F\in\mathbb C[x_1,x_2,x_3],\qquad
G_2=\mathbb C\partial_w,\qquad
F\text{ irreducible and homogeneous}.
\tag{2}
\]
The given slice and the nice condition remain actual polynomial
identities after this constant change. No invariant localization
or nonlinear change of frame is used.

Put \(d=\max_i\deg P_i\), and introduce the homogenizing variable z.
Order the five coordinates as \((x_1,x_2,x_3,z,w)\), with velocity
\[
H=
\bigl(z^dP_1(x/z),z^dP_2(x/z),z^dP_3(x/z),
0,z^dP_4(x/z)\bigr).
\tag{3}
\]
Here \(x/z\) includes \(w/z\). This is only a permutation of the
homogenization already proved to be a homogeneous
quasi-translation with component gcd one. Its image closure is
\[
\mathcal W
=V(F)\times\{z=0\}\times\mathbb A^1_w,\qquad
\mathbb C[\mathcal W]
=\bigl(\mathbb C[x_1,x_2,x_3]/(F)\bigr)[w].
\tag{4}
\]
Consequently H5 is algebraically independent over
\(\mathbb C(H_1,H_2,H_3,H_4)\). This follows from the polynomial
extension in (4), applied to the coordinate ring of the image
closure. It is not inferred from a rank count alone.

There is exactly one independent linear invariant of H, namely z.
Indeed any additional linear relation between the components of H
would give a linear polynomial vanishing on the irreducible
nonlinear hypersurface \(V(F)\) in the original four-space.
Its ideal is (F), so this is impossible by degree.

## 2. The selected published input

De Bondt's Theorem 5.1(iv) applies to a homogeneous complex
five-variable quasi-translation whose component gcd is one, whose
fifth output is algebraically independent of the other outputs,
and which has no two independent linear invariants. It says that
all polynomial invariants expressible in four linear forms belong
to the polynomial ring generated by one invariant of degree at
most one.[^source]

All three gates have been verified in (3)--(4). Since z itself is
a nonconstant invariant expressible in four linear forms, that
degree-at-most-one invariant is nonconstant. The uniqueness of
the invariant linear direction identifies its polynomial ring
with \(\mathbb C[z]\). Thus every H-invariant expressible in four
linear forms lies in \(\mathbb C[z]\).

This is part (iv), which allows one linear invariant. The
no-linear-invariant hypothesis in part (v), and in the subsequent
degree-bound corollary, is false here and is not invoked.

## 3. Descent of the omission restriction

Suppose \(f\in\ker E\) can be written as a polynomial in at most
three constant linear forms in the original four variables.
Let \(m=\deg f\) and homogenize:
\[
\widehat f(x,z)=z^m f(x/z),\qquad
H(\widehat f)=z^{m+d-1}(Ef)(x/z)=0.
\tag{5}
\]
The polynomial \(\widehat f\) is expressible in those three
linear forms and z. Section 2 gives
\(\widehat f\in\mathbb C[z]\). Evaluating at z=1 makes f constant.
This also covers affine linear forms, by absorbing their constant
terms into the polynomial expression.

For any nonzero constant vector c in the original four-space,
\(\ker T_c\) is a polynomial ring in three constant linear forms.
Therefore the exact conclusion is
\[
\boxed{\ker E\cap\ker T_c=\mathbb C
\quad\text{for every nonzero constant }c.}
\tag{6}
\]
In particular it holds for the supplied good partial, but its
conclusion is not restricted to that partial.
It concerns polynomial invariants; no equality of rational
constant fields is inferred.

Equivalently, within the present hypotheses, finding any
nonconstant polynomial E-invariant omitted by a constant
direction contradicts the nonlinear-F alternative. Without
assuming F nonlinear in advance, such an invariant together
with a supplied good direction therefore reduces to the already
recognized linear-F or square-zero cases.

## 4. The full transverse gcd, not the representation factor

Let \(g_0=\gcd(H_1,H_2,H_3,H_4)\), with the zero fourth entry
ignored. There are nonzero transverse entries by (4).
This full gcd is homogeneous. It is invariant under the
homogeneous quasi-translation: every irreducible factor of each
invariant component is invariant, by the degree function of its
locally nilpotent derivation. Hence \(H(g_0)=0\).

Since the full five-component gcd is one,
\(\gcd(g_0,H_5)=1\). The first four terms of \(H(g_0)=0\) are
divisible by \(g_0\), so
\(g_0\mid H_5\partial_wg_0\), and therefore
\(g_0\mid\partial_wg_0\).
Polynomial degree in w forces \(\partial_wg_0=0\).
Thus \(g_0\) is an invariant expressible in the four transverse
linear forms. Section 2 and homogeneity now give
\[
g_0=c z^r\quad(c\in\mathbb C^*,\ r\ge0),\qquad
\gcd(P_1,P_2,P_3)=1.
\tag{7}
\]
For the last assertion, a nonconstant common divisor of the
actual transverse velocities would homogenize to a common
divisor of their three degree-d homogenizations. That
homogenized divisor is not divisible by z and is nonconstant,
contradicting the first assertion of (7).

This argument does not identify the factor called g in the
source's binary-form representation with the full transverse
gcd. It proves the required full-gcd assertion directly.
It also does not replace gcd one by
\((P_1,P_2,P_3)=B\), or identify this velocity ideal with the
primitive fixed-point ideal of a derived intrinsic action.

## 5. Scope and controls

The retained conic example
\(a=xz-y^2+w\) and
\(P=(1,a,a^2,-a^2x+2ay-z)\)
has nonlinear \(F=xz-y^2\) and supplied good partial
\(\partial_w\). Its basic invariant a depends on that coordinate,
and its transverse gcd is one. It is already recognized under
nonlinear coordinates. Thus the restrictions above are not
nonrecognition tests or counterexamples to cancellation.

The result is conditional on a supplied good constant direction.
It neither produces an apex when G2 is zero nor proves primitive
freeness or a common slice. No numerical method, full regression,
historical novelty, human expert review or formal verification is
claimed. All files through pass75 and shared metadata are unchanged.
