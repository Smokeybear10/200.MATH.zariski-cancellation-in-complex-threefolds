## 2. A separating transverse basis and the original centered rows

Put
\[
v=z-t\omega.
\]
Direct differentiation in the exact \((t,z)\) basis gives
\[
\det\frac{\partial(\omega,v)}{\partial(t,z)}
=\omega_t(1-t\omega_z)-\omega_z(-\omega-t\omega_t)
=\mathcal D\omega\ne0.
\tag{3}
\]
Thus \((\omega,v)\) is a separating coefficient basis and L is finite
over \(\mathbb C(\omega,v)\). No assumption on either \(\omega_t\)
or \(\omega_z\) alone is needed. In particular we never assume that
\((t,\omega)\) separates.

Its extended partials commute. Let
\(F_v=\ker_L\partial_\omega\) and
\(F_\omega=\ker_L\partial_v\). These are finite over
\(\mathbb C(v)\) and \(\mathbb C(\omega)\), respectively, and the
remaining partial on each has constants exactly \(\mathbb C\). For
completeness, differentiating the monic minimal polynomial of a constant
over the rational two-variable field forces all its coefficients into
the rational one-variable constant field, by minimality. Finite constant
subextensions have degrees bounded by the degree of L, since adjoining
the transcendental other parameter preserves their degrees. Their union
is therefore finite. The joint constant field remains \(\mathbb C\).
In particular omega is transcendental over \(F_v\), and v is
transcendental over \(F_\omega\). No equality with either rational
constant field is asserted.

Define, at fixed X,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\xi&=P+t(X-\lambda),&R&=r_{\rm old}+\lambda^2t,\\
A_1&=\mathcal Dk+4\lambda t,&\phi&=k+2\lambda t^2-A_1t,\\

The entire original rows become

## 3. The high scalar first forces h1 to vanish

Set \(h=h_1\omega+h_0\) and \(e=D_0-h_1v\). Both are nonzero
elements, by (2) and the independence of omega and v. The entire
derivative matrix of (5), with columns \((\omega,v,\xi)\), is
\[
\begin{pmatrix}
h\xi/2+R_\omega&R_v&X+\lambda\\
e\xi+G_\omega&-h\xi+G_v+eX&2\xi+A_1\\
\xi^2+b_\omega\xi+c_\omega+H_\omega X&
(b_v+X)\xi+c_v+H_vX&2\omega\xi+b+vX
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{7}
\]
Its relevant highest coefficients are
\[
\begin{aligned}
[X^3]J&=-eH_\omega,\\
[X^2\xi]J&=G_\omega+eH_v+hH_\omega-eb_\omega+ehv/2,\\
[X^2]J&=-G_vH_\omega+G_\omega H_v-e\lambda H_\omega
                                      +evR_\omega-ec_\omega,\\
[\xi^3]J&=2R_v-hb_v-h^2\omega+h\lambda.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]
They must all vanish. Since e is nonzero, \(H\in F_v\).
Integrating only by subtracting the displayed expressions gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
G&=e(b-H_v\omega-v\lambda)+G_0,\\
c&=H_v(b-H_v\omega-v\lambda)+vR+c_0,
\qquad G_0,c_0\in F_v,\\
2R&=hb+h(h\omega-\lambda)v+\mathcal Q,
\qquad\mathcal Q\in F_\omega.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]
For example the first relation uses
\(G_\omega=e(b_\omega-H_v-hv/2)\) and
\(\lambda_\omega=h/2\). All divisions in (8),(9) use only the
already nonzero e; the last equality uses no division by h. The symbol
\(\mathcal Q\) is this coefficient function, not the genuine fourth
potential.

After (9), the full \(X\xi^2\) coefficient is \(-\mathcal F/4\),
where
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal F={}&4\mathcal Q_\omega
 +(15h_1^2v-3D_0h_1)\omega^2\\
&+(2Ch_1-4D_0H_{vv}-4D_0h_0+8h_1H_v
                       +4h_1vH_{vv}+18h_0h_1v)\omega\\
&+2Ch_0-4D_0\lambda_0+4(G_0)_v+4h_0H_v
                                  +2h_0^2v+4h_1\lambda_0v.
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]
This follows by expanding (7), so it includes the derivatives of every
moving lower coefficient in (9). Differentiate \(\mathcal F=0\) in v.
The term \(\mathcal Q_\omega\) has zero v derivative by exact
commutation. The result is a polynomial in omega over \(F_v\), whose
quadratic coefficient is \(15h_1^2\); every other term has omega degree
at most one. Transcendence of omega over \(F_v\) therefore gives
\[
h_1=0,\qquad h=h_0\in\mathbb C^*,\qquad d:=D_0\in\mathbb C^*.
\tag{11}
\]
The last two nonzero conditions now follow from (2). Thus the initially
allowed case \(D_0=0\) has been excluded, rather than divided out.

X_*=X,\quad Y_*=Y,\quad Z_*=Z-\phi_0X,\quad

## 5. The remaining high coefficient and exact constant descent

In the normalized notation, (9) reads
\[
\begin{aligned}
G&=d(b-H_v\omega-hv\omega/2)+G_0,\\
c&=H_v(b-H_v\omega-hv\omega/2)+vR+c_0,\\

\qquad H,G_0,c_0\in F_v,\quad\mathcal Q\in F_\omega.
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
\]
The normalized \(X\xi^2\) equation is
\[
\mathcal Q_\omega
=d(H_{vv}+h)\omega-(G_0)_v-hH_v-h^2v/2.
\tag{17}
\]
Its v derivative, followed by coefficient comparison over \(F_v\),
gives \(H_{vvv}=0\) and
\((G_0)_{vv}=-hH_{vv}-h^2/2\). Exact constants then give
\[
\begin{aligned}
H&=av^2/2+b_0v+H_0,\\
G_0&=-(ha/2+h^2/4)v^2+gv+g_0,\\
\mathcal Q&=d(a+h)\omega^2/2-(g+hb_0)\omega+q_0,
\qquad a,b_0,H_0,g,g_0,q_0\in\mathbb C.
\end{aligned}
\tag{18}
\]
Each integration follows by subtracting a known polynomial from an
existing element of its exact constant field. For example Hvv is in
\(F_v\) and has zero v derivative, hence is complex; the same reasoning
applies to each successive difference. No field has been assumed
rational, and the function b is still arbitrary in L.

\(U=k_0(d\omega+hv)-g\). Write \(c_0'=\partial_vc_0\).

&-ahk_0v^2+(ag-b_0hk_0)v+b_0g+dq_0/2,\\

\]
The notation G2 is not the lower function G in (5). Equations (19),(20)

\[
a=-h,\qquad
c_0=-\frac{hg}{2d}v^2+c_Av+c_B,\qquad c_A,c_B\in\mathbb C.
\tag{22}
\]
This argument did not divide by U or g. The remaining split is exactly
the zero and nonzero values of the complex number g.

## 6. The zero-g branch makes the entire scalar vanish

If \(g=0\), equation (19) gives
\([X\xi]J=d(c_A+q_0/2)\). Hence \(c_A=-q_0/2\).
The coefficient function b is still unrestricted. Define
\[
\mathscr L=b+h\omega v/2-b_0\omega.
\]
Equations (16),(18),(22) become
\[
\begin{aligned}
R&=h\mathscr L/2+q_0/2,&
G&=d\mathscr L+h^2v^2/4+g_0,\\
c&=(b_0-hv/2)\mathscr L+c_B,&
H&=-hv^2/2+b_0v+H_0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{23}
\]
At fixed X, put \(r_*=\xi-hv/2\) and
\(T_*=\omega\xi+\mathscr L+vX\). Exact substitution into the
three rows, including their constant coefficients, gives
\[
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
Y&=q_0/2+Xr_*+hT_*/2,\\
Z&=g_0+r_*^2+dT_*,\\
W&=c_B+H_0X+(r_*+b_0)T_*.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{24}
\]
Their differentials at fixed X are combinations of just
\(dr_*\) and \(dT_*\). Therefore the entire three-by-three determinant
is zero. This contradicts (6), which remains nonzero after (12),(13).
This is an identity for arbitrary b in the exact field, not a polynomial
or rational ansatz for b. No assertion that r* or T* is an actual
coordinate is needed for the rank-two differential argument.

## 7. The nonzero-g branch leaves an impossible quadratic coefficient

Suppose instead that \(g\ne0\), and put \(L_0=q_0/2+c_A\in\mathbb C\).
After (22), the two equations (19) reduce to
\[
\begin{aligned}
b_\omega&=-d\omega-2hv+b_0+dL_0/g,\\
b_v&=-2h\omega-h^2v/d+hL_0/g.
\end{aligned}
\tag{25}
\]
Subtract their common explicit polynomial primitive. The difference
has both derivatives zero in L, so it is a complex constant. Thus
\[
b=-\frac d2\omega^2-2h\omega v-\frac{h^2}{2d}v^2
 +(b_0+dL_0/g)\omega+(hL_0/g)v+b_C,\qquad b_C\in\mathbb C.
\tag{26}
\]
The only new division in this branch is by its explicitly nonzero g.
No vanishing value of bC, L0 or any of the other complex constants has
been removed.

Now expand the full original-normalized determinant using (16),(18),
(22),(26). Its entire coefficient of \(X\xi^0\) is
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
[X]J=\frac{3g}{4}N_0^2+(b_0g-2dL_0)N_0
       +\frac{d^2L_0^2}{g}-b_0dL_0-\frac{dg b_C}{2},
\qquad N_0=d\omega+hv.}
\tag{27}
\]
In particular the omega-squared coefficient is \(3d^2g/4\ne0\).
All coefficients of this polynomial lie in \(\mathbb C(v)\subset F_v\).
It cannot vanish because omega is transcendental over \(F_v\), whereas
the nonzero scalar calibration requires every positive-X coefficient
to vanish. This is the required contradiction. The constant coefficient
of J has never been set to zero in this branch.
