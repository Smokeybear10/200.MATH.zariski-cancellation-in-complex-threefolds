# Exact base-ideal content from primitive scheme-fiber nonvanishing

Primitive nonvanishing on every whole quotient fiber combines with
flatness to give an exact content test. If an invariant multiple
hD takes all its values in the extension of a base ideal L, then
h belongs to L itself, including its multiplicities. This does
not require the primitive field D to be fixed-point-free.

For the binary-parameter branch, the consequence is
\(h\in(p,q)^{2e-1}S\) whenever the raw intrinsic field has the
stated image order. It does not exclude the proper parameter
ideal. A primitive field may still have fixed points, while
remaining nonzero on the whole scheme fiber.

## 1. A flat-annihilator lemma

Let S be a Noetherian ring, let A be a flat S-algebra, and let D
be an S-derivation of A. Assume its induced derivation on each
scheme fiber is nonzero:
\[
D_{\mathfrak p}\ne0
\quad\text{on }A\otimes_S\kappa(\mathfrak p)
\quad\text{for every }\mathfrak p\in\operatorname{Spec}S.
\tag{1}
\]
The fibers in (1) are not replaced by their reductions. In
particular (1) entails that each fiber ring is nonzero.

Then, for every ideal L of S,
\[
\boxed{\{c\in S:cD(A)\subseteq LA\}=L.}
\tag{2}
\]
The inclusion from right to left is immediate. For the reverse,
suppose c is in the set on the left but not in L.
The cyclic submodule \(S\overline c\) of \(S/L\) is nonzero.
It contains an element \(\overline b=\overline{rc}\) whose
annihilator is a prime ideal \(\mathfrak p\).

For completeness this associated-prime step is elementary.
Choose a maximal annihilator among the nonzero elements of
the cyclic submodule, possible because S is Noetherian.
If \(uv\overline b=0\) but \(v\overline b\ne0\), maximality
forces the annihilator of \(v\overline b\) to equal that of
\(\overline b\); hence u belongs to the latter. This proves
that the chosen annihilator is prime.[^flat]

Multiplication by \(\overline b\) gives an injection
\(S/\mathfrak p\longrightarrow S/L\).
Flatness, not reducedness or freeness of a fiber, gives the
injection
\[
A/\mathfrak p A\longrightarrow A/LA,\qquad
\overline a\longmapsto\overline{ba}.
\tag{3}
\]
Every D(a) maps to zero in (3), because b is an S-multiple
of c and \(cD(A)\subseteq LA\).
Therefore \(D(A)\subseteq\mathfrak p A\).
Localizing by \(S\setminus\mathfrak p\) makes the induced
derivation on \(A\otimes_S\kappa(\mathfrak p)\) zero, contrary
to (1). This proves (2).

Thus the ideal generated by the residues of D(A) in A/LA
is faithful as an S/L-module. It may nevertheless be a
proper ideal of A/LA. The assertion is about annihilators
coming from the base, not all annihilators in the fiber algebra.

## 2. The retained primitive-fiber hypothesis

The [pass54 whole-fiber theorem](primitive-fiber-nonvanishing-pass54.md)
supplies (1) in the following exact situation:
\[
\begin{gathered}
A\text{ is the ring of a smooth factorial complex threefold},\\
0\ne D\text{ is a primitive LND},\qquad
S=\ker D\simeq\mathbb C[u,v],\qquad
S\subset A\text{ is faithfully flat}.
\end{gathered}
\tag{4}
\]
Its complete saved proof was freshly reread for this application.
The statement is stronger than nonvanishing at some ordinary
point of a reduced fiber: D is nonzero on the local scheme at
the generic point of every component of a closed fiber.

The essential closed-fiber argument localizes at a height-two
prime over the two base parameters, chooses a transcendental
coordinate along that fiber component, and obtains a smooth
surface over its rational function field. The transverse
Jacobian determinant is nonzero in the resulting Artinian
complete intersection by the characteristic-zero socle theorem.
Primitivity makes the corresponding regular Jacobian field a
regular multiple of D. Hence D cannot vanish modulo the
parameter ideal. The saved proof retains scalar extension,
Fitting ideals and nilpotents in this step.

For a height-one base prime, a zero scheme-fiber derivation
would make the nonunit base parameter divide every D-value in
the localized UFD, contradicting primitivity. On the generic
fiber D is plainly nonzero. These three prime heights exhaust
the plane S, so (1) holds for every prime, as required in (3).

This uses the published socle theorem through the complete
pass54 proof, not a new assertion that every quotient fiber
is reduced or is an affine line.[^fiber]

## 3. Application to the content of the derived intrinsic field

In the actual supplied-good-direction setting, assume that
the derived field Q is independent of the original E and put
\(\delta=Q|_A\ne0\). Retain the primitive construction and
quotient hypotheses:
\[
\delta=hD,\qquad 0\ne h\in S=\ker D=\ker\delta,\qquad
S\simeq\mathbb C[u,v],
\quad S\subset A\text{ faithfully flat},
\quad A\text{ as in (4)}.
\tag{5}
\]
The content h is invariant by the LND degree argument in the
[pass75 primitive construction](primitive-good-quotient-scope-pass75.md).
Explicitly, \(\delta(A)\subseteq hA\) implies
\(\delta(h)\in hA\). Local nilpotence forces \(\delta(h)=0\);
the domain identity \(\delta=hD\) then gives \(D(h)=0\).
The same domain identity gives \(\ker\delta=\ker D\).
Thus \(h\in S\), but it is not assumed to be a unit.
The binary parameters belong to S because they are killed
by both E and Q.

For this application use the actual data of the
[binary conormal note](binary-conormal-obstruction-pass77.md):
\(T=\partial_w\), \(Q=[T,E]\), the original polynomial slice
of E, and transverse velocities in \((p,q)^eA\), with
\(e\ge2\).

Let \(L=(p,q)S\) and let \(N\ge1\). Since
\((p,q)^N A=L^N A\), equations (2) and (5) give the exact
equivalence
\[
\boxed{\delta(A)\subseteq(p,q)^N A
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad h\in(p,q)^N S.}
\tag{6}
\]
In particular the given vanishing input with \(N=2e-1\)
forces that exact power of the content. No assumption of
primitive fixed-point-freeness is used.
This sharpens a content conclusion obtained under that
additional assumption, but does not establish the assumption.

If \(L\subseteq\mathfrak m\) and h were a unit at
\(\mathfrak m\), even the weaker vanishing
\(\delta(A)\subseteq\mathfrak mA\) would contradict the
primitive theorem. The actual conclusion of (6) is more
precise: the whole base-ideal thickening belongs to the
content. Dividing by h can remove this forced vanishing.

Nothing in (2) or (6) says that D(A) generates A.
Nor does it exclude fixed points on the reduction of the
fiber, where the socle information can disappear.

There is a further exact first-derivative restriction.
For every \(f\in S\), both \(Ef\) and \(Qf\) vanish.
Thus \(E(Tf)=-Qf=0\). Also the slice makes P4 a unit
modulo \((p,q)^eB\), and
\(0=Ef=\sum_{i\le3}P_i\partial_i f+P_4Tf\)
gives \(Tf\in(p,q)^eB\).
Contracting from \(B=A[s]\) therefore gives
\[
\begin{gathered}
T(S)\subseteq(p,q)^e A,\qquad
T(L^N)\subseteq(p,q)^{N+e-1}A\quad(N\ge1),\\
Th\in(p,q)^{3e-2}A
\quad\text{if }\delta(A)\subseteq(p,q)^{2e-1}A.
\end{gathered}
\tag{7}
\]
For the second inclusion, express an element of \(L^N\) as
an S-linear combination of degree-N monomials in p,q.
Differentiating its coefficient contributes to
\((p,q)^{N+e}A\); differentiating one monomial factor
contributes to \((p,q)^{N+e-1}A\).
The last inclusion follows from (6) with \(N=2e-1\).
Every target ring in (7) is A, not S. No assertion that
T preserves S, or that this restriction can be iterated
on arbitrary elements of A, is made.

## 4. An intrinsic control showing the remaining limitation

Reuse the classical threefold and its primitive LND:
\[
A_0=\mathbb C[x,y,z],\qquad
D_0=x\partial_y+2y\partial_z,\qquad
S_0=\mathbb C[u,v],\quad u=x,\quad v=xz-y^2.
\tag{8}
\]
The presentation
\(A_0=S_0[y,z]/(y^2-uz+v)\) is exact, and its monic relation
gives the free S0-basis
\(\{z^j,yz^j:j\ge0\}\). Thus S0 is faithfully flat in A0.
After inverting u the derivation has slice y/u and kernel
\((S_0)_u\). Intersecting with the free S0-expansion of A0
gives \(\ker D_0=S_0\).
The generator values x and 2y have gcd one, so D0 is primitive.

For \(L_0=(u,v)\) and any integer \(N\ge1\), take
\(\delta_N=u^N D_0\). Its exact content is \(u^N\), and
\[
\delta_N(A_0)\subseteq L_0^N A_0,\qquad
u^N\in L_0^N\setminus L_0^{N+1},\qquad
A_0/L_0A_0=\mathbb C[y,z]/(y^2),\qquad
\overline{D_0}(z)=2y\ne0.
\tag{9}
\]
The primitive coefficient ideal is (x,y), so D0 has fixed
points. It vanishes on the reduced fiber at \(L_0\) but not
on the whole scheme fiber. Meanwhile \(\delta_N\) vanishes
on that entire scheme fiber for every N. Arbitrarily high
raw vanishing is compatible with the primitive theorem:
it is carried by the content exactly as (6) requires.

This example satisfies the intrinsic smoothness, factoriality,
flatness and quotient assumptions. It is not asserted to be
the derived field of the required original nice four-coordinate
action, so it is not a counterexample in the full remaining
branch. It shows that the intrinsic data and high raw image
order alone do not exclude a proper parameter ideal or imply
primitive freeness.

## 5. Scope of the completed step

The new step is the flat-annihilator proof (2) and its exact
content equivalence (6), using the already established
primitive scheme-fiber theorem. To exclude the binary
obstruction, an additional restriction on the actual content
h or on the original four-coordinate realization is needed.
No upper bound on h has been inferred.

No new numerical method, full regression, historical novelty,
human expert review or formal verification is claimed.
