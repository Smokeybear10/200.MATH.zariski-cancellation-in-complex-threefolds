## 3. The full conic and normalized-trace descent

The actual-ring argument of557 supplies an irreducible homogeneous
quadratic H_in over C generating the FULL coordinate-initial
kernel of the actual degree-(4,4,4) three-ring over C(X). Its
leaders are (r,1+2tr,w4), and the first two initials are independent.
In particular H_in involves its W variable, and
\[
\mathcal Q(T,U):=H_{\rm in}(1,15T/2+2\tau,U)=0.
\tag{12}
\]
This polynomial is irreducible in C[T,U], depends on U, and has
total degree two. Indeed the change in the second homogeneous
variable is invertible over C. Dehomogenization of the irreducible
quadratic remains irreducible, and a drop to total degree at most
one would make its homogeneous quadratic divisible by the first
variable. Neither is possible for H_in. In particular we do not
assume that the coefficient of U squared is nonzero.

Put
\[
K=\mathbb C(T,U)\subset F_0,
\qquad [K:\mathbb C(T)]\in\{1,2\}.
\tag{13}
\]
This K is a coefficient-curve field, not the actual three-ring's
base field C(X). The original F0 may be a further finite extension
of K. Let n=[F0:K]. Characteristic zero gives a separable extension,
and differentiation commutes with trace. To see the latter,
extend the derivation to a common normal closure and express
the trace as a sum of the distinct K-embeddings. Uniqueness of
the extended derivation makes every summand commute with it.
Therefore
\[
\overline\mu=\frac1n\operatorname{Tr}_{F_0/K}(\mu)\in K,
\qquad \mathcal L\overline\mu=-\frac4{11}U.
\tag{14}
\]
The right side is in K and the normalization by n fixes it.
This is a descent of a solution to a linear differential equation,
not a rationality assertion about F0 and not an actual coordinate
map. All analysis of rational functions below occurs only after
this justified descent and, in the quadratic case, an involution
split inside K.

## 4. Vanishing U-squared coefficient: the rational case

Suppose the coefficient of U squared in Q is zero. Then
\[
\mathcal Q=q_1(T)U+q_0(T),\qquad
0\ne q_1\in\mathbb C[T]_{\le1},\quad
q_0\in\mathbb C[T]_{\le2}.
\tag{15}
\]
Irreducibility gives gcd(q1,q0)=1. Here K=C(T), and the traced
solution in (14) is a rational function of T.

If q1 has degree one, U has a simple pole at its unique root
beta, with no numerator cancellation. Such a pole cannot be
the image under L of a rational function. At beta nonzero, a
pole of order k>=1 of a rational f contributes a pole of order
k+2 to Lf, with nonzero leading coefficient beta*k*(k+1).
If f is regular there, Lf is regular. At beta=0 a leading term
A*T to the minus k contributes
A*k*(k+7/11)*T to the minus(k+1), again of order at least two;
a regular f gives a regular Lf. Thus neither location can give
the required simple pole. This contradicts (14).

Consequently q1 is a nonzero complex constant, and U is a
polynomial of degree at most two. Its degree is exactly two:
otherwise Q would have total degree at most one, contrary to
Section3. This gives family I in (1), including arbitrary zero
values of u0 and u1 but requiring u2 nonzero.

## 5. Nonzero U-squared coefficient: every branch point is controlled

Suppose the coefficient A of U squared is nonzero. Write
Q=A*U squared+q1(T)*U+q0(T), with A complex, deg q1<=1 and
deg q0<=2. Completing the square INSIDE K gives
\[
U=u_0+u_1T+V,\qquad
V^2=\Pi(T):=q_1(T)^2/(4A^2)-q_0(T)/A.
\tag{16}
\]
Here u0+u1T=-q1/(2A), and V is the existing difference U-u0-u1T.
Irreducibility implies Pi is not a square in C(T). A polynomial
of degree at most two over C which is nonsquare is necessarily
nonzero, squarefree and of degree one or two. A constant, the
zero polynomial, or a quadratic with a repeated root would instead
make Q reducible. Thus K=C(T,V) is genuinely quadratic.

Write the traced solution uniquely as A0(T)+B(T)V with A0,B
rational in T. The involution V maps to -V commutes with the
derivation. Its odd part satisfies
\[
\mathcal L(BV)=-\frac4{11}V,
\qquad B\in\mathbb C(T)^*.
\tag{17}
\]
The rational coefficient B is nonzero because the right side is.
This split prevents cancellation with the even part at either
infinite place from affecting the following order calculations.

First B has no finite poles. At a nonzero point beta which is
not a root of Pi, a pole of B is a pole of BV; the second derivative
term of L creates an uncancelled pole two orders higher, while
the right side of (17) is regular. At T=0 with Pi(0) nonzero,
a pole of order k of BV instead gives a pole of order k+1 with
the same nonzero Euler coefficient k*(k+7/11).

At a simple root beta of Pi, put j=ord_beta B. In the local
Puiseux expansion, BV has leading exponent
\(\epsilon=j+1/2\) in T-beta. If beta is nonzero, its leading
term under L has exponent epsilon-2 and nonzero multiplier
beta*epsilon*(epsilon-1). Half-integrality excludes both zero
factors. The right side has exponent 1/2, so j=2. If beta=0,
the Euler multiplier is epsilon*(epsilon-7/11), also nonzero
for any half-integer epsilon; the exponent is epsilon-1.
Comparison with 1/2 now gives j=1. We have proved
\[
\begin{gathered}
B\in\mathbb C[T]\setminus\{0\},\qquad
\operatorname{ord}_\beta B=2
\quad(\Pi(\beta)=0,\ \beta\ne0),\\
\operatorname{ord}_0B=1\quad\text{if }\Pi(0)=0.
\end{gathered}
\tag{18}
\]
Now let N=deg B>=0 and d_pi=deg Pi in {1,2}. At infinity the
leading exponent of BV as a power of T is N+d_pi/2. For any
monomial with exponent epsilon the Euler operator has leading
multiplier epsilon*(epsilon-7/11) and lowers that exponent by one.
Here epsilon is a positive integer or half-integer, so neither
factor vanishes. Since the right side of (17) has exponent
d_pi/2, it follows that
\[
N=1.
\tag{19}
\]
This checks possible leading cancellation rather than assuming
a degree rule for the differential operator.

If Pi has degree two, its two roots are distinct. Condition (18)
requires total zero order at least four when both are nonzero,
and at least three when one is zero. Both contradict (19).
If Pi has degree one and its root is nonzero, (18) again demands
zero order two, impossible for B of degree one. Therefore
\[
\boxed{\Pi(T)=b_*T,\qquad b_*\in\mathbb C^*.}
\tag{20}
\]
This is family II in (1). It retains the branch at T=0 and the
branch at infinity. No finite branch point was removed by a
denominator or a generic-parameter convention.

## 6. The exact existing-field formulas for m0/h

Both surviving forms have explicit particular solutions. In I,

                       -\frac{2u_2}{39}T^3,
\qquad \mathcal L\mu_{\rm I}=-\frac4{11}U.
\tag{21}
\]
In II, V squared=b_*T gives V_T=V/(2T). Thus

\[
\mu_{\rm II}=-u_0T-\frac{2u_1}{15}T^2
                       -\frac{16}{57}TV,
\qquad \mathcal L\mu_{\rm II}=-\frac4{11}U.
\tag{22}
\]
The divisions by T here are coefficient-field operations on a
nonzero transcendental element. The place T=0 was explicitly
retained in the pole proof.

For the original mu in F0, subtract the appropriate particular
solution. The difference zeta satisfies L*zeta=0. Since
h_T/h=1/(11T), this is equivalent to
(h to the fourth*zeta_T)_T=0. All terms are also s-constant.
Hence h to the fourth*zeta_T is complex. Moreover
\[
(h^7)_T=\frac{7c_1}{11}h^{-4}.
\tag{23}
\]
Exact subtraction and the t-constant field C therefore give
the complete necessary formulas
\[
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{I}:\quad \frac{m_0}{h}
 &=C_0+C_1h^7-u_0T-\frac{2u_1}{15}T^2
                         -\frac{2u_2}{39}T^3,\\
\mathrm{II}:\quad \frac{m_0}{h}
 &=C_0+C_1h^7-u_0T-\frac{2u_1}{15}T^2
                         -\frac{16}{57}TV,\\
&\hspace{10mm}C_0,C_1\in\mathbb C.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{24}
\]
The constants C0,C1 may vanish. The homogeneous h7 term has not
been lost by restricting the original field to the traced field.
Indeed C(T,h7)=C(h) has degree eleven over C(T): h11=c1T,
and h=(h7)^8/(c1T)^5. Thus h7 cannot belong to K of degree at
most two. The traced solution has no nonconstant homogeneous
term of that form, but the original solution may have it.

In II the required V already lies in F0 by (16). Formula (24)
locates mu in C(h,V), and in I it locates mu in C(h), without
claiming equality of either subfield with F0. No new root,
ambient coordinate transformation, or descent of rho is used.
