# Polynomial return and coefficient-field speeds in the nonzero quadratic-cubic gate

Working consequence of376: adjoining the exact one-variable coefficient
field to the actual rings gives polynomial rings, and both intrinsic
speeds are nonzero elements of that field. In particular the full
calibrated determinant is independent of s as well as r and X.

This is not an exclusion or original-ring recognition. A field unit
need not be an original-ring unit, and a polynomial generator after
adjoining a field need not be an original coordinate. The proof uses
the original global slice, not a presumed slice of a restriction.

## 1. Retained actual hypotheses and the polynomial inclusion

Retain the complete actual entry and all conclusions of
[376](principal-paraboloid-quadratic-cubic-nonzero-leading-normal-form-pass376-working.md),
with its actual264 chart and365/369/372 reductions. In particular,
\[
\begin{gathered}
\mathcal B=\mathbb C[X,Y,Z,W]=A_0[u],\qquad E=\partial_u,
\qquad K=\operatorname{Frac}A_0,\\
\deg_E(X,Y,Z,W)=(1,2,2,2),\qquad
I_{\rm initial}=(X^2Z-Y^2)\text{ is the full initial kernel},\\
a_0=EX\ne0,\qquad Ea_0=0,\qquad
N=[u^4]\partial_Z|_{A_0}\in\operatorname{LND}(A_0)\setminus\{0\},\\
Na_0=Nt=Ns=0,\qquad
L=\operatorname{Frac}\ker_{A_0}N=F(s),\qquad
F=\ker_L\partial_s,\qquad [F:\mathbb C(t)]<\infty,\\
K=L(r)=F(s,r)=\mathbb C(y,z,w),\qquad
O=L[r]=L[y,\sigma,\theta]=L[y,z,w],\qquad
Nr=\rho\in L^*.
\end{gathered}
\tag{1}
\]
The selected partial in (1) is the literal actual Z-partial.
All displayed intercepts and coefficient-field elements belong to K
and are killed by E. The variables s,r are algebraically independent
over F. No equality F=C(t) is assumed.

The exact coefficient partials on L commute. The t-partial preserves
F because, for f in F, partial_s(partial_t f)=partial_t(partial_s f)=0.
The formulas from376 imply
\[
\begin{gathered}
n=-\frac{s^2}{4t^2}+f,\qquad f\in F,\qquad
\ell\in F[s],\qquad \sigma\in F[s,r],\\
C=\ell_s+\frac{s^2}{4t^4}\in F[s],\qquad
g=\frac{f''}{2}\in F^*,\qquad
\Lambda=\frac{2sC}{t}+\ell_t\in F[s],\qquad
e=\sigma|_{r=0}\in F[s],\\
\theta=\frac tr\left\{
g(\sigma^2-e^2)-\Lambda(\sigma-e)
+\frac{2sr\sigma}{t^2}+r^2-2tCr\right\},\\
y=r-\frac{\theta}{2t}+\frac{s\sigma}{2t^2}
-\frac{n_t}{2}-tC,\\
z=2ty+\sigma^2+\theta+n,\qquad
w=\ell-2\sigma y-n_t\sigma-n_s\theta .
\end{gathered}
\tag{2}
\]
The expression for theta is a polynomial in F[s,r]: sigma-e is
divisible by r there, so every term in its braces is divisible by r.
This is not a restriction to the open set r nonzero. Consequently
\[
y,\sigma,\theta,z,w\in F[s,r].
\tag{3}
\]

The literal actual map and its full calibration are
\[
\begin{aligned}
Y&=y+\sigma X+tX^2,\\
Z&=z+(2t\sigma+s)X+t^2X^2,\\
W&=w+\theta X+sX^2,\\
J(X)&:=\det\frac{\partial(Y,Z,W)}{\partial(t,s,r)}
       =-\frac{a_0^4}{\rho}\in L^*.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]
Every derivative in this determinant holds the formal actual X fixed;
the t,s derivatives also hold the independent intrinsic r fixed.

Define subrings of the original fraction field by
\[
\mathscr R=F[\mathcal B],\qquad
\mathscr S=F[X,s,r].
\tag{5}
\]
Here adjoining F means the subring generated by F and the indicated
actual ring, not a tensor product asserted to be a domain.
Equations (3),(4) give the initial inclusion
\[
\mathscr R\subseteq\mathscr S.
\tag{6}
\]
Moreover X,s,r are algebraically independent over F. Indeed
X-a0*u belongs to A0 and a0 is nonzero, so X is transcendental
over K because u is. Together with (1), this proves that S is
the stated polynomial ring. Its normality is not used to infer
any equality in (6).

## 2. The original slice forces a0 to be a field unit

The actual condition Na0=0 gives a0 in L=F(s), while a0 belongs
to B and hence to S. Polynomial independence gives
\[
F[X,s,r]\cap F(s)=F[s].
\tag{7}
\]
For example, regard an element of the left ring as a polynomial
in X,r over F[s], embed into F(s)[X,r], and compare its nonconstant
X,r coefficients. Therefore a0 belongs to F[s].

The extension of the original derivation to the fraction field kills
F,s,r and sends X to a0. It consequently preserves S and is exactly
\[
E=a_0(s)\partial_X\quad\text{on }\mathscr S.
\tag{8}
\]
This explicit extension requires no theorem about derivations on a
normalization. The original slice u belongs to B, hence to S by (6).
Inside this polynomial ring its actual defining identity becomes
\[
1=Eu=a_0(s)\,\partial_Xu.
\tag{9}
\]
Both factors on the right belong to S. Its only units are F*, so
\[
\boxed{a_0\in F^*.}
\tag{10}
\]
In particular a0_s=0. Nothing in (9) asserts that a0 is a complex
constant or a unit of the original ring A0.

## 3. Actual derivatives recover s and r polynomially

The ring R is E-stable: E preserves B and kills its adjoined field F.
The literal identities (4), with Ea0=0, give
\[
s=\frac{E^2W}{2a_0^2},\qquad
\sigma=\frac{EY}{a_0}-2tX,\qquad
\theta=\frac{EW}{a_0}-2sX.
\tag{11}
\]
All three right sides belong to R by (10), since a0 inverse and t
belong to F. It follows also that
\[
y=Y-\sigma X-tX^2\in\mathscr R.
\tag{12}
\]
Now use the literal defining remainder of372, already retained in
(2). Its evaluation is
\[
r=y+\frac{\theta}{2t}+\frac{n_t}{2}
  -\frac{s\sigma}{2t^2}+tC.
\tag{13}
\]
The coefficients n_t and C belong to F[s], which is now a subring
of R by the first identity of (11). Hence r belongs to R. Since
X already belongs to R, (6) is an equality:
\[
\boxed{F[\mathcal B]=F[X,s,r].}
\tag{14}
\]
The proof of (14) is a reverse polynomial inclusion; strong field
return alone would not have supplied it.

Adjoin F contained in K to the original decomposition B=A0[u].
It gives R=F[A0][u], and u remains transcendental over K. Thus
the E-kernel of R is F[A0]. On the other hand, (8),(10),(14)
identify that kernel as F[s,r]. Therefore, as subrings of K,
\[
\boxed{F[A_0]=F[s,r].}
\tag{15}
\]
No slice of N or of a derivation on an original three-coordinate
subring has been assumed.

## 4. The selected speed and full scalar have no s dependence

The actual derivation N preserves A0 and kills F, since F is contained
in L=Frac(ker N). It therefore preserves F[A0]=F[s,r]. Applying it
to r gives rho in F[s,r]. The already retained affine-line speed
rho=Nr belongs to L=F(s), so another polynomial intersection gives
\[
\rho\in F[s,r]\cap F(s)=F[s].
\tag{16}
\]
This step uses the actual preservation of the now identified ring,
not just the fact that rho is a rational coefficient-field element.

Every row of (4) is polynomial in s,r,X over F. The fixed-variable
partials partial_t, partial_s and partial_r preserve this polynomial
ring: partial_t preserves F and fixes s,r,X, and the other two are
the indicated polynomial partials. Consequently
\[
J(X)\in F[s,r,X].
\tag{17}
\]
The exact original calibration (4) is an identity in its fraction
field. By (10),(16),(17) it is in fact the polynomial identity
\[
\rho\,J(X)=-a_0^4\in F^*.
\tag{18}
\]
Both factors on the left must be units of F[s,r,X]. Thus
\[
\boxed{\rho\in F^*,\qquad J(X)\in F^*.}
\tag{19}
\]
In particular rho_s=J_s=0, in addition to the previously retained
r and X independence of J. Its t derivative is not asserted zero.
The selected derivation on the adjoined invariant ring is exactly
\[
N=\rho\partial_r\quad\text{on }F[A_0]=F[s,r].
\tag{20}
\]
This is not a claim that r or r/rho is a slice in the original A0.

One useful consequence of (14) is the all-X polynomial-plane return
\[
F[Y|_{X=x_0},Z|_{X=x_0},W|_{X=x_0}]=F[s,r]
\qquad(x_0\in F).
\tag{21}
\]
Indeed (14) reads F[X,Y,Z,W]=F[X,s,r], and quotienting by X-x0
gives (21). The equality also persists after any coefficient-field
extension, with x0 in that larger field. Such a specialization is
an operation on the coefficient-adjoined rings, not a complex
ambient coordinate change.

## 5. Scope and verification boundary

The new consequences of376 are (14),(15),(19),(20),(21). They
strengthen its field return and its originally L-valued speeds to
polynomial return and F-valued speeds. They give further necessary
scalar equations, but do not decide whether the remaining literal
potential family exists.
