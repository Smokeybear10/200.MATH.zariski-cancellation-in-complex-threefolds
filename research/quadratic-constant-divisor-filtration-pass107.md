# Exact degree filtration at a quadratic constant divisor

At an actual invariant divisor whose constant field has degree two,
the surviving degree-two good generator gives an exact conic over
the base DVR. If the first derivative image is generated by p^e,
the j-th image is generated by p^(e ceil(j/2)). All degree modules
have explicit alternating polynomial bases. The construction takes
place in the actual localized algebra, with no completion or
unproved kernel-and-reduction interchange.

## 1. The precise local algebra and its actual source

Let p be an irreducible element of the actual invariant plane
S=C[s_1,s_2]. Put V=S_(p), let K=Frac(V), and write k=V/pV.
For the localized actual algebra B=A_(S minus (p)), the inputs are
\[
\begin{gathered}
B\text{ is a domain},\qquad N\in\operatorname{LND}_V(B),
\quad\ker(N|_B)=V,\quad B_K=K[v],\\
B/pB=k'[w],\qquad [k':k]=2,\qquad\bar N\ne0,\\
\text{there is }U\in B\text{ with }N^3U=0
\text{ and }\bar U\notin k',\\
F_j=\ker(N^{j+1}|_B),\qquad I_j=N^j(F_j)\subseteq V,
\qquad I_1=(p^e).
\end{gathered}
\tag{1}
\]
Both fields have characteristic zero. The notation B here denotes
the base localization of A, not the original polynomial cylinder.

The full generic-divisor and nonzero-derivation statements are
the exact hypotheses of
[divisor91](nonconical-residue-degree-bound-pass91.md).
In the supplied nonconical quadratic frame, its whole-divisor
noncollapse theorem ensures that one of the two actual good
generators has nonconstant residue. Both good generators have
N-degree at most two, so that generator supplies U in (1).
The existence of U is not inferred merely from B/pB being a
polynomial line. No first-normal-action assumption at a closed
fiber is used.

The remaining proof is a local algebra theorem under (1).
The actual smoothness, factorial closure and faithful flatness
have already supplied these inputs; no additional classification
theorem or geometric-integrality assumption enters below.

## 2. A minimal local slice generates the residue constants

The ideal I_1 is nonzero: for an element of positive N-degree,
its penultimate nonzero iterate is a local slice. Since V is a
DVR, choose r in F_1 with Nr=p^e, absorbing an invariant unit.
For any H in F_1, its derivative is a multiple of p^e; subtracting
that multiple of r leaves an invariant. Therefore
\[
\begin{gathered}
F_1=V\oplus Vr,\qquad Nr=p^e,\qquad e\ge1,\\
\bar r\in k'\setminus k,\qquad k'=k[\bar r].
\end{gathered}
\tag{2}
\]
Here e cannot be zero. A slice would give B=V[r] by the finite
Taylor slice formula. Its residue polynomial line k[r] has no
nontrivial algebraic constants over k, contrary to (1).

Because e is positive, bar r is killed by bar N. The latter is
a nonzero translation on k'[w], with kernel k'. If bar r belonged
to k, lift it to a in V. The actual quotient by pB would give
(r-a)/p in B with derivative p^(e-1), contradicting the defining
valuation of I_1. Hence bar r does not belong to k. Degree two
then gives k'=k[bar r]. This is a finite subtraction-and-division
argument, not lifting an infinite p-adic expansion.

## 3. The surviving quadratic element forces a unit coefficient

Since NU belongs to F_1, write NU=alpha r+beta with alpha,beta
in V. The element
gamma=alpha r squared+2beta r-2p^e U is killed by N and therefore
lies in V. Reducing this identity modulo p gives
alpha_bar r_bar squared+2beta_bar r_bar-gamma_bar=0.
If alpha belonged to pV, independence of 1,bar r over k would
force beta into pV too. That would give bar N(bar U)=0,
contradicting the nonconstant polynomial bar U in (1).
Consequently alpha is a unit, and the following are actual elements
and a polynomial over V:
\[
\begin{gathered}
NU=\alpha r+\beta,\qquad
\gamma=\alpha r^2+2\beta r-2p^eU\in V,
\qquad\alpha\in V^*,\\
t=2U/\alpha\in B,\qquad
P_2(X)=X^2+(2\beta/\alpha)X-\gamma/\alpha\in V[X],\\
\boxed{\quad p^e t=P_2(r),\qquad Nt=P_2'(r).\quad}
\end{gathered}
\tag{3}
\]
The monic reduction of P_2 annihilates bar r. It is its
irreducible degree-two minimal polynomial over k. Characteristic
zero makes its derivative at bar r nonzero. Since bar N is a
translation, the identity bar N(bar t)=P_2'(bar r) in k'^*
also shows that bar t is an affine coordinate of k'[w]. Thus
the transverse polynomial parameter is obtained without guessing
a formal lift of w.

## 4. Finite degree modules and all alternating bases

For j at least one the sequences
0 to F_(j-1) to F_j to I_j to 0, with the last map N^j,
prove by induction that F_j is finite over V. Each I_j is an
ideal of the Noetherian ring V. Each F_j is torsion free as a
submodule of the domain B, so is free over the DVR. Localizing
at V minus zero commutes with these invariant kernels; the
generic polynomial line gives rank j+1.

Now r has N-degree one and t has N-degree two by (2)--(3).
For every n at least zero consider
\[
\mathcal B_n=
\{t^j:0\le2j\le n\}\ \cup
\{r t^j:0\le2j+1\le n\},\qquad
\boxed{\quad F_n=\bigoplus_{b\in\mathcal B_n}Vb.\quad}
\tag{4}
\]
There are exactly n+1 listed elements. Their residues are
k-linearly independent: a relation is
P(bar t)+bar r Q(bar t)=0 with P,Q in k[T]. Since bar t is
transcendental over k', every coefficient of P+bar r Q is zero;
independence of 1,bar r over k then makes P and Q zero.

These independent residues imply independent classes in
F_n/pF_n. That module fiber has dimension n+1, so they form
a basis there. Nakayama lifts them to a basis of F_n itself.
This proves each specific fiber injection after proving the
basis. It does not assume that degree kernels commute with
reduction modulo p in general.

Every element of B lies in some F_n because N is locally
nilpotent. Taking the union of (4) therefore gives B=V[r,t].
The exact presentation is
\[
\boxed{\quad
B\simeq V[R_0,T_0]/\bigl(p^eT_0-P_2(R_0)\bigr),
\qquad R_0\mapsto r,\quad T_0\mapsto t.
\quad}
\tag{5}
\]
Surjectivity was just proved by the all-degree bases. The source
is free over V[T_0] with basis 1,R_0 because P_2 is monic
quadratic, and hence has no p-torsion. After inverting p, its
map to B_K=K[r] is an isomorphism. Its original kernel is
therefore zero, proving (5) in the actual ring.

## 5. Exact derivative-image valuations

In K[r], the derivation is p^e partial_r, and t=P_2(r)/p^e.
The basis (4) has exactly one element of degree n, namely
t^(n/2) for even n or r t^((n-1)/2) for odd n. Its leading
r-coefficient is p^(-e floor(n/2)). Every other basis element
has smaller degree and is killed by N^n. It follows that
\[
\begin{gathered}
\boxed{\quad I_n=n!\,p^{e\lceil n/2\rceil}V
       =p^{e\lceil n/2\rceil}V\quad(n\ge1).\quad}\\
I_1=I_2=p^eV,\qquad I_3=I_4=p^{2e}V.
\end{gathered}
\tag{6}
\]
All factorials are units, since the residue characteristic is
zero. The equality identifies ideals, not merely an upper
valuation bound or the derivative of one selected element.

In particular the specified actual c in F_4 has a unique
expansion in 1,r,t,rt,t squared with coefficients in V.
The proof does not assert that its fourth derivative generates
I_4 at every point of the original invariant plane. Nor does
it transfer the DVR bases to a common polynomial basis over
a two-dimensional closed-point local ring.

## Scope and provenance

The hypotheses include one surviving element of degree at most
two. For the actual quadratic frame this comes from the two
good generators and the retained whole-divisor noncollapse
theorem, not from a generic coefficient ansatz. The general
lemma is not asserted for arbitrary affine-line degenerations
without that input or for nonreduced generic divisor fibers.

The current actual admissible-boundary106 and divisor91 proofs
were fully read before this deduction. The finite degree-module
argument is the DVR form of the retained module102 argument;
its proof is included. Geometry independently reconstructed an
equivalent unit-coefficient argument using the F_2 basis, while
Root proposed the valuation target. No completion, external
classification, numerical method, or source-novelty assertion
is used. All files through106 remain unchanged.
