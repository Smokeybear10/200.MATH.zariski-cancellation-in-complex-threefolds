# Actual boundaries for weighted Euler spectral components and Lie closure

## 1. Bounded conclusions

For an actual positive weighted Euler derivation \(D\), the adjoint
operator \(\operatorname{ad}_D\) is locally finite on polynomial
derivations. This gives finite spectral projections of each individual
polynomial field. It does not imply that the Lie algebra generated by
\(D\) and a sliced locally nilpotent derivation \(E\) is finite
dimensional.

The explicit translation frame below proves three boundaries within
the full actual setup of sources 576 and 577:

- \(\operatorname{Lie}_{\mathbf C}(E,D)\) is infinite dimensional,
  although both \(E\) and \(D\) are locally finite individually.
- A spectral component of the actual recovered Euler-induced LND
  \(N\) is not locally nilpotent, and is not even locally finite.
- Another spectral component of that same \(N\) neither preserves
  \(A=\ker E\) nor commutes with \(E\), and does not kill the slice.

All fields come from a full polynomial coordinate frame with a complete
inverse. No leading vector field is assigned independently of that
frame. Its invariant ring is already a known polynomial ring, and the
frame has explicit lower-degree alternatives. These conclusions do
not prove failure of general coordinate descent, a plateau strategy,
or an entry theorem in a globally minimizing frame. No novelty is
claimed, and no external Lie classification theorem is used.

## 2. The safe finite spectral statement

Let \(B=\mathbf C[X_1,\ldots,X_n]\) and let
\[
 D=\sum_i d_iX_i\partial_{X_i},\qquad d_i\in\mathbf Z_{>0}.
\tag{1}
\]
For a monomial polynomial derivation the exact identity is
\[
 [D,X^\alpha\partial_{X_i}]
    =\left(\sum_j d_j\alpha_j-d_i\right)
                     X^\alpha\partial_{X_i}.
\tag{2}
\]
Every polynomial derivation is a finite sum of these eigenvectors.
Thus \(\operatorname{ad}_D\) is locally finite and diagonalizable on
\(\operatorname{Der}_{\mathbf C}(B)\), in the sense of a direct sum
of eigenspaces. For any fixed polynomial field \(P\), its finitely
many spectral parts are obtained by interpolation polynomials in
\(\operatorname{ad}_D\) applied to \(P\). Each therefore belongs to
\(\operatorname{Lie}_{\mathbf C}(D,P)\).

Bracketing two such parts can create new weights: if their weights are
\(r,s\), their bracket, when nonzero, has weight \(r+s\). Formula
(2) bounds the support of one field's adjoint orbit, not the support
of all iterated brackets of several fields.

There are also exact tests for the properties that spectral projection
does not automatically preserve. If \([E,P]=0\), the Jacobi identity
gives
\[
 [E,[D,P]]=[[E,D],P].
\tag{3}
\]
If in addition \(P(u)=0\), then
\[
 [D,P](u)=-P(Du).
\tag{4}
\]
The centralizer of \(E\) is closed under its own Lie brackets, as is
the set of fields killing \(u\). Neither need be stable under
\(\operatorname{ad}_D\): the right sides of (3),(4) need not vanish.
For example \([E,D]\in\mathbf C E\) is a sufficient condition for
adjoint stability of the centralizer, but is not asserted in the
positive-shift situation.

A separate bookkeeping boundary is important. The recovery formula in 577 gives
\(N\), extended to kill the slice, by subtracting a polynomial
multiple of \(E\) from a terminal commutator. A complex Lie algebra
is not automatically closed under polynomial multiplication. Thus
membership of the corrected \(N\) in
\(\operatorname{Lie}_{\mathbf C}(E,D)\) must be justified separately.
In the control below the terminal vertical coefficient is zero, so
that membership does hold.

## 3. A complete four-shear translation frame

Start with
\[
 B=\mathbf C[a,b,c,u],\qquad E=\partial_u,
 \qquad A=\mathbf C[a,b,c].
\tag{5}
\]
The letters \(t,v\) in this note denote explicit polynomials, not
time variables or generators of an extended coefficient field. Put
\[
 t=a+u^2,\qquad v=u+t^2,
 \qquad X=t+v^2,\qquad Y=v+X^2,
 \qquad Z=b+u,\qquad W=c+u.
\tag{6}
\]
The four final polynomials \((X,Y,Z,W)\) are actual coordinates.
Their full inverse is the following successive polynomial substitution:
\[
 v=Y-X^2,\qquad t=X-v^2,\qquad u=v-t^2,
 \qquad a=t-u^2,\qquad b=Z-u,\qquad c=W-u.
\tag{7}
\]
In one order (7) recovers \(v,t,u,a,b,c\) from (6). In the
other order, the definitions of \(t,v,X,Y,Z,W\) recover all four
initial coordinates. Thus both inverse compositions are identities
on the whole affine fourfold. The Jacobian of (6) in the old ordered
variables \((a,b,c,u)\) is one. These statements require no
localization.

The exact \(E\)-degrees are
\[
 \deg_E(t,v,X,Y,Z,W)=(2,4,8,16,1,1),
\tag{8}
\]
all with leading coefficient one. The weighted Euler field for the
actual frame is therefore
\[
 D=8X\partial_X+16Y\partial_Y+Z\partial_Z+W\partial_W.
\tag{9}
\]
The auxiliary tuple \((X,v,Z,W)\) is also a polynomial coordinate
tuple, by the shear \(v=Y-X^2\). For computations with this same
fixed \(D\), it satisfies
\[
 D(X)=8X,\qquad D(v)=16v,\qquad D(Z)=Z,\qquad D(W)=W.
\tag{10}
\]
The weight 16 of \(v\) in (10) is its eigenvalue for the fixed field
\(D\), not its actual \(E\)-degree, which is four. We do not replace
\(D\) by the different Euler field of the auxiliary degree tuple.

## 4. Entire Euler pullback and the actual recovered N

Differentiating all of (7), using (10), gives
\[
 \begin{split}
 D(t)&=8t-24v^2,\\
 D(u)&=16u+48tv^2,\\
 D(a)&=8a-24u^2-24v^2-96utv^2,\\
 D(b)&=b-15u-48tv^2,\\
 D(c)&=c-15u-48tv^2.
 \end{split}
\tag{11}
\]
For instance \(D(u)=16v-2tD(t)\), and \(v=u+t^2\) makes this
the displayed second equation. Then \(D(a)=D(t)-2uD(u)\);
the last two equations follow from \(b=Z-u,c=W-u\).

The complete degree/leader pairs of \(D(a),D(b),D(c),D(u)\) are
\[
 (11,-96),\qquad(10,-48),\qquad(10,-48),\qquad(10,48).
\tag{12}
\]
Since \(A\) is generated by \(a,b,c\), the product rule in
\(A[u]\) proves the exact all-ring shift
\[
 \delta=\max\{11,10,10,10-1\}=11.
\tag{13}
\]
Equality is attained on \(a\). The highest horizontal coefficient
is therefore
\[
 N=-96\partial_a,\qquad N(u)=0,
 \qquad \overline D=\eta^{11}N.
\tag{14}
\]
This is the actual recovered Euler-induced LND. It is nonzero,
primitive and sliced on the known polynomial ring \(A\).
The complete commutator computation also follows from (11):
\[
 (\operatorname{ad}_E)^{11}D=11!N,
 \qquad (\operatorname{ad}_E)^{12}D=0.
\tag{15}
\]
Here the terminal vertical term is zero because \(D(u)\) has
degree ten. In this example \(N\) itself is thus in the complex
Lie algebra generated by \(E,D\), without polynomial multiplication.

## 5. Exact spectral components that lose local nilpotence or A

In the actual coordinates \((X,v,Z,W)\), define the plane Hamiltonian
field, extended to kill \(Z,W\), by
\[
 H_f=f_X\partial_v-f_v\partial_X.
\tag{16}
\]
The map \((a,u)\mapsto(X,v)\) in (6) has Jacobian one. Consequently
\(\partial_a=-H_u\) on the plane. Since \(N(Z)=N(W)=0\), equation
(14) gives the entire identity
\[
 N=96H_u,\qquad
 u=(v-X^2)+2Xv^2-v^4.
\tag{17}
\]
The three summands of \(u\) have respective \(D\)-weights
16, 40, and 64 for \(\mathrm{wt}(X)=8,\mathrm{wt}(v)=16\).
For a polynomial of weight \(w\), its Hamiltonian field has
adjoint weight \(w-24\). Thus the full spectral decomposition
of the recovered \(N\) has weights \(-8,16,40\).

Its weight-16 component is
\[
 N_{16}=96H_{2Xv^2}
       =-384Xv\partial_X+192v^2\partial_v.
\tag{18}
\]
This field is not locally nilpotent. It takes \(v\) to the nonzero
multiple \(192v^2\) of itself, which is incompatible with the degree
function of an LND on a characteristic-zero domain. More directly,
\[
 N_{16}^{\,j}(v)=192^j j!\,v^{j+1}\quad(j\ge0).
\tag{19}
\]
These polynomials are linearly independent, so \(N_{16}\) is not
even locally finite. It is nevertheless an exact spectral projection
of the actual LND \(N\), not a formal proposed component.

The weight-40 component is
\[
 N_{40}=384v^3\partial_X.
\tag{20}
\]
Using the entire inverse \(t=X-v^2,u=v-t^2,a=t-u^2\), it satisfies
\[
 N_{40}(a)=384v^3(1+4ut),\qquad
 N_{40}(u)=-768tv^3.
\tag{21}
\]
As polynomials in the original \(u\), with coefficients in \(A\),
these have degree/leader pairs \((15,1536)\) and \((14,-768)\).
Therefore \(N_{40}(a)\notin A\), and \(N_{40}\) does not kill the
slice. Moreover \([E,N_{40}](u)=E(N_{40}(u))\) has degree thirteen and leader
\(-10752\), so it is nonzero. Hence this spectral component also
fails to commute with \(E\).

## 6. Infinite Lie closure from two exact spectral parts of E

In the same auxiliary actual coordinates,
\[
 E=H_a+\partial_Z+\partial_W.
\tag{22}
\]
The plane part is \(H_a\) because \(E=\partial_u\); the last
two coefficients are one because \(Z=b+u,W=c+u\).
The entire inverse expression for \(a\), grouped into homogeneous
pieces of weights \(8,32,56,80,104,128\), is
\[
 \begin{split}
 a={}&X+(-2v^2+2X^2v-X^4)\\
     &+(-4Xv^3+4X^3v^2)\\
     &+(2v^5-6X^2v^4)+4Xv^6-v^8.
 \end{split}
\tag{23}
\]
All the indicated weights are distinct. The two vertical fields
in (22) have weight \(-1\), different from the Hamiltonian weights
obtained by subtracting 24 from those in (23).

Finite spectral projections under \(\operatorname{ad}_D\), as proved
in Section 2, therefore put both entire polynomial fields
\[
 R=H_{4Xv^6}=-24Xv^5\partial_X+4v^6\partial_v,
 \qquad P=H_{-v^8}=8v^7\partial_X
\tag{24}
\]
in \(\operatorname{Lie}_{\mathbf C}(E,D)\). For every positive
integer \(k\), direct differentiation gives
\[
 [R,v^k\partial_X]=(4k+24)v^{k+5}\partial_X.
\tag{25}
\]
Starting with \(P\), repeated bracketing with \(R\) produces
nonzero complex multiples of \(v^{7+5j}\partial_X\) for every
\(j\ge0\). Their polynomial coefficients are linearly independent
over \(\mathbf C\). Hence the full Lie algebra is infinite
dimensional, despite finite \(D\)-weight support for each of its
individual elements. No classification theorem is needed for this
conclusion.

This also exhibits a non-locally-finite Lie element directly:
\(R(v)=4v^6\), and its successive iterates on \(v\) have strictly
increasing degrees. Thus no inference that every member of the
Lie algebra is locally finite has been used merely because its
two original generators are locally finite.

## 7. What the control does and does not license

Finite \(D\)-spectral projection is a valid polynomial operation
on derivations. It is not automatically an operation within
\(\operatorname{LND}(A)\), within the centralizer of \(E\), or
within the fields killing the given slice. Equations (18)--(21)
separate those failures explicitly for the recovered field, while
(24)--(25) separate finite adjoint support from finite Lie closure.

The original kernel remains the visible polynomial ring
\(A=\mathbf C[a,b,c]\), and the inverse (7) is completely
polynomial. In particular the all-positive frame
\((u,a+u,b+u,c+u)\) has degree tuple \((1,1,1,1)\), lower than
the displayed \((8,16,1,1)\). These are actual polynomial
coordinate changes. The control is not globally minimal.

Nothing here proves that successful LND directions cannot be
found in a Lie closure, that a plateau strategy cannot escape,
or that an arbitrary sliced invariant ring fails to be polynomial.
It rules out the specific automatic closure claims stated in
Section 1. Any use of a spectral or Lie-generated candidate for
coordinate descent still needs its own polynomial LND, kernel,
slice, and degree checks.

## 8. Verification and status

Fresh unsaved algebra checked both complete inverse compositions,
the full Jacobian, all degree/leader data, the entire Euler
eigen-equations, the recovery of \(N\), the full homogeneous
expansions (17),(23), the non-LND spectral component, the genuine
old-\(u\) degrees in (21), and the symbolic all-\(k\) bracket
(25). The general finite-support statement and its limitations
are proved above, not inferred from a finite sample of brackets.

Sources 576, 577, and 578 were read completely. No saved checker was
executed, imported, or compiled, and no canonical source was
edited. This author-held note requires independent full saved
