# The finite second-image obstruction is a section zero scheme

The second degree module is a rank-three vector bundle on the
invariant plane. Its constants split off, and the actual minimal
slice defines a section of the resulting rank-two bundle. The
section's zero ideal is exactly the finite obstruction ideal
\(J\), including its scheme multiplicities. Thus any remaining
obstruction is locally a complete intersection.

A local basis also gives an explicit conic subalgebra of the
actual ring. Equality in every height-one base localization does
not by itself prove that this subalgebra is the whole ring.
That distinction is retained below.

## 1. Actual setting

Use the hypotheses and notation of
[the closed-fiber lemma](second-image-closed-fiber-pass47.md):
\[
C=\ker D\simeq\mathbb C^{[2]},\quad Dr=p,\quad
\operatorname{pl}(D)=pC,\quad
F_i=\ker D^{i+1},\quad I_2=pJ,
\tag{1}
\]
\[
F_1=C\oplus Cr,\qquad p\in J,\qquad
J_{(\pi)}=C_{(\pi)}.
\tag{2}
\]
The actual inclusion \(C\subset A\) is faithfully flat and
factorially closed, and \(A\) is a UFD with
\(A_L=L[r]\), \(L=\operatorname{Frac}C\). The completed
pass-46 local models apply at every base divisor. No polynomial
presentation of \(A\) is assumed.

## 2. Finiteness and reflexivity of the second degree module

Take \(z\in F_2\). On the generic line write
\[
z=a_2r^2+a_1r+a_0,\qquad a_i\in L.
\]
Since \(Dz\in F_1\), the identity
\[
Dz=2pa_2r+pa_1
\]
and (2) give \(pa_2,pa_1\in C\). Consequently
\(pa_0=pz-(pa_2)r^2-(pa_1)r\) belongs to
\(A\cap L=C\). Therefore
\[
F_2\subset p^{-1}(C+Cr+Cr^2).
\tag{3}
\]
It is a submodule of a finite module over the Noetherian ring
\(C\), so it is finite. It is torsion free and has rank three,
because its localization is \(L+Lr+Lr^2\).

Invariant localization commutes with \(\ker D^3\). The
already proved embedded intersection for \(A\) therefore gives
\[
F_2=\bigcap_{\operatorname{ht}\mathfrak p=1}(F_2)_{\mathfrak p}
\quad\text{inside }L+Lr+Lr^2.
\tag{4}
\]
Indeed membership in the right side puts the element in every
height-one base localization of \(A\), hence in \(A\), and
its generic degree bound puts it in \(\ker D^3\).

The standard height-one intersection characterization for a finite
module over a Noetherian normal domain now makes \(F_2\)
reflexive. On the regular surface \(\operatorname{Spec}C\),
a finite reflexive module is locally free. These precise statements
and their full proofs were directly read in Stacks tags
[0AVB](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0AVB) and
[0B3N](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0B3N).
Thus \(F_2\) is a rank-three vector bundle. No theorem about
principality of higher LND image ideals is used in this step.

## 3. Splitting constants and identifying the section ideal

At every closed point \(\mathfrak m\) of \(\operatorname{Spec}C\),
the vector \(1\in(F_2)_{\mathfrak m}\) is unimodular.
Otherwise \(1\in\mathfrak m(F_2)_{\mathfrak m}\subset
\mathfrak mA_{\mathfrak m}\), contradicting faithful flatness.
In a local basis of the free module this means that at least
one coefficient of \(1\) is a unit. Hence constants form a
line subbundle, and
\[
P=F_2/C
\tag{5}
\]
is a rank-two vector bundle. The section \(\sigma=[r]\in P\)
is nowhere zero generically. There is an exact sequence
\[
0\longrightarrow C\xrightarrow{\ 1\mapsto\sigma\ }P
\xrightarrow{\ D^2\ }pJ\longrightarrow0.
\tag{6}
\]
The last map is induced by \(D^2\); its kernel is
\(F_1/C=Cr\).

Work in a local ring \(R=C_{\mathfrak m}\), choose a basis
of \(P_R\), and write \(\sigma=(a,b)\in R^2\).
The quotient in (6) is torsion free. It follows that
\(\gcd_R(a,b)=1\): a nonunit common divisor \(d\)
would make the class of \((a/d,b/d)\) a nonzero torsion
element modulo \(R(a,b)\).

The wedge map
\[
R^2\longrightarrow R,\qquad (x,y)\longmapsto ay-bx
\tag{7}
\]
has kernel \(R(a,b)\) by coprimality in the UFD \(R\),
and image \((a,b)R\). Thus (6) identifies \(pJR\)
with the ideal \((a,b)R\), as rank-one modules. Every such
isomorphism is multiplication by a nonzero member of
\(\operatorname{Frac}R\), after passing to that field.
Both ideals have known valuations: \(JR\) and \((a,b)R\)
become the unit ideal at every height-one prime. The multiplier
divided by \(p\) consequently has zero valuation at every
prime of the UFD \(R\), and is a unit. Therefore the equality
of ideals, not just of their radicals, is
\[
JR=(a,b)R.
\tag{8}
\]

The ideal of the zero scheme of a vector-bundle section is
locally generated by its two components. Equation (8) identifies
that zero scheme with \(V(J)\), retaining all multiplicities.
If \(JR\ne R\), it has height two. The two coprime
generators form a regular sequence: \(a\ne0\), and
coprimality makes multiplication by \(b\) injective modulo
\(a\). Thus the obstruction scheme is locally a zero-dimensional
complete intersection. It is not thereby empty or reduced.

This also records precisely what bundle freeness would fail to
prove: a section of a free rank-two bundle can still have a
nonempty finite zero scheme.

## 4. The resulting local conic subalgebra

Keep the local ring \(R\). Lift the chosen basis of \(P_R\)
to \(z,w\in(F_2)_R\); then
\((F_2)_R=R\oplus Rz\oplus Rw\). After subtracting an
invariant constant from \(r\), write
\[
r=az+bw.
\tag{9}
\]
This shift preserves \(Dr=p\) and the section \(\sigma\).
Because \(Dz,Dw\in F_1\), there exist
\(A_1,B_1,A_2,B_2\in R\) with
\[
Dz=A_1r+B_1,\qquad Dw=A_2r+B_2.
\]
Differentiating (9) and comparing the independent vectors
\(1,r\) gives \(aA_1+bA_2=0\) and
\(aB_1+bB_2=p\). Coprimality therefore yields
\[
A_1=\epsilon b,\qquad A_2=-\epsilon a
\tag{10}
\]
for some \(\epsilon\in R\). Moreover
\[
pJR=D^2((F_2)_R)=p\epsilon(a,b)R.
\]
By (8), \(JR=\epsilon JR\). Nakayama's lemma for the
nonzero finite module \(JR\) makes \(\epsilon\) a unit.
Thus the exact local formulas are
\[
Dz=\epsilon br+B_1,\quad
Dw=-\epsilon ar+B_2,
\quad p=aB_1+bB_2,\quad \epsilon\in R^*.
\tag{11}
\]

Direct differentiation shows that
\[
Q=\epsilon r^2-2B_2z+2B_1w\in R.
\tag{12}
\]
Indeed its derivative is
\(2\epsilon r(p-bB_2-aB_1)=0\).
The subalgebra \(H=R[z,w]\subset A_R\) consequently has
the exact presentation
\[
H\simeq R[Z,W]/
\bigl(\epsilon(aZ+bW)^2-2B_2Z+2B_1W-Q\bigr).
\tag{13}
\]
Over \(L\), the linear coordinate \(aZ+bW\) and its
independent companion make the relation linear in the latter,
with nonzero coefficient proportional to \(p\). It is thus
irreducible. Its coefficients are primitive over \(R\),
because a common prime divisor would divide both \(a^2\)
and \(b^2\), contradicting \(\gcd(a,b)=1\).
Gauss primeness and the generic equality \(H_L=L[r]\)
give the exact global kernel in (13).

Each height-one base localization of \(A_R\) is generated
by its degree-at-most-two module, by the pass-46 models.
Therefore \(H\) and \(A_R\) agree at all those localizations.
This does **not** yet give \(H=A_R\). At a point of
\(V(J)\), both \(a,b\) vanish. The conic relation there
becomes linear, or, if all its remaining coefficients vanish,
identically zero. In the latter case \(H\) has a
two-dimensional closed fiber. The denominator-intersection
argument already established for the actual \(A\) cannot
be assumed for this auxiliary conic ring.

The exact conic subalgebra and section-zero description therefore
constrain the possible closed-fiber corrections. They neither
prove that the actual ring is generated by \(F_2\), nor
produce an invariant ambient coordinate or a polynomial
three-ring. All pass-46 artifacts remain unchanged.
