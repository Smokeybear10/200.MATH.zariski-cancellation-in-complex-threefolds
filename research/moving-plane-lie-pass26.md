# Moving invariant planes: finite adjoint orbits do not give a finite Lie algebra

A locally nilpotent horizontal derivation has a finite commutator orbit
on every algebraic derivation. Its highest commutator with the vertical
action is a genuine commuting tangent LND. These facts follow from
finite generation and the established leading-LND argument.

They do not imply that the original two actions have a joint invariant
coordinate, or that their generated Lie algebra is finite-dimensional.
Both shortcuts fail in an actual polynomial local-slice homotopy with
nonunit plinth and nonconstant modifying coefficient. Intermediate
commutators in that example also fail local nilpotence.

The example has a positive boundary that must not be suppressed: its
primitive highest commutator is a coordinate partial and does have a
slice. The calculation therefore supplies a successful changed plane
in this example, not a counterexample to all highest-commutator repairs.
No universal recognition or priority claim is made.

## 1. Finite adjoint orbits follow from local nilpotence

Let k be a characteristic-zero field, let B be a finitely generated
k-algebra, and let delta be an LND of B. For every derivation Theta, ad_delta is locally
nilpotent on Theta. The exact operator identity is

\[
(\operatorname{ad}_\delta)^n\Theta
 =\sum_{k=0}^n(-1)^k\binom nk
       \delta^{n-k}\Theta\delta^k.
\tag{1}
\]

To prove finiteness, choose algebra generators g_i. Only finitely many
delta^k(g_i) are nonzero. For each of those elements, local nilpotence
also kills Theta(delta^k(g_i)) after finitely many further applications
of delta. For n larger than all the finitely many bounds
k+deg_delta(Theta(delta^k(g_i))), every term in (1) vanishes on every
g_i. A derivation vanishing on the generators is zero.

This statement does not require Theta to be locally nilpotent. It
proves local nilpotence of the adjoint operator on the vector space
Der(B), not that all of its values are locally nilpotent derivations
of B. Nor does it bound the Lie algebra generated by two derivations.

## 2. What the highest moving coefficient really supplies

Now assume B is a domain, delta(lambda)=1, and Delta is a nonzero LND
with Delta(lambda)=0. The slice theorem gives B=K[lambda], where
K=ker delta. Write

\[
\Delta|_K=\sum_{j=0}^{q}\lambda^jD_j,\qquad D_q\ne0,
\qquad \Delta\lambda=0.
\tag{2}
\]

The sum is finite because K is finitely generated, being isomorphic
to B/(lambda). Every D_j is a derivation of K. The highest coefficient
D_q is locally nilpotent: the coefficient of lambda^(nq) in Delta^n(f)
for f in K is D_q^n(f). Since Delta eventually kills f, D_q eventually
kills f. This includes q=0, when Delta is its own coefficientwise
extension. Therefore

\[
Q_q=\frac1{q!}(\operatorname{ad}_\delta)^q\Delta
     =\widetilde D_q
\tag{3}
\]

is a nonzero LND of B, kills lambda and commutes with delta.
The tilde denotes the extension from K fixing lambda.

This is the established leading-LND mechanism, applied in the
horizontal slice coordinates, not a new algebraization theorem.[^leading]
It gives neither a slice for D_q nor primitivity. When B is a UFD,
so is K, and one can take the primitive part over K; its coefficientwise
extension still commutes with delta. Nothing in the argument forces
that primitive part to have a slice either. The earlier
[primitive leading control](leading-derivation-pass23.md) already
separates primitivity from fixed-point-freeness and a slice.

The full polynomial family exp(tau*ad_delta)(Delta) consists of
conjugates of Delta and hence of LNDs. Its separate coefficients
need not all be LNDs. Section 5 gives an exact failure.

## 3. A genuine homotopy and a different horizontal coordinate partial

Take the existing primitive input

\[
R=k[x,y,z],\quad D=x^2\partial_y+2y\partial_z,
\quad C=k[x,h],\quad h=x^2z-y^2,
\]
\[
r=y,\quad p=x^2,\quad d=x,
\quad B=R[\lambda,w]/((1+\lambda x)w-y).
\tag{4}
\]

Thus B=k[lambda,x,w,z] is an actual polynomial algebra and
y=(1+lambda*x)w. The canonical primitive vertical LND is

\[
\Delta\lambda=\Delta x=0,\quad
\Delta w=x^2,\quad
\Delta z=2(1+\lambda x)^2w.
\tag{5}
\]

Its full kernel is k[lambda,x,h], with
h=x^2z-(1+lambda*x)^2w^2, and its full plinth is x^2 times
that kernel. These are the same two-chart data used in the
[pass-25 normalizer proof](coupled-homotopy-pass25.md).

Choose the different unrestricted horizontal LND

\[
\delta=\partial_\lambda+z\partial_x.
\tag{6}
\]

It has delta(lambda)=1. Its explicit invariant coordinates and inverse
are

\[
a=x-\lambda z,\quad b=w,\quad c=z,
\qquad x=a+\lambda c,\quad w=b,\quad z=c.
\tag{7}
\]

Thus B=k[lambda,a,b,c], K=k[a,b,c], and delta=partial_lambda
in this frame. Put V=1+a*lambda+c*lambda^2. Then exactly

\[
\Delta a=-2\lambda V^2b,\qquad
\Delta b=(a+\lambda c)^2,\qquad
\Delta c=2V^2b,\qquad \Delta\lambda=0.
\tag{8}
\]

These are conjugated values of the actual homotopy action (5), not
an unrelated pair of formal vector fields. Local nilpotence and
primitivity are preserved by the polynomial coordinate change.

## 4. The original actions have no nonconstant joint invariant

In this example

\[
\ker\delta\cap\ker\Delta=k.
\tag{9}
\]

Indeed, any joint invariant lies in k[a,b,c]. The constant and
linear lambda coefficients of (8) are

\[
D_0=a^2\partial_b+2b\partial_c,
\qquad
D_1=-2b\partial_a+2ac\partial_b+4ab\partial_c.
\tag{10}
\]

The first has kernel k[a,H], where H=a^2c-b^2. To check this
without guessing its generators, localize at a to obtain the
polynomial b-line over k[a,a^(-1),H]. A putative invariant with
a pole in a has numerator whose nonzero residue becomes a nonzero
polynomial in -b^2 after reduction modulo a. That numerator cannot
be divisible by a in k[a,b,c], a contradiction. Thus the localized
kernel contracts exactly to k[a,H].

Write a joint invariant as P(a,H). Direct differentiation gives

\[
D_1(P)=-2bP_a+(4a^3b-8abc)P_H.
\tag{11}
\]

In k[a,a^(-1),H,b], substitute c=(H+b^2)/a^2. The coefficient
of b^3 in (11) is -8P_H/a, so P_H=0. The coefficient of b
then gives P_a=0. Characteristic zero gives P in k.

The same calculation works for rational P(a,H). Hence even the
joint invariant field in Frac(B) is k. There is no common rational
coordinate, much less a common polynomial plane, from which to start
a simultaneous invariant-plane transport in this example.

This does not say that no changed plane exists. The commuting
highest coefficient in Section 6 gives a different pair of actions.

## 5. A finite adjoint orbit generates an infinite-dimensional Lie algebra

Let Q_j=(ad_delta)^j(Delta)/j!. From (8),

\[
Q_5=-2bc^2\partial_a,
\]
\[
Q_4=(-4abc-10\lambda bc^2)\partial_a+2bc^2\partial_c,
\qquad (\operatorname{ad}_\delta)^6\Delta=0.
\tag{12}
\]

The penultimate commutator is not locally nilpotent. It fixes b,
and its iterates on c are

\[
Q_4^j(c)=(2b)^j j!c^{j+1}\ne0\quad(j\ge1).
\tag{13}
\]

More strongly, the complex Lie algebra generated by delta and Delta
is infinite-dimensional. Both Q_4 and Q_5 belong to that algebra,
and repeated bracketing gives

\[
(\operatorname{ad}_{Q_4})^j Q_5
 =-2\,2^j\frac{(j+3)!}{3!}
      b^{j+1}c^{j+2}\partial_a\quad(j\ge0).
\tag{14}
\]

For the induction, if F=b^ell*c^m is independent of a and lambda,
then

\[
[Q_4,F\partial_a]=(2m+4)bF c\partial_a.
\]

Start with -2bc^2 and apply this identity. The lambda-dependent
term in Q_4 does not contribute, because both coefficients being
compared in that part are independent of a. Formula (14) follows,
and the resulting fields have distinct monomial degrees. They are
linearly independent over k.

The assertion concerns the complex Lie algebra, not the B-module
of derivations. It does not contradict finite generation of that
module or the local nilpotence of ad_delta in Section 1. Only the
single delta-adjoint orbit is finite; alternating Lie words are not
controlled by that fact.

## 6. The highest coefficient supplies a useful new plane in this control

The top Q_5 in (12) has full plinth
bc^2*k[lambda,b,c], up to its nonzero constant coefficient. It
has no slice: every value is divisible by the nonunit b.
Its primitive part, however, is

\[
\partial_a,\qquad \partial_a(a)=1.
\tag{15}
\]

This is an actual commuting coordinate partial, not an unsliced
primitive example. Its joint kernel with delta is k[b,c], and
B=k[b,c][a,lambda]. The variables b,c are not joint invariants
of the original pair: Delta b=(a+lambda*c)^2 and Delta c=2V^2b
are nonzero.

Consequently the finite highest-commutator operation does escape
the old plane in this polynomial test. The missing universal claim
would be that its primitive part, or another controlled member of
the resulting Lie algebra, always supplies a slice or an appropriate
recognition action. Neither finite adjoint nilpotence, the existence
of a leading LND, nor Lie closure proves that claim. The counterexample
to finite-dimensional closure also prevents invoking finite-dimensional
algebraic-group structure without an additional hypothesis.

## 7. Verification and scope

The companion checker uses only unittest and SymPy. It checks the
actual coordinate frame, the original vertical action and its
primitivity, all iterated adjoint coefficients and the polynomial
conjugation identity, the joint-kernel coefficient calculation,
the non-nilpotent penultimate coefficient, the infinite Lie-word
formula in bounded degrees, and the sliced primitive-top control.

~~~sh
/opt/anaconda3/bin/python research/moving_plane_lie_checks_pass26.py
~~~

All eight unittest methods passed in 0.124 seconds. A separate
agent independently reconstructed Q_4, Q_5, the full Lie-word
recurrence and the joint-kernel coefficient argument, with no
mathematical correction requested. No old suite or registry was edited.

The all-degree statements follow from the explicit recurrences and
coefficient arguments above, not from the sampled iterations.
The general adjoint lemma uses finite generation, while the top-LND
argument additionally uses the horizontal slice and Delta(lambda)=0.

No unrestricted horizontal LND has been constructed for arbitrary
primitive local-slice inputs. No reduction of an arbitrary sliced
polynomial four-ring to these homotopies is supplied. All machinery
and recognition boundaries remain credited, with novelty and human
expert review unresolved.
