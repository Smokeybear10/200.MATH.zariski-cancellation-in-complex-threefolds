## 2. Whole polynomial return at a complex X-value

The actual derivation gives
\(\sigma=EY/a_0-2tX\in L[\mathcal B]\).
Conversely (3),(4) put every actual generator in \(L[X,\sigma]\).
Thus, as rings inside \(K(X)\),
\[
L[\mathcal B]=L[X,Y,Z,W]=L[X,\sigma].
\tag{5}
\]
X and sigma are algebraically independent over L: sigma is a line
parameter, while E kills \(L(\sigma)\) and does not kill X.
Consequently evaluation at the complex value \(X=-\alpha\) gives a
genuine equality of polynomial rings
\(L[Y_0,Z_0,W_0]=L[\sigma]\).

Direct substitution, with \(g=-\alpha^2\), yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
Y_0&=\beta+2\alpha^2t\in L,\\
Z_0&=\sigma^2+d\sigma+
       2t\beta+2\alpha^2t^2+e-\alpha s,\\
W_0&=-2\alpha\sigma^2+(-2\beta-\alpha d)\sigma
       +\ell-\alpha e+\alpha^2s .
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]
In particular \(Z_0\) has exact degree two. The combination
\(W_0+2\alpha Z_0\) has linear coefficient \(\alpha d-2\beta\).
If this coefficient vanished, that combination and \(Y_0\) would
belong to L, so the specialized ring would be \(L[Z_0]\).
It could not contain sigma, since every nonconstant polynomial in
the quadratic \(Z_0\) has even degree. Therefore
\[
\boxed{\alpha d-2\beta\ne0.}
\tag{7}
\]
No differentiation of an evaluation point occurs. In this branch the
evaluation point is even complex, although (5) would permit any L-value.
