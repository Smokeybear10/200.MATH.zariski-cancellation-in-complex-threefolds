# The unit second-image case forces the wrong conic fiber

In the remaining rank-two second-degree branch of the actual collapsed
mixed fiber, the normalized second-image ideal cannot be the unit ideal.
If it were, the degree-two module would give a monic conic that agrees
with the actual algebra at every invariant divisor. Its explicit free
basis then proves equality of the whole local algebras. The first-degree
basis element would have nonzero fiber class, contrary to the actual
whole-fiber derivation.

This excludes the unit case, not the remaining mixed fiber. No
smoothness contradiction, quartic-generation assertion, completion, or
unrestricted cancellation statement is needed.

## 1. Actual local hypotheses and divisor generation

Let R=S_m, with maximal ideal n and fraction field K, be the regular
local invariant plane in the supplied nonconical quadratic frame. Work
in the actual collapsed mixed (2,1) sector with L(m) nonzero, after the
full second-degree Artin branch was excluded in107. Write A_R for the
actual domain and N for its locally nilpotent derivation. The retained
data are
\[
\begin{gathered}
\ker(N|_{A_R})=R,\qquad (A_R)_K=K[v],\qquad
N|_{K[v]}=\lambda\partial_v,\quad \lambda\in K^*,\\
F_j=\ker(N^{j+1}|_{A_R})\simeq R^{j+1},\qquad
I_j=N^j(F_j)\subset R,\\
A_R/\mathfrak nA_R=\mathcal D[c],\qquad
\mathcal D=\mathbb C[G]/(G^2(G-1)),\\
\mathcal W_2=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb C}\{1,\bar G\},
\qquad \mathcal W_1=\mathbb C,\qquad
G\in F_2,\qquad \bar N(\bar G)=0.
\end{gathered}
\tag{1}
\]
Here Wj is the image in the entire nonreduced fiber, not only in its
reduction. The element G is the actual centered good normal retained
in the whole-fiber presentation. The minimal polynomial of its
fiber class has degree three, so its class is nonconstant. The actual
base inclusion is faithfully flat. These are precisely the current
rank-two hypotheses, not properties inferred from an abstract conic.
The conclusion W1=C also follows directly: a nonconstant first-degree
residue a+bG would make 1,r,r squared three independent images from F2,
contrary to its image dimension two.[^setting]

Every height-one prime p of R satisfies the actual equality
\[
\boxed{\qquad
(A_R)_{\mathfrak p}
 =R_{\mathfrak p}\big[(F_2)_{\mathfrak p}\big].
\qquad}
\tag{2}
\]
Indeed the residue constants have degree one or two. In degree one,
the finite DVR slice division gives a polynomial algebra generated in
degree one. In degree two, the exact107 conic is generated by elements
of degrees one and two. Its required surviving degree-two element is
supplied by actual good-generator noncollapse91. Localization at these
invariant denominators commutes with the degree kernels, so these
local degree modules are exactly the localization of F2 in (1).
This proves (2) for every height-one prime, not only for a chosen finite
set or for the support of one selected derivative.[^divisors]

## 2. The unit image case supplies a normalized free conic basis

The constant 1 is unimodular in F1 by faithful flatness. Choose a
basis 1,r0 and set P0=Nr0, so I1=P0R with P0 nonzero. The exact
divisor valuations107 and the coprime-row description of F2/F1
normalize the second image as I2=P0 J2. For clarity, this uses only
the local conic matrix and the height-one valuations, not the excluded
full-Artin residue hypothesis: if r0=az+bw after a constant shift,
then I2=epsilon P0(a,b), and coprimality makes epsilon a unit from
the equality of the first and second valuations at every divisor.
Here J2=(a,b) can initially be the unit ideal or a proper
maximal-primary complete intersection.

Assume J2=R, equivalently I2=I1. The exact sequence
0 to F1 to F2 to I2 to 0 splits since I2 is free of rank one.
Choose Y with N squared Y=P0; then 1,r0,Y is a basis of F2.
Write G=A+B r0+C Y with A,B,C in R. If C belonged to n, its
whole-fiber residue would be constant by W1=C, contradicting (1).
Thus C is a unit and 1,r0,G is also a basis.

Since NY-r0 is invariant, define r=NG and P=N squared G=C P0.
The coefficient of r0
in NG is C, which is a unit, giving the exact normal form
\[
\begin{gathered}
F_1=R\oplus Rr,\qquad
F_2=R\oplus Rr\oplus RG,\qquad I_1=I_2=PR,\\
NG=r,\qquad Nr=P\ne0,\qquad
\eta=2PG-r^2\in R,\qquad \bar r=0.
\end{gathered}
\tag{3}
\]
The invariant identity follows by differentiation. The zero class of r
is the actual equality bar N(bar G)=0 from (1). It is not obtained by
discarding nilpotents. In particular P and eta belong to n.

The stronger retained order bound also follows in this normal form.
The two-dimensional piece R+RG has independent whole-fiber residues,
so the purity lemma applies to this piece, even though it does not
apply to F2. Since r belongs to nA_R, the equality 2PG-eta=r squared
lies in n squared A_R. Coefficient contraction in R+RG gives P,eta
in n squared. The following exclusion needs only their membership in
n and the exact zero residue in (3), not this stronger order.[^purity]

## 3. The monic conic is an actual embedded algebra

Let H=R[r,G] inside A_R. Equation (3) induces a surjection
\[
\begin{gathered}
C_0=R[\mathsf r,\mathsf G]/
(\mathsf r^2-2P\mathsf G+\eta)\longrightarrow H,\\
\boxed{\qquad
H\simeq C_0,
\qquad
H=\bigoplus_{j\ge0}RG^j\ \oplus
\bigoplus_{j\ge0}RrG^j.
\qquad}
\end{gathered}
\tag{4}
\]
The source is free over R[Gscr] with basis 1,rscr, because the
equation is monic in rscr. In particular it is torsion free over R.
Over K the equation solves for Gscr, since P is nonzero. Moreover
r is a first-degree element with Nr=P, so r is an affine linear
coordinate of the generic line K[v]. Thus the generic map is the
isomorphism K[rscr] to K[r]=(A_R)_K. Any original kernel element
would be killed by a nonzero element of R, impossible in the
torsion-free source. This proves the exact presentation and the
explicit free R-module basis in (4).

No normality of H is assumed or needed. It is its monic free basis,
not an unspecified Hartogs extension or an assumption of finite
generation as an R-module, that will control the next intersection.

## 4. Finite coefficient intersection gives H=A_R

For every height-one prime p, (2) and the basis in (3) give
H_p=(A_R)_p. Each element f of A_R belongs to K[r], hence has a
unique finite expansion in the K-basis furnished by (4):
\[
\begin{gathered}
f=\sum_{j=0}^{d}\alpha_jG^j
  +\sum_{j=0}^{d}\beta_jrG^j,\qquad \alpha_j,\beta_j\in K,\\
f\in H_{\mathfrak p}\ \text{for all height-one }\mathfrak p
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\alpha_j,\beta_j\in
\bigcap_{\operatorname{ht}\mathfrak p=1}R_{\mathfrak p}=R,\\
\boxed{\qquad A_R=H.\qquad}
\end{gathered}
\tag{5}
\]
Zero coefficients may be inserted to use a common finite bound d.
The displayed intersection for scalar coefficients is elementary in
the UFD R: a reduced fraction with a nonunit denominator has a prime
denominator factor, so cannot belong to the corresponding R_p.
Uniqueness of the localized monic basis forces each coefficient into
each R_p. Thus every actual f belongs to H, and the reverse inclusion
was built into its definition.

This proves an equality of the entire actual local algebras. It does
not exchange a degree kernel with a residue kernel or assert purity
of an arbitrary F_j. Nor is equality at divisors used by itself:
the finite unique coefficient expansion in a globally free basis is
the extra ingredient that makes it sufficient here.

## 5. The actual zero residue contradicts that free basis

Reduce (4) and (5) modulo n. Since P,eta belong to n, they give
\[
\begin{gathered}
A_R/\mathfrak nA_R
\simeq\mathbb C[\mathsf r,\mathsf G]/(\mathsf r^2),
\qquad \mathsf r\mapsto\bar r,\quad\mathsf G\mapsto\bar G,\\
\mathsf r\ne0
\text{ in }\mathbb C[\mathsf r,\mathsf G]/(\mathsf r^2),
\qquad \bar r=0\text{ by (3)}.
\end{gathered}
\tag{6}
\]
This is a contradiction. Equivalently, r is a member of the free
basis (4), so it cannot belong to nH, whereas it belongs to nA_R
by the whole-fiber derivation. The contradiction already occurs in
this single nilpotent class. One need not compare the numbers of
components or invoke the singularity of a conic at a zero first jet.

Therefore J2 cannot be R in the remaining actual rank-two branch.
It must be a proper maximal-primary two-generated complete
intersection, with its possible nonreduced structure retained.
The proper-J2 branch is not excluded by this argument.

## Scope and provenance

The algebra equality in Sections 3–4 is useful independently of the
final fiber contradiction: a monic conic generated by the full F2,
with the exact divisor-generation property (2), is already the whole
local algebra by its finite coefficient intersection. The actual
zero residue in (3) is what makes that equality impossible here.

This proof does not use the selected c's sharp fourth-image valuations,
the nonprincipality of I4, an actual F4 generating set, or smoothness
of A_R. Those properties remain available in the frame, but are not
silently substituted for the precise divisor-generation input. It
does not assert that every algebra with a polynomial generic line is
generated in degree two, or that an arbitrary sliced action has this
frame. No cancellation, global slice, or expert-review claim is made.
