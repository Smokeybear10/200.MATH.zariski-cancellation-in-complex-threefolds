# A coefficient restriction from polynomial plinth curves

Private bounded continuation after pass 17. This is a source-dependent
deduction, with no claim of novelty or human review. It does not solve
threefold cancellation.

Let \(R=\mathbb C[x,y,z]\), let D be a nonzero primitive locally nilpotent
derivation, and write \(C=\ker(D)\) and plinth(D)=pC. Suppose p is nonunit.
The [whole-component argument](whole-plinth-component-pass16.md)
identifies the reduced plinth support with Kaliman's retained curve.
Theorem 5.2(i) of his [final author manuscript](https://www.math.miami.edu/~kaliman/library/Ka.invent.2004.c%2B.pdf)
states that each irreducible component of that retained curve is a
polynomial curve, meaning that its normalization is the affine line.
Only this component statement is used below. It does not assert that
the whole curve is connected or that the plinth ideal is reduced.

## 1. Units on the reduced curve

Write p_red for a squarefree generator of the radical of pC.
For every irreducible component Gamma_i of V(p_red), normalization
gives an injection \(\mathbb C[\Gamma_i]\) into \(\mathbb C[t]\). Consequently every invertible
regular function on Gamma_i is a nonzero constant: it and its inverse
map to polynomials whose product is 1.

Now suppose B belongs to C and (B,p)C=C. Its restriction to each
Gamma_i is therefore a nonzero constant c_i. Whenever Gamma_i and
Gamma_j intersect, evaluation at an intersection point forces
c_i=c_j. The intersection graph of irreducible components of a
connected algebraic curve is connected. Hence B is constant on each
connected component of V(p_red).

If V(p_red) is connected, a single \(c\in\mathbb C^*\) works on all components.
Since C is a polynomial two-ring and p_red is squarefree, vanishing
on V(p_red) gives the exact divisibility

    B = c + p_red Q,       Q in C.

Equivalently, the reduced coordinate ring has unit group \(\mathbb C^*\) in the
connected case. In the disconnected case it has one independent
nonzero constant for each connected component. This follows also
from the product decomposition of the coordinate ring over its
finitely many open-and-closed components.

## 2. A completed squarefree-plinth subcase

Assume in addition that p is squarefree up to a scalar and that its
zero set is connected. Then every invariant extension

    E = A D + B partial_t,       A,B in C,

having a slice is a polynomial-coordinate translation on R[t].

Here is the complete reduction to the previously written construction.
The [general affine-replacement proof](general-invariant-affine-pass17.md)
gives (Ap,B)C=C. In particular (p,B)C=C and (A,B)C=C. Section 1 now
gives B=c+pQ. Choose a minimal local slice r with Dr=p. After scaling
E by c^-1, its second coefficient is 1+p(Q/c). The finite variable-time
flow and its explicit inverse in
[the matrix-lift proof, Section 6](extension-mixing-pass15.md)
apply without any rank or homogeneity hypothesis. They conjugate
E/c to partial_t, so E is conjugate to c partial_t and then to
partial_t after a scalar change of the last coordinate. Its kernel
is therefore abstractly C^[3].

This conclusion inherits the source-dependent geometric proof of
general affine replacement. It is not deduced from a grid search.
The new step here is the elementary unit restriction, not a new
matrix-conjugation construction.

If p is already a unit, D has a slice. Since its invariant ring is
a polynomial two-ring, the slice theorem identifies the base with
\(\mathbb C[u,v,r]\), with D a coordinate partial after scaling.
In this boundary case a slice extension forces the invariant
row (A,B) to be unimodular, and its two-by-two inverse matrix directly
gives polynomial coordinates. Connectedness is not needed in that case.

The same conclusion covers invariant replicas of these primitive
bases. If D'=hD with invariant h, an extension A D'+B partial_t
is simply (Ah)D+B partial_t, to which the preceding theorem applies.
This does not assert a polynomial-curve description for the additional
replica divisor h=0.

## 3. Why the remaining hypotheses cannot be discarded here

Reducedness is a genuine issue for this argument. In C[f,h], the
elements p=f^n h and B=1+fh with n>=2 satisfy (p,B)=1, and the reduced
support is connected. But B-1 is not divisible by p. Pass 17 solves
specified derivations having these data using additional finite-jet
coordinates. The unit argument alone does not provide those coordinates
for an arbitrary derivation with the same plinth multiplicities.

Connectedness is not automatic for primitive polynomial-three-space
derivations. For example,

    D = u(u-1) partial_v + 2v partial_w

is primitive and has the two disjoint reduced plinth components u=0
and u=1. Its invariant ring is generated by u and
q=u(u-1)w-v^2. The triangular plinth calculation gives generator
u(u-1). The coefficient B=u+1 is comaximal with the generator, but
its two restrictions are 1 and 2, not a single constant. This is
not a counterexample to kernel polynomiality. It only excludes
silently replacing the connected-component conclusion by a global
constant assertion. The example has rank at most two.

There is an explicit positive slice control here:

    1 = u(u-1)/2 + (2-u)(u+1)/2.

Thus for A=1, the polynomial v/2+(2-u)t/2 is a slice of
D+(u+1)partial_t. The disconnected example does not suggest a
failure of affine replacement.

No statement that every primitive rank-three plinth support is
connected has been proved or used. Nor may the polynomial-curve
claim be extended to arbitrary replica factors: multiplying D by
an arbitrary invariant can add unrelated fixed divisors.

## 4. Next precise target

For primitive bases with connected plinth support, the unresolved
coefficient issue is concentrated in nilpotent thickening:
B is a nonzero constant modulo the radical, but need not be one
modulo the full plinth ideal. An all-base lift from this radical
congruence to polynomial kernel coordinates would extend the
pass-17 construction beyond its explicit local-slice family.
This is a target, not an established theorem.

A separate algebraic audit checked the unit argument, the reduction
to the existing matrix theorem, and the triangular boundary. No gap
was reported. This is not human expert review or a formal proof.

The separate [principal-modification reduction](arbitrary-invariant-kernel-pass18.md)
provides another exact formulation of the outstanding recognition
question. Neither formulation reduces arbitrary slice derivations
on C^[4] to invariant extensions.
