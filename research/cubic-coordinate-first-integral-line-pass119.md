# A coordinate first integral forces polynomial line-valued velocity

If the rational first-integral field of the actual cubic direction is
generated by a polynomial coordinate of the invariant plane, the speed
vector is polynomially line-valued in the other base variable. Its line
direction can vary with the first variable. The quadratic metric has
rank two or three: rank two makes that direction a constant ambient
vector, while rank three makes the metric determinant a nonzero complex
constant.

These are necessary structural restrictions. They do not assert that
the rational first integral is a polynomial coordinate, that the second
line parameter is an affine coordinate, or that a matrix over the base
defines an ambient automorphism. The rank-three case requires a separate
use of the actual potential and volume, beyond this lemma.

## 1. The additional coordinate-field hypothesis

Retain the exact actual cubic setting and moving-frame conclusions of
[moving-frame118](cubic-moving-frame-constraint-pass118.md). In particular,
\(B=A[u]=\mathbb C[x_1,x_2,x_3,z]\) is in its supplied original
constant-acceleration frame, and \(b=E(x_1,x_2,x_3)^t\) lies in the
actual invariant plane S. The extra hypothesis here is that the field
of rational invariants of the nonzero cubic direction V is generated
by an actual polynomial coordinate s of that plane:

\[
\begin{gathered}
S=\mathbb C[s,t],\quad K=\mathbb C(s,t),\quad
L=\ker(V|K)=\mathbb C(s)=:k,\quad V=\mu\partial_t,
\quad \mu\in K^*,\\
Q\in\operatorname{Sym}_3(k),\quad n\in k^3\setminus\{0\},\quad
n^tb=0,\quad b^tQb=-1,\quad
\Omega=\det[b,b_s,b_t]\ne0,\\
Q'=\kappa n n^t\ne0,\qquad \kappa\in k^*.
\end{gathered}
\tag{1}
\]

Here n is the normalized normal column from118, and primes mean
ordinary s-derivatives. Since V(s)=0, every derivation of K of this
form is a rational multiple of \(\partial_t\). The entries of Q and
n lie in k by the stated invariant-field hypothesis. The rank-one
derivative formula118 then gives the last line: Q is nonconstant,
so Q' is nonzero; after choosing a nonzero component of n, its scalar
coefficient is in k because all entries of Q' and n are in k.
The sign is absorbed into kappa.

Nothing here replaces a rational generator of L by a polynomial
coordinate without proof. That coordinate property is precisely the
additional premise.

## 2. Polynomial parametrization rules out a nondegenerate plane quadric

Let \(W=\ker(n^t:k^3\to k)\). This is a two-dimensional k-vector
space, and \(b(t)\in W\otimes_k k[t]\) is nonconstant by Omega
nonzero. The restriction of Q to W cannot have rank zero because its
value on b is -1. It cannot have rank two either: over an algebraic
closure of k, its binary quadratic form is a nonzero scalar times a
product of two independent linear forms. Their values on b(t) have
nonzero constant product, so both are units of the polynomial ring
\(\overline k[t]\), hence constants. Independence would make b(t)
constant, contradicting \(b_t\ne0\).

Thus Q restricted to W has rank exactly one. It is a scalar multiple
of the square of a k-linear form. The value of that form on b(t) has
constant nonzero square, so is itself constant. With p=b(s,0), this
proves

\[
\operatorname{rank}(Q|_W)=1,\qquad
b(t)-p\in k[t]d_0\quad\text{for a nonzero }d_0\in W,
\qquad
Q(p,p)=-1,\quad Q(p,d_0)=Q(d_0,d_0)=0.
\tag{2}
\]

For a direct construction over k, choose a vector in W of nonzero
quadratic value. Rank one expresses the bilinear form as that value
times the square of its associated normalized linear functional.
This avoids adjoining a square root in the final affine-line statement.
In particular p and d0 are linearly independent over k.

## 3. The line decomposition is polynomial over the whole s-line

Expand b(t)-p in powers of t. Its nonzero coefficient columns belong
to \(\mathbb C[s]^3\) and span the k-line \(kd_0\). Clear
denominators of any nonzero column on that line and divide the gcd of
its three entries. This gives a primitive column d in \(\mathbb C[s]^3\).
There are polynomials r_i(s) with \(\sum_i r_i d_i=1\).
If a coefficient column is \(h_j d\) with \(h_j\in k\), that
Bezout identity gives \(h_j=\sum_i r_i(h_jd_i)\in\mathbb C[s]\).
Consequently

\[
\begin{gathered}
b=p(s)+d(s)H(s,t),\qquad p,d\in\mathbb C[s]^3,\qquad
(d_1,d_2,d_3)\mathbb C[s]=\mathbb C[s],\\
H\in\mathbb C[s,t],\qquad H(s,0)=0,\qquad H_t\ne0,\qquad
W=kp+kd,\\
Q(p,p)=-1,\qquad Q(p,d)=Q(d,d)=0,
\qquad Q'p=Q'd=0.
\end{gathered}
\tag{3}
\]

The last two annihilations use Q'=kappa nn transposed and n annihilating
both p and d. A primitive d is unique up to a nonzero complex scalar,
once its k-line is fixed. This uniqueness is not needed below.

There is no degree-one assertion about H. Nor does primitivity of d
make a matrix completion over \(\mathbb C[s]\) an automorphism of
the original polynomial four-ring. The valid whole-base identity is
the displayed polynomial decomposition. Its determinant is

\[
\Omega=H_t\left(\det[p,p',d]+H\det[p,d',d]\right).
\tag{4}
\]

This follows by differentiating (3); terms proportional to d vanish
against its occurrence in the final determinant column.

## 4. The metric-rank alternatives

Rank one for Q itself is impossible. Set w=Qp. Then w is nonzero
and the rank-one identity with p transposed Qp=-1 is
\(Q=-ww^t\). Since Q'p=0, one has w'=Qp', which lies in
the image line kw of Q. Write w'=lambda w. Differentiating
Q=-ww transposed gives Q'=2lambda Q. Applying this to p gives
0=2lambda w, hence lambda=0 and Q'=0, contradicting (1).

Choose any vector e completing p,d to a basis of k cubed. In that
basis, Q and the normalized normal row have forms

\[
[Q]_{p,d,e}=\begin{pmatrix}-1&0&A\\0&0&B\\A&B&C\end{pmatrix},
\qquad [n^t]_{p,d,e}=(0,0,\nu),\quad \nu\ne0.
\tag{5}
\]

If Q has rank two, B=0 because the determinant in (5) is B squared.
Thus Qd=0 and its kernel is exactly kd. Differentiate Qd=0 and use
Q'd=0 to obtain Qd'=0, hence d' is proportional to d over k.
Every ratio of two nonzero entries of d then has zero s-derivative
and belongs to C. Primitivity of the polynomial column d forces its
remaining common scalar to be a unit of C[s]. Thus d is a constant
nonzero complex column.

If Q has rank three, the lower-right entry of its inverse in the
dual basis is zero, by the corresponding two-by-two minor in (5).
Therefore n transposed Q inverse n=0. Jacobi's determinant derivative
and (1) give

\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\operatorname{rank}Q=2:& d\in\mathbb C^3\setminus\{0\},\\[2pt]
\operatorname{rank}Q=3:&
n^tQ^{-1}n=0,\quad
(\det Q)'=(\det Q)\operatorname{tr}(Q^{-1}Q')=0,
\quad \det Q\in\mathbb C^*.
\end{array}
\tag{6}
\]

These are the only ranks compatible with (1).

## 5. Exact remaining distinctions

In the rank-two case, a constant complex change of the three original
good coordinates can send d to a coordinate vector. Such a constant
linear change is a genuine ambient coordinate change. After absorbing
the corresponding component of p into H, the speed takes the form
\((g(s),P(s,t),f(s))\), with g,f polynomial in s and P_t nonzero.
This does not make g equal to the actual base coordinate s. The
[pullback exclusion118](cubic-polynomial-pullback-exclusion-pass118.md)
cannot be invoked by silently replacing s with an arbitrary g(s).

The rank-three alternative is not contradictory using the geometric
identities in (1) alone. For example, set
\(p=(0,s,s^2+1)\),
\(d=(1,s^4/4-s^2/2,s^5/5-s)\), and b=p+td.
Its polynomial speed row is a unit row, its actual moving-frame
determinant is s squared minus one, its Q is independent of t with
determinant one, and its only nonzero cubic tensor component is
\(-12s/(s^2-1)^2\). The direction d is visibly nonconstant.
These statements follow by direct differentiation of b. They give
coefficient geometry, not an original-coordinate polynomial inverse
or the cubic potential's full unit-volume realization. The latter
conditions can impose further restrictions not used in this lemma.

The proof above is exact algebra under the added coordinate-field
hypothesis. A finite inline symbolic calculation checked the displayed
coefficient example; it does not certify a global actual frame.
No new primary classification theorem, source proof, or numerical
theorem verification was used. All files through118 remain unchanged.

## 6. Forward consequence of the remaining actual conditions

The [ruled-metric exclusion119](cubic-ruled-nondegenerate-exclusion-pass119.md)
now excludes the rank-three alternative by the actual cubic potential
and unit-volume determinant. Its ruling derivative is a rational
multiple of V, so its tensor-annihilation hypothesis follows here.
The [polynomial-curve exclusion119](cubic-polynomial-curve-exclusion-pass119.md)
excludes the rank-two speed form described in Section 5, without making
g(s) a base coordinate. The intervening constant ambient linear change
preserves the actual moving-frame relative degree.

Together these separate proofs show that the actual exact cubic branch
cannot have L=C(s) for an actual polynomial coordinate s of S. The
coordinate-field hypothesis remains an extra premise; no arbitrary
rational first integral has been made polynomial or coordinate. This
note is the common line/rank prerequisite to those exclusions, not an
additional independently counted recognition result. The full saved
proofs were read through EOF here; this implication is not certified
by the finite coefficient control or its companion checker.
