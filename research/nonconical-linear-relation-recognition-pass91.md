# A linear relative relation recognizes the nonconical kernel

In the actual nonconical three-good-coordinate constant-acceleration
setting, a defining relation of relative degree one already implies
\(A=S[t]\). The argument does not require its two linear coefficients
to generate the unit ideal. Faithful-flat contraction removes the
constant term, factoriality supplies an actual polynomial parameter,
and the whole-divisor noncollapse theorem descends the last generator.

## 1. Actual hypotheses and the degree-one branch

Retain the actual setup of
[quadratic-potential90](nonconical-quadratic-potential-pass90.md):
\[
\begin{gathered}
B=\mathbb C[x_1,x_2,x_3,z]=A[u],\qquad A=\ker E,\qquad
Eu=1,\qquad E^2x_i=0\ (i\le3),\qquad E^2z=q\in\mathbb C^*,\\
S=\ker(W|A)=\mathbb C[s_1,s_2],\qquad Wu=0,\qquad
b=(Ex_1,Ex_2,Ex_3)^t\in S^3,\\
\Omega=\det[b,b_{s_1},b_{s_2}]\ne0,\qquad
R=S[A_1,A_2],\qquad A=R[c],\qquad A_\Omega=R_\Omega .
\end{gathered}
\tag{1}
\]
All four displayed generators of B are original polynomial
coordinates, and \(u=Ez/q\) is the canonical slice. The relative
generators and the invariant plane are
the actual ones obtained by completing the unit good-speed row.
Both E and W are actual locally nilpotent derivations.

The retained intrinsic quotient theorem gives faithful flatness of
\(S\to A\), with purely one-dimensional nonempty fibers.
The ring A is a UFD, since \(A[u]\) is a polynomial ring.
The common kernel \(S=\ker E\cap\ker W\) is factorially closed
in B. In particular every irreducible element of S remains prime
in A. An intrinsic local slice also gives
\(A_K=K[v]\), where \(K=\operatorname{Frac}(S)\).

The global ring R has a height-one prime relation in
\(S[T_1,T_2]\). Its degree is at most three by
[cubic-relation90](nonconical-cubic-relation-pass90.md).
This note treats precisely the case in which its irreducible
defining polynomial has degree one:
\[
R=S[T_1,T_2]/(F),\qquad
F=\alpha T_1+\beta T_2+\delta,\qquad
\alpha,\beta,\delta\in S,\quad(\alpha,\beta)\ne(0,0).
\tag{2}
\]
Evaluation sends \(T_i\) to the actual \(A_i\). The polynomial F
is primitive over S: a common nonunit divisor of its three
coefficients would contradict irreducibility and positive relative
degree.

We use one further actual-frame conclusion, not a claim about
arbitrary hypersurfaces. For every irreducible \(p\in S\), put
\(V=S_{(p)}\), \(k=\operatorname{Frac}(S/(p))\), and
\(\overline A=A_V/pA_V\). The
[whole-divisor theorem90](nonconical-cyclic-fiber-omission-pass90.md)
proves
\[
\overline A\text{ is a nonzero domain of dimension one},\qquad
\operatorname{trdeg}_k k[\overline A_1,\overline A_2]=1.
\tag{3}
\]
Its proof uses the original last partial, the cyclic Jacobian,
and the exact universal generator pencil. Thus (3) concerns the
image of R on the whole A-divisor fiber. It does not identify
that image with \(R_V/pR_V\).

## 2. Contracting the constant term and extracting an actual parameter

The evaluated equation in (2) places \(\delta\) in
\((\alpha,\beta)A\). Faithful-flat contraction gives
\[
\delta\in(\alpha,\beta)A\cap S=(\alpha,\beta)S.
\tag{4}
\]
Choose \(f,g\in S\) with \(\alpha f+\beta g=-\delta\).
Then \(Y_1=A_1-f\) and \(Y_2=A_2-g\) satisfy
\(\alpha Y_1+\beta Y_2=0\).

Primitivity and (4) imply
\(\gcd_S(\alpha,\beta)=1\), with the usual interpretation when
one coefficient is zero. This does not assert
\((\alpha,\beta)S=S\). Factorial closure of S in A ensures
that the nonzero coefficients have no common irreducible factor
in A either.

Suppose first that both coefficients are nonzero. In the UFD A,
\(\alpha\mid\beta Y_2\) and coprimality imply
\(\alpha\mid Y_2\). Define \(t=-Y_2/\alpha\in A\).
The relation then gives \(Y_1=\beta t\).
If \(\alpha=0\), primitivity and (4) make \(\beta\) a unit;
take \(t=Y_1/\beta\). If \(\beta=0\), then \(\alpha\) is a
unit and take \(t=-Y_2/\alpha\). In every case,
\[
t\in A,\qquad
A_1=f+\beta t,\qquad A_2=g-\alpha t,\qquad
R=S[\beta t,\alpha t]\subset S[t]\subset A.
\tag{5}
\]
The existence of \(t\) in the actual ring A, rather than only
its fraction field, is the essential syzygy step.

## 3. The generic fiber is already generated by this parameter

At least one coefficient in (5) is invertible over K, so
\(R_K=K[t]\). By (1), localization at the nonzero base element
\(\Omega\) identifies A and R. Localizing further to K therefore
gives the exact equality
\[
A_K=R_K=K[t],\qquad c\in K[t].
\tag{6}
\]
The element t is transcendental over K, since the actual generic
fiber \(A_K=K[v]\) has dimension one.

Only generic equality is used at this step. Alternatively, the
earlier fraction-field equality would suffice: a nonconstant
\(t\in K[v]\) makes \(K[v]\) integral over \(K[t]\); equal
fraction fields and normality of \(K[t]\) then give the same
conclusion. No birational parametrization is silently treated as
an isomorphism.

## 4. Whole-divisor noncollapse removes every coefficient pole

Write the uniquely determined polynomial \(c\in K[t]\) as
\(\sum_{j=0}^m c_jt^j\), with \(c_j\in K\). Fix an irreducible
\(p\in S\) and use V and k from (3). Suppose at least one
coefficient does not belong to V. Choose the smallest integer
\(n\ge1\) such that every \(p^nc_j\) belongs to V. Then
\[
p^n c=f_p(t),\qquad
f_p(T)\in V[T],\qquad
0\ne\overline f_p(T)\in k[T],\qquad
\overline f_p(\overline t)=0\text{ in }\overline A .
\tag{7}
\]
The nonzero reduction follows from the minimality of n, since
V is a DVR with uniformizer p. The equation can be reduced
because both c and t belong to the actual \(A_V\).

A nonzero constant \(\overline f_p\) would contradict the
nonzero fiber. Otherwise (7) says that \(\overline t\) is
algebraic over k. Equation (5) would then make both
\(\overline A_1\) and \(\overline A_2\) algebraic over k,
contradicting (3). No claim that \(\overline t\) must be a
k-valued scalar is needed.

Thus every coefficient \(c_j\) belongs to every height-one
localization \(S_{(p)}\). A UFD is the intersection of those
localizations inside its fraction field: a reduced denominator
of a fraction outside S has an irreducible prime factor.
Consequently all \(c_j\) belong to S. Combining this with
(5) and \(A=R[c]\) proves
\[
\boxed{\quad c\in S[t],\qquad A=S[t]\simeq\mathbb C^{[3]},
\qquad B=S[t,u],\quad E=\partial_u.\quad}
\tag{8}
\]
Here \(t\in A\) supplies the reverse inclusion \(S[t]\subset A\).
The equality does not require t to be one of the original
relative generators or an S-linear combination of them.

## 5. Scope and the codimension-two distinction

Recognition of A does not imply \(R=A\) or a unit coefficient
row. For a simple algebra-only illustration, let
\(S_0=\mathbb C[r,s]\), \(A_0=S_0[t]\), and
\(R_0=S_0[st,rt]\). The primitive linear relation between
the two displayed generators has coefficient ideal \((r,s)\),
which is proper. Moreover \(R_0\ne A_0\): modulo \((r,s)A_0\)
the image of \(R_0\) is \(\mathbb C\), while t remains an
indeterminate. This illustration is not asserted to be the
completed original nonconical frame of (1).

The proof above bypasses that codimension-two issue by first
extracting t in A and then descending the coefficients of c
using every whole generic divisor fiber. It recognizes the
relative degree-one branch even when \(\Omega\) is nonconstant.
It does not settle the degree-two or degree-three branches,
prove that an arbitrary sliced action has the required
three-good-coordinate frame, or establish general cancellation
or novelty.
