# An exponent budget for an exact same-prime triangular presentation

Private bounded proof, September 17, 2026. General cancellation remains
UNSOLVED. The result concerns an already supplied exact global presentation,
not the existence of such a presentation. Novelty is not asserted.

## 1. Statement and conventions

Let R=C^[3], let D be a nonzero locally nilpotent derivation, and let
A=ker(D)=C[a,b] be its actual embedded kernel. Let f be an irreducible
nonconstant element of A. Suppose e>=0, N>=1, and there are actual elements
t_0,...,t_N of R such that D(t_0)=f^e and the map T_i -> t_i gives the
EXACT A-algebra presentation

    R=A[T_0,...,T_N]/(f^(a_i) T_i-G_i(T_0,...,T_(i-1)) : 1<=i<=N), (1)

where every a_i is a positive integer and G_i belongs to
A[T_0,...,T_(i-1)]. The displayed ideal is the entire kernel. There is
no localization or additional saturation implicit in (1). Neither
monicity nor irreducibility of the individual reduced G_i is assumed.

Write

    s_0=0,   s_i=a_1+...+a_i,   s=s_N.                          (2)

Then e>=s. More precisely, the greatest common divisor in R of all
values D(h), h in R, is, up to a nonzero complex scalar,

    gcd_R D(R)=f^(e-s).                                        (3)

Consequently D is primitive if and only if e=s. Every presentation
satisfying (1) has N<=e, whether or not D is primitive.

Here primitive means that no nonunit of R divides every D(h). Formula
(3) is a statement about a greatest common divisor. It does NOT assert
that the ideal generated by D(R) equals the principal ideal (f^(e-s)).
That ideal need not be principal, even when D is primitive.

The exponent e is attached to the CHOSEN local slice t_0. It is not
assumed to be the exponent of the generator of the plinth ideal
D(R) intersect A. Section 5 gives an elementary control separating them.

## 2. The reduced generic algebra is a separable field

Factorial closure of A in R shows that f is irreducible in R: any
factorization of f in R has both factors in A. Units of both rings are
C*, so a nontrivial factorization would contradict irreducibility in A.
Since R is a UFD, f is prime in R. Moreover

    A intersect fR=fA.                                        (4)

Indeed, if a=fh belongs to A, then 0=D(a)=fD(h), so h belongs to A.

Set C=A/fA. Reduction of the exact presentation (1) gives

    R/fR=B[T_N],
    B=C[T_0,...,T_(N-1)]/(Gbar_1,...,Gbar_N).                  (5)

The final variable is absent from every reduced relation. As R/fR is
a domain, B is a domain. Equation (4) makes the composite C -> B ->
B[T_N] injective. The dimensions are

    dim C=1,   dim(R/fR)=2,   dim B=1.                         (6)

Let K=Frac(C) and L=Frac(B). Both are function fields of integral affine
complex curves. The inclusion K -> L is therefore algebraic, and it
is finite because L is finitely generated over K. In fact

    B_K=B tensor_C K=L.                                       (7)

For B_K is a finitely generated K-domain all of whose generators are
algebraic over K. It is thus a finite-dimensional domain over K, hence
a field with fraction field L. Characteristic zero makes L/K separable.

No global finiteness or flatness of B over C is needed here. In
particular this step does not invoke Kaliman's finite-factor theorem,
miracle flatness, or the monic survivor construction in628. Only the
generic extension (7) is being asserted.

Define, with the derivatives evaluated at the images of the variables,

    J=product_(i=1)^N (partial Gbar_i / partial T_(i-1)) in B.  (8)

Then J is nonzero. To prove this, localize the exact presentation of B
at C minus zero. The resulting presentation of L has N variables
T_0,...,T_(N-1) and the N relations Gbar_1,...,Gbar_N. Its relative
Jacobian matrix over K is lower triangular, with the factors in (8)
on its diagonal.

If that matrix were singular over L, there would be a nonzero column
(v_0,...,v_(N-1)) over L annihilated by it. The K-derivation of the
polynomial ring with values in L defined by T_j -> v_j would vanish
on each displayed relation, and hence on their entire ideal. It would
descend to a nonzero K-derivation L -> L. This is impossible for a finite
separable extension: differentiating the separable minimal polynomial
of each algebraic element shows its derivative is zero. Thus the
Jacobian is invertible over L. Its determinant is J, so J is nonzero
already in B. In particular every factor in (8) is nonzero.

Exactness of (1) is used here as well as in (5). Without it, annihilating
the displayed relations would not necessarily define a derivation of
the actual generic residue field.

## 3. The full derivative recurrence

Define actual elements P_i of R recursively by P_0=1 and

    P_i=sum_(j=0)^(i-1)
        f^(s_(i-1)-s_j) (partial G_i/partial T_j)(t_0,...,t_(i-1)) P_j.
                                                                  (9)

All powers of f in (9) are nonnegative. Differentiating the i-th
relation of (1), and using that D kills its coefficients in A, gives

    f^(a_i) D(t_i)=sum_(j=0)^(i-1) (partial G_i/partial T_j) D(t_j).

Induction on i, after multiplying by f^(s_(i-1)), proves

    f^(s_i) D(t_i)=f^e P_i       for 0<=i<=N.                 (10)

Since every a_h is positive, reduction of (9) modulo f retains only
the term j=i-1. Hence

    P_i mod f=product_(h=1)^i (partial Gbar_h/partial T_(h-1)),
    P_N mod f=J.                                               (11)

These products lie in the embedded domain B in (5). The nonvanishing
of J proves that each P_i is not divisible by f in R, including P_0.

Let ord_f denote the prime-factor valuation in the UFD R. Equation
(10), with P_i not divisible by f, gives

    ord_f D(t_i)=e-s_i.                                       (12)

In particular (10) at i=N cannot hold with e<s: it would force P_N
to be divisible by f after canceling f^e. Therefore e>=s, and every
integer on the right side of (12) is nonnegative.

## 4. Content and primitivity

Now cancellation in (10) gives the polynomial identities

    D(t_i)=f^(e-s_i) P_i.                                    (13)

Every one of these values is divisible by f^(e-s), and D vanishes on
A. The Leibniz rule and the generation R=A[t_0,...,t_N] therefore
show that every value of D is divisible by f^(e-s).

Conversely any irreducible common divisor of all D(h) divides
D(t_0)=f^e, so it must be associated to f. Equation (12) at i=N
shows that its greatest possible exponent is exactly e-s. This proves
(3). It also proves the primitivity equivalence, not merely the
implication from primitivity to e=s.

Equivalently, D/f^(e-s) is a primitive LND of R. This is an actual
derivation by (13) and generation. Because f belongs to ker D,
its iterates are D^j/f^(j(e-s)), so local nilpotence follows from
local nilpotence of D. No conclusion about its exact plinth generator
is required.

## 5. Whole polynomial controls and the two distinct ideals

First use the already published control retained in622, Section 4.
In R=C[x,y,z] put

    r=xz-y^2,   b=r^2-xy,
    D(x)=0,   D(y)=2xr,   D(z)=x+4yr.                         (14)

Then D(r)=x^2 and D(b)=0. Localizing at x gives
R_x=C[x,x^(-1),b,r] with D=x^2 partial_r. In particular D is locally
nilpotent on R. Its actual kernel is A=C[x,b]: modulo x, the map
C[b] -> C[y,z] sends b to y^4 and is injective, so successive
cancellation of x contracts A_x intersect R to A.

There is the exact global presentation

    R=A[T_0,T_1,T_2]/(xT_1-T_0^2+b, xT_2-T_0-T_1^2),        (15)

where T_0=r, T_1=y, T_2=z. The equations eliminate b and T_0,
respectively; the reverse formulas are exactly those in (14), so
the quotient is the entire polynomial ring, not a saturation.
Here a_1=a_2=1, e=2, and

    P_1=2r,   P_2=x+4yr,   J=-4y^3 in R/xR.                 (16)

Thus the content is 1. Nevertheless the ideal generated by D(R)
is proper: both D(y) and D(z), and therefore all values, vanish
at x=y=z=0. This explicitly prevents interpreting (3) as a claim
that the image-generated ideal is principal.

For the chosen-slice distinction, take R=C[x,b,w], D=partial_w,
and the exact three-stage chain

    t_0=b+x^4w,  t_1=x^3w,  t_2=xw,  t_3=w,
    xt_1=t_0-b,  x^2t_2=t_1,  xt_3=t_2.                    (17)

The relations eliminate t_0,t_1,t_2, giving both directions of
the polynomial presentation. The positive exponents are (1,2,1),
so s=e=4, but D has unit plinth because D(w)=1. Thus the theorem
cannot replace this chosen e by a smaller exact plinth exponent
while keeping the same presentation and t_0.

## 6. Dependencies, checks, and scope

The proof is elementary algebra under (1). The cylinder observation
in622 is reused, with its whole exact-presentation premise. The
published source of (14) is Dasgupta--Gaifullin, Example 4.15,
already identified and checked in
[622](global-triangular-presentation-cylinder-obstruction-pass622-working.md).
No new example or rank classification is claimed here.

A bounded archive search found the cylinder and one-generator
obstructions621--622 and the linear two-stage collapse628, but not
this explicit exponent-content formula. That is a limited deduplication
check, not a proof of novelty or a literature-wide absence claim.

Fresh unsaved calculation f63c75 verifies the entire derivation in
(14), both quotient relations, (16), nilpotence on generators, the
gcd and proper image-generated ideal, and all of (17). It also checks
the full recurrence for a three-stage nonlinear symbolic control with
exponents (1,2,1). The uniform proof, not these finite checks,
establishes (3). No saved checker was run, imported, or compiled.

Only exact global chains with powers of the SAME prime f are covered.
Nothing here produces such a chain, bounds the degree of a G_i, or
recognizes an arbitrary invariant kernel or modification. N<=e is
a restriction on this chosen exact presentation, not a general
coordinate-entry result. No cancellation theorem, human expert
certification, formal-proof certification, or novelty clearance is claimed.
