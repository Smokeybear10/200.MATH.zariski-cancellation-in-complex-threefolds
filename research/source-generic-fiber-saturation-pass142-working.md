# The actual generic fiber gives the full compatibility saturation

The three coefficient-compatibility equations recover more than the
selected first-tail factor by a gcd. After their common denominator
is inverted, their quotient ring is exactly the corresponding open
subset of the actual generic affine line. Contracting the ideal gives
the full selected prime ideal, with no first-tail equation needed.

The proof uses the actual projected rational field and its specified
coefficient subfield. It does not show that a formally persistent
component automatically obeys the compatibility equations, and it
does not establish first-tail primitivity or global cancellation.

## 1. The actual source rings

Use the dense-chart source data139, with
\[
\begin{gathered}
F=\operatorname{Frac}A=\mathbb C(y,z,w_0),\qquad
y,z,w_0\text{ algebraically independent over }\mathbb C,\\
L=\mathbb C(\tau,\rho)(e)\subset F,\qquad
F=L(v),\qquad L[y,z]=L[v],\\
\Omega(U,V)\in L[U,V],\qquad w_0=\Omega(y,z),\\
\ker\bigl(L[U,V]\longrightarrow L[v],\ (U,V)\longmapsto(y,z)\bigr)
=(g),\qquad g\text{ irreducible}.
\end{gathered}
\tag{1}
\]
The projected source presentation follows from the actual
localization \(A_s=\mathbb C[y,z,w_0]_{s_0}\); the whole-line
equality and the expression for \(w_0\) are the chart and tail
conclusions139.[^1] In the intended application
\(\tau,\rho\) are the independent speed ratios and
\(L/\mathbb C(\tau,\rho)\) is finite separable, so a primitive
element \(e\) exists. Only the displayed polynomial representative
of \(w_0\), not its particular Taylor formula, enters the ring proof.

Choose common-denominator rational expressions in the actual
source field:
\[
\begin{gathered}
q,p_\tau,p_\rho,p_e\in\mathbb C[\xi_1,\xi_2,\xi_3],
\qquad q\ne0,\qquad
q_0=q(y,z,w_0)\ne0,\\
\alpha=p_\alpha(y,z,w_0)/q_0\quad(\alpha=\tau,\rho,e),\\
R=\mathbb C[y,z,w_0]_{q_0}\subset F,\qquad
S=\mathbb C[\tau,\rho,e]\subset R,\qquad
\operatorname{Frac}S=L.
\end{gathered}
\tag{2}
\]
The nonvanishing of \(q_0\) uses algebraic independence of the
source triple. The ring \(S\) is its actual image inside \(R\);
it is not a freely generated three-variable polynomial ring.

Define the polynomial compatibility data
\[
\begin{gathered}
\Delta(U,V)=q(U,V,\Omega(U,V)),\qquad
\Delta(y,z)=q_0\ne0,\\
A_\alpha(U,V)
=p_\alpha(U,V,\Omega(U,V))-\alpha\Delta(U,V),\\
\mathfrak a=(A_\tau,A_\rho,A_e)\subset L[U,V].
\end{gathered}
\tag{3}
\]
All coefficient values \(\tau,\rho,e\) in these equations are
the specified elements of \(L\). In particular
\(\Delta\ne0,\ g\nmid\Delta\), and \(A_\alpha\in(g)\).

## 2. The exact graph presentation, including relations in \(S\)

Let \(\boldsymbol\xi=(\xi_1,\xi_2,\xi_3)\) be independent
variables, and write \(q=q(\boldsymbol\xi)\) when forming a
localized polynomial ring. There is an isomorphism of
\(S\)-algebras
\[
\begin{gathered}
Q:=S[\boldsymbol\xi]_q/
 \bigl(p_\alpha(\boldsymbol\xi)-\alpha q(\boldsymbol\xi):
             \alpha=\tau,\rho,e\bigr),\\
Q\simeq R,\qquad
\boldsymbol\xi\longmapsto(y,z,w_0).
\end{gathered}
\tag{4}
\]
Here is a direct proof, without ignoring any algebraic relations
among the coefficient generators. Put
\(P=\mathbb C[\boldsymbol\xi]_q\).
The natural map \(P\to Q\) is surjective. Indeed, the quotient
\(Q\) is generated by the images of \(P\) and \(S\), but its
three coefficient generators satisfy
\(\alpha=p_\alpha(\boldsymbol\xi)/q(\boldsymbol\xi)\).
Thus the image of \(P\) already contains all of \(S\).

Actual evaluation defines a homomorphism \(Q\to R\).
Its composite with \(P\to Q\) is the isomorphism \(P\to R\)
coming from algebraic independence of \(y,z,w_0\).
Consequently \(P\to Q\) is injective as well as surjective,
and \(Q\to R\) is its inverse after this identification.
The evaluation is \(S\)-linear, proving (4).

In particular, every relation belonging to the actual ring
\(S=\mathbb C[\tau,\rho,e]\) is already accounted for.
No extra relation is omitted by the three displayed graph
equations, and no polynomial independence of \(\tau,\rho,e\)
was used.

## 3. Base change is the actual generic fiber

Localize (4) at \(S\setminus\{0\}\). Since \(R\) is a domain
containing \(S\), this is an actual subring of \(F\), and gives
\[
\begin{gathered}
L[\boldsymbol\xi]_q/\mathfrak j
 \simeq R\otimes_S L
 =(S\setminus\{0\})^{-1}R\\
=L[y,z,w_0,q_0^{-1}]
=L[v,q_0^{-1}],\\
\mathfrak j=
 \bigl(p_\alpha(\boldsymbol\xi)-\alpha q(\boldsymbol\xi):
             \alpha=\tau,\rho,e\bigr)
 \subset L[\boldsymbol\xi]_q .
\end{gathered}
\tag{5}
\]
Localization commutes with the polynomial presentation and its
quotient. The final equality uses \(L[y,z]=L[v]\) and
\(w_0=\Omega(y,z)\); it is an equality inside \(F\), not just
an abstract isomorphism.

The last ring is the generic affine line with the zeros of
\(q_0(v)\) removed. The element \(q_0\) lies in \(L[v]\) and is
nonzero, but it need not be a unit of \(L[v]\). Thus (5) is
not being called the whole generic line unless the chosen
denominator is already a coefficient-field unit.

The polynomial
\(\xi_3-\Omega(\xi_1,\xi_2)\) evaluates to zero under (5).
Because (5) is the exact presentation, this polynomial belongs
to \(\mathfrak j\) itself, not only to its radical or to some
prime component. Adding it as a generator does not change
the quotient.

Eliminate \(\xi_3\) using this relation. Its substitution sends
\(q(\boldsymbol\xi)\) to \(\Delta(U,V)\) and the three graph
equations to \(A_\tau,A_\rho,A_e\). Therefore
\[
L[U,V]_\Delta/\mathfrak a_\Delta
 \simeq L[v,q_0^{-1}],\qquad
(U,V)\longmapsto(y,z),
\qquad
\mathfrak a_\Delta=(g)_\Delta .
\tag{6}
\]
The final ideal equality follows because the isomorphism is the
specified evaluation map. Before localization that map has
kernel \((g)\) by (1), and \(\Delta\) has the nonzero image
\(q_0\). Localizing its exact kernel gives \((g)_\Delta\).

## 4. Contraction gives full ideal saturation

For an ideal \(I\) in a ring and an element \(d\), write
\(I:d^\infty=\{f:\ d^r f\in I\text{ for some }r\ge0\}\).
Contracting (6) to \(L[U,V]\) yields
\[
\boxed{\quad
(A_\tau,A_\rho,A_e):\Delta^\infty=(g).
\quad}
\qquad
(\Phi,A_\tau,A_\rho,A_e):\Delta^\infty=(g)
\tag{7}
\]
for the actual first tail \(\Phi\), or for any polynomial
\(\Phi\in(g)\).
Indeed \(\mathfrak a_\Delta=(g)_\Delta\), and the prime ideal
\((g)\) is already \(\Delta\)-saturated because \(g\nmid\Delta\).
Adding \(\Phi\) does not change the localized ideal, proving
the second assertion.

This is a full ideal equality. It excludes residual
lower-dimensional or embedded conditions after saturation
under these exact source-ring hypotheses. A factor-only gcd
argument would not by itself justify that stronger conclusion;
the graph presentation (4) supplies the additional information.

In particular there exist an integer \(r\ge0\) and
\(B_\tau,B_\rho,B_e\in L[U,V]\) such that
\[
\Delta^r g=B_\tau A_\tau+B_\rho A_\rho+B_eA_e .
\tag{8}
\]
This is a finite polynomial certificate of the saturation
membership. No bound for \(r\) is claimed. The earlier
factor-stripping exponent \(\deg\Phi\) bounds factor
multiplicities in a gcd; it is not asserted to bound the
scheme-level exponent in (8).

If \(q=1\), then \(\Delta=1\), and already
\(\mathfrak a=(g)\) without saturation. More generally the
localization and contraction in (6)--(7) retain the chosen
denominator explicitly.

## 5. An actual example showing why the primitive element matters

The actual cover family140 gives a uniform illustration.[^3]
For \(n\ge2\), its projected source coordinates satisfy
\(a=y-(z-w_0)w_0\), \(b=z-w_0\), with
\(L=\mathbb C(a,b)\), \(\tau=b/a^n\), \(\rho=1/a^n\), and
\(e=a\). In independent source variables put
\(a_0=\xi_1-(\xi_2-\xi_3)\xi_3\),
\(b_0=\xi_2-\xi_3\). One can take
\[
\begin{gathered}
q=a_0^n,\quad p_\tau=b_0,\quad p_\rho=1,\quad
p_e=a_0^{n+1},\qquad \Omega(U,V)=V-b,\\
\widehat a=U-bV+b^2,\quad \Delta=\widehat a^n,\qquad
(A_\tau,A_\rho)_\Delta=(\widehat a^n-a^n)_\Delta,\\
A_e=\widehat a^n(\widehat a-a),\qquad
(A_\tau,A_\rho,A_e)_\Delta=(\widehat a-a)_\Delta .
\end{gathered}
\tag{9}
\]
The first source substitution gives \(a_0\mapsto\widehat a\)
and \(b_0\mapsto b\), proving every identity in (9).
The pair \((\widehat a,V)\) is a coordinate pair over \(L\).
Over this field, the two base equations alone therefore leave
the \(n\) distinct choices \(\widehat a=\zeta a\),
\(\zeta^n=1\). None is removed by inverting \(\Delta\).
The equation for \(e\) selects exactly \(\widehat a=a\).

This is an actual-frame example where the primitive element is
needed to present the full specified coefficient-field fiber from
the source equations alone. It is not an extra-factor example
for the first tail: that family's actual
\(\Phi=a^n(\widehat a-a)\) is already primitive. In particular,
no failure of the base equations together with \(\Phi\) is
being claimed for this example.

## 6. Exact scope of the strengthening

The compatibility equations recover the actual generic
line scheme without including its first-tail multiple.
Thus this result strengthens codimension-one factor selection
to an exact generic-fiber presentation.

It does not prove that \(\Phi\) itself generates \((g)\).
Although all \(A_\alpha\) vanish on the selected component,
they have not been shown to vanish on every component of
\(\Phi\). The full formal persistence theorem141 does not
identify a component's rational substitution with the
specified coefficient embedding used in (2)--(5).[^2]

The ring argument needs the actual rational source presentation,
the actual coefficient subfield, and the whole-line equality
in (1). It neither infers those data from a relative Jacobian
alone nor proves a polynomial inverse for a new formal component.
No global polynomial presentation of \(A\), arbitrary
coordinate-entry result, or cancellation conclusion is claimed.
