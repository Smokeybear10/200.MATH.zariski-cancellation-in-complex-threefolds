# The quadratic graph in the cubic repeated-root chart is impossible

In the supplied actual chart \(q=\rho^2-t^3\), the branch
\((\deg_v y,\deg_v\theta)=(1,2)\) cannot satisfy the calibrated
nonzero constant determinant. Its complete coefficient identities
force that determinant, in the affine parameter \(y\), to equal
\(-c\,l(y)/(4A)\), where \(l\) has degree one and \(A\ne0\).
If \(c\ne0\) this is nonconstant; if \(c=0\) it is zero.

This is a working exclusion of one branch of one supplied frame.
It does not exclude the entire cubic model or solve cancellation.

## 1. Exact hypotheses and the graph cofactor

Use the actual rebuilt cubic chart of
[237](repeated-root-rebuilt-chart-line-exclusion-pass237-working.md),
with full initial kernel \((X^2Z-W^2+XY^3)\), its literal \(Z\) pivot,
and the complete identities in
[239](radical-minus-polynomial-centered-eliminant-pass239-working.md).
In particular \(L\) is the exact finite separable extension of
\(\mathbb C(t,\rho)\), all coefficient partials commute, and
\[
\begin{gathered}
Y=y+tX,\quad Z=z+\sigma X+qX^2,\quad W=w+\theta X+\rho X^2,\quad
q=\rho^2-t^3,\\
L[y,z,w]=L[v],\quad J(X)=J_0=-a^3\in L^*,\\
R=2\theta+k_\rho\ne0,\qquad
\deg_v y=1,\quad \deg_v\theta=2 .
\end{gathered}                                                     \tag{1}
\]
Use \(z_0\) for a formal centered plane variable, distinct from
the projected coordinate \(z\). Put
\[
\kappa=k_\rho,\quad c=k_{\rho\rho},\quad
z_0=V+\kappa/2,\qquad
\theta+\kappa/2=P(y),\qquad P(U)=AU^2+BU+C,\quad A\in L^* .
\tag{2}
\]
Thus \(R=2P(y)\), and the centered plane kernel is generated by
\(G=z_0-P(U)\).

The actual cofactor identities and the \(i\le2\) conclusion of
[238](repeated-root-return-excess-order-bound-pass238-working.md)
give \(q_1\mid R\). Because \(G_{z_0}=1\), the gradient identity
also gives \(R\mid q_1\). Hence \(q_1=\eta R\) for \(\eta\in L^*\).
The full centered eliminant, denoted \(H\), therefore has the form
\[
H=G^2M+2\eta z_0G .                                               \tag{3}
\]
Indeed \(H=GQ_1\), and \(Q_1-2\eta z_0\) vanishes on the graph,
so it is divisible by \(G\).
The leading part \(H_6=U^6\) forces \(\deg M=2\) and
\[
M_2=U^2/A^2 .                                                     \tag{4}
\]
There is no \(Uz_0\) or \(z_0^2\) term in \(M_2\).

The centered expression from239 satisfies
\[
H(U,0)=p(U)^2,\quad [z_0]H=0,\quad
p(U)=-U^3+\frac{k_{tt}}2U^2+
       \left(n_t-\frac{\kappa k_{t\rho}}2\right)U+
       \frac{\kappa^2c}{8}-\frac{\kappa n_\rho}{2}-\ell .
\tag{5}
\]
Putting \(z_0=0\) in (3) gives \(P^2M(U,0)=p^2\).
Unique factorization in \(L[U]\) implies \(P\mid p\).
Write \(p=Pl\), where \(l=l_1U+l_0\) has degree exactly one.
Then \(M(U,0)=l^2\), and (4) shows
\[
M=l^2+e z_0,\qquad
Pe=2l^2+2\eta .                                                   \tag{6}
\]
The second identity is precisely the vanishing of the coefficient
of \(z_0\) in (3), divided by the nonzero polynomial \(P\).

Comparing the leading coefficients in \(p=Pl\), then the two
highest coefficients in (6), gives
\[
l_1=-1/A,\qquad e=2/A^3,\qquad
l_0=-B/(2A^2),\qquad B=-Ak_{tt}/3 .
\tag{7}
\]
The last equality uses \([U^2]p=k_{tt}/2\).
Here \(e\) is a coefficient in \(L\), not an original leading
coordinate coefficient.

## 2. Raw coefficients force the leading graph coefficient to be constant

From (3) and (6), the coefficients of \(z_0^3\) and \(z_0^2\) are
\[
[z_0^3]H=e,\qquad
[z_0^2]H=l^2-2eP+2\eta .
\tag{8}
\]
In particular the latter has degree two, with coefficients
\[
[U^2z_0^2]H=-3/A^2,\qquad
[Uz_0^2]H=-3B/A^3=k_{tt}/A^2 .
\tag{9}
\]
Every coefficient of \(z_0^4\) and \(z_0^5\) is zero.

Compare these identities with the complete centered polynomial239,
retaining all lower potentials. Its coefficients of \(z_0^5\),
\(Uz_0^4\), \(z_0^4\), \(U^2z_0^3\), and \(Uz_0^3\), in that order,
give
\[
\begin{gathered}
k_{\rho\rho\rho}=k_{t\rho\rho}=0,\quad
c_\rho=c_t=0,\quad n_{\rho\rho}=3c^2/8,\\
k_{tt\rho}=0,\qquad n_{t\rho}=c\,k_{t\rho}/2 .
\end{gathered}                                                     \tag{10}
\]
Thus, on writing \(d=k_{t\rho}\), both \(d_t\) and \(d_\rho\)
are zero. These are consequences, not initial constant-coefficient
assumptions.

The coefficient of \(U^3z_0^2\) gives
\[
\frac23k_{ttt}+c=0 .
\tag{11}
\]
After (10), the coefficient of \(U^2z_0^2\) is
\[
2n_{tt}-d^2-\frac c2k_{tt}=-3/A^2 .
\tag{12}
\]
Differentiate this equality with respect to \(\rho\).
The derivative of its left side is zero: \(n_{tt\rho}
=\partial_t(cd/2)=0\), and all remaining derivatives vanish by (10).
Since \(A\ne0\), its right side gives
\[
A_\rho=0 .                                                       \tag{13}
\]

The scalar coefficient of \(z_0^3\), simplified only using (10), is
\[
e=\frac{c^2\kappa}{4}-4\ell_\rho-2c n_\rho .
\tag{14}
\]
The coefficient of \(Uz_0^2\), using the same identities, is
\[
-4\ell_t-2d n_\rho-c n_t+\frac{c\kappa d}{2}
       =\frac{k_{tt}}{A^2}.
\tag{15}
\]
The \(\rho\)-derivative of the left side of (15) is
\[
-4\ell_{t\rho}-\frac{3c^2d}{4},
\]
which is exactly the \(t\)-derivative of the right-hand expression
for \(e\) in (14). The \(\rho\)-derivative of the right side of
(15) is zero by (10) and (13). Consequently \(e_t=0\).
Since \(e=2/A^3\), this proves
\[
A_t=0 .                                                          \tag{16}
\]
All differentiations here apply to identities between elements
of \(L\), after the complete coefficient-dependent centering.
The centering derivative is not omitted. No integration or
choice of rational primitives in \(L\) is needed.

## 3. Derivatives of the quadratic graph and the projected intercept

Use \(h=y\) as an affine parameter of \(L[v]\). In this section
extend the coefficient partials to \(L[h]\) fixing \(h\).
This is a parameter change solely for calculating the determinant.
Since \(y_v\in L^*\), that determinant is \(J_0/y_v\), still a
nonzero element of \(L\), independent of \(h\).
No new original ambient coordinate or newly normalized \(N\) is asserted.

Equations (7), (10), (11), (13), and (16) imply
\[
B_\rho=0,\quad B_t=Ac/2,\quad
l=-h/A-B/(2A^2),\quad l_t=-c/(4A),\quad l_\rho=0 .
\tag{17}
\]
Put \(p_1=n_t-\kappa d/2\), the coefficient of \(U\) in \(p\).
The coefficient of \(U\) in \(p=Pl\) gives
\[
C=-A p_1-\frac{B^2}{2A}.
\tag{18}
\]
Its \(\rho\)-derivative is zero by (10).
Also
\[
(p_1)_t=n_{tt}-d^2/2
       =\frac c4k_{tt}-\frac{3}{2A^2}
\]
by (12). Differentiating (18) and using (17) therefore yields
\[
C_\rho=0,\qquad C_t=cB/4+3/(2A).
\]
It follows that
\[
P_\rho=0,\qquad
P_t=Ach/2+cB/4+3/(2A)
     =-\frac{A^2c}{2}l+\frac{3}{2A},\qquad
P_h=-2A^2l .
\tag{19}
\]

The exact centered third potential from239 is
\[
R(w-D)=-p(h),\qquad
D=-\frac d2h-\frac{n_\rho}{2}
          -\frac c4\theta+\frac{c\kappa}{8}.
\]
As \(R=2P(h)\) and \(p(h)=P(h)l(h)\), polynomial cancellation
in the line ring gives
\[
w=D-l/2,\qquad \theta=P-\kappa/2 .
\tag{20}
\]
Differentiating (20) with \(h\) fixed, and using all the identities
above, gives
\[
\begin{gathered}
\theta_t=P_t-d/2,\quad \theta_\rho=-c/2,\\
w_t=-cP_t/4+c/(8A),\qquad w_\rho=c^2/16 .
\end{gathered}                                                     \tag{21}
\]
For example, the terms containing \(cd\) in \(w_t\) cancel between
\(-n_{\rho t}/2\), \(-c\theta_t/4\), and \(c\kappa_t/8\).
The value of \(w_\rho\) uses \(n_{\rho\rho}=3c^2/8\).

## 4. The calibrated scalar determinant cannot survive

In the actual reduced row identity of universal184, set
\[
B_1=f_t(h,\theta)=-3h^2+k_{tt}h+d\theta+n_t,\qquad
C_1=f_\rho(h,\theta)+2w .
\]
The coefficient partials in these definitions act before evaluation.
The total differentials, however, can be computed in the coordinates
\((t,\rho,h)\). At \(X=0\) the three reduced rows are
\(dh,\ R\,d\theta+B_1dt+C_1d\rho,\ dw\).
Their determinant is
\[
J_{\rm new}=(R\theta_t+B_1)w_\rho
                   -(R\theta_\rho+C_1)w_t=J_0/y_v\in L^* .
\tag{22}
\]
Direct substitution of (19)--(21) gives
\[
\begin{gathered}
R\theta_t+B_1=2PP_t+p'(h),\qquad
p'(h)=P_hl+Pl_h=-2A^2l^2-P/A,\\
C_1=cP/2-l,\qquad R\theta_\rho+C_1=-cP/2-l .
\end{gathered}
\]
Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
J_{\rm new}
&=\left(2PP_t-2A^2l^2-P/A\right)\frac{c^2}{16}
 -\left(-cP/2-l\right)
                  \left(-cP_t/4+c/(8A)\right)\\
&=\boxed{-\frac{c\,l}{4A}} .
\end{aligned}                                                     \tag{23}
\]
The last simplification uses \(P_t=-A^2cl/2+3/(2A)\);
all terms involving \(P\) and \(l^2\) cancel.

Since \(l=-h/A-B/(2A^2)\), the coefficient of \(h\) in (23)
is \(c/(4A^2)\). A nonzero scalar in \(L\) cannot have this
nonzero coefficient. Thus (22) would force \(c=0\), but then
(23) is zero, contradicting (22) again.
This excludes the entire \((1,2)\) branch under (1).

## 5. Verification boundary

The argument uses the complete centered polynomial, the actual
cofactor divisor, and the nonzero determinant, not an arbitrary
formal parametrized curve. Coefficient partials are distinguished
from differentiation after evaluation, and the affine parameter
change is used only with its explicit determinant factor.

The cubic graph of degree three and higher projected-degree pairs
are not treated here. The full cubic model, arbitrary-frame entry,
unrestricted cancellation, historical novelty and expert review
remain unresolved. Independent coefficient calculations and AI
reviews support this written working argument but do not establish
those stronger claims.
