# Rank-one semigroup-minimal actual translation controls, pass 565

## 1. Result and scope

For every integer \(n\ge4\), the translation derivation on a polynomial
fourfold has an actual tame coordinate frame with positive degree tuple
\((n,n+1,n+2,n+3)\), initial-field rank one, and no degree in the additive
semigroup generated by the other three. Nevertheless, one polynomial
elementary replacement recovers the slice. This is an actual realization
of the tuple \((4,5,6,7)\), not just a polynomial embedding of one orbit
or an assigned collection of initial forms.

The exact coordinate-partial shifts are also computed. For \(n=4\) they
are \((11,10,9,5)\), not the extremal assigned tuple
\((15,14,13,12)\) used in
[562](rank-sensitive-cofactors-and-rank-one-semigroup-boundary-pass562-working.md).
Thus this control realizes its degree tuple but does not realize all of
its abstract numerical data.

The distinction between a monomial replacement and a polynomial
replacement is essential. The semigroup condition prevents a descending
replacement by a single monomial in the other coordinates. It does not
prevent cancellation between two higher-source-weight monomials. The
frames below are explicitly not globally minimizing frames. No arbitrary
coordinate-entry, cancellation, or historical novelty claim is made.

## 2. Four elementary shears and the complete inverse

Work in
\[
B=\mathbb C[a,b,c,u]=A[u],\qquad
A=\mathbb C[a,b,c],\qquad E=\partial_u.
\tag{1}
\]
All degrees in this note are degrees in \(u\) over \(A\), equivalently
actual \(E\)-degrees. Fix an integer \(n\ge4\), and define
\[
\begin{aligned}
X_1&=a+u^n,\\
X_2&=b+u^{n+1},\\
X_3&=c+u^{n+2}+u^3,\\
X_4&=u+X_2^2-X_1X_3.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
These expressions are a composition of four actual elementary
automorphisms. First add the displayed polynomials in the unchanged
coordinate \(u\) to \(a,b,c\), one coordinate at a time. Then add
\(X_2^2-X_1X_3\) to the remaining coordinate \(u\). Each inverse
subtracts the same polynomial while fixing the other coordinates.
In particular the composite has Jacobian determinant one.

For a complete inverse expressed in the new coordinates, put
\[
U=X_4-X_2^2+X_1X_3.
\tag{3}
\]
Then the inverse is
\[
u=U,\qquad
a=X_1-U^n,\qquad
b=X_2-U^{n+1},\qquad
c=X_3-U^{n+2}-U^3.
\tag{4}
\]
Both compositions can be checked without localization. Substitution of
(2) into (3) gives \(U=u\); (4) then recovers each of \(a,b,c\).
Conversely, substitute (4) into (2). The first three new coordinates
become exactly \(X_1,X_2,X_3\). The fourth becomes
\(U+X_2^2-X_1X_3=X_4\). These identities prove invertibility on the
entire fourfold, not merely on a dense open set or one fiber.

## 3. Exact degrees and the full initial prime

Expansion of the fourth coordinate gives the uniform identity
\[
X_4=-u^{n+3}-a u^{n+2}+2b u^{n+1}-c u^n-a u^3+b^2-ac+u.
\tag{5}
\]
The two terms of degree \(2n+2\) have canceled. Since \(n\ge4\),
every exponent on the right other than \(n+3\) is strictly smaller
than \(n+3\). Consequently
\[
(d_1,d_2,d_3,d_4)=(n,n+1,n+2,n+3),\qquad
(c_1,c_2,c_3,c_4)=(1,1,1,-1).
\tag{6}
\]
Here \(c_i\) means the leading coefficient in \(u\), not the old
coordinate \(c\).

Use independent source variables \(Z_1,Z_2,Z_3,Z_4\), and rescale only
the fourth actual coordinate by the complex constant \(-1\). The
initial map for the rescaled frame is
\[
\vartheta:\mathbb C[Z_1,Z_2,Z_3,Z_4]\longrightarrow A[\eta],
\qquad
(Z_1,Z_2,Z_3,Z_4)\longmapsto
(\eta^n,\eta^{n+1},\eta^{n+2},\eta^{n+3}).
\tag{7}
\]
Its image is exactly
\(\mathbb C[\eta^n,\eta^{n+1},\eta^{n+2},\eta^{n+3}]\).
Thus its initial field has transcendence degree one over \(\mathbb C\).
Since consecutive degrees have greatest common divisor one, that field
is \(\mathbb C(\eta)\); for example \(\eta=\eta^{n+1}/\eta^n\).

The **full** initial prime, not a selected factor or one chosen relation,
is
\[
I_n=\left(
Z^\alpha-Z^\beta:
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^4,
\ \sum_i d_i\alpha_i=\sum_i d_i\beta_i
\right).
\tag{8}
\]
Indeed, collect the monomials of an arbitrary source polynomial by
their weight \(\sum_i d_i\alpha_i\). Its image under (7) vanishes
if and only if the sum of coefficients in every weight block is zero.
Each such block is a linear combination of the binomials in (8), by
subtracting one reference monomial in that block. This proves both
inclusions in (8), including all relations of the map. The image is a
one-dimensional domain, so \(I_n\) is prime of height three. For the
unrescaled frame, the full initial prime is its pullback under
\(Z_4\mapsto-Z_4\).

## 4. Semigroup minimality and its precise consequence

All four degrees are distinct and belong to the interval
\([n,2n-1]\), since \(n+3\le2n-1\) for \(n\ge4\). A sum of two
or more positive degrees from this tuple is at least \(2n\).
A sum with just one summand is another, distinct degree. It follows
that
\[
d_j\notin
\left\{\sum_{i\ne j}\beta_i d_i:
\beta_i\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\right\}
\quad\text{for every }j.
\tag{9}
\]
The zero sum is also different from every \(d_j>0\). Together with
the greatest-common-divisor calculation, this is a minimal set of
four generators of the numerical semigroup it generates.

For clarity, consider an actual single-monomial elementary replacement
\[
X_j\longmapsto
\gamma X_j+\lambda\prod_{i\ne j}X_i^{\beta_i},
\qquad \gamma\in\mathbb C^*,\quad\lambda\in\mathbb C.
\tag{10}
\]
When \(\lambda\ne0\), the added monomial has exact \(E\)-degree
\(\sum_{i\ne j}\beta_i d_i\): its leading coefficient is a product
of nonzero constants, so it does not vanish. A degree decrease in
(10) would require that degree to equal \(d_j\), which (9) forbids.
The cases \(\lambda=0\) and a constant monomial cannot lower a
positive degree either. Thus none of the four coordinates can be
lowered by this single-monomial test. This conclusion does not concern
an arbitrary polynomial in the other coordinates.

## 5. Entire transported partials and exact all-ring shifts

Let \(T_i=\partial/\partial X_i\) be the actual coordinate partials
transported to \(A[u]\). Define the following polynomials in the
old slice:
\[
P_n=n u^{n-1},\qquad Q_n=(n+1)u^n,\qquad
R_n=(n+2)u^{n+1}+3u^2.
\tag{11}
\]
Differentiating the whole inverse (3)--(4), not just its leading terms,
gives the complete table. Entries containing \(X_j\) mean the actual
polynomials in (2).

| Partial | \(T_i(a)\) | \(T_i(b)\) | \(T_i(c)\) | \(T_i(u)\) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| \(T_1\) | \(1-P_nX_3\) | \(-Q_nX_3\) | \(-R_nX_3\) | \(X_3\) |
| \(T_2\) | \(2P_nX_2\) | \(1+2Q_nX_2\) | \(2R_nX_2\) | \(-2X_2\) |
| \(T_3\) | \(-P_nX_1\) | \(-Q_nX_1\) | \(1-R_nX_1\) | \(X_1\) |
| \(T_4\) | \(-P_n\) | \(-Q_n\) | \(-R_n\) | \(1\) |

Equivalently, if
\[
(v_1,v_2,v_3,v_4)=(X_3,-2X_2,X_1,1),
\tag{12}
\]
then
\[
T_i=\bigl(\delta_{1i}-P_n v_i\bigr)\partial_a
   +\bigl(\delta_{2i}-Q_n v_i\bigr)\partial_b
   +\bigl(\delta_{3i}-R_n v_i\bigr)\partial_c
   +v_i\partial_u.
\tag{13}
\]
These are commuting locally nilpotent derivations because they are the
actual partial derivatives of the polynomial coordinate frame (2).
No formal leading-field substitution is being used to establish this.

The exact degrees of the four \(v_i\) are
\(n+2,n+1,n,0\). The coefficient of \(\partial_c\) in each column
has degree exactly \(n+1+\deg_u v_i\), strictly above the coefficients
of \(\partial_a\) and \(\partial_b\). Its leading coefficient is
nonzero; the Kronecker constant cannot cancel it. Therefore the
horizontal shifts and their leading fields on \(A\) are
\[
\begin{aligned}
(k_1,k_2,k_3,k_4)&=(2n+3,2n+2,2n+1,n+1),\\
(N_1,N_2,N_3,N_4)
&=\bigl(-(n+2),\ 2(n+2),\ -(n+2),\ -(n+2)\bigr)\partial_c.
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
\]
Here \(N_i\) is defined by taking the coefficient of \(u^{k_i}\)
in \(T_i|_A\). The full leading family has generic rank one and
common kernel \(\mathbb C[a,b]\) inside \(A\).

These are also exact shifts on the entire polynomial ring. For any
nonzero \(F\in A[u]\), the horizontal terms in (13) have degree at
most \(\deg_u F+k_i\). The vertical term has degree at most
\(\deg_u F-1+\deg_u v_i\), whenever that term is nonzero, and this
is no larger. Thus, with \(\deg 0=-\infty\),
\[
\deg_E(T_iF)\le\deg_E F+k_i\quad(F\in B).
\tag{15}
\]
Equality is attained on the invariant coordinate \(F=c\), whose
degree is zero. This proves exactness, rather than only an upper
bound for the four shifts. The vertical terms in the table are
retained throughout.

For \(n=4\), (6) and (14) specialize to
\[
(d_i)=(4,5,6,7),\qquad
(k_i)=(11,10,9,5),\qquad
(N_i)=(-6,12,-6,-6)\partial_c.
\tag{16}
\]
In particular the rank-one bound of 562 is not attained in every
column here. Its extremal assigned costs \(d_i+k_i=19\) for this
degree tuple are different from the actual costs \((15,15,15,12)\)
in (16). No extremal-shift realization is inferred.

## 6. Higher-source-weight cancellation gives an actual descent

In the source coordinate ring the polynomial
\[
H=X_2^2-X_1X_3
\tag{17}
\]
has weight \(2n+2\) for the weights (6). Its two monomials have
the same weight, and their initial images cancel. Their full actual
evaluation is
\[
H=-u^{n+3}-a u^{n+2}+2b u^{n+1}-c u^n-a u^3+b^2-ac.
\tag{18}
\]
It has degree exactly \(n+3<2n+2\). Since \(X_4=u+H\), the
actual elementary replacement
\[
X_4\longmapsto X_4-X_2^2+X_1X_3=u
\tag{19}
\]
lowers its degree from \(n+3\) to one, fixing the other three
coordinates. Its polynomial inverse adds \(X_2^2-X_1X_3\).
The added polynomial is not a single monomial of weight \(n+3\);
it is a cancellation between two monomials of larger source weight.
This is exactly why (9) does not obstruct (19).

After (19), subtract \(u^n\), \(u^{n+1}\), and \(u^{n+2}+u^3\)
from the first, second, and third coordinates, respectively.
This gives the actual frame \((a,b,c,u)\). Every step is elementary
and polynomial. The degree sum of the original frame is
\[
S_n=4n+6\longrightarrow\infty,
\tag{20}
\]
whereas its value after (19) is \(3n+4\), and the final frame has
degree sum one. The global minimum for this translation is exactly
one: the adapted frame attains it, while degree sum zero would put
every polynomial generator in \(\ker E\) and force \(E=0\).
Consequently none of the frames (2) is a globally minimizing frame.

## 7. Boundary and verification record

The family proves actual realizability of the rank-one semigroup-minimal
degree tuples in (6), with a complete inverse and full transported
derivations. It rules out using semigroup minimality alone as a test
for the absence of a degree-lowering polynomial elementary move.
It does not contradict the sufficient single-monomial lowering
criterion in 562, nor does it describe a globally minimal frame of
an arbitrary sliced derivation.

The two retained pass-124 sources were read completely:
[elementary descent obstruction](coordinate-degree-elementary-descent-obstruction-pass124.md)
and [stabilized Nagata bypass](nagata-stabilized-degree-bypass-pass124.md).
Their actual-ring distinction between a polynomial identity and a
coordinate replacement is respected here. Unlike the Nagata frames
in that first note, the present frames do admit the immediate
polynomial elementary decrease (19). No theorem about Nagata maps,
ordinary spatial multidegrees, or polynomial embeddings is required
for the identities above.

The proofs of both inverse orders, all degrees, the entire initial
prime, and the exact shifts are uniform in every integer \(n\ge4\).
Fresh unsaved algebra additionally checked the complete inverse
compositions and degree/shift data for \(n=4,5,6,8\), and the whole
Jacobian and inverse-Jacobian tables for \(n=4\). These checks support
the displayed proofs; sample calculations are not used to establish
the uniform result. No saved checker was executed, imported, or
compiled, and no canonical record or earlier source was edited.
