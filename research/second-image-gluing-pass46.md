# The second image ideal is the exact quadratic equation gate

After the complete divisor classification, one ideal measures whether
the local quadratic equations can be replaced by a single global
quadratic equation using the actual minimal local slice. That ideal
is the whole invariant ring exactly when the second image ideal is
principal. The possible obstruction is confined to finitely many
points on the plinth divisor.

This is a conditional equation-recognition result, not a proof that
the ideal is principal and not a recognition of the resulting
hypersurface as affine three-space. The relevant literature treats
higher image ideals as a substantive additional problem.

## 1. Actual hypotheses and the second image ideal

Retain the actual cylinder and primitive intrinsic action of
[the minimal-slice theorem](minimal-quadratic-slice-pass46.md):
\[
A[s_*]\simeq\mathbb C^{[4]},\quad
C=\ker D\simeq\mathbb C^{[2]},\quad
\operatorname{pl}(D)=pC,\quad Dr=p.
\tag{1}
\]
Here \(C\subset A\) is factorially closed and faithfully flat,
and \(A\) is a UFD. In the remaining degree-two frame, the
preceding proofs give a quadratic full residue cover at every
prime factor of \(p\), followed by its full DVR presentation.
The already-recognized polynomial alternatives are not being
reclassified in this note.

Write \(L=\operatorname{Frac}C\), so \(A_L=L[r]\). Define
\[
F_i=\ker(D^{i+1}:A\to A),\qquad
I_2=D^2(A)\cap C.
\tag{2}
\]
These are the standard LND filtration and second image ideal.
The exact full plinth gives
\[
F_1=C\oplus Cr.
\tag{3}
\]
Indeed, if \(Dz\in C\), then \(Dz=ap\) for some \(a\in C\),
and \(z-ar\in C\). The sum is direct because \(Dr\ne0\).

For any \(c=D^2z\in C\), one has \(Dz\in F_1\).
Its derivative therefore belongs to \(pC\), by (3). Hence
\[
I_2\subset pC,\qquad I_2=pJ
\tag{4}
\]
for a uniquely determined ideal \(J\subset C\). In addition,
\(D^2(r^2)=2p^2\), so
\[
p\in J.
\tag{5}
\]
All factors and multiplicities in (4) concern the primitive full
plinth, not a selected nonminimal slice value.

## 2. All remaining support of J has codimension two

Invariant localization commutes with \(F_i\) and with \(I_2\):
one can clear the invariant denominator of a preimage under
\(D^2\), and \(A_R\cap L=R\). For every irreducible
\(\pi\in C\), put \(R=C_{(\pi)}\).

If \(p\in R^*\), then \(A_R=R[r/p]\) and integration
gives \((I_2)_R=R=pR\). If \(\pi\mid p\), the complete
local model has
\[
A_R=R[T,Z],\quad \pi^nZ=T^2-\eta,\quad
DT=\pi^n,\quad DZ=2T,
\tag{6}
\]
after multiplying the actual slice by an invariant unit.
In particular \(D^2Z=2\pi^n\). Together with (4), this gives
\((I_2)_R=pR\). Thus in all cases
\[
J_{(\pi)}=C_{(\pi)}.
\tag{7}
\]
Consequently either \(J=C\), or \(C/J\) is supported at a
finite set of closed points. Equation (5) places those points
on \(V(p)\). This does not prove that the set is empty.

There is also an exact filtration interpretation:
\[
F_2/F_1\simeq I_2=pJ,
\tag{8}
\]
with the isomorphism induced by \(D^2\). Surjectivity follows
from the definition of \(I_2\): if \(D^2z\in C\), then
\(D^3z=0\), so \(z\in F_2\).

No projectivity or freeness theorem is needed for (4)--(8).
Even knowing that the two modules on the left of the exact
sequence are free would not make their quotient free: over
\(\mathbb C[u,v]\), the map
\((a,b)\mapsto ua+vb\) has image \((u,v)\), while its
kernel is the free module generated by \((-v,u)\).
That module example is not asserted to arise from the actual
frame or from its image ideal.

## 3. Exact equivalence with one global quadratic equation

Under the preceding hypotheses, the following are equivalent:

1. \(J=C\).
2. \(I_2=pC\), equivalently \(I_2\) is principal.
3. There are \(Y\in A\) and \(a,b\in C\) such that
   \[
   A=C[r,Y]\simeq
   C[\mathsf T,\mathsf Y]/
   (p\mathsf Y-\mathsf T^2-a\mathsf T-b),
   \qquad DY=2r+a.
   \tag{9}
   \]

The equivalence of the first two statements follows from (4)
and (7). If \(I_2\) is generated by one polynomial, its
valuation at every prime is that of \(p\); unique factorization
makes its generator a constant-unit multiple of \(p\).

Assume \(I_2=pC\), and choose \(Y\in A\) with
\(D^2Y=2p\). By (3),
\(DY=2r+a\) for some \(a\in C\). Therefore
\[
D(pY-r^2-ar)=0.
\]
There is \(b\in C\) with
\[
pY=r^2+ar+b.
\tag{10}
\]
It remains to prove that these two elements generate all of
\(A\), rather than only a subalgebra with the same generic ring.

Set \(H=C[r,Y]\). For each prime \(\pi\mid p\), reduction
of (10) is a monic quadratic equation for \(\bar r\).
The actual minimality argument gives
\(\bar r\notin\kappa\), so that quadratic is exactly its
irreducible minimal polynomial over \(\kappa\). Its derivative
\(2\bar r+\bar a\) is a nonzero member of the residue field.
Thus the proof of the minimal-slice theorem applies directly
to the already supplied \(Y\): its residue is a parameter
of the full residue line, and residue injection plus saturation
gives
\[
H_{C_{(\pi)}}=A_{C_{(\pi)}}.
\tag{11}
\]
If \(\pi\nmid p\), the same equality follows immediately
from \(A_{C_{(\pi)}}=C_{(\pi)}[r]\) and (10).

The global presentation of \(H\) in (9) is exact. The relation
is primitive and irreducible over the UFD \(C\), is linear
in \(\mathsf Y\) over \(L\), and is monic in
\(\mathsf T\). Its quotient embeds in \(L[r]\), with
free \(C\)-module basis
\[
\{Y^k,rY^k:k\ge0\}.
\tag{12}
\]
One can also check independence directly in \(L[r]\): these
elements have distinct degrees \(2k\) and \(2k+1\) in
\(r\), with nonzero leading coefficients.

For \(z\in A\subset H_L=L[r]\), expand it in the basis
(12), with finitely many coefficients in \(L\). Equality
(11) puts each coefficient in every \(C_{(\pi)}\).
Since \(C\) is a UFD, their intersection inside \(L\)
is \(C\). Thus all coefficients belong to \(C\), and
\(z\in H\). This proves \(A=H\).

Conversely, (9) gives \(D^2Y=2p\), so \(p\in I_2\).
Together with (4), it gives \(I_2=pC\). This completes
all implications, including the full global generation gate.

## 4. What the supplied frame has not established

The actual-frame arguments prove (7), not \(J=C\). They
therefore remove every divisorial obstruction to (9), but do
not remove a possible obstruction at closed points of the
plinth curve. The first-image support theorem in
[pass 44](compatible-slice-support-pass44.md) concerns
\(A/D(A)\), not \(A/D^2(A)\) or the quotient of
\(A/D(A)\) by the image of \(C\). Applying that theorem
to the latter modules would require an additional proof.

Even if (9) is established, it is a hypersurface presentation
over the invariant plane, not three polynomial coordinates
for \(A\). A further recognition argument using its actual
coefficients or the supplied ambient frame would still be
required. The present equivalence does not assert that affine
space is characterized by quadratic generic divisor covers.

The earlier [exact second-image computation](primitive-noninvariant-bridge-pass21.md#3-the-complete-second-image-ideal)
is for a specified Freudenburg family. It does not prove
principality for an arbitrary intrinsic action on an unknown
stably polynomial threefold.

## 5. Primary-source comparison and verification boundary

Dasgupta and Lahiri's
[*On image ideals of nice and quasi-nice derivations over a UFD*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.13787v3),
version 3 dated 9 December 2023, was read directly: abstract,
Introduction, definitions, Section 3's filtration setup, and
Theorems 3.5 and 3.17 with their stated hypotheses. The
Introduction calls principality of all image ideals for LNDs
on an actual \(k^{[3]}\) Freudenburg's Freeness Conjecture.
The paper treats specified relative nice and quasi-nice
derivations on polynomial planes over UFDs or PIDs, with
additional hypotheses in the principal cases. Those coordinate,
rank and base hypotheses have not been supplied for the present
intrinsic \(D\). Its results therefore do not imply \(J=C\)
here. This is a statement audit of that primary version, not
a claim to have settled the conjecture's subsequent status.

The terminology and filtration are inherited image-ideal
mechanisms. The conditional equivalence above uses the newly
proved local models and elementary coefficient contraction.
No new freeness theorem, historical novelty, arbitrary-frame
existence, or unrestricted cancellation conclusion is claimed.
No extra numerical suite is introduced for this ideal-theoretic
equivalence.
