# Whole-plane projection for the two independent bad-linear quartics

**Status: working proof under the saved actual chart184, pending independent review.**

For either supplied full initial relation
\[
F_{\rm a}=X^2Z+Y^2W,\qquad F_{\rm b}=X^2Z+XYW-Y^4,
\tag{1}
\]
the projected entries \(y,\vartheta\) generate the entire generic
parameter ring over the exact coefficient field. The proof includes
zeros of the return coefficient and uses no plane-embedding theorem.
It is not an original-ring invariant-coordinate or recognition result.

## 1. Actual hypotheses, potentials and notation

Retain the full hypotheses and constructions of
[universal chart184](weight-four-universal-chart-potentials-pass184-working.md):
\[
\begin{gathered}
B=\mathbb C[X,Y,Z,W]=A[u],\quad E=\partial_u,\quad A=\ker E,\quad
\deg_E(X,Y,Z,W)=(1,1,2,2),\\
\ker\theta_0=(F_{\rm a})\ \hbox{or}\ (F_{\rm b}),\qquad
a=EX\ne0,\quad N=[u^3](\partial_Z|_A),\\
L=\operatorname{Frac}\ker_A N,\quad A_L=L[v],\quad Nv=1,\quad
t=EY/a,\quad \rho=(E^2W/2)/a^2,\\
[L:\mathbb C(t,\rho)]<\infty,\qquad
Y=y+tX,\quad Z=z+\sigma X+qX^2,\quad
W=w+\vartheta X+\rho X^2 .
\end{gathered}
\tag{2}
\]
The ratios \(t,\rho\) are independent. Their unique partials on \(L\)
commute and fix \(v\). The source supplies
\[
L[y,z,w]=L[v],\quad \mathbb C(y,z,w)=L(v),\quad
J_0=\det\frac{\partial(y,z,w)}{\partial(t,\rho,v)}
 =-a^3\in L^* .
\tag{3}
\]
Both the whole ring and the calibrated scalar are actual-coordinate
inputs, not assumptions on arbitrary formal polynomials.

Write \(H_j\) for the divided directional Taylor polynomial in the
universal source, and use independent \(U,V,W_0\). Put
\[
\begin{gathered}
f=H_2(q)+H_1(k)+n,\qquad S=H_3(q)+H_2(k)+H_1(n),\\
g_4=H_4(q)+H_3(k)+H_2(n),\qquad k,n,\ell,m\in L,\\
\mathcal R(U)=f_V(U,V)=k_\rho-cU,\qquad
c=2t\ \hbox{for }F_{\rm a},\quad c=1\ \hbox{for }F_{\rm b},\\
\mathcal A(U,V)=S_V=f_\rho,\qquad
\mathcal P=\mathcal R W_0+S-\ell,\qquad
\Psi=\mathcal A W_0+g_4-\ell_tU-\ell_\rho V,\\
z=q_\rho w+f(y,\vartheta),\quad
\mathcal P(y,\vartheta,w)=0,\quad
\Psi(y,\vartheta,w)=m .
\end{gathered}
\tag{4}
\]
Here \(q=-t^2\rho\) or \(q=-t\rho+t^4\), respectively. In particular
\(q_{\rho\rho}=0\), \(c\in L^*\), \(c_\rho=0\), and
\(\mathcal R\) is independent of \(V\).
The zero-return argument in the universal source proves
\[
R:=\mathcal R(y)\ne0\quad\hbox{in }L[v].
\tag{5}
\]
It does not make \(R\) a unit. Its proof allows constant \(y\):
if \(R=0\), then \(y\in L\), \(y_\rho\ne0\) by the full determinant,
and the constant-term determinant has positive degree in \(v\).

For evaluated quantities define
\[
\begin{gathered}
A=\mathcal A(y,\vartheta),\quad
B_1=S_U(y,\vartheta)-cw,\\
C_i=S_i(y,\vartheta)+\mathcal R_i(y)w-\ell_i,\quad i=t,\rho .
\end{gathered}
\tag{6}
\]
The \(i\)-partials here differentiate coefficients before evaluation.
They are not total derivatives of \(\mathcal R(y)\).
The gradient identities are
\[
(\mathcal P_U,\mathcal P_V,\mathcal P_{W_0})=(B_1,A,R),\qquad
(\Psi_U,\Psi_V,\Psi_{W_0})=(C_t,C_\rho,A)
\tag{7}
\]
after evaluation. They follow from
\(\mathcal A_U=\mathcal R_t\), \(\mathcal A_V=\mathcal R_\rho\),
\((g_4)_U=S_t\), and \((g_4)_V=S_\rho\).

## 2. Cleared elimination and exact generic degree

Eliminate \(W_0\) without inverting \(\mathcal R\) in a ring:
\[
\mathcal H=\mathcal R\{g_4-\ell_tU-\ell_\rho V-m\}
             +\mathcal A(\ell-S)
           =\mathcal R(\Psi-m)-\mathcal A\mathcal P .
\tag{8}
\]
This is a polynomial in \(L[U,V]\), vanishes at \((y,\vartheta)\),
and has exact degree five. The universal source reconstructs its
top part as \(-U^4V\) for \(F_{\rm a}\) and \(-U^5\) for \(F_{\rm b}\).
In particular \(\mathcal H\ne0\).

The first relation in (4) and \(R\ne0\) give, in the fraction field,
\[
w=\frac{\ell-S(y,\vartheta)}R,\qquad
L(y,\vartheta)=L(y,z,w)=L(v),\qquad
L[y,\vartheta,w]=L[v].
\tag{9}
\]
For the last equality, \(f_V=\mathcal R\) is independent of \(V\),
so \(f=f_0(U)+\mathcal R(U)V\). Alternatively the actual
\(\vartheta\in L[v]\), together with the formula for \(z\), gives
both containments directly. No polynomial inverse of \(R\) is asserted.

The prime kernel of \(L[U,V]\to L[v]\) is generated by an
irreducible \(G\). Then \(G\mid\mathcal H\), and
\[
\deg G=\max(\deg_v y,\deg_v\vartheta)\le5.
\tag{10}
\]
Here at least one degree is positive, by (9). To reconstruct the
equality independently of closed-embedding results, let \(T\) be
transcendental over \(L(v)\) and put \(\zeta=Ty+\vartheta\).
Its degree in \(v\) is \(d=\max(\deg_vy,\deg_v\vartheta)\).
For any positive-degree polynomial \(p(v)\) over a field \(K\),
\(p(v)-Z_0\) is irreducible in \(K[v,Z_0]\); regarding it over
\(K(Z_0)\) gives \([K(v):K(p(v))]=\deg p\). Thus
\[
[L(T)(v):L(T)(\zeta)]=d.
\tag{11}
\]
If \(D=\deg G\), the variable substitution \(V=Z_0-TU\)
preserves irreducibility after adjoining \(T\), and the resulting
polynomial has degree \(D\) in \(U\). Its leading coefficient is
\(G_D(1,-T)\ne0\). Gauss's lemma preserves irreducibility over
\(L(T,Z_0)\). Equation (9) identifies the extension defined by
its root \(U=y\), \(Z_0=\zeta\) with the extension in (11).
Hence \(D=d\). This also handles constant \(y\) or constant
\(\vartheta\), without assuming either one.

## 3. Calibrated gradient identity

The actual constant determinant in the universal source is
\[
J_0=\left[
dy\wedge(R\,d\vartheta+B_1dt+A\,d\rho)\wedge dw
\right]_{dt\wedge d\rho\wedge dv}.
\tag{12}
\]
Differentiating \(\mathcal P(y,\vartheta,w)=0\) gives
\[
R\,dw=-B_1dy-A\,d\vartheta-C_tdt-C_\rho d\rho.
\]
Equations (7)--(8), with both potentials imposed only after
differentiation, give
\[
\begin{gathered}
\mathcal H_U(y,\vartheta)=RC_t-AB_1,\qquad
\mathcal H_V(y,\vartheta)=RC_\rho-A^2,\\
R^2J_0=C_U\mathcal H_U(y,\vartheta)+C_V\mathcal H_V(y,\vartheta),\\
C_U=R(\vartheta_\rho y_v-y_\rho\vartheta_v)+Ay_v,\qquad
C_V=R(y_t\vartheta_v-\vartheta_ty_v)-B_1y_v .
\end{gathered}
\tag{13}
\]
For an explicit expansion, the final two lines equal \(R\) times
\[
-(RC_t-AB_1)(y_\rho\vartheta_v-y_v\vartheta_\rho)
 +(RC_\rho-A^2)(y_t\vartheta_v-y_v\vartheta_t)
 +(AC_t-B_1C_\rho)y_v,
\]
which is \(R^2J_0\) by (12) and the differential of the first
potential. This is a cleared identity in \(L[v]\). It does not
claim regularity of an inverse at a zero of \(R\).
In particular \(G\) is a simple factor of \(\mathcal H\), and
for \(\mathcal H=GQ\) one has
\(Q(y,\vartheta)\ne0\) and \(Q(y,\vartheta)\mid R^2\).
No unit assertion about this cofactor is required below.

## 4. Finite integral contraction for a squarefree center

Set \(C=L[y,\vartheta]\) and \(O=L[v]=C[w]\). The extension is
finite integral: a nonconstant one of \(y,\vartheta\) supplies
the monic equation \(p(T)-p(v)=0\), divided by its leading
coefficient, for \(v\) over \(C\). In particular \(w\) has a
monic equation over \(C\). The two potential equations imply
\[
Rw\in C,\qquad Aw\in C .
\tag{14}
\]

Put \(\lambda=k_\rho/c\in L\), so \(\mathcal R=c(\lambda-U)\).
The center polynomial
\[
p(V)=S(\lambda,V)-\ell
\tag{15}
\]
has degree at most two in \(V\). In the first form the only cubic
term of \(S\) is \(-U^2V\); in the second it is \(4tU^3\).
The quadratic coefficient in either center is \(k_{\rho\rho}/2\).
Polynomial division by \(U-\lambda\) gives
\[
S(U,V)-\ell=p(V)+(U-\lambda)D(U,V)
             =p(V)-\mathcal R(U)\overline D(U,V),
\quad \overline D=D/c,\quad \deg D\le2.
\tag{16}
\]
This division is by a monic formal polynomial; \(1/c\in L^*\).
It is not division by an original coordinate.
At the actual curve,
\[
p(\vartheta)=R(\overline D(y,\vartheta)-w),\qquad
p'(\vartheta)=A+R\overline D_V(y,\vartheta).
\tag{17}
\]

If \(p\ne0\) is squarefree, including a nonzero constant, choose
\(h,j\in L[V]\) with \(hp+jp'=1\). Evaluation of (17) yields
\[
1=i_0+i_1w,\qquad i_0,i_1\in I:=(R,A)C,
\tag{18}
\]
where
\(i_0=Rh(\vartheta)\overline D+j(\vartheta)A+
Rj(\vartheta)\overline D_V\) and \(i_1=-Rh(\vartheta)\).
Let \(w^s+b_{s-1}w^{s-1}+\cdots+b_0=0\) be a monic relation
over \(C\). Multiply by \(i_1^s\) and replace \(i_1w\) by
\(1-i_0\). The resulting equality lies in \(C\), since
\(C\subset O\) is injective. Modulo \(I\) its leading term is
one and all other terms vanish. Thus \(I=C\).
Combining a \(C\)-linear identity \(1=c_RR+c_AA\) with (14)
gives \(w\in C\).

If \(p=0\) identically, (16) instead gives
\(R(w-\overline D(y,\vartheta))=0\). The domain and (5) directly
give \(w=\overline D(y,\vartheta)\in C\). This case is separate
from the squarefree argument.

## 5. The repeated center and the degree-five cone lemma

The only remaining center is
\[
p(V)=\kappa(V-\vartheta_0)^2,\qquad
\kappa=k_{\rho\rho}/2\in L^*,\quad \vartheta_0\in L .
\tag{19}
\]
The double root belongs to \(L\) by the quadratic formula for a
repeated root. Equation (16) puts the first potential into
\[
\mathcal P=\mathcal R(W_0-\overline D)
                 +\kappa(V-\vartheta_0)^2 .
\]
Define
\[
P_c=R,\qquad Q_c=\vartheta-\vartheta_0,\qquad
S_c=-(w-\overline D(y,\vartheta))/\kappa .
\tag{20}
\]
Then \(P_cS_c=Q_c^2\), \(P_c\ne0\), and the formulas recover
\(y=\lambda-P_c/c\), \(\vartheta=Q_c+\vartheta_0\), and
\(w=\overline D(y,\vartheta)-\kappa S_c\).
Consequently \(L[P_c,Q_c,S_c]=L[v]\).
Equation (10) gives \(\deg_vP_c,\deg_vQ_c\le5\).

To check that the vertex is avoided, the formal gradient of
the first potential is
\[
\begin{gathered}
B_1=-c(w-\overline D)-R\overline D_U,\qquad
A=2\kappa Q_c-R\overline D_V,\qquad
\mathcal P_{W_0}=R .
\end{gathered}
\tag{21}
\]
At a common zero of \(P_c,Q_c,S_c\) over an algebraic closure
of \(L\), all three displayed entries vanish. The middle row
of (12) would then be zero, contradicting \(J_0=-a^3\in L^*\).
This evaluates an already-formed coefficient identity. It does
not differentiate a parameter value or a moving double root.
Thus \((P_c,Q_c,S_c)L[v]=L[v]\).

Every hypothesis of the independently proved
[degree-five cone lemma184](vertex-free-quintic-cone-projection-pass184-working.md)
now holds. It gives \(L[P_c,Q_c]=L[v]\), hence
\[
\boxed{L[y,\vartheta]=L[v].}
\tag{22}
\]
Together with Section 4 this exhausts all possible center
polynomials of degree at most two and proves (22) for both forms.

## Scope and verification

The universal chart184, the projected-degree proof175, the original
whole-plane and cone proofs175, and the new degree-five cone lemma
were read completely for this argument. The generic-line degree
proof and finite integral contraction are reconstructed above.
No normality of the projected plane ring was assumed.

The conclusion is a polynomial plane embedding over the exact
field \(L\), of degree at most five. It is not the descent of a
coordinate to the unlocalized original ring or a recognition
theorem. This note does not exclude constant \(y\), infer an
original invariant correction, or classify the selected component.
No checker was written, executed, imported or compiled; no earlier
artifact was edited. No arbitrary-entry, unrestricted cancellation,
novelty, human expert or proof-assistant claim is made.
