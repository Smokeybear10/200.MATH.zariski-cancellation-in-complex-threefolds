# The whole double has an exact second-degree dichotomy

In the supplied collapsed whole-double quadratic sector with nonzero
speed normal, a hidden degree-two component parameter exists exactly
when the first two derivative-image ideals agree. In that case the
entire localized algebra is the cyclic conic, with bases in every
degree. Otherwise the whole second-degree residue space is exactly
C plus C G, the first-degree residue space is C, and the residual
second-image ideal is a proper maximal-primary complete intersection.

The second alternative is not excluded here. The mixed-fiber proof110
does not transfer: G squared now has zero whole residue, and there is
no simple component on which Nc becomes a unit.

## 1. Actual inputs and the component parameter

Let R=S_m be the regular local invariant surface of the supplied
quadratic frame, with maximal ideal n. Work in its actual faithfully
flat domain A_R with full-kernel LND N. Retain a whole collapsed fixed
double fiber and L(m) nonzero. The exact whole-fiber and degree-module
results99--102 give
\[
\begin{gathered}
\ker(N|_{A_R})=R,\qquad (A_R)_K=K[v],\qquad K=\operatorname{Frac}R,\\
F_j=\ker(N^{j+1}|_{A_R})\simeq R^{j+1},\qquad
I_j=N^j(F_j)\subset R,\\
\mathcal A=A_R/\mathfrak nA_R
 =\mathbb C[c,G]/(G^2),\qquad
\bar N=\lambda G\partial_c,\quad\lambda\in\mathbb C^*,\\
G\in F_2,\quad c\in F_4,\qquad
\mathfrak nA_R=(F,G^2),\quad F\in F_2,\\
W_j=\operatorname{im}(F_j\longrightarrow\mathcal A),
\qquad \dim_{\mathbb C}W_j\le j+1.
\end{gathered}
\tag{1}
\]
Here F is the actual centered first good normal from volume99;
it is not the name of a degree module. The symbols G and c denote
their original elements as well as their residue classes when the
ring is specified. In particular 1,G are independent whole residues,
whereas G squared is zero there.[^whole]

At every height-one prime of R, the actual algebra has the degree-one
slice or the degree-two conic model107. In either case its first two
derivative-image ideals agree, and the whole localized algebra is
generated by its degree-two module. This uses the actual generic-divisor
noncollapse theorem for the good generators, not flatness alone.[^dvr]

A hidden parameter will mean an actual U in F2 whose image on the
reduced line C[c] is nonconstant. Localized elements are allowed:
clearing a base denominator outside m preserves this property and
the degree bound.

## 2. A hidden parameter gives the entire cyclic conic

Suppose such a U exists, and write its whole residue as
A(c)+G B(c), with A nonconstant. Then
\(\overline{NU}=\lambda G A'(c)\) is nonzero in the generic
Artinian double fiber. The general cyclic degree-module theorem103
applies to this actual U, even though it need not be an original good
generator. With r=NU and p=N squared U it gives
\[
\begin{gathered}
p\in\mathfrak n\setminus\{0\},\qquad \eta=2pU-r^2\in\mathfrak n,\\
\boxed{\quad
A_R=R[U,r]\simeq
R[\mathsf U,\mathsf r]/(\mathsf r^2-2p\mathsf U+\eta).
\quad}\\
NU=r,\quad Nr=p,\qquad I_1=I_2=pR,\\
F_j=\bigoplus_{2i\le j}R U^i
 \ \oplus\!\bigoplus_{2i+1\le j}R rU^i.
\end{gathered}
\tag{2}
\]
For clarity, the basis gate is independence of the j+1 indicated
monomials in the single generic Artinian double fiber: first reduce
a relation modulo its nilradical, then use that a nonzero polynomial
in the transcendental reduced U is a unit and cannot kill the nonzero
nilpotent class of r. Rank and Nakayama give each actual basis. Their
union gives the algebra. Monicity of the conic gives a torsion-free
source, and generic equality gives its injection.[^cyclic]

Reducing (2) shows that U is an affine parameter on the reduced
whole line: A(c)=alpha c+beta with alpha nonzero. Consequently
\(\bar r=\lambda\alpha G\), and
\(\dim W_1=2\), \(\dim W_2=3\). This is a local conic
recognition, not a polynomial coordinate system for A or A[u].

## 3. Without a hidden parameter, all second-degree residues are Artin-valued

Assume no U in F2 has nonconstant reduced residue. For arbitrary
U in F2, subtract its constant reduced value and put V=U-U_red.
Its exact whole residue is G beta(c). We prove beta constant.
This extends the h-only theorem102 to arbitrary U; it is not assumed
from the statement of that theorem.

The actual volume-normal chart99 has normal parameters x,y=G of
weights two,one, coefficient field C(c), and leading derivation
\(D_*=\gamma x\partial_y+\lambda_0y\partial_c\), with
gamma constant and lambda0 a nonzero constant. Higher terms have
strictly higher operator weight. The difference V-G beta(c) belongs
to the whole ideal (F,G squared), whose elements have weight at least
two. Thus beta(c)y is the weight-one part of V.[^weights]

If gamma is nonzero, the weight-four term of N cubed V is
\[
D_*^3(\beta(c)y)=
3\gamma^2\lambda_0\beta'(c)x^2
+6\gamma\lambda_0^2\beta''(c)xy^2
+\lambda_0^3\beta'''(c)y^4=0.
\tag{3}
\]
The x squared coefficient makes beta constant. Higher terms of V
or N cannot affect this weight.

Suppose gamma is zero and beta is nonconstant. The residues
1,G,V are 1,G,G beta(c), hence are independent. They give an actual
R-basis of F2, with injective whole-fiber map. Since N kills these
three residues, N(F2) is contained in nF2.

Extend 1 to a basis 1,r0 of F1, subtract an invariant from r0,
and write r0=a0G+b0V. Torsion-freeness of F2/F1, which embeds
in R under N squared, gives gcd(a0,b0)=1. Coefficient comparison
and the invariant conic give
\[
\begin{gathered}
NG=\theta b_0r_0+C_1,\qquad
NV=-\theta a_0r_0+C_2,\qquad\theta\in R\setminus\{0\},\\
Q_0=\theta r_0^2-2C_2G+2C_1V\in R,\\
C_1,C_2,\theta a_0^2,\theta a_0b_0,\theta b_0^2,Q_0
\in\mathfrak n,\qquad
C_1,C_2,Q_0\in\mathfrak n^2.
\end{gathered}
\tag{4}
\]
No unit assumption on theta is needed here. The first memberships
follow from N(F2) contained in nF2 and faithful-flat contraction for
Q0. For the square memberships use the actual flat identity
\(\mathfrak nA_R/\mathfrak n^2A_R
=(\mathfrak n/\mathfrak n^2)\otimes_{\mathbb C}\mathcal A\).
At each first base coefficient, the quadratic monomials G squared,
GV,V squared vanish on the whole double. Independence of 1,G,G beta(c)
then kills the first coefficients of C1,C2,Q0 separately.

The leading weight-two term of NV is lambda0 beta'(c)y squared.
But its actual expression C2-theta a0 squared G-theta a0 b0 V
has weight at least three by (4): base elements of n have weight
at least two, and C2 in n squared has weight at least four. This
contradicts nonconstant beta. All coefficient differentiation in c
has been retained, and local nilpotence on a completed ring is not
being presumed.

In either first-normal case, therefore, the no-hidden hypothesis gives
W2=C{1,G}. The proof used the actual weighted chart and flat module,
not the existence or action of a simple companion.

## 4. Equality of the first two image ideals forces a hidden parameter

The element 1 extends to a basis of F1, so I1 is principal and
nonzero, say I1=PR. Suppose I2=I1. Choose U in F2 with
N squared U=P and put r=NU. Then F1=R{1,r}, and the exact
sequence with last map N squared gives F2=R{1,r,U}.
Set eta=2PU-r squared in R and let C0 be the actual subalgebra
R[r,U] of A_R. There is an injective monic presentation
\[
\begin{gathered}
C_0\simeq R[\mathsf U,\mathsf r]/
(\mathsf r^2-2P\mathsf U+\eta),\\
C_0=\bigoplus_{j\ge0}R U^j
 \ \oplus\bigoplus_{j\ge0}R rU^j,\qquad
\boxed{\quad C_0=A_R.\quad}
\end{gathered}
\tag{5}
\]
Monicity makes the source free over R[U]. Its map becomes an
isomorphism over K, because P is nonzero and r is an affine
generic coordinate. The source is R-torsion-free, so this proves
injection and the displayed infinite free R-basis.

For the boxed equality, every height-one localization of the actual
algebra is generated by its degree-two module, by the models stated
after (1). Kernel localization commutes with invariant denominators,
so C0_p=(A_R)_p at each such prime p. An arbitrary element of A_R
has a finite expansion over K in the basis U^j,rU^j. Equality at p
puts each of those same coefficients in R_p, by uniqueness of that
free basis. Their intersection over all height-one primes is R,
since R is a UFD. All coefficients therefore lie in R, proving (5).
This does not infer finiteness from generic finiteness or equality
of arbitrary modules merely from divisorwise comparison.

The whole derivation in (1) has square zero. Consequently the
invariant P=N squared U has zero value at m and lies in n.
Also r=NU is nilpotent on the whole fiber, so eta belongs to n.
Reduction of (5) is C[U,r]/(r squared). Its reduced U is a
coordinate of the reduced line. Thus U is a hidden parameter.

## 5. The exact alternatives, including the first-degree module

Combining the preceding sections gives the equivalent conditions
\[
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
&\text{some }U\in F_2\text{ has nonconstant reduced residue}\\
&\quad\Longleftrightarrow I_2=I_1\\
&\quad\Longleftrightarrow \dim W_2=3\\
&\quad\Longleftrightarrow \dim W_1=2.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{6}
\]
Here is the remaining first-degree and residual-ideal argument.
If there is no hidden parameter, extend the independent classes
1,G to an R-basis 1,G,H of F2 and subtract constants times 1,G
so that H has zero whole residue. Write a first-degree generator,
after subtracting its invariant constant, as r=aG+bH. The same
conic construction gives a primitive pair a,b and
\[
\begin{gathered}
NG=\epsilon br+B_1,\quad NH=-\epsilon ar+B_2,
\quad P=Nr=aB_1+bB_2,\quad\epsilon\in R^*,\\
I_1=PR,\qquad I_2=PJ_2,\qquad
J_2=(a,b)\subsetneq R,\quad\sqrt{J_2}=\mathfrak n,\\
a,b\in\mathfrak n\setminus\{0\},\qquad
W_1=\mathbb C,\qquad W_2=\mathbb C\{1,G\},\\
Q=\epsilon r^2-2B_2G+2B_1H\in R,
\qquad B_1\in\mathfrak n,\quad B_2,Q\in\mathfrak n^2,\\
P\in\mathfrak n^2,\qquad I_2\subseteq\mathfrak n^3.
\end{gathered}
\tag{7}
\]
To verify the unit epsilon, first obtain a nonzero coefficient theta
as in (4). The exact formula is I2=theta P(a,b). At every height-one
prime I1 and I2 have equal valuation by107; primitivity of a,b makes
the minimum of their two valuations zero. Thus theta has no prime
factor and is a unit in R. Section 4 rules out J2=R in this
alternative. A proper two-generated primitive ideal in the regular
local surface is maximal-primary and a regular sequence. This also
makes both a and b nonzero nonunits.

It follows that r has zero whole residue, so W1=C. Conversely the
hidden case has dim W1=2 by Section 2, proving the last equivalence
in (6). No reduction-kernel equality for the whole F1 or F2 is being
assumed in the proper alternative.

For the last line of (7), N kills all W2, giving B1,B2 in n.
The elements r and H lie in nA_R. Hence Q+2B2G belongs to
n squared A_R. Purity of the independent piece R{1,G}, or its
flat first-coefficient argument, gives Q,B2 in n squared.
The formula for P then gives the stated orders. This argument does
not extract the coefficient of G squared, whose residue is zero.

## 6. The remaining obstruction and proof boundaries

In the cyclic alternative the algebra is recognized only as its exact
relative conic over R. Smoothness still allows higher-order P, as in
the intrinsic models103--104. The Catalan family104 supplies a hidden
degree-two U even though its specified c has degree four; it is not
an actual original E-frame or a proof that every such frame has U.

In the proper alternative, no proof here produces a hidden parameter
or excludes J2. The independent piece 1,G,G squared used in110 is
unavailable. Also (7) does not give the mixed sharp membership for
a squared, does not prove B1 nonzero or coprime to b, and cannot
justify that note's divided quartic without a new argument. The
nonzero nilpotent Nc still permits one-element purity, but it is
not a unit at any reduced point of this whole-double fiber.

The new contribution is the complete actual alternative and the
unit-image criterion, with the arbitrary-U nilpotent argument made
explicit. The cyclic construction itself is retained103. No new
numerical method or primary-source theorem is used. The cited
whole-fiber100, degree-module102, cyclic103, Catalan104 and volume99
proofs were freshly read through EOF for this deduction. Files through110
remain unchanged. Global recognition and arbitrary-frame entry remain
open, and no cancellation or source-novelty claim is made.
