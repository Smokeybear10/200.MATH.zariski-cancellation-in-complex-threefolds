# The odd coefficient row of the actual quadratic involution

The four actual original coordinates force a unimodular odd-coefficient
row for the sign involution of the global conic. Conjugating that
involution by actual slice translations strengthens the restriction:
the good odd column can never be a nonzero scalar multiple of the
speed column at a closed base point. If the good odd column vanishes
identically, the conic parameter r is an actual anti-invariant ambient
coordinate for the involution, fixed by E, with a triangular polynomial
inverse.

Neither a unimodular coefficient row nor its completion is asserted
to be a polynomial coordinate change in the original variables.

## 1. Actual hypotheses and the sign involution

Retain the entire supplied nonconical quadratic frame and the proved
global presentation in [the frontier115, equations (1) and
(4)](quadratic-global-conic-frontier-pass115.md). In particular the
following are actual elements and an actual original coordinate ring:

\[
\begin{gathered}
S=\mathbb C[s,t],\qquad C=S[U,u],\qquad
B=C\oplus Cr
 =S[U,r,u]/(r^2-2PU+\eta),\qquad P\ne0,\\
B=\mathbb C[x_1,x_2,x_3,z],\qquad E=\partial_u,\qquad q\in\mathbb C^*,\\
x_{\rm tr}=bu+dU+er+f,\qquad b,d,e,f\in S^3,\\
z=\frac q2u^2+k_4U^2+k_3rU+k_2U+k_1r+k_0,
\qquad k_i\in S.
\end{gathered}
\tag{1}
\]

The direct sum in (1) is a free rank-two C-module decomposition.
The ring B is a domain. The four displayed original coordinates
generate B as a complex polynomial algebra, not just generically.
The speed column b is unimodular and has an actual completion
\([b,v_2,v_3]\in\operatorname{GL}_3(S)\). These are retained
actual-frame facts, not extra conclusions from a proposed tuple.

The C-automorphism sigma fixing C and sending r to minus r is an
actual polynomial involution of B. Write
\(x^-=(x-\sigma x)/2\). Its four original-coordinate differences are

\[
x_i^-=e_i r\quad(1\le i\le3),\qquad
z^-=(k_3U+k_1)r.
\tag{2}
\]

The fixed ring of this involution is exactly C, by the direct sum and
characteristic zero. It commutes with E. No linearization theorem is
used to make it a polynomial automorphism: this follows from the
already established presentation and the actual equality of B with
the original polynomial ring.

## 2. Polynomial generation gives the whole unit ideal

For an automorphism of a polynomial algebra, the ideal generated by
all differences a-sigma(a) is generated by the differences of any
polynomial generating set. Indeed the difference of a product is a
sum of multiples of the factor differences, and induction handles
every monomial and polynomial. Applied to the four original
coordinates, (2) therefore generates this ideal in B.

On the other hand, every element of B has the unique form c_0+rc_1
with c_i in C, so its difference is 2rc_1. In particular the whole
difference ideal is rB. Cancelling the nonzero element r in the
domain B gives J B=B for
\(J=(e_1,e_2,e_3,k_3U+k_1)C\). The extension C to B is free and
faithfully flat, so J=C. Equivalently, one can express r polynomially
in the original coordinates and take its odd coefficient; that
coefficient is 1 and belongs to J. Thus

\[
\boxed{\quad
(e_1,e_2,e_3,k_3U+k_1)S[U,u]=S[U,u].
\quad}
\tag{3}
\]

There is no division by P, by a good coefficient, or by a local
slice in this argument.

Let I=(e_1,e_2,e_3) in S. Modulo I, (3) says that the polynomial
k_1+k_3U is a unit in (S/I)[U,u]. A polynomial over any commutative
ring is a unit precisely when its constant coefficient is a unit and
all its positive-degree coefficients are nilpotent. For the necessary
direction, reduce modulo every prime to a polynomial ring over a
domain and also evaluate the constant term. Conversely a finite set
of nilpotent coefficients generates a nilpotent ideal, so a finite
geometric series constructs the inverse. Consequently (3) is
equivalent to

\[
(I,k_1)=S,\qquad k_3\in\sqrt I.
\tag{4}
\]

This includes nonreduced I. At every closed base point where e=0,
k_3 vanishes and k_1 is nonzero. It does not in general give k_3 in I,
nor a universal bound on the power of k_3 that belongs to I.

## 3. Actual slice translations constrain every shifted good column

Fix any alpha in S. The derivation alpha r E is locally nilpotent
on B, since E kills alpha r. Its exponential is a genuine polynomial
automorphism with its negative exponential as inverse. Define

\[
\begin{gathered}
\sigma_\alpha=\exp(2\alpha rE)\sigma,
\qquad v=u+\alpha r,\qquad C_\alpha=S[U,v],\\
\sigma_\alpha r=-r,\qquad \sigma_\alpha v=v,\qquad
\sigma_\alpha^2=1,\qquad B=C_\alpha\oplus C_\alpha r,\\
x_{\rm tr}=bv+dU+f+(e-\alpha b)r,\\
z=\frac q2v^2+k_4U^2+k_2U+k_0
       +\frac q2\alpha^2(2PU-\eta)
       +r(k_3U+k_1-q\alpha v).
\end{gathered}
\tag{5}
\]

Composition here means applying sigma first and then the exponential
to ring elements. Equivalently sigma_alpha is conjugate to sigma by
exp(alpha r E). All equations can also be checked just by substituting
u=v-alpha r in (1). The original variables remain the same actual
polynomial generators. The new slice v is not asserted to be the
canonical slice Ez/q.

Apply Section 2 to this involution and its free coefficient ring
C_alpha. With \(I_\alpha=((e-\alpha b)_1,(e-\alpha b)_2,
(e-\alpha b)_3)\subset S\), the result and its exact unit-polynomial
interpretation are

\[
\begin{gathered}
(e-\alpha b,\ k_3U+k_1-q\alpha v)S[U,v]=S[U,v],\\
(I_\alpha,k_1)=S,\qquad
\alpha,k_3\in\sqrt{I_\alpha}.\\
\text{In particular, }\quad
I_\alpha=S\quad\text{for every }\alpha\in\mathbb C^*.
\end{gathered}
\tag{6}
\]

For the multivariable unit test, reduce modulo primes as before;
the constant coefficient is k_1 and the other two coefficients are
k_3 and -q alpha. The hypothesis q nonzero is essential to the
constraint on alpha.

It follows that at no closed base point can e be a nonzero scalar
multiple of b. Thus at every such point either e=0 or the two columns
b,e are linearly independent. This holds also over a two-component
free fiber, where sigma has no fixed point; no branch-locus or
fixed-point tangent argument is required.

To state the stronger coefficient version, use the actual completed
frame to write e=alpha_0 b+beta v_2+gamma v_3, with all three
coefficients in S. Its invertibility identifies I_alpha_0 with
(beta,gamma). Equation (6) gives
alpha_0,k_3 in sqrt((beta,gamma)) and
(beta,gamma,k_1)=S. In particular the radicals of the ideals generated
by e and by the two-by-two minors of [b,e] are equal. This is a
radical equality, not an equality of those two ideals.

The retained actual volume identity supplies compatible, separate
linear restrictions. If L=b_s cross b_t, then
L dot (x_tr-bu) lies in S by
[volume88, Section 2](constant-acceleration-volume-descent-pass88.md).
The S-linear independence of 1,r,U in the global F2 basis yields
L dot d=L dot e=0. These are consequences of the actual coordinate
volume, not assumptions in the odd-row argument. They do not turn
the coefficient changes above into original-coordinate inverses.

## 4. A genuine coordinate consequence when the good odd column is zero

Suppose e vanishes identically. Since S is reduced, (4) then gives
k_3=0 and k_1 in C*. The involution fixes each of the three original
good coordinates. An automorphism of
C[x_1,x_2,x_3][z] fixing its coefficient ring must send z to
a z+F(x_1,x_2,x_3), with a a nonzero complex constant. To see this,
compare z-degrees with its inverse over the coefficient domain;
both degrees must be one, and the leading coefficients must be units.

The involution is nontrivial because it sends the nonzero r to -r.
Its square is the identity, so a=-1: the alternative a=1 forces
F=0 in characteristic zero. Comparing with (2) gives the actual
triangular coordinate pair and inverse

\[
\boxed{\quad
\sigma z=-z+F(x_1,x_2,x_3),\qquad
r=\frac{z-F(x_1,x_2,x_3)/2}{k_1},\qquad
z=k_1r+F(x_1,x_2,x_3)/2.
\quad}
\tag{7}
\]

Thus x_1,x_2,x_3,r are actual polynomial coordinates of B and
Er=0. This is an anti-invariant ambient coordinate for sigma and an
invariant of E, not just a primitive vector in a coefficient module.
The proof uses the original
polynomial inverse only where it is already supplied, through the
fact that sigma is an automorphism of that exact polynomial ring.
It constructs the final inverse in (7) without recovering S or U
from a proposed tuple.

More generally, if e is globally a multiple alpha b with alpha in S,
(6) with this alpha gives alpha=0, so this case reduces to e=0.
Since b has an S-linear left inverse, a generic proportionality
between b and e also has its coefficient in S and has the same
conclusion. The alternative is generic rank two for [b,e], with the
pointwise restriction of Section 3.

## 5. Limits and verification scope

The argument does not prove e=0. In the other case, it gives unit
ideals and radical restrictions on actual coefficients. A matrix
completion over S or S[U] need not be an automorphism of the original
four-coordinate ring: its coefficients have not thereby been
recovered in a new tuple. No such completion is used as a coordinate
recognition step here.

The subsequent [fixed-surface recognition proof116](quadratic-fixed-surface-recognition-pass116.md)
applies plane cancellation and the linear-plane theorem to (7).
That coordinate implication uses an actual inverse, not just row
unimodularity. Its later scope check also shows that e=0 is already
excluded in the exact relative-degree-two branch; this is not a new
nonempty solved sector of that branch. Entry of an
arbitrary sliced action into the original quadratic frame remains
unproved, so no unrestricted cancellation conclusion is made.
