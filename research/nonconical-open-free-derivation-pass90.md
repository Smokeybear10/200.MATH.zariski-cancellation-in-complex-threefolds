# The remaining volume coefficients give a free intrinsic field off Omega

Under the actual hypotheses of the nonconical quadratic-potential
proof, the linear and constant coefficients of the original-coordinate
conormal determinant give a fixed-point-free LND of \(A_\Omega\)
whose kernel is \(S_\Omega\). This is an actual derivation of the
localized kernel, not a formal vector field on a proposed presentation.
No global slice or removal of the divisor \(\Omega=0\) is concluded.

## 1. The actual hypersurface after the quadratic reduction

Use the complete setting and notation of
[quadratic-potential90](nonconical-quadratic-potential-pass90.md).
In particular,
\[
\begin{gathered}
A_\Omega=S_\Omega[y_1,y_2],\qquad
S=\mathbb C[s_1,s_2]=\ker(W|A),\qquad
B_\Omega=A_\Omega[u],\\
\mathcal C=[b,b_{s_1},b_{s_2}],\qquad
\det\mathcal C=\Omega\in S_\Omega^*,\qquad
\rho,\chi\in S_\Omega,\quad q\in\mathbb C^*,\\
x_{\rm tr}=b(u+\rho)+b_{s_1}y_1+b_{s_2}y_2,\qquad
z=\frac q2u^2+\chi-q\Psi(y_1,y_2).
\end{gathered}
\tag{1}
\]
The polynomial \(\Psi\) is the exact base-valued quadratic form
from that proof. The symbols \(x_1,x_2,x_3,z\) are the original
four coordinates of \(B=\mathbb C^{[4]}\), while the five
presentation variables below are not independent in \(B_\Omega\).

The kernel of \(S_\Omega[Y_1,Y_2]\to A_\Omega\) is a
height-one prime in a UFD, hence is generated by an irreducible
polynomial \(F\). In the genuine polynomial ring
\(\mathscr P=S_\Omega[Y_1,Y_2,u]\), lift the coordinates by
\[
\begin{gathered}
X_{\rm tr}=b(u+\rho)+b_{s_1}Y_1+b_{s_2}Y_2,\qquad
X_4=\frac q2u^2+\chi-q\Psi(Y_1,Y_2),\\
\mathscr P/(F)=B_\Omega,\qquad
\mathcal K=\det
\frac{\partial(X_1,X_2,X_3,X_4,F)}
     {\partial(s_1,s_2,Y_1,Y_2,u)} .
\end{gathered}
\tag{2}
\]
Partial derivatives of base coefficients are the ordinary
derivatives on \(S_\Omega\); formal \(Y_1,Y_2,u\) are held fixed.

## 2. The actual conormal determinant is a base unit

The conormal sequence has kernel generated by \(dF\).
Its map \(B_\Omega\to B_\Omega^5\), \(1\mapsto dF\), is
injective: \(F\) has a nonzero partial modulo \(F\) in
characteristic zero, and \(B_\Omega\) is a domain.
The images of \(dX_1,dX_2,dX_3,dX_4\) are a basis of
\(\Omega_{B_\Omega/\mathbb C}\), because the \(X_i\) recover
the original polynomial coordinates after passage to the quotient.
Their lifts together with \(dF\) therefore form a basis of
\(B_\Omega^5\). Consequently \(\mathcal K\) is a unit modulo \(F\).

This unit belongs to the base, not merely to the larger ring.
Indeed \(S=\ker E\cap\ker W\) inside the UFD \(B\).
Factorial closure of both LND kernels implies that every irreducible
of \(S\) remains irreducible, hence prime, in \(B\).
Factoring \(\Omega\) into those primes shows
\[
B_\Omega^*=S_\Omega^*,\qquad
\mathcal K\equiv\kappa\pmod F,\quad \kappa\in S_\Omega^*.
\tag{3}
\]
This is the same actual-generation conormal argument used in
[relative-quadratic-generation79, Section 2](relative-quadratic-generation-pass79.md),
with the unit calculation adapted to the explicit localization.
It does not require \(F\) to be an ambient coordinate.

## 3. Row reduction and the two remaining coefficients

For \(i,k\in\{1,2\}\), define all three base coefficients by
\[
\begin{gathered}
b_{s_i s_k}=\Gamma^0_{ik}b+
             \Gamma^1_{ik}b_{s_1}+\Gamma^2_{ik}b_{s_2},
\qquad \Gamma^j_{ik}\in S_\Omega,\\
g_i=\rho_{s_i}+\sum_{k=1}^2\Gamma^0_{ik}Y_k,\qquad
T_{ji}=\sum_{k=1}^2\Gamma^j_{ik}Y_k\quad(j=1,2),\\
Z_i=\chi_{s_i}-q\Psi_{s_i}
       +q\rho g_i+q\sum_{j=1}^2g_jT_{ji},\qquad
G_i=F_{s_i}-\rho F_{Y_i}-\sum_{j=1}^2F_{Y_j}T_{ji}.
\end{gathered}
\tag{4}
\]
Each \(Z_i\) has \(Y\)-degree at most two and is independent
of \(u\). The symmetry already used in the quadratic-potential
proof gives \(\Psi_{Y_i}=g_i\).

Multiply the first three rows of the already computed matrix in
(2) by \(\mathcal C^{-1}\). Its first three rows, in the column
order of (2), are
\((g_1,g_2,0,0,1)\),
\((u+\rho+T_{11},T_{12},1,0,0)\), and
\((T_{21},u+\rho+T_{22},0,1,0)\).
The fourth row initially has \(u\)-entry \(qu\) and
\(Y_i\)-entry \(-qg_i\).
Subtracting \(qu\) times the first row and adding
\(qg_1,qg_2\) times the next two rows removes these entries.
Its two remaining entries are exactly \(Z_1,Z_2\):
the terms \(-qug_i\) and \(q(u+\rho)g_i\) cancel.
Removing the \(Y\)-entries of the fifth row similarly gives
\(G_i-uF_{Y_i}\).

The column permutation to \(u,Y_1,Y_2,s_1,s_2\) has positive
sign. Hence the complete determinant identity, after passage to
the actual ring, is
\[
\frac{\kappa}{\Omega}
=Z_1G_2-Z_2G_1+
u\bigl(Z_2F_{Y_1}-Z_1F_{Y_2}\bigr)
\quad\text{in }A_\Omega[u].
\tag{5}
\]
There is no additional \(u\)-coefficient hidden in the left side,
by (3). Comparing coefficients therefore gives
\[
Z_2F_{Y_1}-Z_1F_{Y_2}\in(F),\qquad
Z_1G_2-Z_2G_1=\frac{\kappa}{\Omega}\text{ in }A_\Omega,
\qquad (Z_1,Z_2)A_\Omega=A_\Omega .
\tag{6}
\]
The last conclusion is an exact unit-ideal identity.
It is not just a generic nonvanishing assertion.

## 4. The descended field is an actual fixed-point-free LND

Define a derivation of the formal relative polynomial ring by
\(\widetilde D=Z_2\partial_{Y_1}-Z_1\partial_{Y_2}\), killing
\(S_\Omega\). The first relation in (6) makes it preserve \((F)\).
Thus it descends to the actual derivation
\[
D:S_\Omega[y_1,y_2]\longrightarrow S_\Omega[y_1,y_2],
\qquad Dy_1=Z_2,\quad Dy_2=-Z_1,\quad D(S_\Omega)=0.
\tag{7}
\]
The coefficient values in (6) imply that \(D\) is nowhere zero.
Local nilpotence is a separate gate and follows as follows.

Put \(K=\operatorname{Frac}(S)\). The retained intrinsic local-slice
theorem gives \(A_K=K[v]\).
Every \(K\)-derivation of \(K[v]\) is
\(\lambda(v)\partial_v\) with \(\lambda(v)\in K[v]\).
For the localization of (7), that polynomial divides both
\(Dy_1\) and \(Dy_2\). The unit ideal in (6) remains unit after
localization, so \(\lambda(v)\in K^*\).
Every element of \(A_\Omega\subset K[v]\) is therefore killed
by a finite iterate of \(D\). Injectivity of this inclusion proves
local nilpotence in \(A_\Omega\) itself.
Moreover the generic kernel is \(K\), and
\(A_\Omega\cap K=S_\Omega\), as follows by applying the original
nonzero \(W\) with kernel \(S_\Omega\). We conclude
\[
\boxed{\quad
0\ne D\in\operatorname{LND}_{S_\Omega}(A_\Omega),\qquad
\ker D=S_\Omega,\qquad D(A_\Omega)A_\Omega=A_\Omega.
\quad}
\tag{8}
\]
The last identity expresses fixed-point-freeness, not that \(1\)
is a value \(D(a)\) of one element. No global slice for \(D\)
has been assumed or proved.

One can clear denominators on a finite set of generators of \(A\):
for some \(N\), \(\Omega^N D\) preserves \(A\).
Because \(D\Omega=0\), this is again an actual LND and its kernel
on \(A\) is \(S\). Clearing denominators may introduce zeros on
\(\Omega=0\), so this does not yield a fixed-point-free global
intrinsic action. Nor does (8) alone identify \(A_\Omega\) with
\(S_\Omega[t]\), supply an \(S_\Omega\)-section, or prove the
required geometric fiber conditions for a further recognition theorem.

## 5. The cubic relation gives a unit relative gradient and smoothness

The separate [cubic-relation proof90](nonconical-cubic-relation-pass90.md)
constructs a formal polynomial \(\Phi\in S_\Omega[Y_1,Y_2]\)
of degree at most three, with \(\Phi_{Y_i}=Z_i\), and proves
\(\Phi(y_1,y_2)=\zeta\in S_\Omega\).
For the defining irreducible polynomial \(F\) used here, write
\[
\begin{gathered}
0\ne\Phi-\zeta=F H,\qquad H\in S_\Omega[Y_1,Y_2],\\
Z_i=H F_{Y_i}\text{ in }A_\Omega,\qquad
\overline H\in A_\Omega^*,\qquad
(F_{Y_1},F_{Y_2})A_\Omega=A_\Omega .
\end{gathered}
\tag{9}
\]
The first identity follows from the actual principal presentation
kernel. Differentiate it formally, and then pass modulo \(F\),
to obtain the second. The unit pair in (6) makes
\(1=\overline H(a_1F_{Y_1}+a_2F_{Y_2})\) for suitable
\(a_1,a_2\in A_\Omega\). This proves both remaining assertions
of (9), without a gcd-to-unit inference.

The gradient in (9) makes the relative conormal map
\(A_\Omega\to A_\Omega^2\), \(1\mapsto(F_{Y_1},F_{Y_2})\),
a split injection with rank-one projective cokernel.
The hypersurface is of finite presentation, so the standard
smoothness criterion gives a smooth morphism
\(\operatorname{Spec}A_\Omega\to\operatorname{Spec}S_\Omega\)
of relative dimension one.[^smooth]
The faithful flatness and nonempty curve fibers remain the
inherited actual quotient properties.

There is also a useful exact cofactor refinement when the degree
\(d=\deg_YF\) is at least two. The unit computation (3) gives
\(A_\Omega^*=S_\Omega^*\), so \(\overline H=\eta\) for
\(\eta\in S_\Omega^*\). Hence \(H-\eta\in(F)\).
But \(\deg_Y H\le3-d<d\). A nonzero multiple of \(F\)
cannot have such a degree over the domain \(S_\Omega\).
Therefore
\[
2\le d\le3\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
H=\eta\in S_\Omega^*,\qquad
\Phi-\zeta=\eta F
\quad\text{in }S_\Omega[Y_1,Y_2].
\tag{10}
\]
No assertion that \(H\) is formally constant is made for \(d=1\).

Smoothness in this section does not give geometrically connected
or geometrically integral fibers, and it does not produce a
base section. The localized Weitzenbock control in
[source-scope90, Section 5](homogeneous-speed-recognition-source-scope-pass90.md)
is smooth and factorial, has a free intrinsic action and a
quadratic equation, yet has a nontrivial quadratic residue-field
extension on a base divisor. It is not an actual realization of
this nonconical frame, but it shows why those abstract local
properties alone do not complete the remaining recognition step.
