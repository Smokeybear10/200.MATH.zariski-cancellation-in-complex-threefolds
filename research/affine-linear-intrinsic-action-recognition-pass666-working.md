# An affine-linear intrinsic action recognizes a threefold cylinder base

Status: complete written argument, two full-source internal reviews passed.
General cancellation remains UNSOLVED. Novelty is UNKNOWN.
This is a conditional recognition result, not a construction of the
required action on an arbitrary cylinder base.

## 1. Statement and exact hypothesis

Work over the complex numbers. Let A be a finitely generated domain and
let B=A[u] be isomorphic to a polynomial ring in four variables. Write
E=partial_u, so ker E=A and E(u)=1. Let L be a NONZERO locally nilpotent
derivation of A, extended to B by L(u)=0. Thus [E,L]=0.

**Proposition.** If there is an actual polynomial coordinate system
X_1,...,X_4 of B in which every L(X_i) is affine-linear, then

    A is isomorphic to C^[3].                         (1)

The hypothesis concerns L, not E. No affine-linearity, triangularity,
or preservation of these coordinates is initially required of E.
An affine-linear tangent approximation or a generic coordinate system
after localization does not satisfy the hypothesis.

The proof uses the established kernel theorems stated in Section 6.
The remaining steps, including the needed plinth computation, are
given below.

## 2. The elementary locally nilpotent divisibility lemma

For a nonzero element f in a characteristic-zero domain with a locally
nilpotent derivation D, define deg_D(f) as the largest n with D^n(f)
nonzero. The Leibniz formula, characteristic zero, and the domain
hypothesis imply

    deg_D(fg)=deg_D(f)+deg_D(g).

If D(f)=fg is nonzero, the left side has degree deg_D(f)-1, whereas
the right side has degree at least deg_D(f). This is impossible.
Consequently

    f divides D(f)  implies  D(f)=0.                 (2)

This lemma will be applied to the actual LND E on B.

## 3. Affine actions without a fixed point

Write the affine-linear coefficient vector as

    L(X)=N X+b,

where N is a constant matrix and b a constant vector. Local nilpotence
on the L-stable vector space spanned by 1,X_1,...,X_4 implies that N
is nilpotent. There is a fixed point precisely when b is in im N.

If there is no fixed point, choose a row vector lambda with
lambda N=0 and lambda b=1. Then q=lambda X is a nonconstant linear
coordinate and L(q)=1. The slice theorem gives

    B=(ker_B L)[q],
    ker_B L is isomorphic to B/(q)=C^[3].            (3)

For completeness, the slice theorem follows from the finite Taylor
projection pi(f)=sum_{j>=0}(-q)^j L^j(f)/j!: pi maps B to ker L,
and f=sum q^j pi(L^j f)/j!. This also identifies the quotient by q.

Commutation implies that E preserves ker_B L. Its restriction is a
nonzero LND, since u belongs to ker_B L and E(u)=1. The kernel theorem
for LNDs on C^[3] therefore gives

    ker(E|ker_B L)=ker(L|A) is isomorphic to C^[2].  (4)

Let q_0 be the coefficient of u^0 in q under the specified expansion
B=A[u]. Because L fixes u, L(q_0)=1. Applying the same slice theorem
inside A gives A=(ker_A L)[q_0], proving (1) in this case. No surface
cancellation hypothesis or cancellation in dimension three was used.

## 4. Affine actions with a fixed point

Translate a fixed point to the origin, so L(X)=N X. Let z be that
point, let E(z) be the value of E there, and let du_z denote the
differential of the given polynomial u. Commutation and invariance give

    N E(z)=0,    du_z N=0,    du_z(E(z))=1.          (5)

Indeed, evaluating [E,L](X)=0 at z gives the first equation, since
L vanishes at z. Differentiating L(u)=du_X(NX)=0 at z gives the
second. The last is E(u)=1 evaluated at z.

Equations (5) show that ker N is not contained in im N. A nilpotent
Jordan block of size at least two has its kernel contained in its
image. Therefore N has a block of size one. N is nonzero, since L
is nonzero and its constant term has been removed. The only possible
Jordan partitions of four are consequently

    (2,1,1)  or  (3,1).                             (6)

In particular (2,2) and (4) are excluded by the actual slice u,
not by an assumption on the fixed locus.

### 4.1. Jordan type (2,1,1)

Choose actual linear coordinates x,y,z,w with L=x partial_y.
The ideal generated by L(B) is exactly (x). Since [E,L]=0, E preserves
this ideal: E(Lf)=L(Ef), and Leibniz handles ideal multiples. Hence
E(x) belongs to (x). By (2), E(x)=0.

### 4.2. Jordan type (3,1): the full plinth computation

Choose actual linear coordinates x,y,z,w with

    L=x partial_y+y partial_z,    h=2xz-y^2.

Put K=C[x,h,w]. Inverting x gives

    B_x=C[x,x^(-1),h,w,y],    L=x partial_y.         (7)

We claim

    ker_B L=K,    ker_B L^2=K direct-sum yK.        (8)

To prove the second equality, (7) expresses any element of ker L^2
as P+yQ with P,Q in C[x,x^(-1),h,w]. Write it as
x^(-n)(P_0+yQ_0), with n>=0 minimal and P_0,Q_0 in K. If n>0,
polynomiality in B implies, after reduction modulo x,

    P_0(0,-y^2,w)+y Q_0(0,-y^2,w)=0.               (9)

The first summand has only even powers of y and the second only odd
powers. Both vanish. The substitution C[h,w] -> C[y,w], h -> -y^2,
is injective, so both P_0 and Q_0 are divisible by x in K. This
contradicts minimality. Thus n=0. The representation is unique by
(7). The same argument with Q=0 proves the first equality in (8).

The plinth ideal is L(B) intersect ker L. If f=L(g) and L(f)=0,
then g belongs to ker L^2, so (8) gives g=P+yQ and f=xQ. Conversely
L(yQ)=xQ for every Q in K. Thus the FULL ideal is

    pl(L)=xK,    not just an ideal containing x.    (10)

E preserves both ker L and pl(L), by commutation. Consequently
E(x) belongs to xK, and (2) gives E(x)=0 again.

### 4.3. Recognition from the fixed coordinate

In both cases x is an actual coordinate of B=C[x,y,z,w], and E(x)=0.
Moreover ker E=A is regular: regularity descends from the faithfully
flat polynomial extension A -> A[u]=B (equivalently, use the local
polynomial-ring criterion for regularity). E is nonzero because
E(u)=1. Bhatwadekar--Gupta--Lokhande, Theorem 3.5, now yields

    A=C[x]^[2].                                    (11)

This proves (1). The cited theorem is applied to E, not to L.

## 5. A transitive abelian normalization criterion

**Corollary.** Retain B,A,E,L as in Section 1, without assuming
affine-linearity of L. Suppose H is a finite-dimensional complex
vector subspace of LND(B), its members commute pairwise, and

    [L,H] is contained in H.

If H has generic orbit rank four, then A is isomorphic to C^[3].
There is no premise that E belongs to H or to the Lie algebra
generated by H and L.

Here is the justification that actual coordinates, rather than
generic orbit parameters, are available. Choose a basis of H and
exponentiate its commuting LNDs. They give an algebraic vector-group
action. Every orbit of a unipotent group on an affine variety is
closed. A rank-four orbit on the irreducible fourfold Spec B is also
open and dense, hence is all of Spec B.

One elementary proof of closedness is useful here. If the boundary
of an orbit in its affine closure were nonempty, its nonzero stable
ideal would contain a nonzero invariant: take a finite-dimensional
stable subspace and use the invariant-vector property of unipotent
representations. That invariant is constant on the dense orbit,
and hence constant on its closure. It cannot both be nonzero and
vanish on a nonempty boundary, a contradiction.

In a transitive abelian action, an element fixing one point fixes
every point. After quotienting out the global kernel, the action
is therefore simply transitive. In characteristic zero a quotient
of a vector group by a closed subgroup is a vector group; the orbit
map identifies Spec B with this four-dimensional quotient. Thus
there are actual polynomial coordinates X_1,...,X_4 in which H is
the space of constant translation fields.

Write L=sum f_i partial_{X_i}. The normalization condition says

    [L,partial_{X_j}]=-sum_i (partial_{X_j} f_i)
                                      partial_{X_i} belongs to H.

Every partial derivative of every f_i is therefore constant. Each
f_i is affine-linear. Section 1 applies and proves the corollary.

For a hypothetical non-polynomial base, this rules out generic rank
four for every such normalized abelian family with nonzero intrinsic
L. It does NOT rule out rank three and does not construct H or L.
