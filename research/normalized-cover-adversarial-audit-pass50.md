# Adversarial re-audit of normalized-cover surjectivity

The [pass-49 normalized-cover proof](normalized-cover-surjectivity-pass49.md)
survives this bounded re-audit. No gap was found at the whole-fiber support,
proper-normalization, peripheral-loop or homology-diagram steps under the
actual stated hypotheses. This record gives those steps with their maps and
quantifiers exposed, rather than treating the earlier clean reviews as proof.

This is a fresh reconstruction by the geometry agent that wrote the original
note, not an additional statistically independent reviewer, external expert
certification or a formal proof-assistant verification. Frozen files through
pass 49 remain unchanged.

## 1. Hypotheses actually used

The setting under audit is a smooth factorial affine complex threefold
\(X=\operatorname{Spec}A\), an actual nontrivial additive-group quotient
\(\pi:X\to S\simeq\mathbb A^2\), and the exceptional factorization
\[
E\xrightarrow{\kappa}Z\xrightarrow{\theta}\Gamma.
\]
The following supplied properties are substantive.

- The pullback of each irreducible component of the reduced curve \(\Gamma\)
  is an irreducible reduced surface. In particular the normal divisor
  multiplicity is one.
- The complement map is a product with \(\mathbb A^1\).
- The curve map \(\theta\) is finite and dominant on its components. After
  removing a finite set \(F\), the curves are smooth, the covering is
  unramified, and \(E^*\simeq Z^*\times\mathbb A^1\) over \(Z^*\).
- Normalization of \(E\) and \(Z\) is finite, and the componentwise
  dominant maps give a morphism \(\kappa^\nu:E^\nu\to Z^\nu\).
- \(H_3(X;\mathbb Q)=H_4(X;\mathbb Q)=0\).

Kaliman's actual quotient construction supplies the factorization and the
diagram used below; its finiteness theorem is applied with its homological
hypotheses, not to an arbitrary curve factorization.[^kal] The original
\(A[s_*]\simeq\mathbb C^{[4]}\) supplies smoothness, factoriality and
contractibility without identifying \(A\) with a polynomial three-ring.
The previous retained-curve identification fixes the primitive plinth scope.

For the support argument itself, \(H_3(X)=0\) already gives the needed
surjectivity of a relative boundary map. The additional \(H_4(X)=0\) used
in pass 49 strengthens that map to an isomorphism; it causes no extra gap.
No rationality of the normalization curves, free action on a special fiber,
or smoothness of that fiber is needed here.

## 2. Excision works for every chosen whole-fiber neighborhood

Put \(L=\pi^{-1}(F)\), \(X^*=X\setminus L\) and
\(\widehat X=X\setminus E\). Fix any Euclidean open set \(U\supset L\).
Its closed complement \(X\setminus U\) lies inside the open \(X^*\), so
the ordinary excision hypothesis is satisfied. Therefore
\[
H_4(U,U\setminus L;\mathbb Q)
\simeq H_4(X,X^*;\mathbb Q).
\tag{1}
\]
The long exact sequence of \((X,X^*)\) and \(H_3(X)=0\) imply that every
\(\alpha\in H_3(X^*)\) is a boundary of a relative class on the right.
Transporting that class through (1) and taking its boundary gives
\(\alpha_U\in H_3(U\setminus L)\) mapping to \(\alpha\).

The correctly typed local map is
\[
H_3(U\setminus L)
\longrightarrow H_3(U\setminus L,U\setminus E)
\xrightarrow{\mathrm{Thom}}H_1(E^*\cap U)
\longrightarrow H_1(Z^*).
\tag{2}
\]
Here \(E^*\cap U\) is a closed smooth real-codimension-two submanifold of
\(U\setminus L\); its normal bundle has the complex orientation. Naturality
for open inclusions identifies (2) with \(\phi'(\alpha)\). Thus
\[
\operatorname{im}\phi'
\subseteq\operatorname{im}\bigl(H_1(E^*\cap U)\to H_1(Z^*)\bigr)
\quad\text{for every such }U.
\tag{3}
\]
Neither ordinary excision nor this Thom map requires compactness of \(L\),
a deformation retraction onto the fiber, or a uniform tubular radius along
it. Individual singular chains have compact images; this is compatible with
both spaces being noncompact.

## 3. Finite normalization excludes nonuniform approach at infinity

Finite normalization is proper and closed in the complex topology.[^norm]
There is also a direct affine check. A finite affine algebra is generated by
finitely many elements integral over the base algebra. Embed its spectrum
as a closed subvariety of the base times an affine space. Each new coordinate
satisfies a monic equation over the base. Over a compact subset of the base,
the coefficients are bounded, so elementary root bounds bound every new
coordinate. The inverse image of the compact set is closed and bounded,
hence compact. A convergent sequence in the image of any closed subset then
lifts into the inverse image of a compact sequence-plus-limit set; a
convergent subsequence proves the image is closed.

Now choose disks around all finitely many points of \(Z^\nu\) above \(F\).
Let \(V\) contain precisely the disks centered at reached points, and put
\(O=(\kappa^\nu)^{-1}(V)\). By the definition of reached,
\(\nu_E^{-1}(L)\subset O\), even if that inverse image is singular or has
many components. Closedness gives the open set
\[
W=E\setminus\nu_E(E^\nu\setminus O),\qquad L\subset W,
\qquad\nu_E^{-1}(W)\subset O.
\tag{4}
\]
Because \(E\) is closed in \(X\), there is an ambient open
\(U\supset L\) with \(E\cap U\subset W\). It may be shrunk inside
disjoint base neighborhoods of the points of \(F\).

Equation (4) is one neighborhood of the entire fiber set at once. A sequence
approaching an excluded normalization disk from increasingly distant parts
of the fiber cannot invalidate it. No properness of \(\pi\) or
\(\kappa^\nu\) has been assumed. Only the finite map \(\nu_E\) is used.
On \(E^*\), normalization is an isomorphism, so the image in (3) is in the
span of loops about reached points only.

## 4. No hidden relation can cancel an unreached peripheral loop

For each connected component \(T\) of \(Z^*\), let \(\overline T\) be
its smooth projective compactification and \(P=\overline T\setminus T\).
The pair sequence and local orientations give the portion
\[
H_2(\overline T;\mathbb Q)\longrightarrow
\bigoplus_{p\in P}\mathbb Q[p]\longrightarrow H_1(T;\mathbb Q).
\tag{5}
\]
The first map sends the fundamental class to the diagonal vector, and its
image is the entire kernel of the second map. Thus this diagonal is the
only relation among puncture loops. Genus cycles create no additional
relation among those loops.

Each affine normalization component has a point at infinity, not among the
finite points above \(F\). A vector supported only at finite punctures can
therefore be diagonal only if it is zero. The finite loops are independent.
Different components contribute a direct sum, so cross-component cancellation
is impossible. Singular identifications in \(Z\) create no extra relations:
the relevant points have been removed and \(Z^*\) is already normal.

## 5. The global diagram provides exactly the needed implication

The right map in the source's homology diagram is an isomorphism because
\(\widehat X\simeq\widehat S\times\mathbb A^1\). The middle map is ordinary
\(\theta_*\), not an inverse-degree transfer. On \(E^*\), the smooth
reduced hypersurface equation has a nonzero normal differential. The
restriction along it is the smooth product projection followed by an
unramified curve cover. Hence the quotient map is transverse there, with
normal degree one, as required for the Thom identifications. The covering
degree along the curve is already included in \(\theta_*\).

Exactness consequently gives
\[
\theta_*\beta\in\operatorname{im}\phi
\Longrightarrow\beta\in\operatorname{im}\phi'.
\tag{6}
\]
Above each normalized base branch at \(q\), a finite positive-degree map
supplies at least one normalized cover point. Select an allegedly unreached
point for its branch and arbitrary points for the other branches. Weight
their loops by the reciprocals of their positive ramification indices. The
pushforward is the sum of all base-branch loops, which is the image under
\(\phi\) of the small sphere at \(q\).

Equation (6) forces that weighted sum into \(\operatorname{im}\phi'\).
Equations (3) and (4) put the same image in the span of reached loops.
Independence from (5) is a contradiction. This reconstructs the asserted
normalized surjectivity without a special-fiber regularity premise.

## 6. Failure controls and exact verdict

The existing [deleted-line control](pointwise-plinth-second-audit-pass17.md),
Sections 2–3, remains a useful genuine-action test. For
\(D=u\partial_v+2v\partial_w\), remove the invariant line
\(u=0,v=-1\) from affine three-space. A finite nodal factorization still has
an unreached normalization point, and the off-divisor product persists.
But the altered space has \(H_3(X;\mathbb Q)=\mathbb Q\). Its missing-branch
linking sphere is not a boundary from \(H_4(X,X^*)\), so it cannot be moved
into every neighborhood of the fibers that remain. This locates the failure
at (1)–(3), rather than contradicting the actual theorem. The control is
inherited; no new example or test count is claimed.

Dropping finiteness of normalization would invalidate the closed-set step
in (4). Replacing the affine normalization by a compact curve with no
additional puncture would invalidate the finite-loop independence used in
(5). Replacing the actual diagram by only surjectivity of \(\kappa\) would
discard (6). None of these changes occurs in the saved hypotheses.

The no-pole corollary also remains valid: at a reached point, pulling back
\(t^nf=u\), with \(n>0\) and \(u\) a local unit, contradicts regularity
of \(f\) because the parameter vanishes. The resulting full invariant ring
embeds in the finite normalization over the exceptional base curve. The
saved note correctly passes to fraction fields after invariant localization;
it does not identify the intermediate curve with the full invariant spectrum.

No correction to the frozen pass-49 proof is requested. This audit does not
turn normalized surjectivity into an unconditional assertion about arbitrary
morphisms, nor does it settle a global-generation or cancellation gap. No
numerical method, project regression or new source theorem is claimed.
