# Original coordinate generation forces a relative quadratic equation

In the nonlinear supplied-good nice branch, the actual relative
hypersurface equation can be chosen linear in its second generator
and of degree at most two in its first. The determinant of the
original coordinate reconstruction, not two-generator status alone,
forces this form. It also proves that the derived good field has
third coordinate iterate zero in the original frame.

Neither conclusion establishes primitive fixed-point-freeness or
unrestricted cancellation. All supplied-frame and intrinsic-quotient
hypotheses of the preceding binary reduction remain in force.

## 1. The actual reconstruction retained from pass78

Use the precise setting of
[binary-relative-hypersurface78](binary-relative-hypersurface-pass78.md):
\[
\begin{gathered}
B=\mathbb C[x_1,x_2,x_3,w]=A[\sigma],\qquad
E=P\cdot\partial\in\operatorname{LND}(B),\quad E\sigma=1,\quad E(P)=0,\\
T=\partial_w,\qquad [E,[T,E]]=0,\qquad Q=[T,E],\qquad
0\ne\delta=Q|_A,\\
S=\ker\delta=\mathbb C[u,v],\qquad
A=S[Y,Z]\simeq S[\mathsf Y,\mathsf Z]/(F).
\end{gathered}
\tag{1}
\]
Here Y,Z on the left denote actual invariant generators; the
symbols on the right are formal. Below we write Y,Z for the
formal variables too and specify when an equation is in A.
The irreducible equation \(F\in S[Y,Z]\) is primitive over S.
The source of (1) is the actual nice action with its original
slice, a supplied constant good partial, a nonlinear ternary
velocity cone, and the published-input intrinsic plane and
faithful-flatness theorem. No arbitrary hypersurface is substituted.

The binary parameters \(p,q\in S\) have unit ideal, and the
three transverse velocities are \(H=\mathcal H(p,q)\), where
the \(h_i\) are coprime independent homogeneous binary forms
of common degree \(e\ge2\). The primitive binary normal gives
a unimodular row \(\nu\in S^{1\times3}\), annihilating H
and both ordinary S-derivatives \(H_u,H_v\).
The explicit pass78 construction supplies
\[
\begin{gathered}
M=[H,V,W]\in\operatorname{SL}_3(S),\qquad
M^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda\\\mu\\\nu\end{pmatrix},
\qquad c\in S,\quad P_4=P\in S,\\
x_{\mathrm{tr}}=H\sigma+VY+Wc,\qquad
w=Z+P\sigma,\qquad \delta Y\ne0.
\end{gathered}
\tag{2}
\]
Only in the remainder of this note, P without an index denotes
the fourth velocity. The original four-entry velocity is no
longer denoted by that single letter. In (2), the last nonzero
value is the proved identity \(\delta Y=\omega G(p,q)/e\)
from pass78, using its Wronskian theorem. It includes dependent
unit parameter pairs.

The displayed reconstruction is global in the actual ring.
In particular it identifies
\(S[Y,Z,t]/(F)\) with B by \(t\mapsto\sigma\).
The elements u,v,Y,Z,t are not claimed to be five independent
coordinates of B.

## 2. The five-variable determinant is a complex unit modulo F

In the genuine polynomial ring \(R=S[Y,Z,t]\), set
\(R_c=Wc\), and lift the original coordinates by
\[
X_{\mathrm{tr}}=Ht+VY+R_c,\qquad X_4=Z+Pt,
\qquad
\mathcal J=\det\frac{\partial(X_1,X_2,X_3,X_4,F)}
 {\partial(u,v,Y,Z,t)}.
\tag{3}
\]
Then
\[
\mathcal J\equiv\kappa\pmod F
\qquad\text{for some }\kappa\in\mathbb C^*.
\tag{4}
\]
Here is the actual-generation gate. In the conormal sequence of
\(B=R/(F)\), the kernel of
\(\Omega_{R/\mathbb C}\otimes_R B\to\Omega_{B/\mathbb C}\)
is generated by dF. The map \(B\to B^5\) sending 1 to dF
is injective: F is irreducible in characteristic zero, so at
least one of its partials is nonzero modulo F, and B is a
domain. The images of \(dX_1,\ldots,dX_4\) are a basis of
\(\Omega_{B/\mathbb C}\), because these are precisely the
original four polynomial coordinates under the actual isomorphism.
Their lifts, together with dF, therefore form a basis of \(B^5\).
Their determinant is a unit in B. The actual ring B is a
complex polynomial ring, whose only units are \(\mathbb C^*\),
proving (4). This does not assume F is an ambient coordinate.

For \(i=u,v\), define the following polynomials, differentiating
the displayed S-coefficients by the ordinary partials of S:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\alpha_i&=(\lambda H_i)t+(\lambda V_i)Y+\lambda(R_c)_i,\\
\beta_i&=(\mu H_i)t+(\mu V_i)Y+\mu(R_c)_i,\\
\ell_i&=(\nu V_i)Y+\nu(R_c)_i,\qquad
\mathcal D=\ell_v\partial_u-\ell_u\partial_v.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]
Thus \(\ell_i\in S[Y]\) has degree at most one in Y and
does not depend on t or Z. The absence of t follows from
\(\nu H_i=0\), which is the binary tangent-plane identity.
The derivation \(\mathcal D\) in (5) acts only on u,v and
holds the formal Y,Z,t fixed.

Multiply the first three rows of the Jacobian in (3) by
\(M^{-1}\). This is multiplication of an already computed
matrix, not a nonlinear coordinate-change rule for Jacobians.
Since \(\det M=1\), the determinant is unchanged and becomes
\[
\det\begin{pmatrix}
\alpha_u&\alpha_v&0&0&1\\
\beta_u&\beta_v&1&0&0\\
\ell_u&\ell_v&0&0&0\\
tP_u&tP_v&0&1&P\\
F_u&F_v&F_Y&F_Z&0
\end{pmatrix}
=\mathcal D F-bF_Y+(Pa-t\mathcal D P)F_Z,
\tag{6}
\]
where \(a=\alpha_u\ell_v-\alpha_v\ell_u\) and
\(b=\beta_u\ell_v-\beta_v\ell_u\). This fixes the
orientation by the orders in (3); no unspecified sign remains.

## 3. Integrability removes one coefficient and constrains F

Write \(b=\rho t+b_0\) and \(a=\chi t+a_0\). Explicitly,
\[
\rho=(\mu H_u)\ell_v-(\mu H_v)\ell_u\in S,
\qquad
\chi=(\lambda H_u)\ell_v-(\lambda H_v)\ell_u\in S[Y],
\qquad \deg_Y\chi\le1.
\tag{7}
\]
The assertion \(\rho\in S\), rather than just S[Y], is a
full integrability consequence. Put \(b_i=\mu H_i\).
Since \(\nu H_i=0\), the inverse frame gives
\(H_i=(\lambda H_i)H+b_iV\). Taking the dot product
with \(\nu\) after differentiating \(H_u\) by v and
\(H_v\) by u yields
\(b_u\nu V_v=b_v\nu V_u\), because mixed partials of H
commute. This is exactly the coefficient of Y in \(\rho\);
it vanishes. All remaining coefficients of \(\rho\) lie in S.

Set \(\psi=P\chi-\mathcal D P\in S[Y]\), of Y-degree
at most one. Equation (6) is affine in t. Since (4) holds
in the genuine polynomial extension \(B=A[t]\), its
t-coefficient gives the exact relation
\[
-\rho F_Y+\psi F_Z\in(F)\subset S[Y,Z].
\tag{8}
\]
The constant term also satisfies
\(\mathcal D F-b_0F_Y+Pa_0F_Z\equiv\kappa\pmod F\).
Neither identity follows from a generic affine-line fiber alone.

## 4. Exact cofactors of the original partial

In B the original partial T satisfies \(TX_i=0\) for
\(i\le3\), \(TX_4=1\), and \(TF=0\). The invertible
matrix in (6) therefore determines its values on the five
presentation generators by Cramer's rule:
\[
\begin{aligned}
Tu&=-\kappa^{-1}F_Z\ell_v,&
Tv&=\kappa^{-1}F_Z\ell_u,\\
TY&=\kappa^{-1}F_Z b,&
Tt&=\kappa^{-1}F_Z a,\\
TZ&=\kappa^{-1}(\mathcal D F-bF_Y).
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]
These are equalities after passage to B, not values assigned
to an arbitrary polynomial derivation on R. In particular,
the unit denominator is exactly (4), not a Jacobian minor
which has only been shown generically nonzero.

On the presentation generators \(E=\partial_t\), so
\(Q=[T,E]\) is minus the t-derivative of the coefficients
in (9). Its actual values are
\[
\begin{gathered}
Qu=Qv=0,\qquad
QY=-\kappa^{-1}\rho F_Z,\qquad
QZ=\kappa^{-1}\rho F_Y,\qquad
Qt=-\kappa^{-1}\chi F_Z.
\end{gathered}
\tag{10}
\]
The known nonvanishing \(\delta Y=QY\ne0\) in (2)
forces \(\rho\ne0\) and \(F_Z\ne0\) in A. No
primitivity of Q or of its restriction is assumed here.

## 5. Descent to an actual quadratic equation over S

Let \(K=\operatorname{Frac}S\), and write
\(\psi/\rho=r_1Y+r_0\in K[Y]\).
The derivation
\[
L_0=\partial_Y-(r_1Y+r_0)\partial_Z
\quad\text{on }K[Y,Z],\qquad
U=Z+\frac{r_1}{2}Y^2+r_0Y,
\qquad K[Y,Z]=K[Y,U]
\tag{11}
\]
is a genuine triangular locally nilpotent derivation with
\(L_0Y=1\), \(L_0U=0\). Equation (8) says
\(F\mid L_0F\) in this polynomial ring. In the coordinates
(Y,U), a nonzero Y-derivative has strictly smaller Y-degree
than the original polynomial. Thus \(L_0F=0\), and
\(F=f(U)\) for some \(f\in K[U]\).

By primitivity and Gauss's lemma, F is still irreducible
over K, so f is irreducible and nonconstant. The image of U
in \(A_K\) is algebraic over K. Since characteristic zero
is separable, the derivation \(\delta\), which kills K,
kills every element algebraic over K. But
\(\ker(\delta|_{A_K})=K\): this is the exact localization
of \(\ker(\delta|_A)=S\) by its nonzero elements.
Thus the image of U belongs to K, and f has degree one.
No algebraic closedness of K is assumed.

It follows that the original polynomial F has exactly the
possible monomials Z, Y squared, Y, and 1. Its coefficients
already belong to S, so, up to its initial complex-unit choice,
\[
\boxed{\quad F=a_2(u,v)Z+b_2(u,v)Y^2+c_2(u,v)Y+d_2(u,v),
\qquad a_2\ne0.\quad}
\tag{12}
\]
The four coefficients are primitive as a tuple over S.
The index 2 distinguishes these coefficients from the earlier
matrix polynomials a,b; it is not a degree assertion in u,v.
They may have arbitrarily high base degree.
No coefficient, including \(a_2\), has been shown to be a unit.

## 6. A bounded derived-flow conclusion in the original frame

Substitute (12) into (10). Since Q kills S and \(\chi\)
is affine in Y, one obtains
\[
QY=-\frac{\rho a_2}{\kappa}\in S\setminus\{0\},\qquad
QZ=\frac{\rho}{\kappa}(2b_2Y+c_2),\qquad
Qt=-\frac{a_2}{\kappa}\chi(Y).
\tag{13}
\]
Therefore \(Q^2Y=0\), while \(Q^2Z,Q^2t\in S\).
Equation (2) reconstructs every original coordinate as an
S-linear combination of t,Y,Z and elements of S. Hence
\[
\boxed{Q^3x_1=Q^3x_2=Q^3x_3=Q^3w=0.}
\tag{14}
\]
The pass78 Wronskian theorem proves that \(Q^2(x)\) is
not rationally parallel to \(Q(x)\), so it is nonzero.
Thus the derived coordinate flow degree is exactly two
in this stated nonlinear supplied-good branch. This does
not answer the same question outside these hypotheses or
after an arbitrary supplied-slice adjustment.

## 7. Controls, proof boundary and provenance

For the retained conic seed, use intrinsic base (u,v)=(a,c),
\(H=(1,a,a^2)^t\), \(\nu=(a^2,-2a,1)\),
\(V=(0,1,2a)^t\), \(W=(0,0,1)^t\), \(P=-c\).
The actual relative equation is \(F=Z-a-Y^2\).
The formulas give \(\ell_a=2Y\), \(\ell_c=1\),
\(\kappa=-1\), \(\rho=1\), and \(\psi=-2Y\).
Thus \(Ta=1\), \(Tc=-2Y\), \(QY=1\), and \(QZ=2Y\),
as direct differentiation of the retained inverse confirms.
This is an already recognized action, not a new example.

An inline exact symbolic calculation checked the expansion of
the displayed five-by-five determinant and its five cofactors.
That is a sign control, not a new standalone unittest method
or a universal proof. The determinant, integrability, generic
factor and original-flow arguments are written above in full.

The proof supplies neither an S-point of the hypersurface nor
an everywhere nonzero primitive relative Jacobian field.
For example, a presentation of the form (12) alone permits
the retained smooth hypersurface \(uZ-Y^2-v=0\), whose
primitive relative field has the proper fixed-point ideal
\((u,Y)\). Its ambient algebra happens to be polynomial;
it is not asserted to realize all the present original-frame
data. This distinguishes the normal form from the unresolved
primitive-freeness problem.
