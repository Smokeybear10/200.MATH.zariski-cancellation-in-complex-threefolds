# Four residual models for the supplied rank-one bad-quadratic sector

Status: working finite-frontier synthesis. The homogeneous classification
is already proved in [184](weight-four-positive-bad-rank-normal-forms-pass184-working.md).
The new conclusion here is its combination with the retained203 and211
recognition results and the product-radical exclusions227--233.
No recognition or exclusion of the four residual models is claimed.

## 1. The precise reduction

Let
\[
\mathscr B=\mathbb C[X,Y,Z,W]=A[u],\qquad E=\partial_u,\qquad
\deg_E(X,Y,Z,W)=(1,1,2,2),\qquad A=\ker E.
\tag{1}
\]
These are actual polynomial coordinates. With
\(a=EX,\ b=EY,\ e=E^2Z/2,\ h=E^2W/2\), assume the entire kernel of
the leading evaluation
\[
\mathbb C[\mathsf X,\mathsf Y,\mathsf Z,\mathsf W]\longrightarrow A[\zeta],
\qquad(\mathsf X,\mathsf Y,\mathsf Z,\mathsf W)
\longmapsto(a\zeta,b\zeta,e\zeta^2,h\zeta^2)
\]
is generated by an irreducible weight-four polynomial \(F\).
The weights are \(1,1,2,2\). Assume the homogeneous quadratic part
of \(F\) in the two bad variables has rank exactly one.

**Finite-frontier conclusion.** Either \(A\simeq\mathbb C^{[3]}\)
by a retained theorem, or an actual homogeneous polynomial change of
the four coordinates gives the same exact degree profile and one of
the following full initial kernels:
\[
\boxed{\begin{array}{ll}
(Z^2-XYW-X^4),&(Z^2-XYW-X^4-Y^4),\\
(Z^2-X^2W-XY^3),&(Z^2-X^2W-Y^4).
\end{array}}
\tag{2}
\]
The four models are distinct under the homogeneous group used below.
This does not assert distinctness under arbitrary polynomial
automorphisms, or the existence of an actual sliced frame in any of
these four classes.

## 2. Actual homogeneous transformations and their inverses

The permitted maps are
\[
\binom{X'}{Y'}=M\binom XY,\qquad
\binom{Z'}{W'}=N\binom ZW+
 \binom{S_2(X,Y)}{T_2(X,Y)},\qquad
M,N\in\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb C).
\tag{3}
\]
Here \(S_2,T_2\) are homogeneous quadratics with complex coefficients.
The inverse first uses \(M^{-1}\) to recover \(X,Y\), subtracts the
two quadratics at that recovered pair, and then uses \(N^{-1}\).
Both compositions are the identity. All denominators are nonzero
complex constants.

After a constant bad change, a nonzero scalar multiple of \(F\) is
\(Z^2+A_2Z+B_2W+C_4\). The actual square completion
\(Z'=Z+A_2/2\) gives
\[
F=Z'^2-q_2(X,Y)W-p_4(X,Y),\qquad
q_2=-B_2,\qquad p_4=A_2^2/4-C_4.
\tag{4}
\]
Multiplying the generator by a nonzero scalar never changes its ideal.
For \(q_2\ne0\), write \(p_4=q_2T_2+r_4\).
The actual translation \(W'=W+T_2(X,Y)\) replaces \(p_4\) by \(r_4\).
Thus the residual quartic is its class modulo \(q_2\operatorname{Sym}^2\).

For clarity, this is also the complete residual invariant under (3),
not just a convenient construction. Once the goods are fixed, a bad
linear transformation preserving the square direction has the form
\(Z'=\alpha Z+A_2,\ W'=\beta W+\gamma Z+B_2\), with
\(\alpha\beta\ne0\). Substitution and square completion give
\[
q_2'=\frac{\alpha^2}{\beta}q_2,\qquad
p_4'=\alpha^2p_4-\frac{\alpha^2}{\beta}q_2B_2+
                 \frac{\alpha^2\gamma^2}{4\beta^2}q_2^2.
\tag{5}
\]
The new completed square coordinate is
\(Z'-A_2+\alpha\gamma q_2/(2\beta)\).
Consequently the residue changes only by a nonzero scalar and a
good-variable linear substitution. The vanishing, distinct-root type,
and double-root type of \(q_2\) are invariant.

## 3. The six nonzero-coefficient models

If \(q_2\) has distinct roots, a good linear change and a bad scaling
make \(q_2=XY\). Its residual quartic is
\(a_4X^4+b_4Y^4\). The actual diagonal map
\[
X'=rX,\quad Y'=sY,\quad Z'=zZ,\quad
W'=\frac{z^2}{rs}W
\]
changes its coefficients to \(z^2a_4/r^4,\ z^2b_4/s^4\).
Each nonzero coefficient can therefore be normalized to one.
Interchanging \(X,Y\) interchanges the two residues. This gives
\[
Z^2-XYW,\qquad Z^2-XYW-X^4,\qquad
Z^2-XYW-X^4-Y^4.
\tag{6}
\]
For example take \(z=1\), choose \(r^4=a_4\) when \(a_4\ne0\)
and \(r=1\) otherwise, and similarly for \(s\).
The choices are complex constants. The stabilizer of the unordered
root pair of \(XY\) is diagonal up to interchange, so the three
zero/nonzero patterns are inequivalent under (3).

If \(q_2\) has a double root, normalize it to \(X^2\).
The residual is \(a_4XY^3+b_4Y^4\).
When \(b_4\ne0\), the actual map
\(X'=X,\ Y'=Y+a_4X/(4b_4)\) removes the \(X'Y'^3\) coefficient
after expressing the old quartic in the new goods.
The remaining difference from \(b_4Y'^4\) is divisible by \(X'^2\)
and is removed by a quadratic \(W\)-translation.
The diagonal maps
\(X'=rX,\ Y'=sY,\ Z'=zZ,\ W'=z^2W/r^2\)
change the two residual coefficients to
\(z^2a_4/(rs^3),z^2b_4/s^4\).
They normalize each remaining nonzero coefficient. The three models are
\[
Z^2-X^2W,\qquad Z^2-X^2W-XY^3,\qquad Z^2-X^2W-Y^4.
\tag{7}
\]
The zero residue, a nonzero multiple of \(XY^3\), and a residue with
nonzero \(Y^4\) coefficient are distinct. Indeed the stabilizer
substitution \(X\mapsto rX,\ Y\mapsto sY+cX\), modulo \(X^2\),
sends their coefficient pair to
\((a_4rs^3+4b_4s^3c,b_4s^4)\). It preserves exactly these three
possibilities. This agrees with the passive-coordinate calculation above.

Every polynomial in (6)--(7) is irreducible, regardless of shared
factors of \(q_2,p_4\). Treated as a polynomial in \(W\), its
coefficients are \(-q_2\) and \(Z^2-p_4\). Their gcd is one because
the latter has \(Z^2\)-coefficient one. The polynomial is primitive
and linear over the fraction field of \(\mathbb C[X,Y,Z]\), so
Gauss's lemma applies.

## 4. The omitted coefficient and the actual full-kernel gate

If \(q_2=0\), irreducibility of \(Z^2-p_4(X,Y)\) is equivalent to
\(p_4\) not being a square of a binary quadratic.
A rational square which is polynomial is already a polynomial square
by unique factorization. The nonsquare binary quartic types are
\[
X^3Y,\qquad X^2Y(X-Y),\qquad
XY(X-Y)(X-\lambda Y),\quad \lambda\notin\{0,1\},
\tag{8}
\]
up to complex linear substitution and scalar. The last parameters
have the usual unordered-cross-ratio identifications. The zero
quartic and the multiplicity patterns \(4\) and \(2+2\) are squares
and are excluded by irreducibility, not by a dynamical argument.

Apply all transformations to the actual frame (1).
Every new coordinate is a nonzero homogeneous source polynomial of
weight one or two. None belongs to the full ideal \((F)\), whose
nonzero homogeneous elements have weight at least four.
Its leading evaluation is therefore nonzero, so its actual E-degree
is exactly its assigned weight. This preserves the exact \((1,1,2,2)\) profile.
The invertible graded change transports the entire leading kernel,
not merely one relation. Its inverse gives the same statement.

A constant good or bad normalization need not preserve an old selected
partial, coefficient derivation, or return branch. For each normalized
full kernel, rebuild its actual selected chart before applying the
retained theorem for that kernel. The assertion here needs no
coefficient-field coordinate to lift to the original polynomial ring.
