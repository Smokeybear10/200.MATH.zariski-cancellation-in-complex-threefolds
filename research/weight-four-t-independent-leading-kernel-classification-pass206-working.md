# Which weight-four leading kernels have a t-independent ratio?

Status: working exact classification inside a supplied actual1122 frame.
The condition \(q_t=0\) means that the entire leading equation is
independent of the other linear coordinate \(Y\). Its irreducibility
leaves exactly two normal forms, under actual weighted polynomial
coordinate changes:
\[
\mathsf Z^2-\mathsf X^2\mathsf W,\qquad
\mathsf Z\mathsf W-\mathsf X^4.
\tag{1}
\]
Also, \(q_{\rho\rho}\ne0\) follows automatically from \(q_t=0\)
and the full weight-four kernel. This removes one redundant hypothesis
from the zero-R theorem205. It does not remove \(R=0\), preserve a
selected pivot through every normalization, or give arbitrary entry.

## 1. Actual hypotheses and the ratio evaluation ideal

Use the supplied actual setup of
[universal184](weight-four-universal-chart-potentials-pass184-working.md):
\[
\begin{gathered}
B=\mathbb C[X,Y,Z,W]=A[u],\quad E=\partial_u,\quad A=\ker E,\\
\deg_E(X,Y,Z,W)=(1,1,2,2),\\
a=EX,\quad b=EY,\quad e=E^2Z/2,\quad h=E^2W/2,\\
\theta_0:(\mathsf X,\mathsf Y,\mathsf Z,\mathsf W)
       \longmapsto(a\tau,b\tau,e\tau^2,h\tau^2),\\
\ker\theta_0=(F),\qquad
F\text{ irreducible and weighted homogeneous of weight four},
\qquad F_{\mathsf Z}\ne0.
\end{gathered}
\tag{2}
\]
Here \(\tau\) is an indeterminate over \(A\), the entire kernel is
specified, and the named elements of \(B\) are polynomial coordinates.
Universal184 supplies the exact finite separable field
\[
L/\mathbb C(t,\rho),\qquad
t=b/a,\quad \rho=h/a^2,\quad q=e/a^2\in L,
\tag{3}
\]
where \(t,\rho\) are algebraically independent. Its two partials
extend uniquely and commute. No rationality of \(L\) is assumed.

Put \(H(T,Q,P)=F(1,T,Q,P)\). The ratio evaluation map has exactly
the principal kernel
\[
\phi:\mathbb C[T,Q,P]\longrightarrow L,\qquad
(T,Q,P)\longmapsto(t,q,\rho),\qquad \ker\phi=(H).
\tag{4}
\]
Here is a direct verification that uses the full initial ideal.
Localizing the source of \(\theta_0\) at \(\mathsf X\) gives
\[
\mathbb C[\mathsf X^{\pm1},T,Q,P],
\quad T=\mathsf Y/\mathsf X,\quad
Q=\mathsf Z/\mathsf X^2,\quad P=\mathsf W/\mathsf X^2,
\quad F=\mathsf X^4 H(T,Q,P).
\tag{5}
\]
Its kernel is \((H)\), since \(\mathsf X\) is now a unit.
The image of \(\mathsf X\) is \(a\tau\), which is transcendental
over the subfield generated by \(t,q,\rho\). Thus restricting to
\(\mathbb C[T,Q,P]\) is precisely the map \(\phi\), with the same
kernel contracted from the Laurent extension. This contraction is
\((H)\), as can also be checked coefficient by coefficient in
\(\mathsf X\). In particular \(H\) is nonconstant and irreducible.

## 2. Independence of t is independence of the original Y slot

Differentiate \(\phi(H)=0\) using the exact \(t\)-partial:
\[
\phi(H_T)+\phi(H_Q)q_t=0.
\tag{6}
\]
If \(q_t=0\), then \(H_T\in(H)\). A nonzero derivative \(H_T\)
has smaller \(T\)-degree than \(H\), so this divisibility is
impossible. Therefore \(H_T=0\). Characteristic zero makes \(H\)
independent of \(T\), and (5) makes \(F\) independent of
\(\mathsf Y\).

Conversely, if \(F\) is independent of \(\mathsf Y\), then
\(H_T=0\). The selected-pivot hypothesis \(F_{\mathsf Z}\ne0\)
gives \(H_Q\ne0\). This polynomial has smaller \(Q\)-degree than
\(H\), so it does not belong to \((H)\). Thus \(\phi(H_Q)\ne0\),
and (6) implies \(q_t=0\). We have proved the exact equivalence
\[
q_t=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad F_{\mathsf Y}=0
\tag{7}
\]
in this supplied coordinate system. This is not a statement that
an arbitrary coordinate system can be changed to satisfy it.

## 3. The second rho derivative cannot vanish as well

The simultaneous constants of the two partials on \(L\) are
\(\mathbb C\). To justify this, separability and finiteness in (3)
give
\(\Omega_{L/\mathbb C}=L\,dt\oplus L\,d\rho\).
An element killed by both partials therefore has zero differential.
In a finitely generated characteristic-zero field extension an
element with zero differential is algebraic over the ground field:
if it were transcendental, it could be included in a separating
transcendence basis, where its differential would be nonzero.
Since \(\mathbb C\) is algebraically closed, that element lies in
\(\mathbb C\).

Suppose now that \(q_t=0\) and \(q_{\rho\rho}=0\).
The element \(c_1=q_\rho\) is killed by both partials, hence
belongs to \(\mathbb C\). The same holds for
\(c_0=q-c_1\rho\). This gives the nonzero initial relation
\[
q=c_1\rho+c_0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mathsf Z-c_1\mathsf W-c_0\mathsf X^2\in\ker\theta_0.
\tag{8}
\]
Its weight is two. The ideal generated by a homogeneous
weight-four polynomial contains no nonzero polynomial of weight
two in this positively graded ring. This contradicts (2). Hence
\[
q_t=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad q_{\rho\rho}\ne0.
\tag{9}
\]
The whole initial ideal is essential; a single leading relation
of weight four would not justify this argument.

## 4. Two normal forms, with actual polynomial inverses

By (7), a t-independent ratio has
\[
F=Q_2(\mathsf Z,\mathsf W)
 +\mathsf X^2 L_1(\mathsf Z,\mathsf W)+c\mathsf X^4.
\tag{10}
\]
If \(Q_2=0\), the polynomial is divisible by \(\mathsf X^2\)
and is not irreducible. The quadratic form \(Q_2\) therefore has
rank one or two. All changes below fix \(X,Y\), and are a
constant invertible linear change of \(Z,W\) followed by additions
of constant multiples of \(X^2\). Each is an actual polynomial
automorphism, with inverse obtained by subtracting those multiples
and applying the inverse constant matrix.

In rank one, a constant linear change and a nonzero scalar
multiple of the equation put it in the form
\[
F=\mathsf Z^2+\alpha\mathsf X^2\mathsf Z
 +\beta\mathsf X^2\mathsf W+c\mathsf X^4.
\tag{11}
\]
Set \(Z'=Z+\alpha X^2/2\) and \(c'=c-\alpha^2/4\).
The equation becomes \(\mathsf Z'^2+\beta\mathsf X^2\mathsf W+
c'\mathsf X^4\). If \(\beta=0\), it factors over \(\mathbb C\);
this also covers \(c'=0\). Thus \(\beta\ne0\). Define
\(W'=-\beta W-c'X^2\). Then
\(F=\mathsf Z'^2-\mathsf X^2\mathsf W'\), with explicit inverse
\[
Z=Z'-\alpha X^2/2,\qquad
W=-(W'+c'X^2)/\beta.
\tag{12}
\]

In rank two, a constant linear change puts the quadratic part
in the form \(\mathsf Z\mathsf W\). Write
\[
F=\mathsf Z\mathsf W+\alpha\mathsf X^2\mathsf Z
 +\beta\mathsf X^2\mathsf W+c\mathsf X^4,\qquad
\gamma=\alpha\beta-c.
\tag{13}
\]
The product \((\mathsf Z+\beta\mathsf X^2)
(\mathsf W+\alpha\mathsf X^2)\) equals \(F+\gamma\mathsf X^4\).
If \(\gamma=0\), \(F\) factors, so \(\gamma\ne0\).
Put \(Z'=Z+\beta X^2\) and \(W'=(W+\alpha X^2)/\gamma\).
Then \(F=\gamma(\mathsf Z'\mathsf W'-\mathsf X^4)\), and the
inverse is
\[
Z=Z'-\beta X^2,\qquad W=\gamma W'-\alpha X^2.
\tag{14}
\]

The transformed bad coordinates still have E-degree exactly two.
Their displayed weight-two leading forms are nonzero, and none
can lie in the full weight-four initial kernel. The good degrees
remain one. The weighted automorphism transports the entire
initial kernel, not merely one relation. Thus (1) is an exhaustive
normal-form list in the specified t-independent sector.

This does not identify the selected derivation \(N\), coefficient
field, or evaluated \(R\) before and after a change of pivot.
Whenever those matter, a fresh chart calculation is required.
In particular this section makes no unsupported claim that an
R-zero or R-nonzero branch is preserved by every normalization.

## 5. Consequence for zero R and the remaining problem

Retain the original chosen chart, and assume \(q_t=0\) and that
its evaluated \(R\) is identically zero in \(L[v]\). Equation (9)
supplies the nonzero second derivative needed in
[the t-independent zero-R recognition theorem205](weight-four-t-independent-zero-r-recognition-pass205-working.md).
That theorem then proves \(A\simeq\mathbb C^{[3]}\), without a
normal-form change or a claim about preservation of R under one.

The zero-R hypothesis remains an additional assumption. Neither
this lemma nor theorem205 excludes or recognizes the nonzero-R
branches. The full cancellation target also includes leading
kernels depending on \(Y\), other time profiles, and arbitrary
coordinate entry. These remain outside the conclusion.
