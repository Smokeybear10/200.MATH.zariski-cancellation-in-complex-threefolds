## 7. An injective positive-weight initial map contradicts the LND

Put \(k_X=\mathbb C(X)\), and use a formal initial variable \(\Xi\)
independent of \(L(X)\). The coordinate profile (19),(21) gives
the weighted initial map
\[
\begin{aligned}
k_X[\mathsf Z,\mathsf Y,\mathsf W]&\longrightarrow L(X)[\Xi],\\
(\mathsf Z,\mathsf Y,\mathsf W)&\longmapsto
(g_0\mathscr L\,\Xi,\ g_0^2\eta\,\Xi^2,\
                                 g_0^2\mathscr N\,\Xi^2).
\end{aligned}
\tag{27}
\]
Its three image elements are algebraically independent.
Indeed their generated fraction field contains the two ratios
(23), which are independent over \(k_X\), and the additional
element \(g_0\mathscr L\Xi\), which is transcendental over
\(L(X)\). Conversely the three image elements are recovered from
that element and the two ratios. Thus the initial map is injective.

Let \(F\) be a nonconstant polynomial in the ACTUAL coordinates
\((Z_1,Y_1,W_1)\) over \(k_X\).
Its top component for the positive weights (1,2,2) is nonzero
and has positive weight. Injectivity of (27) says that its
evaluated leading term cannot cancel. Consequently F has positive
Delta-degree, and \(\Delta F\ne0\). This proves
\[
\ker_R\Delta=k_X.
\tag{28}
\]
It contradicts the nonzero actual LND on the polynomial three-ring
(22), whose kernel has fraction-field transcendence degree two by
the explicit local-slice argument of Section5.
In particular a formal differential control with no actual
polynomial-ring entry would not supply this final contradiction.

The contradiction excludes the entire family493, including \(d=0\).
Under the retained conic assumptions, the \(m_{tt}\ne0\) branch
therefore cannot occur.
