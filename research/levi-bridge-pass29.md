# Finite Lie closure without solvability: the unipotent-radical bridge

Date: 2026-09-13.

There is a stronger sufficient recognition criterion than the finite
solvable criterion in pass 28. If a sliced LND on C^[4] has
finite-dimensional Lie closure with all partials of an actual ambient
coordinate frame, its kernel is C^[3]. The closure need not be
solvable. This is a deduction from published structural theorems,
not a novelty claim or a solution of general cancellation.

The additional primary input is Bruce, du Plessis and Wall,
*Determinacy and unipotency*, Theorem (3.6): the unipotent radical
of an affine algebraic group acting algebraically and transitively
on real or complex affine space is itself transitive. This applies
to arbitrary algebraic actions, not just affine-linear ones.[^bdw]

## 1. The exact statement

Let B=C[x_1,...,x_n], let delta be an LND of B, and let

\[
\mathfrak g
=\operatorname{Lie}_{\mathbb C}
  \langle\delta,\partial_{x_1},\ldots,\partial_{x_n}\rangle
\tag{1}
\]

denote the actual complex Lie algebra generated by these derivations.
If dim_C(g) is finite, then delta is triangular in some polynomial
coordinate system of B. No solvability assumption on g is needed.

If n=4 and delta admits a slice s, then

\[
\ker\delta\simeq\mathbb C^{[3]}.
\tag{2}
\]

More generally, the same conclusions hold whenever the additive
subgroup generated by delta lies in an affine algebraic subgroup of
Aut(A^n) acting transitively. Equation (1) is a concrete sufficient
way to obtain that situation. Generic transitivity alone for an
arbitrary non-unipotent group is not substituted for transitivity.

## 2. Integrating the finite closure

All generators in (1) are LNDs. Kraft and Zaidenberg's Theorem
3.1.1(1) therefore gives a connected algebraic group G in Aut(A^n)
with Lie algebra g. Its proof constructs G as the group generated
by the corresponding additive one-parameter subgroups, so G contains
the entire standard translation subgroup and acts transitively.[^kz]

The group is affine. For example, the algebraic action on the
finitely generated coordinate ring admits a finite-dimensional
G-stable subspace containing algebra generators. Since the action
is effective, this supplies a faithful finite-dimensional algebraic
representation. This is the usual linearity argument for an
algebraic subgroup of the automorphism group of an affine variety.

Finite dimension is used here together with the LND generators.
It is not being confused with local finiteness of an arbitrary
derivation action, and no assertion is made that every member of
g is an LND. In particular, a nonzero Levi factor is allowed.

## 3. The radical is transitive, but need not contain delta

Set U=R_u(G). By Bruce--du Plessis--Wall Theorem (3.6), U acts
transitively on A^n.[^bdw]

The directly read complex proof is useful for auditing the Levi
issue. Let G_x be an isotropy group and K_x a maximal compact
subgroup of it. The Euclidean homogeneous space G/G_x forces K_x
to be a maximal compact subgroup of G. Its complexification, which
is its Zariski closure in G, is a maximal reductive subgroup of G
contained in G_x. It is a Levi complement to U. Thus G_x U=G,
which proves transitivity of U. The theorem does not linearize the
given action or assume that the particular additive subgroup under
investigation is normal.

Now put H=exp(C delta), with H trivial if delta=0. The following
step retains H even when it is not a subgroup of U. Let

\[
\pi:G\longrightarrow G/U,\qquad
K=\pi^{-1}(\pi(H)).
\tag{3}
\]

The image pi(H) is a closed algebraic subgroup and is unipotent,
being a quotient of H. Thus K is closed, and on complex points

\[
K=UH=HU.
\tag{4}
\]

Indeed pi(g)=pi(h) implies gh^(-1) belongs to U. There is an exact
sequence of algebraic groups

\[
1\longrightarrow U\longrightarrow K
 \longrightarrow\pi(H)\longrightarrow1.
\tag{5}
\]

Extensions of unipotent algebraic groups are unipotent, so K is
unipotent.[^milne] It is transitive because it contains U, and it
contains H by construction. Normality of U suffices; normality
of H is not asserted or needed. In characteristic zero pi(H) is
either trivial or an additive one-parameter group, although this
stronger observation is not needed for (5).

This is the actual replacement for the invalid step delta in Lie(U).
It produces a possibly larger unipotent subgroup containing delta,
not a claim that the whole original G is unipotent.

## 4. Actual triangular coordinates and the sliced fourfold case

Kraft and Zaidenberg's Theorem 5.2.2 applies to the transitive
unipotent algebraic group K, a special case of a nested unipotent
group. It gives a polynomial automorphism conjugating K into the
unipotent de Jonquieres group.[^kz] Hence delta is strictly triangular
in actual polynomial coordinates. This proves the first assertion
of Section 1 in every n.

When n=4 and delta(s)=1, the slice decomposition is
B=(ker delta)[s]. The additive action is therefore proper: its
action map identifies Ga times Spec(B) with the closed relation
Spec(B) times_(Spec(ker delta)) Spec(B) inside Spec(B) squared.
The final unnumbered corollary of Dubouloz--Finston--Jaradat on
printed p. 1963 then makes this proper triangular action on A4 a
translation.[^dfj] Its invariant ring is C^[3], proving (2).

The dimensional restriction belongs to this last recognition step.
We do not claim that arbitrary proper triangular actions in every
dimension are translations. A sliced intrinsic tangent direction
on A follows after (2) by choosing coordinates of A; no particular
element of the original g is asserted to descend with a slice.

## 5. The nonsolvable control now passes

Use the already checked pass-28 example, with two passive variables
z_1,z_2 adjoined:

\[
u=x+y^2,\qquad v=y,\qquad
\delta=(1+4y(x+y^2))\partial_x-2(x+y^2)\partial_y
       =\partial_u-2u\partial_v.
\tag{6}
\]

The full closure with the original four coordinate partials is

\[
\mathfrak g=
(\mathfrak{sl}_2\ltimes\mathbb C^2)\oplus\mathbb C^2,
\qquad\dim\mathfrak g=7.
\tag{7}
\]

The five-field bracket table and nonlinear inverse are already
proved and checked in the [pass-28 frame note](translation-frame-pass28.md).
The two new dimensions in (7) are the central fields partial_(z_1)
and partial_(z_2). In the coordinates u,v,z_1,z_2 the integrating
group is (SL2 semidirect Ga^2) times Ga^2, and its unipotent radical
is the transitive four-dimensional translation group.

Delta does not belong to the Lie algebra of that radical because
of its nonconstant coefficient -2u. Nevertheless U H is the
five-dimensional unipotent group with Lie algebra spanned by

\[
\partial_u,\ \partial_v,\ u\partial_v,
\ \partial_{z_1},\ \partial_{z_2}.
\tag{8}
\]

This makes the distinction in Section 3 concrete. The original
nonsolvable frame fails the nilpotent and solvable tests but meets
the new finite-closure criterion. The known invariant coordinate
v+u^2 and slice u independently confirm the conclusion; no new
sample classification or standalone checker is needed.

## 6. Finite certificates and the remaining quantifier

A positive finite-closure certificate can consist of finitely many
explicit polynomial vector fields spanning a complex vector space L,
constant-coefficient expressions placing delta and all coordinate
partials in L, and a constant-coefficient bracket table proving
[L,L] is contained in L. Then the actual algebra (1) is contained
in L and is finite-dimensional. The displayed basis need not
consist entirely of LNDs: the integration input is the known LND
generating set in (1).

This is broader than requiring every sufficiently long Lie word to
vanish. It does not supply a universal degree bound, a terminating
search for L, or a proof that a given sliced delta has finite closure
with an arbitrary prescribed ambient frame.

For a fixed cylinder B=A[s]=C^[4], allowing the frame itself to vary
gives an exact equivalence:

1. A is C^[3].
2. There exists an actual polynomial coordinate frame of B whose
   partials have finite-dimensional closure with delta=partial_s.

The implication 2 implies 1 is this note. For 1 implies 2, choose
three coordinates of A together with s; all four partials commute.
Thus constructing a successful unspecified frame for every cylinder
would solve the original recognition problem. We have proved the
recognition implication, not that universal existence statement.
We also do not claim the converse for a fixed prescribed frame.
