# Every compatible quadratic in the faithful model lies in the bad-coordinate algebra

For the faithful pass57 model with its fixed bad pair, every actual
function of generic total degree at most two can be classified.
The actual good-column graph identities force each such function
into the smaller algebra generated by the bad pair alone. Hence
no choice of two actual quadratic good generators, or even any
collection of them, can realize the required full frame.

This extends the invertible-row repair obstruction of
[pass58](faithful-frame-rank-obstruction-pass58.md). No invertibility
assumption on a good-generator change matrix is made here. The bad
pair remains fixed; changing that pair is outside this conclusion.

## 1. Actual ring and generic coordinates

Retain the faithful model
\[
R=\mathbb C[a,v]_{(a,v-1)},\quad L=\mathbb C(a,v),\qquad
\mathcal A=R[t,z]/(t^2-v-az),\quad
\mathcal B=\mathcal A[s],\quad E=\partial_s.
\tag{1}
\]
Its actual polynomial-cylinder localization, smooth faithful
quotient, supplied slice, and minimum-three slice gauge were proved
in [pass57](faithful-quadratic-slice-boundary-pass57.md). Here \(v\)
is a unit, \(a\) is not, and
\[
\mathcal A=R[z]\oplus tR[z].
\tag{2}
\]
Define
\[
X=w=as+tz,\quad q=ts+z^2,\quad Y=vw+t,\quad T=Y-vX=t.
\tag{3}
\]
The generic bad pair is \((X,Y)\), equivalently \((X,T)\), an
invertible linear change over \(R\). The exact formulas are
\[
\mathcal B_L=L[X,T],\qquad
z=\frac{T^2-v}{a},\qquad
q=\frac{XT}{a}-\frac{v(T^2-v)}{a^2},
\]
\[
EX=a,\qquad EY=av,\qquad ET=0.
\tag{4}
\]
The generic inverses do not invert \(a\) in the actual ring.
Put
\[
\mathcal W_2=\mathcal B\cap L[X,T]_{\le2}.
\tag{5}
\]
This means generic total degree, not time degree or the intrinsic
degree of the other derivation on \(\mathcal A\).

## 2. The complete six-element quadratic module

The exact answer is
\[
\boxed{\mathcal W_2=
R\cdot1\oplus RT\oplus RX\oplus RX^2\oplus Rz\oplus Rq.}
\tag{6}
\]
Start with any generic quadratic
\[
F=A X^2+BXT+CT^2+DX+JT+F_0,
\qquad A,B,C,D,J,F_0\in L.
\tag{7}
\]
Substitute \(X=as+tz,\ T=t\), and reduce \(t^2=v+az\).
The unique monic-basis expression is
\[
\begin{aligned}
F={}&a^2A s^2+s(2aA\,tz+aB\,t+aD)\\
&+aA z^3+(vA+aB)z^2+(vB+aC)z+vC+F_0\\
&+t(Dz+J).
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]
Uniqueness follows because the basis (2) stays free over \(L[z]\),
and adjoining \(s\) is a polynomial extension.

If \(F\) is actual, the coefficient of \(s\) gives
\(aA,aB,aD\in R\); the constant odd part gives \(D,J\in R\).
The constant even part also gives
\[
aA,\quad vA+aB,\quad vB+aC,\quad vC+F_0\in R.
\tag{9}
\]
Since \(aB\in R\) and \(v\) is a unit, (9) forces \(A\in R\).
The \(s^2\) condition is then automatic. Set
\[
b=aB,\qquad c=aC+vB,\qquad f=F_0+vC.
\tag{10}
\]
These belong to \(R\), and direct substitution in (4) gives
\[
F=A X^2+DX+JT+f+cz+bq.
\tag{11}
\]
Conversely every expression (11) with six coefficients in \(R\)
is actual and generically quadratic. The six elements in (6)
are independent over \(L\): first their \(XT\) coefficients
detect \(q\), then their \(T^2\) coefficients detect \(z\);
the four remaining monomials are independent. This proves (6).

The proof does not assume that all pairs of good generators are
invertible coefficient changes on \(z,q\). Indeed,
\[
vz+aq=XT,\qquad \mathcal B=R[X,T,q],
\tag{12}
\]
using that \(v\) is a unit and the actual pass57 slice formula.
Thus multiplication of bad coordinates can recover a module-basis
element. A matrix-invertibility argument would not exhaust the
present algebra-generation question.

## 3. Euler and Hessian compatibility remove every pole

Write an arbitrary element of (6) as
\[
Q=A X^2+DX+JT+F+cz+bq,
\qquad A,D,J,F,c,b\in R.
\tag{13}
\]
Let \(f_1=a,\ f_2=av\). Impose explicitly the necessary
good-column identities of
[pass44, equations (9) and (14)](generic-bad-pair-pass44.md):
\[
\mathscr H h=2h,\qquad
Q_2=\frac14(X,Y)\operatorname{Hess}_{f_1,f_2}(h)
\binom{X}{Y},\qquad
\mathscr H=f_1\partial_{f_1}+f_2\partial_{f_2}.
\tag{14}
\]
Here \(h=E^2Q\), and \(Q_2\) is the homogeneous quadratic part
over \(L\). These equations come from the true good coordinate
partials and their adjoint bounds, not from quadraticity alone.
The two speeds generate exactly \(L\), so their base partials
are unambiguous.

Since \(E^2z=E^2q=0\), one has \(h=2a^2A\). Moreover
\(\mathscr H=a\partial_a\), holding \(v\) fixed. The first
equation of (14) forces \(\partial_aA=0\), hence
\[
A\in S:=\mathbb C(v)\cap R=\mathbb C[v]_{(v-1)}.
\tag{15}
\]
The equality follows by checking the \(v-1\) valuation of a
reduced rational function of \(v\). This ring is closed under
differentiation in \(v\). The exact Hessian calculation in
pass58 therefore gives
\[
Q_2=A X^2+A'XT+\frac{A''}{2}T^2.
\tag{16}
\]
The actual expression (13) has quadratic coefficients \(b/a\)
at \(XT\) and \(c/a-vb/a^2\) at \(T^2\). Thus the required
coefficient identities are exactly
\[
b=aA',\qquad c=vA'+\frac a2A''.
\tag{17}
\]
Substitution in the entire function, including its constant
term, yields
\[
Q=A X^2+A'XT+\frac{A''}{2}T^2+DX+JT+\ell,\qquad
\ell=F-\frac v2A''\in R.
\tag{18}
\]
In particular,
\[
\boxed{Q\in\mathcal W_2\text{ satisfying (14)}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad Q\in R[X,T].}
\tag{19}
\]
This is a statement about each actual quadratic separately,
with no assumption on how multiple good functions were chosen.

## 4. Exact normal form with the linear-gradient identities

The remaining linear part of the actual graph theorem asks
for \(k\in L\) with
\[
\mathscr Hk=k,\qquad
Q_1=(\partial_{f_1}k)X+(\partial_{f_2}k)Y,
\tag{20}
\]
where \(Q_1\) is the homogeneous linear part. In (18) its
coefficients in \((X,Y)\) are \(D-vJ,J\). Therefore (20) gives
\[
k=f_1(D-vJ)+f_2J=aD.
\tag{21}
\]
The Euler equation forces \(\partial_aD=0\), so \(D\in S\).
Differentiating \(k=f_1D(f_2/f_1)\) gives
\[
\partial_{f_1}k=D-vD',\qquad
\partial_{f_2}k=D',\qquad J=D'.
\tag{22}
\]
The complete normal form is consequently
\[
\boxed{
Q=A(v)X^2+A'(v)XT+\frac{A''(v)}2T^2
+D(v)X+D'(v)T+\ell(a,v),\quad
A,D\in S,\quad\ell\in R.}
\tag{23}
\]
Conversely every expression (23) is actual, lies in
\(\mathcal W_2\), and satisfies (14) and (20) with
\(h=2a^2A,\ k=aD\). Thus this is an if-and-only-if
classification of the specified graph identities.
It is not a converse asserting the existence of an ambient
coordinate system or of the full adjoint frame.

## 5. No compatible good generators for this fixed pair

The actual algebra \(R[X,T]\) is strictly smaller than
\(\mathcal B\). In particular, the unique generic polynomial
expression for \(z\) has \(T^2\) coefficient \(1/a\), so
\[
z\notin R[X,T].
\tag{24}
\]
Any collection of quadratics satisfying (19), and hence
any pair satisfying the full normal form (23), generates
with \(X,Y\) only a subalgebra of \(R[X,T]\).
It cannot generate \(\mathcal B\).

This excludes arbitrary nonlinear choices of good functions
provided their resulting generic degrees remain at most two
and the bad pair stays fixed. It does not merely exclude
the invertible-row repair family from pass58. Even adding
further individually compatible quadratics cannot evade (24).

The example retains its supplied slice, smooth faithful
quotient, independent bad speeds, and polynomial-cylinder
localization. Those properties do not supply the missing
full graph identities. Changes of the bad pair or departure
from the generic quadratic class remain outside this result.
