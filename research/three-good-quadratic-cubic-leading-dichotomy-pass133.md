# A quadratic-or-primitive-cubic dichotomy for actual coordinate initials

Every actual sliced dense three-good quadratic frame has a leading
relation of weighted degree two or three. In the quadratic case the
acceleration is removable by a quadratic change of the last coordinate.
Otherwise there is a unique primitive mixed-cubic relation, up to a
nonzero scalar.

This supplies the leading-relation existence step in the stated class.
It does not prove either subsequent correction condition or supply such
a coordinate frame for an arbitrary sliced action.

## 1. Statement

Assume
\[
\begin{gathered}
B=\mathbb C[x_1,x_2,x_3,z]=A[u],\qquad E=\partial_u,\qquad A=\ker E,\\
E^2x_i=0,\qquad a_i=Ex_i,\qquad E^3z=0,\qquad q=E^2z\ne0,\\
\operatorname{trdeg}_{\mathbb C}\mathbb C(a_1,a_2,a_3)=2,\\
P(a_1,a_2,a_3)\ne0
\quad\text{for every nonzero homogeneous }
P\in\mathbb C[T_1,T_2,T_3].
\end{gathered}
\tag{1}
\]
All four displayed original generators are actual polynomial coordinates.
The slice \(u\) need not be one of them or be an ambient coordinate.
Let
\[
W_2=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb C}\{a_i a_j:1\le i\le j\le3\}.
\tag{2}
\]
Exactly one of the following occurs.

1. There is a homogeneous quadratic \(J_2\) with \(q=2J_2(a)\).
   The actual elementary change \(z'=z-J_2(x)\) satisfies \(E^2z'=0\).
2. One has \(q\notin W_2\), and there are a nonzero homogeneous linear
   form \(L\) and a homogeneous cubic \(F_3\), with
   \(\gcd(L,F_3)=1\), such that
   \[
   L(a)q=2F_3(a).
   \tag{3}
   \]
   The pair \((L,F_3)\) is unique up to simultaneous nonzero scalar.

The quadratic polynomial in the first alternative is unique as well,
by homogeneous cone density. If \(q=0\), the first alternative holds
with \(J_2=0\) directly; it is excluded in (1) only to keep the last
coordinate's exact degree equal to two throughout the proof.

## 2. The actual first relation and its multiplicity estimate

Write \(x_i=b_i+a_i u\) and \(z=c_0+c_1u+(q/2)u^2\).
The actual leading-derivation construction for \(T=\partial_z\) gives
a nonzero locally nilpotent derivation \(N\) of \(A\) that kills
all \(a_i\) and \(q\). Its kernel \(S\) has transcendence degree two,
by the local-slice argument.[^1] No affine-plane presentation for
\(S\) is needed here.

Assign original source weights \((1,1,1,2)\) to
\((X_1,X_2,X_3,Z)\). The map
\[
\begin{gathered}
\theta:\mathbb C[X_1,X_2,X_3,Z]\longrightarrow A[\tau],\\
X_i\longmapsto a_i\tau,\qquad Z\longmapsto(q/2)\tau^2
\end{gathered}
\tag{4}
\]
has image of transcendence degree three. Its image is contained in
\(S[\tau]\), and its three good images are algebraically independent
by the last condition in (1). Thus its kernel is a height-one prime,
generated by an irreducible weighted homogeneous polynomial \(\Phi\).
Put \(d=\operatorname{wt}\Phi\). The polynomial \(\Phi\) involves \(Z\),
since no relation in the three good images is possible.
In particular \(\Phi_Z\notin(\Phi)\), in characteristic zero.

For clarity, the following estimate is reconstructed here; the
multiplicity note also gives its sharpness and coefficient refinement.[^2]
For a nonzero polynomial \(P\) in the original coordinates, let \(m\)
be its original weighted degree and \(P_m\) its top weighted part.
If
\[
P_m=\Phi^k\Psi,\qquad k\ge1,\qquad\Phi\nmid\Psi,
\tag{5}
\]
then
\[
\deg_E P(x,z)\ \ge\ m-4k.
\tag{6}
\]
Indeed the actual dense cofactor bound says that \(T\) raises
\(E\)-degree by at most two on every element of \(A[u]\).[^3]
Differentiating the top weighted part \(k\) times in \(Z\) gives
\(\partial_Z^k P_m\equiv k!\Psi\Phi_Z^k\pmod\Phi\).
Its evaluation under (4) is nonzero. Lower weighted pieces cannot
contribute at weight \(m-2k\). Consequently
\[
\deg_E T^kP(x,z)=m-2k
\le \deg_E P(x,z)+2k,
\tag{7}
\]
which proves (6).

These statements concern the algebra of the four coordinate initials,
not an assertion that those initials generate all of
\(\operatorname{gr}_E B=A[\tau]\).

## 3. Invariant growth excludes weighted degree four and higher

Identify an invariant \(f\in A\setminus\mathbb C\) with its unique
polynomial \(P\) in the original coordinates. Its positive original
weighted degree is \(m\), whereas its \(E\)-degree is zero.
The coefficient of \(u^m\) is zero, so \(P_m\in(\Phi)\).
Write \(P_m=\Phi^k\Psi\) with exact multiplicity \(k\ge1\).
Equation (6) gives
\[
kd\le m\le4k.
\tag{8}
\]
There are nonconstant invariants because \(\operatorname{trdeg}A=3\).
Thus \(d\le4\).

Suppose \(d=4\). Equation (8) forces \(m=4k\) and
\(\operatorname{wt}\Psi=0\). All original weights are positive,
so \(\Psi\) is a nonzero complex constant. Therefore the highest
original-weight form of every nonconstant invariant is a scalar
multiple of a power of \(\Phi\).

Here is a direct dimension argument, without a finite initial-basis
assumption. Let \(F_MA\) consist of the invariants of original weighted
degree at most \(M\). The top-part map embeds
\(F_MA/F_{M-1}A\) into the homogeneous polynomials of weight \(M\).
The preceding paragraph bounds its dimension by one when \(4\mid M\),
and by zero otherwise. Also \(F_0A=\mathbb C\). Hence
\[
\dim_{\mathbb C} F_MA\le \lfloor M/4\rfloor+1.
\tag{9}
\]
Choose three algebraically independent invariants \(f_1,f_2,f_3\),
and let \(D>0\) be the largest of their original weighted degrees.
Their monomials of total exponent at most \(j\) are linearly independent,
and all belong to \(F_{Dj}A\). It follows that
\[
\binom{j+3}{3}\le \dim_{\mathbb C}F_{Dj}A
\le \lfloor Dj/4\rfloor+1
\quad\text{for every }j\ge0,
\tag{10}
\]
an impossibility for large \(j\). Thus \(d\ne4\), and \(d\le3\).

The positivity of the original weights is essential to this counting
argument. No such assertion is made for weights reassigned after
adjoining new invariant generators.

## 4. The two remaining weighted forms

One has \(d\ne1\), since a weight-one relation would involve only the
three goods. If \(d=2\), dependence on \(Z\) forces, after scaling,
\[
\Phi=Z-J_2(X),\qquad q=2J_2(a).
\tag{11}
\]
The elementary inverse of \(z'=z-J_2(x)\) is
\(z=z'+J_2(x)\). Since \(E^2J_2(x)=2J_2(a)=q\), the displayed
second-iterate vanishing follows.

If \(d=3\), there can be only one power of \(Z\), and
\[
\Phi=L(X)Z-F_3(X),\qquad L\ne0.
\tag{12}
\]
Irreducibility of \(\Phi\) implies \(\gcd(L,F_3)=1\), and
(4) gives (3). If \(q\) belonged to \(W_2\), a weight-two
relation (11) would instead generate the kernel, a contradiction.
Any other mixed pair gives another weight-three element of
the principal ideal \((\Phi)\), so differs by a constant multiple.
This proves the dichotomy.

## 5. Scope of the reduction

In the supplied dense quadratic class, existence of the leading
primitive relation is no longer an additional assumption once
\(q\notin W_2\). The unresolved conditions are now the two actual
lower-degree corrections in the retained criterion.[^4]
Neither equation (3) nor the growth argument asserts that they pass.

There also remains a separate, unrestricted question: whether an
arbitrary sliced polynomial fourfold admits a frame of the form (1).
This note does not address that entry question. General cancellation
therefore remains unsolved. The argument is a private working proof,
not a proof-assistant certificate, expert endorsement, or novelty claim.
