# Squarefree plinths: exact divisor models and the remaining point defects

General complex-threefold cancellation remains UNSOLVED. This note proves
an exact local description, an all-degree divisorial formula, and a finite
exact criterion for all-image principality. It does not identify an
arbitrary stably polynomial threefold with affine space.
The local description is also a special case of established PID results;
the proof below is self-contained. Novelty is UNKNOWN.

## 1. Hypotheses and conclusions

Let k be the complex field and A a finitely generated factorial
k-domain of dimension three. Let D be a primitive nonzero locally
nilpotent derivation.
Assume

    C=ker_A D=k[a,b],   pl(D)=D(A) intersect C=pC,   Dr=p,
    p=f_1 ... f_s nonconstant and squarefree in C.

Here primitive means that D(A) is contained in no principal ideal
generated by a nonunit of A. The f_i are pairwise nonassociate primes.
Put K=Frac(C), F_n=ker D^(n+1), and I_n=D^n(F_n), with I_0=C.
Equivalently I_n=D^n(A) intersect C.

For every i, let O_i=C_(f_i), let kappa_i=Frac(C/f_i C), and put
A_i=A tensor_C O_i. There is an integer d_i>=2 and a monic polynomial
G_i(T) in O_i[T] of degree d_i such that, retaining the supplied r,

    A_i=O_i[r,y_i],       f_i y_i=G_i(r),
    A_i/f_i A_i=L_i[y_i], L_i=kappa_i[rbar], [L_i:kappa_i]=d_i.       (1)

The reduction of G_i is irreducible and separable. The action on the
special fiber is a nonzero constant translation in y_i. In particular
this fiber is geometrically reduced, but need not be geometrically
connected. Its geometric components are d_i affine lines.

For every n>=0 the exact divisorial closure of the image ideal is

    I_n**=h_n C,  h_n=product_i f_i^(n-floor(n/d_i)).                 (2)

The double dual is identified inside K. More precisely there is an
actual ideal J_n of C with

    I_n=h_n J_n,  (J_n)_(q)=C_(q) for every height-one prime (q).     (3)

There is one finite closed set containing Supp(C/J_n) for EVERY n.
Put N=lcm(d_1,...,d_s). All the image ideals are principal if and
only if J_n=C for 0<=n<=N. This is a finite exact ideal criterion,
not an inference from numerical samples. In addition,

    product_i f_i^floor(n/d_i) belongs to J_n.                       (4)

Consequently J_n=C when n<min_i d_i. In general (2) does not prove
J_n=C. Even principality of every I_n would not prove cancellation.
No polynomial-cylinder hypothesis is needed for these conclusions.

## 2. Prime divisors and the residual curve

The kernel C is factorially closed in A: D-degree is additive on
nonzero products. Every unit of A is invariant, and hence A*=C*=k*.
If a prime f of C factored in A, its factors would lie in C, a
contradiction. Since A is a UFD, fA is prime and fA intersect C=fC.

The same holds after localizing at C minus (f). Write O=C_(f),
kappa=Frac(C/fC), and R=A tensor_C O. Its special fiber

    T=R/fR=(A/fA) tensor_(C/fC) kappa

is an affine kappa-domain of dimension one: A/fA has transcendence
degree two over k, C/fC injects into it and has transcendence degree
one, and localization gives the indicated relative transcendence degree.

Primitivity implies that the induced derivation delta on T is nonzero.
Indeed, if all derivatives vanished after localization, the domain A/fA
and the nonzero images of C minus (f) would imply D(A) subset fA.

Every invariant in T is algebraic over kappa. Otherwise T would have
an invariant transcendence basis of size one, and its fraction field
would be algebraic over an invariant rational function field. In
characteristic zero a derivation vanishing on a field vanishes on
every algebraic extension of that field, contradicting delta nonzero.
Every nonzero algebraic element of T is a unit, by its minimal
polynomial over kappa. A nonzero local-slice derivative can therefore
be inverted inside ker(delta). The slice theorem gives

    T=L[t],   L=ker(delta),   L/kappa finite separable.

Finiteness follows also from L=T/(t), which is a finitely generated
algebraic field extension of kappa. This argument works at every
height-one base prime, without squarefreeness or stable polynomiality.

## 3. Elementary one-stage proof when the plinth exponent is one

Now take f=f_i and write p=f beta in O, with beta a unit. The
supplied r satisfies Dr=f beta. Its reduction rbar lies in L.
Let Gbar be its monic minimal polynomial over kappa and choose a
monic coefficient lift G in O[T] of the same degree d. Then

    y=G(r)/f belongs to R,       Dy=beta G'(r).                      (5)

Let H=O[r,y] subset R. Gauss's lemma gives the exact presentation

    H=O[T,Y]/(fY-G(T)).                                          (6)

Indeed f and G are coprime in O[T]; the displayed primitive linear
polynomial in Y is irreducible, and after inverting f its quotient
is K[T]. Evaluation in K[r] has exactly this kernel.

Reducing (6) modulo f gives H/fH=L_0[Y], where L_0=kappa[rbar]
is a field. The natural map from this ring to R/fR is injective.
Its restriction to L_0 is injective, and ybar is transcendental
over L_0: its derivative beta_bar Gbar'(rbar) is nonzero, whereas
every element algebraic over L_0 is killed by delta. Separability
of Gbar is essential here. Consequently

    H intersect fR=fH.                                          (7)

The local-slice theorem gives R[1/f]=K[r]=H[1/f]. Given z in R,
choose the least N>=0 with f^N z in H. If N>0, that element lies
in H intersect fR=fH. Dividing the equality by f in the domain R
contradicts minimality. Hence N=0, and R=H.

This proves (1), including L=L_0, so the SUPPLIED minimal slice
generates the whole residual constant field. If d=1, write
G(T)=T-c. Equation (5) gives Dy=beta, a unit invariant, yielding
a slice. This contradicts the full localized plinth fO. Thus d>=2.

The argument proves generation, not merely agreement of fraction
fields or an inclusion of a candidate modification. It uses the
actual divisibility contraction (7), not an unsupported global
gluing assertion.

For source comparison, Barkatou and El Kahoui's Theorem 3.1 and
Lemma 4.4 give triangular presentations over a PID for a fixed-point-
free LND with reduced special fibers. The number of stages is the
maximum plinth multiplicity. Their result retains a supplied
minimal slice. Our height-one models satisfy those assumptions:
the generic and special fiber actions are free, and the special
fiber is reduced. The independent proof above handles exponent
one directly. Their PID assumption does not hold for C=k[a,b].[^pid]

## 4. The entire local filtration and global image envelope

In (6), monicity of G makes R a free O[Y]-module with basis
1,r,...,r^(d-1). Thus

    e_n=r^j y^q,   n=j+dq,  0<=j<d,

is an O-basis indexed by all nonnegative integers. In K[r], e_n
has exact r-degree n and leading coefficient f^(-q). Distinct
basis elements have distinct degrees, so cancellation cannot
lower the degree of a nonzero linear combination at its highest
index. Since D=p partial_r,

    (F_n)_(f)=Oe_0+...+Oe_n,
    D^n(e_n)=n! beta^n f^(n-floor(n/d)).                            (8)

This is an all-degree proof, not interpolation from finitely many
derivatives. Invariant localization commutes with F_n and I_n,
so (8) proves the exact valuation in (2) at f_i. At primes not
dividing p, the slice r/p gives A_(q)=C_(q)[r] and I_n C_(q)=C_(q).

The ideal I_n is nonzero since it contains n!p^n, and is finitely
generated since C is Noetherian. The height-one intersection
description of rank-one reflexive modules over a normal domain
now gives (2). UFD divisibility gives I_n subset h_n C, hence
the actual ideal J_n=h_n^(-1)I_n subset C with (3). A proper
ideal having no height-zero or height-one support in the affine
plane has zero-dimensional, hence finite, support. The element
n!p^n proves (4), since n! is a unit.

The point defects in (3) cannot be discarded by replacing an
ideal by its double dual. For example (a,b)**=C but (a,b)!=C.

### 4.1. A single finite support works in every degree

For each i, spread the exact presentation (1) from O_i to a
principal open of Spec C. This requires only finitely many
invariant denominators: those in the coefficients of G_i, one
putting y_i in a localization of A, and those expressing a finite
C-algebra generating set of A as polynomials in r,y_i over O_i.
Let c_i be their product, also multiplied by p/f_i. None of
these factors belongs to (f_i), and therefore c_i does not.
These choices give the actual equality

    A[1/c_i]=C[1/c_i][r,y_i],    f_i y_i=G_i(r).                  (14)

The relation is exact by the same Gauss argument as in (6);
G_i stays monic of degree d_i. Its degree-distinct
basis therefore computes all I_n on this SAME open. Since p/f_i
is now a unit, J_n C[1/c_i]=C[1/c_i] for all n. On D(p) the slice
already gives the same conclusion for all J_n.

Consequently the closed set

    Z=V(p,c_1,...,c_s)                                         (15)

supports every C/J_n. A height-one prime containing p equals
some (f_i), but cannot contain c_i. Thus Z has no height-one
point and is finite or empty. Finite generation of A, not merely
finite generation of each F_n separately, supplies this uniformity.
This set can be larger than the actual union of defect supports.

### 4.2. A finite exact criterion and an explicit adapted basis

For N=lcm(d_1,...,d_s), the floor formula gives

    h_(n+N)=h_n h_N  for all n>=0.                              (16)

If x in F_n and y in F_N, all terms but one in the (n+N)-th
Leibniz derivative of xy vanish. Thus

    D^(n+N)(xy)=binom(n+N,n) D^n(x)D^N(y),
    I_n I_N subset I_(n+N),  J_n J_N subset J_(n+N).              (17)

The binomial coefficient is invertible in k. If J_0,...,J_N
are unit ideals, (17) proves the same for every n by induction
in each residue class modulo N. The converse is immediate.
By (2), I_n is principal precisely when J_n=C. This proves
the finite criterion stated above. It does not prove its finite
premises for the unknown A.

There is also a concrete finite witness version. Suppose elements
e_j in F_j, 0<=j<=N, have been exhibited with e_0=1 and

    D^j(e_j)=j!h_j.

For n=qN+j, 0<=j<N, define e_n=e_N^q e_j. The leading term
over K[r] or the iterated Leibniz formula gives

    D^n(e_n)=n! h_N^q h_j=n!h_n.

These e_n form a C-basis of A: distinct D-degrees give independence,
and any element of degree n has top derivative a C-multiple of
n!h_n, so subtraction of that multiple of e_n reduces its degree.
In particular A is free of rank N over C[e_N], on e_0,...,e_(N-1).
This is a finite algebraic certificate for an adapted basis, not
a coordinate system identifying A with k^[3]. The control (12)
already has such a basis without being polynomial.

## 5. What global reconstruction does and does not give

There is an embedded intersection

    A=intersection_(height-one q in C) A_(q), inside A_K=K[r].      (9)

To prove it, any height-one prime P of A meeting C nontrivially
is generated by a prime q in C, by factorial closure, and has
P intersect C=qC. Its localization is covered by A_(q). Primes
of A contracting to zero are covered by A_K. Apply the normal
domain's intersection of height-one local rings inside Frac(A).

All F_n are finite C-modules. An explicit bound sufficient for
this is

    F_n subset p^(-n)(C+Cr+...+Cr^n).                             (10)

For induction, differentiate a polynomial in r representing an
element z of F_n and apply the bound for F_(n-1). Since D=p
partial_r, every positive-degree coefficient of z belongs to
p^(-n)C. The constant coefficient has the same bound, because
p^n z minus the resulting polynomial lies in A intersect K=C.
The case n=0 is immediate. Noetherianity proves finiteness.

Localizing kernels in (9) gives the same height-one intersection
for F_n. Hence F_n is reflexive, locally free over the regular
surface Spec C, and free by Quillen-Suslin. This conclusion does
not make I_n=F_n/F_(n-1) reflexive or principal. The earlier
pass102 already proves all-degree local freeness under the actual
flat inclusion; the normal-UFD proof here does not need flatness.[^modules]

Equation (9) uses the exact EMBEDDED local rings, including their
coefficient data. It is not a claim that abstract local isomorphism
types, residue degrees d_i, or their image valuations determine
global coordinates. Nor does it identify an arbitrary proposed
subalgebra with A. Such a subalgebra may have additional divisors
over closed base points and fail (9).

For the cylinder B=A[u] with Du=0, coefficientwise comparison gives

    ker_B D=C[u],   I_n(D on B)=I_n C[u]=h_n J_n C[u].              (11)

Evaluation at u=0 shows that J_n C[u]=C[u] only if J_n=C.
Thus adjoining the cylinder variable does not itself erase these
defects. No theorem is supplied here forcing their vanishing
from an abstract isomorphism B=k^[4].

## 6. Exact controls and the remaining cancellation obligation

Even an actual polynomial base permits bad closed fibers. On
A=k[x,y,z], take D=x partial_y+2y partial_z, C=k[x,h],
h=xz-y^2, and r=y. The full plinth is xC: over C_(x), the
one-stage relation xz=y^2+h gives the local formula (8) with
d=2; elsewhere the derivative Dy=x is a unit. Since every
invariant derivative belongs to C and has x-adic valuation at
least one, this proves the full global ideal xC. The closed
fiber (x,h)=(0,0) is k[y,z]/(y^2), and D fixes the line x=y=0.
Its generic divisor fiber is instead k(h)(sqrt(-h))[z], reduced
and free. This actual A[u]=k^[4] control excludes upgrading the
generic-fiber statements to all closed fibers.

A second control rules out replacing the polynomial-cylinder
hypothesis by smoothness, factoriality, contractibility and even
principality of EVERY image ideal. Put

    R=k[a,b,s,t]/(f t-s^5+b),   f=b^2-a^3,
    D=f partial_s+5s^4 partial_t.                                (12)

This is the exponent-one member of the established second-kind
Koras-Russell family, with parameters (d,ell,alpha2,alpha3)=
(2,1,3,5), under x=b, y=t, z=-a, t_P=-s. The published family
has ell>=1, is smooth and contractible, and ML(R)=k[a,b]. Thus
R is not k^[3].[^kr]

The algebraic assertions can be checked directly. The primitive
linear relation embeds R in C_f[s]. Monicity in s gives the
unique C-basis {s^i t^j:0<=i<5,j>=0}. In C_f[s] these terms have
distinct degrees i+5j. Therefore

    ker D=C,  F_n=direct_sum_(i+5j<=n) C s^i t^j,
    I_n=f^(n-floor(n/5))C for every n>=0.                         (13)

The full plinth is irreducible and squarefree and all J_n=C.
The same basis gives faithful flatness of C->R and C[u]->R[u].
Also R/fR=k[a,s,t]/(s^10-a^3) is a domain, so f is prime;
R_f=C_f[s] is factorial and Nagata's criterion makes R factorial.
Primitivity follows from gcd(f,5s^4)=1. Smoothness follows directly:
if the gradient of F=f t-s^5+b vanished, F_t=f and F_s=-5s^4
would give f=s=0; F=0 would give b=0, hence a=0, but then F_b=1.
The closed fiber at (a,b)=(0,0) is k[s,t]/(s^5).

No polynomial-cylinder isomorphism for (12) is established here.
Petitjean computes ML(R), not ML(R[u]). The verified first-kind
cylinder theorem of Dubouloz does not apply to this second-kind
equation.[^cylinder] The elementary bounds are only
k subset ML(R[u]) subset k[a,b]. This is a source limitation,
not a claim that a literature-wide open status has been certified.

The proposed implication with the ACTUAL premise A[u]=k^[4]
and a squarefree full plinth remains unproved. Moreover no proof
is supplied that every cancellation candidate has such a D.
Even solving this conditional problem would leave that separate
existence obligation for the original conjecture. The next useful
argument must exploit actual polynomial-cylinder coordinates,
not just the divisor models, local freeness, flatness or all-image
principality, which the controls above already distinguish.
