# Literature scope of actual quadratic-graph recognition

The three primary papers checked here do not supply a theorem directly
taking the raw independent-defect, degree-two actual-frame hypotheses
and concluding cancellation without an additional applicability step.
They do supply established recognition theorems once an invariant
ambient coordinate has been obtained. That recognition endpoint is
not a new claim of
[the pass64 proof](actual-quadratic-graph-cancellation-pass64.md).

This is a bounded applicability comparison, not an assertion that no
other theorem subsumes the result and not a novelty certification.

## 1. The result being compared

The input is the actual branch of
[the generic bad-pair theorem](generic-bad-pair-pass44.md), not merely
a ring generated by four quadratic functions over an invariant base.
With \(k=\mathbb C\), it has
\[
B=k[x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4],\qquad
E\in\operatorname{LND}_k(B),\qquad E(s_*)=1,\qquad E^3x_i=0.
\]
Writing \(T_i=\partial_{x_i}\), the two good actual partials have
\(\operatorname{ad}_E^2T_i=0\), and the two bad actual partials have
\(\operatorname{ad}_E^3T_i=0\). The two bad defect tops are independent.
The surviving bad projection has rank two; equivalently in the
inherited reduction, the actual speeds \(f=Ex_3,\ g=Ex_4\) are
algebraically independent. The inherited acceleration vector is
\[
E^2x=(h_1,h_2,0,0)^{\mathsf t}.
\]

The flow condition \(E^3x_i=0\) is a bound on degree in the action
parameter. It is not a bound of two on the spatial polynomial degree
of the coefficients of \(E\). Nor does
\(\operatorname{ad}_E^2T_i=0\) mean \(E^2x_i=0\).

The result in pass64 is \(\ker E\simeq k^{[3]}\) for this specified
branch. It does not assert that every sliced LND admits this frame,
and it does not close every branch of the two-good/two-bad adjoint
profile.

Two hypotheses useful for comparisons already follow from the
supplied slice, without recognition of the kernel:

- \(E\) is irreducible: a common nonunit divisor of \(E(B)\) would
  divide \(E(s_*)=1\).
- The action is proper: \(B=(\ker E)[s_*]\), and translation on
  the second factor has action map isomorphic onto the closed
  equal-quotient locus in the product.

Neither observation constructs an invariant polynomial coordinate
of the ambient four-ring.

## 2. Three exact primary comparisons

### 2.1. Derksen, van den Essen and van Rossum

Primary source:
[The Cancellation Problem in Dimension Four, author-uploaded manuscript](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228571174_The_cancellation_problem_in_dimension_four),
Nijmegen Report 0022, October 2000. Read Theorem 4.4 and
Corollary 4.5, printed pp. 8--9; the selected complete proofs and
their Lemmas 4.2--4.3 and Corollary 3.10 were also read.

Theorem 4.4 assumes a Dedekind domain \(R\supseteq\mathbb Q\)
and an \(R\)-LND on the actual ring \(R^{[3]}\) with a slice.
It concludes that the kernel is \(R^{[2]}\).
Corollary 4.5 treats a sliced LND on \(k[X,Y,Z,W]\) whose
\(W\)-coefficient belongs to \(k[W]\); the fixed-\(W\) case applies here.

Matched before the graph argument: characteristic zero, the
polynomial four-ring, and the original slice.
Not yet supplied: an invariant ambient coordinate \(I\), hence the
actual relative presentation \(B=k[I]^{[3]}\).
The intrinsic plane \(C\simeq k^{[2]}\) is not a Dedekind replacement.

After pass64 constructs \(I\), Theorem 4.4 applies with \(R=k[I]\)
to the unchanged \(E\) and unchanged slice. This is exactly the
established recognition endpoint used in the proof. The theorem
does not require the flow, adjoint, or defect-rank restrictions.

### 2.2. Kaliman

Primary source:
[Proper Ga-actions on C4 preserving a coordinate](https://msp.org/ant/2018/12-2/ant-v12-n2-p01-s.pdf),
Algebra & Number Theory 12 (2018), 227--258.
Theorem 0.1 is on printed p. 227, PDF page 2 counting the cover.
Theorem 0.2 and Corollary 0.3, including the displayed deduction
of Theorem 0.1, occupy printed pp. 228--229, PDF pages 3--4.

Theorem 0.1 recognizes every proper additive-group action on
\(\mathbb C^4\) that preserves an ambient coordinate as a
polynomial translation. Our slice supplies properness; the
coordinate-preservation premise is supplied only after pass64's
extraction of \(I\).

The more general Theorem 0.2 is not a shortcut using an arbitrary
invariant function: it imposes a specified curve fibration,
generic invariant-plane and fiber conditions. Its bundle conclusion
has additional normality and fiber-translation assumptions.
Those are not established merely by the raw adjoint profile.

Thus Theorem 0.1 is an alternative recognition endpoint after
\(I\), with no low-degree assumption. Its broader proper-action
formulation does not itself provide the missing graph-to-coordinate
implication. The selected statements and introductory deduction
were read, not every proof in the article.

### 2.3. Dasgupta and Gupta

Primary source:
[Nice derivations over principal ideal domains, arXiv:1701.03635v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1701.03635v1).
Theorem 3.6 is on printed/PDF pp. 6--7; Corollary 3.8 is on p. 8;
Theorem 4.1 is on p. 9. The selected Section 3 proofs and
Section 4 statement were read in the
[retained primary text](../tmp/pdfs/dg-nice-1701.03635v1.txt).

Theorem 3.6 assumes an irreducible nice LND on an actual
three-variable polynomial ring over a PID \(R\supseteq\mathbb Q\),
and concludes that its kernel is \(R^{[2]}\).
Corollary 3.8 assumes irreducibility, \(DX_1=0\), and \(D^2X_i=0\) for
\(i=2,3,4\), and concludes a polynomial three-ring kernel.
Theorem 4.1 reproduces Wang's classification of irreducible
two-quasi LNDs on \(K[X,Y,Z]\), over a characteristic-zero field.

Our slice matches irreducibility, and \(E^2x_3=E^2x_4=0\)
matches two coordinate conditions. But the field theorem has
three variables, while the four-variable corollary requires
a fixed actual coordinate and niceness on the remaining three.
The two good accelerations need not vanish.
The defect-distribution rank is not the coordinate rank used there.

Consequently these statements do not directly cover the raw
branch. The reproduced Wang theorem is attributed to Wang;
this audit does not claim a fresh read of his original paper.

## 3. Exact applicability boundary

| Candidate | Step still required from the actual graph |
| --- | --- |
| Derksen--van den Essen--van Rossum 4.4 / 4.5 | Produce a fixed ambient coordinate giving a genuine relative polynomial three-ring. |
| Kaliman 0.1 | Produce a fixed ambient coordinate; properness is already available. |
| Dasgupta--Gupta 3.6 / 3.8 / 4.1 | Establish the specified relative-ring or stronger coordinate-nilpotence premises. |

In the first two rows, the missing coordinate is now proved by
pass64, not postulated. The exact formula in its nonconstant-graph
branch is
\[
I=Q_2-\rho Q_1-D(X,Y),\qquad
\rho\in k,\quad D\in k[X,Y],\quad I\in C\subseteq\ker E.
\]
It is an ambient coordinate because
\[
Q_2=I+\rho Q_1+D(X,Y)
\]
is the inverse polynomial shear with the other three coordinates
unchanged. No coordinate status of \(I\) inside the intrinsic
plane \(C\) is needed.

The graph argument leading to this formula uses the full actual
Schur determinant and actual coordinate-field generation. Its
moving-direction alternative is excluded by the rational-function
degree argument. Those steps must not be replaced by the assertion
that the endpoint theorem already supplies an invariant coordinate.

The remaining constant-graph alternative proves \(B=C[X,Y]\).
The original slice then forces \((f,g)C=C\), and the explicit
unimodular change gives \(\ker E=C^{[1]}\).
That alternative is proved directly in pass64 rather than by
assuming a fixed-coordinate theorem applies to the original frame.

Accordingly, the precise comparison is:

- The final recognition after coordinate extraction is established
  literature and is credited as such.
- No directly subsuming theorem with the raw actual-frame input
  was found among these three checked primary papers.
- This finite result does not establish originality of the
  extraction argument or rule out a different theorem elsewhere.

## 4. Deduplication, access and limits

The audit started with the retained
[proper-flow scope note](proper-quadratic-flow-scope-pass55.md),
[mixed-time-degree addendum](mixed-time-degree-literature-addendum.md),
[generic graph proof](generic-bad-pair-pass44.md), and the
[completed recognition proof](actual-quadratic-graph-cancellation-pass64.md).
The invariant-coordinate and relative-base literature boundaries
were already recorded there; they are not presented here as new
discoveries.

A bounded search used combinations of “locally nilpotent,”
“quadratic,” “slice,” “four variables,” “degree two,” and
“quasi-nice,” followed by primary-domain filtering.
Search snippets and unrelated results were not used as theorem
evidence. Exactly the three papers above were selected for
theorem-level comparison. Paper dates come from the primary
documents, not search crawl timestamps.

The generalized Luroth input and its attribution are audited in
pass64 itself. This note does not add it as a fourth comparison
candidate or reclassify it as a four-variable recognition theorem.

No numerical verification is relevant to this source-applicability
comparison. No checker, registry record, external contact, or
submission was created. Earlier proof files and shared metadata
were not changed.
