# Rank-one cylinders and finite unipotent closures

The full cylinder hypothesis still does not give a proof that intrinsic
LND rank one is impossible. This investigation does give a stronger
necessary condition: a rank-one candidate cannot admit a finite
unipotent algebraic action on its cylinder that contains the slice
translation and has generic orbit dimension three.

In particular, for such a candidate, adjoining a transverse ambient
coordinate translation to the slice translation and the unique
intrinsic action cannot have a finite-dimensional all-LND Lie closure.
This is a conditional obstruction, not a construction of a suitable
finite closure from polynomiality. It does not prove cancellation,
triviality of the intrinsic ML invariant, novelty, or expert verification.

## 1. The rank-one hypothesis and the valid filtration transfer

Work over the complex numbers with
\[
B=A[s]\simeq\mathbb C^{[4]},\qquad E=\partial_s.
\tag{1}
\]
Assume, for this investigation, that the generic rank of all intrinsic
LNDs of \(A\) is one. The inherited quotient and primitive-saturation
results give
\[
C=\operatorname{ML}(A)=\ker D\simeq\mathbb C^{[2]},\qquad
\operatorname{LND}(A)=CD,
\tag{2}
\]
where \(D\) is primitive and nonzero. Extend \(D\) to \(B\) by
\(D(s)=0\). Its unique intrinsic LND filtration is
\(F_j=\ker(D^{j+1}:A\to A)\).

The word unique concerns actions on \(A\). Alhajjar's Proposition 3.5
proves that automorphisms between semi-rigid domains preserve their
unique filtrations. It does not assert preservation by automorphisms
of a polynomial extension.[^filtration]

There is an exact relative extension that does follow from (2):
\[
\{L\in\operatorname{LND}(B):L(s)=0\}=C[s]D.
\tag{3}
\]
To prove it, specialize such an \(L\) at each scalar \(s=c\).
The induced derivation \(L_c\) of \(A\) is an LND, so is a multiple
of \(D\) by (2). Choose \(a_0\in A\) with \(D(a_0)\ne0\).
For each algebra generator \(a_i\) of \(A\), the polynomial
\[
D(a_0)L(a_i)-D(a_i)L(a_0)\in A[s]
\tag{4}
\]
vanishes at every scalar value of \(s\), hence is zero. Thus
\(L=qD\) on the fraction field. Primitivity of the extension of
\(D\) to the UFD \(A[s]\) gives \(q\in A[s]\); the standard
invariant-multiple criterion for an LND gives \(D(q)=0\). Hence
\(q\in C[s]\). Conversely every such multiple is locally nilpotent.

Likewise every automorphism of \(B\) fixing \(s\) preserves each
\(F_j[s]\). Specialize it and its inverse at \(s=c\), apply the
intrinsic filtration theorem, and interpolate the finitely many
coefficients modulo the complex vector subspace \(F_j\subset A\).
The same conclusion holds for the inverse, giving equality.

These conclusions cover all relative actions and automorphisms, not
just a selected frame. They do not apply to a coordinate translation
that moves \(s\). Even stabilization of one principal fiber ideal
\((s-c)B\) by an LND forces \(L(s)=0\), by the invariant-divisibility
lemma, so does not provide a route around (3).

## 2. A rank-three group supplies two intrinsic directions

Here is the additional conditional result. Its proof uses published
unipotent homogeneous-space and affine-plane results. The argument
does not assume that the sliced subgroup is normal.

**Proposition.** Let \(A\) be an affine complex domain of dimension
three, put \(B=A[s]\), and let a connected unipotent algebraic group
\(G\) act regularly on \(\operatorname{Spec}B\). Assume that the
action contains the one-parameter subgroup generated by
\(E=\partial_s\), and that its generic orbit dimension is three.
Then \(A\) admits two commuting LNDs independent over
\(\operatorname{Frac}A\).

Polynomiality of \(B\) is not required for this proposition. Its
application below uses the actual polynomial cylinder to supply
transverse coordinate translations. In particular the conclusion is
not being inferred from an action on one chosen invariant plane.

### 2.1 An invariant principal open, not just a rational chart

Brion's Theorem 2, with its proof in Section 5.2, gives a dense
\(G\)-stable open subset
\[
U\simeq\mathbb A^3\times Y
\tag{5}
\]
whose projection to \(Y\) is the rational quotient and whose fibers
are exactly the \(G\)-orbits. We may take \(Y\) affine. There is no
punctured-line factor here: in the unipotent case the proof of the
homogeneous-space theorem uses only additive-group torsors. This is
the unipotent specialization of the published result, not a claim
that every solvable homogeneous space is an affine space.[^brion]

Let \(I\) be the ideal of the closed complement of \(U\) in
\(\operatorname{Spec}B\). If the complement is empty take \(h=1\).
Otherwise \(I\ne0\) is \(G\)-stable. The regular action is locally
finite on \(B\). A nonzero finite-dimensional \(G\)-submodule of
\(I\) has a nonzero fixed vector because \(G\) is unipotent.
Choose this vector as \(h\). Thus
\[
0\ne h\in B^G,\qquad \operatorname{Spec}B_h\subset U.
\tag{6}
\]
Every \(G\)-invariant function on the product (5) comes from \(Y\):
its restriction to each affine-space fiber is constant, so all
nonconstant fiber coefficients vanish. Consequently (6) is the
product of \(\mathbb A^3\) with a principal open \(Y_h\subset Y\).
Writing \(R=\mathcal O(Y_h)\), we obtain actual ring equalities
and an isomorphism
\[
R=(B_h)^G=(B^G)_h,\qquad B_h\simeq R^{[3]}.
\tag{7}
\]
Localization commutes with invariants here because its denominator
\(h\) is invariant. Since \(E\) belongs to the action,
\(B^G\subseteq A\), so \(h\in A\) and
\[
B_h=A_h[s].
\tag{8}
\]

This step avoids assuming that the full invariant ring is finitely
generated, that an arbitrary rational quotient is affine, or that
an arbitrary dense orbit is the whole fiber. The entire generic
fiber is in the invariant principal product (7).

### 2.2 The invariant field and the geometric generic orbit

Put \(K=\operatorname{Frac}R\). By the rational-quotient statement,
\[
K=\operatorname{Frac}(B)^G,\qquad
\operatorname{trdeg}_{\mathbb C}K=1.
\tag{9}
\]
Equations (7)–(8) give
\[
(A_h\otimes_R K)[s]\simeq K^{[3]}.
\tag{10}
\]
After any field extension, its right side stays a polynomial domain.
In particular the geometric generic fiber is integral. Its generic
orbit has dimension three. Over an algebraic closure of \(K\),
unipotent orbits in an affine variety are closed, so an open dense
orbit is the whole fiber. Thus the transitivity assertion is also
checked on the full geometric generic fiber, not inferred from
Rosenlicht's dense-open assertion alone.[^closed]

Under the additional hypothesis \(B=\mathbb C^{[4]}\), this
one-variable invariant field is rational. The dominant rational
map from \(\mathbb A^4\) to the quotient curve is nonconstant on
some affine line: choose a regular point where a nonconstant
quotient function has nonzero differential, and a line in a
direction on which it is nonzero. Hence the curve is dominated by
an open subset of \(\mathbb A^1\); the one-variable Lüroth theorem
gives \(K\simeq\mathbb C(t)\). This rationality is not needed for
the next descent, which works over every characteristic-zero field.

### 2.3 Affine-plane cancellation and separable descent

Set \(P=A_h\otimes_R K\). Equation (10) says that \(P\) is a
two-dimensional affine \(K\)-domain with \(P[s]=K^{[3]}\).
For an algebraic closure \(\overline K\), put
\(\overline P=P\otimes_K\overline K\). Then
\[
\overline P[s]=\overline K^{[3]},\qquad
\overline P\simeq\overline K^{[2]}.
\tag{11}
\]
The second implication is the established two-dimensional
cancellation theorem. One can reconstruct precisely the portion
needed here from the classical LND surface statements: the UFD
\(\overline P\) cannot be rigid, since one-cylinder rigidity would
contradict the constant ML invariant of \(\overline K^{[3]}\).
A primitive LND on this affine factorial surface has a slice.
Its one-dimensional invariant ring is a rational affine UFD with
only constant units, hence \(\overline K^{[1]}\). This proves
the second isomorphism in (11).[^surface]

Equation (11) makes \(P\) a separable affine-plane form. All
coefficients in a polynomial isomorphism and its inverse lie in
one finite extension of \(K\), necessarily separable in
characteristic zero. Kambayashi's theorem therefore gives
\[
P=K[u,v].
\tag{12}
\]
The theorem is used for a field, not for a two-dimensional ring
or an arbitrary affine-space form.[^forms]

### 2.4 Clearing denominators gives actual intrinsic LNDs

The derivations \(\partial_u,\partial_v\) of (12) kill \(K\).
On finitely many algebra generators of \(A_h\), their values have
denominators in \(R\setminus\{0\}\). Choose a common nonzero
denominator \(q\in R\). Multiplying each derivation by a
sufficient power of \(q\) makes it preserve \(A_h\).

Now choose a sufficiently large common power of \(h\) so that
the resulting derivations preserve the original finite generating
set of \(A\), not just \(A_h\). Since both derivations kill
\(R\), they kill \(h\) and \(q\). The resulting nonzero fields
\[
L_1=h^Nq^M\partial_u\big|_A,\qquad
L_2=h^Nq^M\partial_v\big|_A
\tag{13}
\]
are consequently locally nilpotent and commute. They remain
independent over the fraction field, since multiplication by a
nonzero scalar cannot change the independence of
\(\partial_u,\partial_v\). This proves the proposition.

Equivalently, one can spread the two-sided coordinate description
(12) to a further invariant principal open before clearing the
denominators. Neither method asserts that these fields are
primitive, free or sliced on the whole \(A\).

## 3. A concrete obstruction for a rank-one polynomial cylinder

Return to (1)–(2). If a finite-dimensional Lie algebra
\(\mathfrak g\subset\operatorname{Der}(B)\) contains \(E\)
and every member is locally nilpotent, its actual action integrates
to a unipotent algebraic group. The finite PBW-module, Engel and
exponential reconstruction is in
[pass 28, Section 2](finite-lie-recognition-pass28.md). This is not
an inference from local nilpotence of a generating list alone.

The proposition excludes generic rank three for this group.
Generic rank four is already excluded by the transitive unipotent
recognition theorem in the same note, which would give
\(A=\mathbb C^{[3]}\), inconsistent with (2). Thus
\[
E\in\mathfrak g\subseteq\operatorname{LND}(B),\quad
\dim_{\mathbb C}\mathfrak g<\infty
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\operatorname{rank}_{\operatorname{Frac}B}\mathfrak g\le2
\tag{14}
\]
for a rank-one candidate satisfying (1).

Choose any actual ambient coordinate translation \(T\) that moves
some member of \(C\). Such a translation exists in every ambient
coordinate frame: otherwise the common kernel of its four partials
would contain the nonconstant ring \(C\).
The three fields \(E,D,T\) are independent over
\(\operatorname{Frac}B\). Indeed \(E,D\) kill \(C\), whereas
\(T\) does not, and \(E,D\) are already independent because
\(E(s)=1\), \(D(s)=0\), \(D\ne0\). Hence
\[
\operatorname{Lie}\langle E,D,T\rangle
\text{ is not both finite-dimensional and all-LND.}
\tag{15}
\]
This statement applies to the unique intrinsic direction, not just
to the leading coefficient of one chosen ambient frame.

There is also a two-translation certificate. Choose actual coordinate
partials \(T_1,T_2\) such that \(E,T_1,T_2\) have generic rank
three. Such a pair exists by linear algebra from a four-partial
frame. If their Lie closure is finite-dimensional and all-LND,
then intrinsic rank is at least two, or the rank-four case already
closes cancellation. The conditional input involves only part of
the ambient frame; it does not require a transitive group a priori.

## 4. The universal step is still missing

The valid semi-rigid transfer (3) concerns actions fixing the
cylinder parameter. The ambient translations that must move
\(C\) fall outside that relative assertion. No argument here
shows that their closure with \(E,D\) is finite-dimensional, or
that all members are locally nilpotent. Equation (15) instead
states the exact obstruction a genuine rank-one candidate would
have to exhibit.

The bounded investigation therefore strengthens a necessary
condition on a rank-one candidate without ruling one out. It does
not reuse a non-factorial Danielewski example, a contractible
threefold with an unproved polynomial cylinder, or a polynomial
threefold lacking the uniqueness premise as a counterexample to
the missing universal statement. No such counterexample has been
produced here.

The next proof gap is substantive: force some finite unipotent
closure transverse to the intrinsic invariant plane, or supply a
different descent argument that works despite the failure of
finite closure. The original full-cylinder hypothesis has not
been shown to supply either. No new numerical checker is needed
for this source-dependent structural deduction; finite sample
tests would not certify that existence quantifier.

## Primary-source scope

[^filtration]: Bachar Alhajjar, [*LND-Filtrations and Semi-Rigid Domains*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1501.00445), Propositions 2.2, 3.2–3.5 and the proof of Proposition 3.4, PDF pp. 4, 6–8, directly read. Filtration preservation in Proposition 3.5 is for isomorphisms of semi-rigid domains themselves. The one-variable theorem assumes rigidity of the base, not merely rank one.

[^brion]: Michel Brion, [*Homogeneous varieties under split solvable algebraic groups*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2101.12452), v2, Theorems 1–2, pp. 1–2; the unipotent part of the homogeneous-space proof, Section 5.1, pp. 10–11; complete Section 5.2, pp. 12–13, directly read. Theorem 2 supplies a stable product open with orbit fibers. Its unipotent specialization has no multiplicative factor. No global quotient finite-generation theorem is being inferred.

[^closed]: J. S. Milne, [*Algebraic Groups*, corrected 2022 text](https://www.jmilne.org/math/Books/iAG2022.pdf), Theorem 17.64, printed p. 374, statement and proof read. It states closedness of unipotent orbits on affine algebraic schemes. The affine full generic fiber is justified separately by (7) and (10).

[^surface]: Nikhilesh Dasgupta and Neena Gupta, [*An algebraic characterization of the affine three space*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1709.00169), v2, Lemma 2.2, Lemma 2.5 and Theorem 2.6, PDF pp. 3–4, directly read. The surface slice lemma has an algebraically closed characteristic-zero field hypothesis, which is why it is applied over \(\overline K\), not prematurely over \(K\). These are recalled classical inputs; the paper credits the standard curve and LND sources. The reconstruction above uses them only in dimension two.

[^forms]: Amartya Kumar Dutta, Neena Gupta and Animesh Lahiri, [*On Separable A2 and A3-forms*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1802.03777), v2, Theorem 2.8, PDF p. 2, directly read. This is their explicit statement of Kambayashi's 1975 Theorem 3: a separably algebraic affine-plane form over a field is trivial. The original 1975 full proof was not newly accessed in this pass; no claim is made to have re-proved it or to extend it to arbitrary affine-space forms.
