# Relative completion: the unit-ideal case is already a theorem

Date: 2026-09-13.

The commuting-complement hypothesis left open in
[pass 26](simultaneous-frame-pass26.md) is not an additional obstruction
once the actual relative plane and the unit coefficient ideal have been
established. A published two-variable LND theorem supplies a slice and
coordinates under exactly those assumptions. This note checks that
application and gives a finite correction of any polynomial Bezout
complement to a commuting coordinate complement.

The remaining universal question is whether a sufficiently strong
tangent LND and a suitable relative presentation exist. Neither is
deduced here. This is an application of established results, not a
novelty claim or a solution of general cancellation. The proof is not
expert-reviewed or Lean-verified.

For the actual stable-threefold target, an intrinsic free action
already proves polynomiality without any rank bound, by the
[earlier Kaliman--Saveliev application](intrinsic-quotient-surface-pass.md).
The present result removes an artificial complement/integrability
requirement from a relative-plane construction; it does not enlarge
that already known free-action recognition regime.

## 1. Exact hypotheses and inherited theorem

Let S be a Noetherian domain containing Q, let B=S[u,v], and let

\[
V=a\partial_u+b\partial_v\ne0
\tag{1}
\]

be an S-linear locally nilpotent derivation. The image ideal is

\[
I(V):=V(B)B=(a,b)B.
\tag{2}
\]

This is an ideal generated by values, not an assertion that the
additive image V(B) is an ideal. Fixed-point-free means I(V)=B.
Over the domain S, units of B are exactly units of S.

Bhatwadekar and Dutta, Theorem 4.7, applies to a Noetherian
characteristic-zero domain and an LND of its two-variable polynomial
ring. Its comaximal-coefficient case gives

\[
\ker V=S[F_0],\qquad B=(\ker V)[Z].
\tag{3}
\]

The theorem also treats a regular-sequence case, which does not supply
the last assertion in (3). Only its comaximal case is used here.
Comaximality implies the source's irreducibility condition: a principal
ideal containing all values must contain 1. The full statement and
proof, together with the standing base-ring convention, were read
directly. [Bhatwadekar--Dutta, *Kernel of Locally Nilpotent R-Derivations
of R[X,Y]*, Trans. AMS 349 (1997), Theorem 4.7, pp. 3315--3316](https://repository.ias.ac.in/3203/1/311.pdf).

To extract a normalized slice from (3), extend scalars from ker V to
its fraction field. A nonzero locally nilpotent derivation of a
one-variable polynomial ring over a characteristic-zero field sends
that variable to a nonzero constant: positive-degree coefficients
would survive under every iterate, by the usual leading-degree
calculation. Hence V(Z)=h lies in ker V. Thus V=h partial_Z on (3),
and I(V)=hB. The unit-ideal assumption forces h to be a unit. Therefore
P=Z/h is a polynomial slice and

\[
V(P)=1,\qquad B=S[F_0,P].
\tag{4}
\]

This is where the known two-variable theorem supplies more than a
Keller pair. No implication from Jacobian one alone is used.

## 2. The relative completion equivalence

For the LND (1), the following conditions are equivalent:

1. Its two coefficients generate B.
2. V has a polynomial slice.
3. There are actual S-coordinates P,F with V=partial_P.
4. There is an S-derivation W commuting with V and with
   det(V,W) in S*.
5. There is such a W that is itself locally nilpotent.

Here determinants are taken in the actual basis partial_u,partial_v.
The implications 2 implies 1 and 4 implies 1 follow from (2) and the
determinant formula. Section 1 proves 1 implies 3. Actual coordinates
in 3 provide W=partial_F, proving 5, and 5 implies 4 is immediate.
This proves all directions without an assumption on a putative
Jacobian inverse. A complement's determinant can be normalized to 1
by a scalar in S*.

Consequently there is no counterexample to complement existence
with all of the hypotheses in Section 1 and I(V)=B. A particular
chosen Bezout complement may fail to commute or be locally nilpotent,
as pass 26 shows. Its failure cannot obstruct the existence of a
different complement.

## 3. Correcting any Bezout complement, in every degree

### 3.1 The divergence identity

An LND V on a polynomial ring over a Q-domain has divergence zero.
Indeed, exp(tV) is an S[t]-automorphism of S[t,u,v]. Its relative
Jacobian determinant is a unit of S[t,u,v], hence lies in S*, and
specializing t=0 makes it 1. Differentiating at t=0 gives

\[
\operatorname{div}V=a_u+b_v=0.
\tag{5}
\]

This is the classical volume-preservation argument; see also
[Derksen's author notes, Lemma 5.11](https://sites.lsa.umich.edu/hderksen/wp-content/uploads/sites/614/2018/09/LND1.pdf).

Choose any Bezout complement

\[
W_0=c\partial_u+e\partial_v,
\qquad ae-bc=1,
\qquad h=c_u+e_v.
\tag{6}
\]

Expanding the two coefficients of the bracket and differentiating
ae-bc=1 gives

\[
[V,W_0]=hV.
\tag{7}
\]

For example its u-coefficient is
a c_u+b c_v-c a_u-e a_v. Subtracting a(c_u+e_v),
the remainder is
b c_v-c a_u-e a_v-a e_v
=-c(a_u+b_v)=0, using the v-derivative of ae-bc=1.
The other coefficient is analogous.

Every other determinant-one complement is uniquely W_0+qV for
some q in B, because V,W_0 form a B-basis. Its divergence is
h+V(q). Thus the class

\[
[h]\in B/V(B)
\tag{8}
\]

is independent of the Bezout complement and is the exact obstruction
to a commuting determinant-one correction. The quotient here is an
S-module, not generally a quotient ring. Under the present hypotheses
the obstruction vanishes, as the following finite formula shows.

### 3.2 A finite right inverse to V from its genuine slice

Use the polynomial slice P obtained in Section 1. If V^(N+1)(h)=0,
put

\[
g=\sum_{j=0}^{N}
 \frac{(-1)^jP^{j+1}V^j(h)}{(j+1)!}.
\tag{9}
\]

Leibniz differentiation, V(P)=1, and cancellation of adjacent
summands give V(g)=h. The final uncancelled term contains
V^(N+1)(h) and is zero. Thus

\[
W=W_0-gV,\qquad [V,W]=0,\qquad \det(V,W)=1.
\tag{10}
\]

All operations in (9)--(10) are finite polynomial operations. They
require an actual slice, not merely a function whose derivative is
an invertible element on an open subset. The cited theorem proves
that a slice exists; (9) alone is not an algorithm that finds one
without that existence input.

### 3.3 Explicit dual-coordinate repair

One can also avoid an implicit completeness assertion for W.
The divergence identity makes the following polynomial primitive
well defined:

\[
F=-\int_0^u b(t,0)\,dt+\int_0^v a(u,t)\,dt,
\qquad F_u=-b,\quad F_v=a.
\tag{11}
\]

In particular V(F)=0 and Jac(P,F)=V(P)=1. To see that these are
actual coordinates, use (4), which is already proved by the source:
F lies in S[F_0], say F=q(F_0). Since the Jacobian of the actual
coordinates P,F_0 is a unit j in S*, the identity
1=Jac(P,F)=j q'(F_0) makes q affine linear with unit slope.
Therefore B=S[P,F] and ker V=S[F].

The determinant identity in (10) gives W(F)=1. Commutation implies
V(W(P))=0, so W(P)=k(F) for some k in S[F]. Define

\[
\widehat P=P-\int_0^F k(t)\,dt.
\tag{12}
\]

This is an actual coordinate shear of S[P,F], and

\[
V(\widehat P)=1,\quad V(F)=0,\quad
W(\widehat P)=0,\quad W(F)=1.
\tag{13}
\]

Hence V,W are the actual partials in the coordinates widehat P,F.
In particular W is locally nilpotent, with no Jacobian-conjecture
step. Formula (9) is elementary slice integration, not a claim of a
new LND structure theorem.

## 4. Exact tangent-field application and its missing quantifier

Suppose B=C^[4]=A[s] and V is a nonzero ambient LND with V(s)=0.
Assume its ambient rank is at most two in the coordinate sense:
there are actual coordinates z_1,z_2,u,v of B with V(z_1)=V(z_2)=0.
This is not the generic rank of a collection of derivations or a
statement about two algebraically independent invariants. Set
S=C[z_1,z_2]. If I(V)=B, Sections 1--2 produce a polynomial P with
V(P)=1. Since (s) is V-stable, V descends to an LND of
B/(s), identified with A, and the class of P is its slice.
No claim that A was already polynomial enters this argument.

This gives exactly the intrinsic sliced-action hypothesis required
by the [previously source-audited recognition step](intrinsic-frame-repair-pass24.md#5-the-exact-published-hypothesis-boundary).
The present note establishes the slice implication, rather than
re-proving that recognition theorem.

Primitivity is not enough. For the familiar triangular field
z_1 partial_u+u partial_v on C[z_1,z_2,u,v], the coefficients have
gcd 1 but generate the proper ideal (z_1,u). It is tangent to s=z_2,
and no polynomial slice or determinant-unit complement exists.
This elementary boundary example is not presented as a new control
or an obstruction to all possible tangent fields.

If V is the coefficientwise extension of an intrinsic LND D of A,
as in [the established extraction](intrinsic-frame-repair-pass24.md#2-a-nonzero-tangent-lnd-is-already-constructible),
then I(V)=I(D)B. Faithful flatness of A -> A[s] shows
I(V)=B if and only if I(D)=A. With only V(s)=0 this equivalence
need not hold: V=(s-1)partial_u+u partial_v is not fixed-point-free
on C[s,z,u,v], but its reduction at s=0 has the slice -u.
The positive implication from an ambient slice to a quotient slice
is valid in both situations.

Thus the sufficient requirement left in this particular algorithm is to
produce a tangent LND with the unit image ideal and rank at most two,
or another sufficient relative structure. The construction of a
nonzero primitive tangent LND alone supplies neither condition.
No universal frame change realizing them has been proved here.
This is not the sharp intrinsic existence target: a free intrinsic
action of any rank already suffices by the preceding
Kaliman--Saveliev result. The rank condition belongs to this
coordinate-completion algorithm, not to that recognition theorem.

## 5. A broader published sufficient structure: an actual A2-fibration

The literal relative plane can be weakened. Babu and Das,
Theorem 4.4, proves that a fixed-point-free relative LND on an
A2-fibration over a Noetherian Q-ring has a slice. Here an
A2-fibration means a finitely generated flat algebra whose fiber
over every prime of the base is a two-variable polynomial algebra.
The theorem also identifies the kernel as an A1-fibration.
[Babu--Das, *Structure of A2-fibrations having fixed point free
locally nilpotent derivations*, Theorem 4.4 and the introduction's
definitions](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2001.07632).

For the present problem it is therefore enough to exhibit a
Noetherian Q-subalgebra S of ker V such that B is an actual
A2-fibration over S and I(V)=B. The resulting slice descends
through (s) exactly as above. This does not demand an a priori
global coordinate complement over S.

The base, flatness and every fiber must actually be checked. Two
invariants, a generically polynomial fiber, or the absolute identity
B=C^[4] does not establish these relative hypotheses. Likewise,
A[s]=C^[4] is not a statement that B is stably polynomial over a
selected invariant subalgebra S. No such replacement is made here.
