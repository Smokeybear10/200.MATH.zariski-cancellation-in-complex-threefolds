# Weight-four invariants in the actual quartic cusp branch

**Status: a necessary coefficient condition and a conditional actual-coordinate theorem.**

Retain every actual cusp hypothesis159 and the quartic graph
hypotheses163. If this fixed frame has a nonconstant invariant
of original weight at most four, that invariant is an actual
polynomial coordinate. Existence is not proved. A necessary
condition for existence is that a specified algebraic coefficient
be a complex polynomial in the ratio \(\tau\) of degree at most four.

## 1. Hypotheses and the full weight-four ansatz

Write
\[
B=\mathbb C[X,Y,Z,W]=A[u],\quad E=\partial_u,\quad
(\deg_E X,\deg_E Y,\deg_E Z,\deg_E W)=(1,1,2,2).
\tag{1}
\]
The full weighted initial kernel is generated by
\(\mathsf X\mathsf Z-\mathsf Y^3\), with original weights
\((1,1,2,2)\). Set \(H=XZ-Y^3\), \(a=EX\ne0\),
\(\tau=EY/a\), and \(\rho=(E^2W/2)/a^2\).
The actual chart has
\[
\begin{gathered}
Y=y+\tau X,\quad
Z=z+\sigma X+\tau^3X^2,\quad
W=w+\vartheta X+\rho X^2,\\
H=-y^3+(k_\tau y+r\vartheta+n)X+kX^2,\quad
r=k_\rho,\quad k,n\in L.
\end{gathered}
\tag{2}
\]
In the fixed-axis quartic graph branch163,
\[
r=\frac1{c_1\tau+c_0},\quad c_1\in\mathbb C^*,
c_0\in\mathbb C,\quad
k=r\rho+k_0,\quad k_0\in K:=\ker_L\partial_\rho .
\tag{3}
\]
The ratios \(\tau,\rho\) are algebraically independent.
The finite coefficient field is not assumed rational.

By162, no nonconstant invariant has original weight at most
three, because \(r\) in (3) is nonconstant. Thus an invariant
\(I\) of weight at most four has weight exactly four.
Its nonzero weighted top lies in the full initial kernel,
and therefore equals \((\mu X+\nu Y)H\) for complex \(\mu,\nu\), not
both zero. Every remaining monomial has weight at most three.
Consequently the full, unrestricted ansatz is
\[
\begin{split}
I={}&(\mu X+\nu Y)H+(\alpha X+\beta Y+\gamma)Z\\
 &+(\delta X+\epsilon Y+\zeta)W+Q(X,Y),\qquad
Q\in\mathbb C[X,Y],\quad\deg Q\le3 .
\end{split}
\tag{4}
\]
All displayed scalar coefficients are complex constants.
No restriction to rational, coefficient-dependent, or
numerically chosen corrections is made.

## 2. The top time coefficient forces a normalized skeleton

Because \(E=a\partial_X\) in the genuine chart, every
positive \(X\)-coefficient of \(I\) vanishes. The cubic one is
\[
(\mu+\nu\tau)k+(\alpha+\beta\tau)\tau^3+
(\delta+\epsilon\tau)\rho+Q_3(1,\tau)=0,
\tag{5}
\]
where \(Q_3\) is the homogeneous cubic part.
Taking \(\partial_\rho\) and using (3) gives
\[
\mu+\nu\tau+(\delta+\epsilon\tau)(c_1\tau+c_0)=0 .
\tag{6}
\]
Since \(\tau\) is transcendental and \(c_1\ne0\), comparison
gives \(\epsilon=0\), \(\mu=-\delta c_0\),
\(\nu=-\delta c_1\). Thus \(\delta\ne0\).
Divide \(I\) and every scalar correction by \(\delta\).
This preserves invariance and the property of being a coordinate.
After renaming the other coefficients the form is
\[
\begin{gathered}
I=C_Z(X,Y)Z+(X+\zeta)W+P(X,Y),\\
C_Z=-c_0X^2-c_1XY+\alpha X+\beta Y+\gamma,\\
P=(c_0X+c_1Y)Y^3+Q(X,Y),\qquad \deg Q\le3 .
\end{gathered}
\tag{7}
\]
The remaining part of (5) now says
\[
(c_1\tau+c_0)k_0
=(\alpha+\beta\tau)\tau^3+Q_3(1,\tau)
\in\mathbb C[\tau]_{\le4}.
\tag{8}
\]
This condition is necessary only. Lower coefficients in (4)
have not yet been solved, and (8) is not asserted sufficient.

## 3. A nonzero slope would give an impossible critical point

Put
\[
\ell=c_1\zeta+\beta,\qquad
\kappa=\gamma-\alpha\zeta-c_0\zeta^2.
\tag{9}
\]
At \(X=-\zeta\), the coefficient \(C_Z\) equals
\(\ell Y+\kappa\).

Suppose \(\ell\ne0\). The critical equations
\(I_W=X+\zeta=0\) and \(I_Z=C_Z=0\) uniquely give
\(X_*=-\zeta\), \(Y_*=-\kappa/\ell\).
At this pair, \(I_Y=\ell Z+P_Y=0\) uniquely determines \(Z_*\),
and \(I_X=(C_Z)_X Z+W+P_X=0\) uniquely determines \(W_*\).
Thus \(I\) has a critical point in the original four-space.

At that critical point, in coordinate order \((X,Y,Z,W)\),
its Hessian has block form
\[
\left.\operatorname{Hess} I\right|_*=
\begin{pmatrix} M&J\\J^{\mathsf T}&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
J=\begin{pmatrix}(C_Z)_X|_*&1\\ \ell&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad \det\left(\left.\operatorname{Hess} I\right|_*\right)=\ell^2\ne0 .
\tag{10}
\]
Here \(M\) is some symmetric two-by-two matrix whose entries
are irrelevant to this determinant.

Write \(E=\sum p_i\partial_{x_i}\). Differentiating \(EI=0\)
and evaluating at this critical point yields
\((\operatorname{Hess} I)(p_i)=0\); the terms differentiating
the \(p_i\) have zero first derivatives of \(I\).
Hence all \(p_i\) vanish at that point. This contradicts
the global slice identity \(Eu=1\).
It follows that
\[
\beta=-c_1\zeta .
\tag{11}
\]
This is a critical point in the original polynomial ring,
not over a moving generic coefficient field.

## 4. A zero constant is excluded by the integral-intercept argument

With (11), write
\[
D(X,Y)=-c_0X-c_1Y+\alpha+c_0\zeta,\qquad
C_Z=(X+\zeta)D+\kappa .
\tag{12}
\]
If \(\kappa=0\), let \(x=X+\zeta\) and
\(J=W+D(X,Y)Z\). These are actual polynomial changes with
inverse \(X=x-\zeta\), \(W=J-D(x-\zeta,Y)Z\).
Divide \(P(x-\zeta,Y)\) by the polynomial variable \(x\):
\[
P(x-\zeta,Y)=xR(x,Y)+p(Y),\quad
p(Y)=P(-\zeta,Y),\quad \deg p=4,\quad [Y^4]p=c_1\ne0.
\tag{13}
\]
Set \(J_1=J+R(x,Y)\). This further triangular change has
the polynomial inverse \(J=J_1-R(x,Y)\), and
\(I=xJ_1+p(Y)\).

For completeness the obstruction does not require a time-degree
bound on the new \(J_1\). We still have \(Ex=a\ne0\) and
\(E^2x=E^2Y=0\). In the localization at \(a\), write
\(x=at\), \(Y=y_0+bt\), with \(b=EY\), \(Et=1\), and \(Ey_0=0\).
The invariant \(I=xJ_1+p(Y)\) has constant term \(p(y_0)\)
in this polynomial variable \(t\), so \(I=p(y_0)\).
Since the leading coefficient of \(p\) is the nonzero
complex constant \(c_1\), this equality makes \(y_0\)
integral over the original normal polynomial ring.
Thus \(y_0\) is a polynomial in the actual coordinates
\((x,Y,Z,J_1)\). Degree in the independent coordinate \(J_1\)
now gives \(1=4\deg_{J_1}y_0\), or zero on the right if
\(y_0\) is independent of \(J_1\). Both are impossible.
This is the integral-intercept argument158 applied after
explicit polynomial shears. Therefore \(\kappa\ne0\).

## 5. The resulting actual coordinate and the remaining gap

Equations (7), (11), and (12) give the polynomial coordinates
\((X,Y,I,J)\), where \(J=W+DZ\). Their explicit inverse is
\[
Z=\kappa^{-1}\{I-(X+\zeta)J-P(X,Y)\},\qquad W=J-DZ .
\tag{14}
\]
Only the nonzero complex constant \(\kappa\) is inverted.
Substitution verifies both orders on the entire four-space.
Thus any nonconstant invariant of weight at most four in
this actual branch is an actual invariant coordinate.

This does not produce such an invariant. Its existence would
require (8), (11), and the remaining time coefficients.
In particular the algebraic coefficient
\(- (c_1\tau+c_0)k_0=\rho-(c_1\tau+c_0)k\)
must be a complex polynomial of degree at most four.
The companion164 specialization argument proves algebraicity
and restricts its values on a divisor, not this polynomial
degree bound.

The actual163 example has \(c_1=1,c_0=0,k_0=0\) and
\(I=-YH+Z+XW\), with \(\zeta=\beta=0,\kappa=1\).
It satisfies this conditional theorem; it does not prove
uniform existence. No global cancellation solution follows.
