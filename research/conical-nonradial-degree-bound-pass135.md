# Nonradial acceleration bounds the degree of a conical speed image

For an actual sliced three-good quadratic frame, suppose the rank-two
speed image is a nonlinear homogeneous cone. If the acceleration does
not have radial degree two in its finite invariant coefficient field,
the cone equation has degree two or three.

The bound uses a good original coordinate partial. Its original weight
drop is one, while its slice-filtration increase is at most three.
This is a conditional leading-equation restriction, not recognition of
the conical class or proof that the radial-degree condition is automatic.

## 1. Actual hypotheses and the radial derivation

Assume
\[
\begin{gathered}
B=\mathbb C[x_1,x_2,x_3,z]=A[u],\qquad E=\partial_u,\qquad A=\ker E,\\
E^2x_i=0,\quad a_i=Ex_i,\qquad E^3z=0,\quad q=E^2z,\\
\operatorname{trdeg}_{\mathbb C}\mathbb C(a_1,a_2,a_3)=2,\\
\ker\bigl(\mathbb C[T_1,T_2,T_3]\longrightarrow A,\ T_i\mapsto a_i\bigr)
=(H),\qquad H\text{ irreducible homogeneous},\quad d=\deg H\ge2.
\end{gathered}
\tag{1}
\]
All original generators are actual polynomial coordinates. The given
global slice need not be an ambient coordinate. At rank two, a nonzero
homogeneous relation supplies the homogeneous height-one prime in (1);
the restriction \(d\ge2\) excludes the linear, already recognized case.

The actual leading-part construction for the last partial supplies a
nonzero \(N\in\operatorname{LND}(A)\) fixing the speeds and \(q\).[^1]
Put \(L=\operatorname{Frac}(\ker_A N)\). An intrinsic local slice gives
\(\operatorname{Frac}A=L(v)\), with \(v\) transcendental over \(L\).
In particular \(\operatorname{trdeg}_{\mathbb C}L=2\).
No identification of \(\ker N\) with an affine plane is needed.

Write \(K_a=\mathbb C(a_1,a_2,a_3)\). Then \(L/K_a\) is finite and
separable. For completeness, choose independent speeds \(s,t\).
The finitely generated field \(\operatorname{Frac}A\) is finite over
\(\mathbb C(s,t,v)\). Elements of \(L\) linearly independent over
\(\mathbb C(s,t)\) remain independent after adjoining the transcendental
\(v\); this bounds \([L:\mathbb C(s,t)]\) by that finite degree.
Characteristic zero gives separability.

In fact \(L\) is the relative algebraic closure of \(K_a\) in
\(\operatorname{Frac}A\): an element algebraic over \(K_a\) is killed
by \(N\), by differentiation of its separable minimal polynomial.
Thus the coefficient field does not depend on a choice of generic
component after extending scalars.

The Euler derivation \(\sum T_i\partial_{T_i}\) preserves \((H)\),
because \(H\) is homogeneous. It descends to the speed ring and its
fraction field, and extends uniquely through \(L/K_a\). Denote this
extension by \(\mathcal R\). Thus
\[
a_i,q\in L,\qquad \mathcal R(a_i)=a_i,\qquad
\mathcal R:L\longrightarrow L.
\quad\text{Assume }\mathcal R(q)\ne2q.
\tag{2}
\]
The conclusion below is \(d\le3\). In particular (2) makes \(q\ne0\),
so the original bad coordinate has exact \(E\)-degree two.
There is no premise that \(q\) belongs to the polynomial speed ring.

## 2. The entire initial-map kernel

Let \(\eta\) be transcendental over \(L\), and put
\(K_{\rm good}=\mathbb C(\eta a_1,\eta a_2,\eta a_3)\).
Its transcendence degree is two. Indeed its homogeneous evaluation
kernel is \((H)\): that kernel is contained in the speed kernel by
evaluation at \(\eta=1\), and contains \(H\) by homogeneity.

On \(L(\eta)\) extend \(\mathcal R\) to kill \(\eta\), and set
\[
\mathcal D=\mathcal R-\eta\partial_\eta,\qquad
\mathcal D(\eta a_i)=0,\qquad
\mathcal D\bigl((q/2)\eta^2\bigr)
=\tfrac12\eta^2\bigl(\mathcal R(q)-2q\bigr)\ne0.
\tag{3}
\]
If the bad initial were algebraic over \(K_{\rm good}\), separability
would force its derivative under \(\mathcal D\) to vanish. Therefore it
is transcendental over that field. The original-weight initial map is
\[
\begin{gathered}
\theta:\mathbb C[X_1,X_2,X_3,Z]\longrightarrow A[\eta],\\
X_i\longmapsto a_i\eta,\qquad Z\longmapsto(q/2)\eta^2,\qquad
\operatorname{wt}(X_i)=1,\quad\operatorname{wt}(Z)=2,\\
\operatorname{trdeg}_{\mathbb C}\operatorname{im}\theta=3,
\qquad \ker\theta=(H(X_1,X_2,X_3)).
\end{gathered}
\tag{4}
\]
The last equality follows because the prime kernel has height one and
contains the prime ideal generated by \(H\), which remains irreducible
when the extra variable \(Z\) is adjoined. It describes the entire
initial-map kernel, not a chosen relation inside a larger ideal.

This identifies the algebra of the four coordinate initials only.
It does not assert that they generate \(\operatorname{gr}_E B\).

## 3. A good original partial raises slice degree by at most three

Choose \(j\in\{1,2,3\}\) for which \(H_{X_j}\ne0\), and put
\(D_j=\partial_{x_j}\), holding the other original coordinates fixed.
Since \(H\) is irreducible and the characteristic is zero,
\(H_{X_j}\notin(H)\).

Here is the actual cofactor bound needed for this choice.[^2]
Write \(c=Ez\) and let
\[
F_\lambda=\exp(\lambda E)(x_1,x_2,x_3,z)
=(x_1+\lambda a_1,\ x_2+\lambda a_2,\ x_3+\lambda a_3,
z+\lambda c+\lambda^2q/2).
\tag{5}
\]
It is an automorphism over \(\mathbb C[\lambda]\), with original
Jacobian determinant one. The coefficient column of
\(\exp(\lambda E)D_j\exp(-\lambda E)\) is the \(j\)-th column
of \(\operatorname{Jac}(F_\lambda)^{-1}\).
Its signed minors use two good rows and the bad row.
Before cancellation their degree in \(\lambda\) is at most four.
Every possible degree-four term is a coordinate of
\(da_i\wedge da_k\wedge dq\). These three differentials are dependent
because their functions belong to the transcendence-degree-two
field \(L\). Consequently the entire column has degree at most three,
and \((\operatorname{ad}_E)^4D_j=0\).

For any \(f\in A\), evaluating this last identity gives \(E^4D_j(f)=0\).
It also gives \(E^4D_j(u)=0\), since \(Eu=1\).
Thus both \(D_j(A)\subset A[u]_{\le3}\) and
\(D_j(u)\in A[u]_{\le3}\). The latter is an actual vertical
coefficient bound, not an inference from horizontal coefficients.
For a term \(f u^\ell\), differentiating \(f\) raises degree by at
most three, and differentiating \(u^\ell\) raises it by at most two.
Therefore
\(\deg_E D_jP\le\deg_E P+3\) on every nonzero actual polynomial,
with zero derivatives given degree \(-\infty\).
No localization of \(D_j\) as an LND is used.

## 4. Multiplicity and growth

Take a nonzero original polynomial \(P\) of weighted degree \(m\),
with highest part
\(P_m=H^k\Psi\), \(k\ge1\), \(H\nmid\Psi\).
Differentiation in \(X_j\) lowers original weight by one. Modulo \(H\),
\[
\begin{gathered}
\partial_{X_j}^{\,k}(H^k\Psi)
\equiv k!\,\Psi H_{X_j}^{\,k}\pmod H,\qquad
\theta(\Psi H_{X_j}^{\,k})\ne0,\\
\deg_E D_j^{\,k}P(x,z)=m-k
\le\deg_E P(x,z)+3k,\qquad
\deg_E P(x,z)\ge m-4k.
\end{gathered}
\tag{6}
\]
The nonzero evaluation follows from (4), primeness, and characteristic
zero. Terms of smaller original weight cannot contribute at weight
\(m-k\). Thus the exact degree on the second line is justified.
This is the multiplicity mechanism of133 with a weight-one original
partial rather than its weight-two bad partial.[^3]

For a nonconstant invariant \(f=P(x,z)\), its \(E\)-degree is zero.
Its positive-weight top must lie in the full kernel (4), so it has
a factorization as above. Equation (6) gives
\[
kd\le m\le4k.
\tag{7}
\]
Nonconstant invariants exist, since \(\operatorname{trdeg}A=3\).
Hence \(d\le4\).

If \(d=4\), (7) forces \(m=4k\) and makes \(\Psi\) a complex constant.
Every nonconstant invariant therefore has original-weight initial form
equal to a scalar multiple of \(H^k\).
Let \(F_MA\) be the invariants of original weight at most \(M\).
The highest-part map on each filtered quotient then gives
\[
\dim_{\mathbb C}F_MA\le\lfloor M/4\rfloor+1.
\quad\text{But}\quad
\binom{n+3}{3}\le\dim_{\mathbb C}F_{Dn}A
\quad(n\ge0),
\tag{8}
\]
where \(D\) is the largest original weight of three chosen algebraically
independent invariants. Their monomials of total exponent at most \(n\)
justify the second inequality. Cubic growth contradicts the first
linear bound for large \(n\). Thus \(d\ne4\), proving \(d\in\{2,3\}\).

No finite generation of the invariant initial algebra, termination of
a subduction algorithm, or change to zero-weight invariant generators
has been assumed.

## 5. The precise remaining alternative

The argument proves the conditional conclusion
\(\mathcal R(q)\ne2q\Longrightarrow\deg H\le3\).
Thus a nonlinear conical equation of degree at least four forces
\(\mathcal R(q)=2q\) under the actual hypotheses.

If \(q\ne0\) and \(\mathcal R(q)=2q\), then \(\mathcal D\) in (3) kills all four
initials. It is nonzero because \(\mathcal D(\eta)=-\eta\).
These initials cannot have transcendence degree three: a derivation
of the characteristic-zero three-dimensional function field
\(L(\eta)\) killing three independent elements would vanish by
separable algebraicity. They already contain the rank-two good
initial field, so their transcendence degree is exactly two.
The full initial-map kernel then has height two, and (4) is false.
An invariant's initial part need not be divisible by \(H\);
the one-prime growth proof above therefore does not apply.

Neither the degree-two nor degree-three conclusion recognizes the
remaining action. The radial-degree-two alternative is retained,
not excluded. No bare speed or acceleration pattern is asserted
to arise from a polynomial coordinate frame.
