# The actual nonplanar fiber excludes every three-generator set containing h

The fixed surface has an obstruction that covers all noninvariant
candidates over the retained pair, without a degree bound:
\[
\boxed{R\ne\mathbb C[g,h,j]\quad\text{for every }j\in R.}
\tag{1}
\]
In fact the conclusion is stronger:
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Spec}(R/(h))\text{ does not admit a closed
embedding into }\mathbb A^2.}
\tag{2}
\]
Consequently \(h\) cannot belong to any three-element algebra
generating set of \(R\), even if the other two generators change.

The proof uses two actual irreducible components of the fiber
\(h=0\), their scheme-theoretic intersection of length one, and
the actual identification of two points on one normalization.
A plane embedding would force a principal equation incompatible
with that identification.

This does not decide whether some triple replacing \(h\)
generates \(R\). The abstract bound \(3\le\mu_{\mathbb C}(R)\le4\)
and the prescribed ambient primitive-LND realization in
\(\mathbb C^{[3]}\) remain unresolved.

## 1. Fixed data and two components of the actual fiber

Keep the unchanged normalization and conductor gluing:
\[
B=\mathbb C[u,y,z]/(A(u)z-y(y-1)),\qquad
A(u)=(u-1)(u-i),\qquad d=u^4-1,\quad e=du.
\tag{3}
\]
The first conductor pair identifies the split lines
\((u,y)=(1,0)\) and \((i,0)\) with their shared scaled parameter.
In particular the points
\[
(1,0,0)\quad\text{and}\quad(i,0,0)
\tag{4}
\]
have the same image in \(\operatorname{Spec}R\).
The other two pairs, the shared parameters, and all coefficients
remain those of the [four-generator proof](nodal-elimination-pass23.md).

Write
\[
b(u)=\frac{1-i}{4}(u+1)(u+i),\qquad
a(u)=-\frac i2A(u)\left(u+\frac{1+i}{2}\right),
\]
\[
q(u)=-u b(u)^2,\qquad r(u)=\frac i8uA(u),\qquad
g=a(u)+b(u)y,\quad h=q(u)z+r(u)y.
\tag{5}
\]
Thus \(R=\mathbb C[d,e,g,h]\), and the normalization map
\(\nu:\operatorname{Spec}B\to\operatorname{Spec}R\) is finite.
It is an isomorphism off \(d=0\) and makes only the stated
conductor identifications.

Consider the following two reduced closed curves in
\(\operatorname{Spec}B\):
\[
\widetilde C_0=V(u),\qquad
\widetilde C_1=V(y,z).
\tag{6}
\]
The first is an affine line with coordinate \(y\), since
\(A(0)=i\) and \(z=y(y-1)/i\). The second is an affine line
with coordinate \(u\).

Both lie in the actual zero fiber of \(h\). On
\(\widetilde C_0\), both \(q\) and \(r\) vanish; on
\(\widetilde C_1\), both \(y\) and \(z\) vanish.
Let \(C_0,C_1\) be their reduced images in \(\operatorname{Spec}R\).
Finiteness makes these images closed, and each is irreducible
of dimension one. They are distinct irreducible components
of \(\operatorname{Spec}(R/(h))\), since \(R\) is an integral
surface and \(h\) is nonzero.

The restriction to \(C_0\) is particularly explicit:
\[
d=-1,\qquad e=h=0,\qquad
g=\frac{1+i}{4}(1+y).
\tag{7}
\]
Hence \(C_0\cong\mathbb A^1\), with exact prime ideal
\[
\mathfrak p_0=(d+1,e,h)R.
\tag{8}
\]
Indeed the quotient is generated by \(g\), and (7) shows that
no nonzero polynomial in \(g\) vanishes on the curve.

Let \(\mathfrak p_1\) be the kernel of restriction to the section
\(\widetilde C_1\). Its coordinate ring embeds in \(\mathbb C[u]\)
through
\[
d=u^4-1,\qquad e=u(u^4-1),\qquad g=a(u),\qquad h=0.
\tag{9}
\]
The algebra \(\mathbb C[u]\) is finite over this image, since
\(u\) satisfies \(u^4=d+1\). Their fraction fields agree because
\(u=e/d\) away from \(d=0\). Thus \(\mathbb A^1_u\) is the
normalization of \(C_1\).

The points \(u=1\) and \(u=i\) on that normalization are
identified, by (4). Every regular function on \(C_1\), when
pulled back to \(\mathbb C[u]\), therefore has equal values
at \(1\) and \(i\). No assertion that the entire image ring
is determined by this one condition is needed below.

## 2. Their only intersection has scheme-theoretic length one

The curve \(C_0\) is entirely outside the conductor, since
\(d=-1\) there. No point of it is identified with any other
normalization point. Consequently the only point of
\(C_0\cap C_1\) is
\[
o=\nu(0,0,0).
\tag{10}
\]

This is also a scheme-theoretic computation, not just a point
count. At \(o\), the normalization is an isomorphism.
The value \(A(0)=i\ne0\) lets us eliminate \(z\), and
\[
\mathcal O_{\operatorname{Spec}R,o}
 \cong \mathbb C[u,y]_{(u,y)}.
\tag{11}
\]
There the two reduced curve ideals are
\[
(\mathfrak p_0)_o=(u),\qquad
(\mathfrak p_1)_o=(y).
\tag{12}
\]
For the second equality, the original section ideal is
\((y,z)\), and \(z=y(y-1)/A(u)\) lies in \((y)\).
For the first, use \(C_0=V(u)\); it also follows directly
from (8), since \(e=u(u^4-1)\) has a unit factor at \(o\).

Therefore
\[
\mathcal O_{\operatorname{Spec}R,o}/
 ((\mathfrak p_0)_o+(\mathfrak p_1)_o)\cong\mathbb C.
\tag{13}
\]
There are no other intersection points. Equivalently,
\(R/(\mathfrak p_0+\mathfrak p_1)\) is a zero-dimensional
algebra of length one. The same intersection length is obtained
inside the fiber \(R/(h)\), because \(h\) belongs to both
prime ideals.

The normalization of \(C_1\) is an isomorphism near this point.
Thus the intersection corresponds to a single simple zero at
\(u=0\) on the normalized line.

## 3. The principal-component contradiction

Assume, for contradiction, that the actual fiber admits a closed
embedding into \(\mathbb A^2\). Algebraically there is a
surjection
\[
\mathbb C[X,Y]\longrightarrow R/(h).
\tag{14}
\]
The inverse image of the prime defining \(C_0\) is a height-one
prime of \(\mathbb C[X,Y]\). It is generated by a single
irreducible polynomial \(L(X,Y)\), since this polynomial ring
is a UFD. This elementary principal-prime fact is the only
ambient-plane input.

It follows that the ideal of \(C_0\) in \(R/(h)\) is principal,
generated by the image of \(L\).
Restrict that image to \(C_1\) and then pull back to its
normalization. We obtain a polynomial
\[
\ell(u)\in\mathbb C[u].
\]

It is nonzero because \(C_0\) and \(C_1\) are distinct
components. Its only zero is \(u=0\), because the only
intersection is (10), and finite normalization has no
other preimage of that smooth point.
The zero has order exactly one: the quotient of the local
ring of \(C_1\) by \(\ell\) computes the intersection in
(13), and this local ring is the DVR
\(\mathbb C[u]_{(u)}\).
Consequently
\[
\ell(u)=c u,\qquad c\in\mathbb C^*.
\tag{15}
\]

But \(\ell\) is the pullback of a regular function on \(C_1\).
The identification (4) forces
\[
\ell(1)=\ell(i).
\]
Equation (15) would give \(c=ci\), a contradiction.
This proves (2).

The proof does not assume that the entire fiber is reduced
or classify its other components. It uses the two reduced
component primes, their actual intersection scheme, and the
finite normalization of \(C_1\). Nor does it assume that a
smooth affine line in the plane has already been rectified
to a coordinate axis: its height-one prime is principal
without such a theorem.

More generally, the same argument would require the
intersection multiplicity \(\ell_0\) at this sole smooth
point to satisfy \(i^{\ell_0}=1\), hence \(4\mid\ell_0\).
The actual multiplicity is one, not a multiple of four.

## 4. Consequences for all noninvariant generators

If \(R=\mathbb C[h,f_1,f_2]\) for any \(f_1,f_2\in R\),
then quotienting by \(h\) gives a surjection
\(\mathbb C[X,Y]\to R/(h)\). This contradicts (2).
Therefore
\[
\boxed{h\text{ belongs to no three-element algebra generating
set of }R.}
\tag{16}
\]
Taking \(f_1=g,f_2=j\) proves (1) for every \(j\), with no
invariance or degree restriction.

This closes the monogenic search over the specific
\(S=\mathbb C[g,h]\) from
[pass 26](actual-surface-realization-pass26.md). In its exact
rank-nine free basis, the determinant of the columns
\(1,j,\ldots,j^8\) can never be a nonzero constant.

There is also a precise specialization statement. For every
\(j\in R\), its power-basis determinant specialized at \(H=0\)
is either the zero polynomial or a nonconstant polynomial
in \(G\). Otherwise the specialized nine columns would be
a \(\mathbb C[G]\)-basis of \(R/(h)\), making that algebra
monogenic over \(\mathbb C[G]\), contrary to (2).

The quantifier is important: every proposed \(j\) fails
somewhere on the fiber \(H=0\). This does not assert that
there is one closed base point at which every possible
generator fails, or that all the individual finite fibers
are nonmonogenic.

Equivalently, under any hypothetical hypersurface embedding
of \(R\) in affine three-space, the function \(h\) cannot
be the restriction of an ambient coordinate. This is a
constraint on that specific function, not a prohibition on
all possible embeddings.

## 5. An explicit genuine noninvariant candidate also fails

The second element of the rank-nine basis is a natural
noninvariant candidate:
\[
j_*=
u-\frac12\bigl(1-i+2ig\bigr)u^2\in R.
\tag{17}
\]
Its membership in the actual ring follows from the exact
conductor interpolation basis, not from localization.
For the descended action
\[
\partial u=0,\quad \partial y=uA(u),\quad
\partial z=u(2y-1),
\]
one computes
\[
\partial j_*=-\frac{1+i}{4}(u^4-1)u^3\ne0.
\tag{18}
\]
Thus this candidate is genuinely noninvariant.

On \(C_0\), \(j_*=0\). On the normalized section \(C_1\),
\[
j_*(u,0,0)=-\frac u2 K(u),
\qquad
K(u)=u^4-\frac{1+i}{2}u^3+
                  \frac{1-i}{2}u-2.
\tag{19}
\]
The polynomial \(K\) has a root \(\zeta\), and
\(\gcd(K,u(u^4-1))=1\). Thus every root is nonzero and
off the conductor. At \((\zeta,0,0)\), the triple
\((j_*,g,h)\) equals \((0,a(\zeta),0)\).
The smooth point of \(C_0\) with
\[
y_0=\frac{a(\zeta)-a(0)}{b(0)},\qquad
z_0=\frac{y_0(y_0-1)}i
\]
has exactly the same triple. These are distinct actual
surface points. This is a bounded exact check on a
noninvariant basis element, not the proof of the universal
statement (16).
