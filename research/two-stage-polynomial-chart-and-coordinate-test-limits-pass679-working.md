## 1. The localized chart is an open subset of ordinary affine four-space

Use676's exact whole presentation

    B=C[a,b,V,k,S]/(a²S+2aV²k+V⁴+a(1-V)+b³),
    A=1-V+2V²k+aS.

Here A is a regular function, not the unknown ring in the cancellation
problem. The relation is aA+V⁴+b³=0. After inverting A,676 gives

    Q=B_A=C[A,A^(-1),V,b,S,T]/G,
    G=V²T+b³S+AV+A(A-1), T=V²S-2Ak.

Set

    v0=V/A, s0=S/A²,
    h=1+v0+v0²T+b³s0 in C[v0,b,s0,T].                  (1)

Substitution gives G=A(Ah-1). Since A is a unit, there is an exact
isomorphism

    Q = C[v0,b,s0,T,h^(-1)],
    A=h^(-1), V=v0/h, S=s0/h².                          (2)

The inverse fixes b,T and uses the first line of (1). Both compositions
recover all generators. No additional open subset has been removed.
In the same coordinates, the other original generators are

    a=-v0⁴/h³-b³h,
    k=v0²s0/(2h³)-Th/2.                                (3)

These are elements of the stated localization and satisfy the whole B
equation. They do not supply a polynomial coordinate system for B.

In particular, A=1 corresponds exactly to h=1. Thus the previously studied
threefold

    C0=C[V,b,S,T]/(V²T+b³S+V)

already occurs as a fiber of the explicit polynomial h on affine four-space.
Its Picard group Z, proved in678, also shows that h is not a coordinate:
a coordinate would have every scalar fiber isomorphic to affine three-space,
whose Picard group is zero.

Every LND on Q kills the unit h, hence also A=h^(-1). A whole B action
moving A therefore does not restrict to an LND on this chart. Transporting
chart LNDs and clearing invariant denominators cannot produce such an
action. In particular, translations on the polynomial ambient four-space
cannot simply be restricted as additive actions after inverting a function
they do not fix.

These statements sharply distinguish three claims: the chart is a
polynomial principal open; h is not a polynomial coordinate; polynomiality
of the WHOLE B remains unknown in this investigation.

## 2. The removed divisor has an explicit normalization and conductor

The missing divisor A=0 has coordinate ring

    D=B/(A)
     =C[a,b,V,k,S]/(b³+V⁴, aS+2V²k+1-V).              (4)

Indeed the first B relation is aA+V⁴+b³, so imposing A=0 gives precisely
these two equations. The functions a and V generate the unit ideal in D:

    1=V-aS-2V²k.

Consequently D(a) and D(V) cover Spec(D). On the first chart,

    D_a=C[V,b]/(b³+V⁴)[a,a^(-1),k].                    (5)

On the second, put t=-b/V. The cusp relation gives V=t³, b=-t⁴,
and t is a unit. Eliminating k yields

    D_V=C[t,t^(-1),a,S].                               (6)

Both charts are domains with nonempty overlap, so D is a domain.

Its normalization is

    Dbar=C[t,a,k,S]/(aS+2t⁶k+1-t³),
    V=t³, b=-t⁴.                                      (7)

To prove this, the displayed polynomial is primitive and linear in S over
C[t,a,k], hence defines a domain. Its S-, a-, and k-derivatives are a,S,2t⁶.
If all vanish, a=S=t=0, contradicting its equation. Thus Dbar is smooth,
in particular normal. The map in (7) identifies Dbar with D[t]: t³=V is
monic, and t=-b/V belongs to Frac(D). Therefore Dbar is finite, birational,
and normal over D, which proves it is the full normalization.

The conductor, viewed as an ideal of Dbar or its contained ideal in D, is

    conductor(Dbar/D)=t⁶Dbar=(V²,Vb,b²)D.               (8)

Here is a global proof. On D(a), (5) reduces the question to the cusp
C[t³,t⁴] inside C[t], with polynomial and Laurent variables adjoined.
Its exponent semigroup misses 1,2,5 and contains every integer at least6:
use 6,7,8 and add multiples of3. Linear independence of powers of t then
gives conductor t⁶C[t], with the same result over C[a,a^(-1),k]. On D(V)
the normalization is already an isomorphism, and t⁶ is a unit. These two
charts cover, proving the first equality in (8). Finally Dbar=D[t] is
generated as a D-module by 1,t,t². Hence t⁶Dbar is generated by t⁶,t⁷,t⁸,
which are V²,-Vb,b². This proves the second equality without an unstated
saturation step.

In particular D is not normal: t is integral but absent on the cusp chart
(5). Neither this fact nor the normalization calculation proves B is not
polynomial. A divisor in a smooth polynomial ambient space can be singular
and nonnormal. These formulas specify the actual boundary omitted by (2).

## 3. An invariant polynomial has ruled weighted leading forms

Let R=C[Z1,...,Zn], and suppose a nonzero LND D kills a nonzero polynomial g.
For ANY weights in a totally ordered abelian group, let H be the weighted
leading form of g. Then Frac(R) is ruled over C(H) and over C(q) for every
nonzero factor q of H. Constant H or q causes no exception.

The standard degeneration step is as follows. The subgroup generated by
the finitely many weights is finitely generated and torsion-free. Decompose
D into its finitely many homogeneous components, and take the nonzero
component delta of largest degree e. For homogeneous r of degree d,

    delta^m(r) is the degree-(d+me) component of D^m(r).  (9)

Indeed all other compositions have strictly smaller degree. Thus delta is
locally nilpotent. The highest-degree part of D(g)=0 gives delta(H)=0.
Factorial closure of an LND kernel gives delta(q)=0 for every factor q.
This uses neither positive weights nor a well-order. It is the standard
extreme-component argument; compare [Liendo, Lemma1.10 and its proof](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0812.0802),
and its ordered-group formulation in [Krasikov--Rassolov, Lemma1.9](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.17670v1).
It is not an assertion that interior homogeneous components are LNDs.

For completeness, choose r with delta(r)=c!=0 and delta(c)=0, and put
s=r/c. After inverting c, the slice theorem gives

    Frac(R)=K(s), K=Frac(ker(delta)), delta(s)=1.         (10)

The coefficient field K is finitely generated over C. To see this directly,
write the finitely many generators Zi of Frac(R) as rational functions in s
with coefficients in K, and let K0 be the field generated by those finitely
many coefficients. Then K(s)=K0(s)=Frac(R). Constants of differentiation
with respect to s in K0(s) are K0 in characteristic zero, so K=K0.

Since C(H) and every C(q) are contained in K, (10) proves ruledness over
each of these fields. This is stronger than separable uniruledness: the
auxiliary rational extension can be the field itself, so the required
algebraic extension has degree one.

## 4. Every residual-variable coordinate change still passes the Mulay test

In the monolinear presentation of B in Section1, the distinguished variable
is a, its exponent is2, the linear variable is S, and the residual polynomial
at a=0 is

    g=V⁴+b³ in R=C[V,b,k].

For any polynomial automorphism sigma of R, including wild ones, set

    g'=sigma(g), D=sigma partial_k sigma^(-1).

Then D is a nonzero LND and D(g')=0. Section3 applies for every allowed
weight assignment after that coordinate change and for every factor of
the resulting leading form. No degree bound on sigma is imposed.

For this particular g the other preliminary conditions also hold. The cusp
polynomial is irreducible. Over the algebraic closure of C(lambda), the
polynomial lambda-V⁴ has simple zeros and is not a cube in the rational
V-field. Hence b³+V⁴-lambda is irreducible there. This proves geometric
integrality of the generic fiber and regularity of C(V,b,k)/C(g). Both
properties persist under sigma.

Mulay's [stated Main Theorem](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jalgebra.2026.05.043)
requires these preliminary conditions and separable uniruledness over the
leading-form field and certain factor fields. All of those necessary tests
therefore pass for EVERY sigma above. The Section5 proof of Mulay's paper
was not accessible; this is an application of its completely inspected
statement, supported by the independent argument in Section3.

This also covers automorphisms of C[a,V,b,k] over C[a]: reducing such an
automorphism and its inverse modulo a gives an automorphism of R. It does
not cover arbitrary changes mixing a with the residual variables, or a
different monolinear presentation not obtained this way. Passing the tests
does not establish polynomiality. It does justify parking this entire
residual-coordinate-search route rather than repeating more finite samples.
