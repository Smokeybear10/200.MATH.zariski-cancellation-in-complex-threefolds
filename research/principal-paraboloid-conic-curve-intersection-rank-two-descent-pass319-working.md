# Original curve intersection and a rank-two action after the conic slice gate

Working result: the exact generic-plane equality of318 descends to two
commuting, generically independent locally nilpotent derivations of the
original kernel A. Their common invariant field is the retained curve
field. A known commuting-derivations theorem then identifies the original
curve intersection and reduces recognition to finitely many possible
dependent fibers.

If the original speed a is complex constant, recognition is immediate.
Otherwise the argument below proves the actual action and the precise
finite-fiber reduction, not the removal of its exceptional fibers. It does
not turn a coordinate over the curve field into an original coordinate.

## 1. Complete retained entry and the smaller hypotheses used here

Retain the full actual reciprocal-conic hypotheses of the fully read
[316](principal-paraboloid-conic-factor-unit-scalar-rigidity-pass316-working.md)
and the original-slice conclusions of the fully read
[318](principal-paraboloid-conic-original-slice-speed-pass318-working.md).
Thus the original chart has profile1222, full initial kernel
\((X^2Z-Y^2)\), its literal selected derivation N, and both actual
return statements. The following established consequences are the exact
inputs needed for the present descent:
\[
\begin{gathered}
\mathcal B=\mathbb C[X,Y,Z,W]=A[u],\qquad E=\partial_u,\qquad
K=\operatorname{Frac}A,\\
0\ne N\in\operatorname{LND}_{\mathbb C}(A),\qquad
\mathbb C\subset\mathcal F\subset\operatorname{Frac}\ker_A N,
\qquad [\mathcal F:\mathbb C(t)]<\infty,\\
K=\mathcal F(s,\sigma),\qquad
\mathcal F[A]=\mathcal F[s,\sigma],\qquad
Ns=0,\quad N\sigma=\rho\in\mathcal F^*,\\
a=EX\in A\cap\mathcal F^*,\qquad Ea=0.
\end{gathered}
\tag{1}
\]
The notation \(\rho\) here denotes only the speed \(N\sigma\), not a
new leading ratio. The two elements s,sigma are algebraically independent
over \(\mathcal F\), and every equality in (1) is inside K. In particular
\(\mathcal F[A]\) means the ring generated by A and the subfield
\(\mathcal F\) there, not an unspecified tensor product.

For orientation,318 obtains the generic-plane equality by actual
derivatives, after the original slice has forced a to be a field unit:
\[
s=\frac{E^2W}{2a^2},\qquad
\sigma=\frac{EY}{a}-2tX,\qquad
\mathcal F[\mathcal B]=\mathcal F[X,s,\sigma].
\tag{2}
\]
Taking the E-kernel gives the equality in (1). Neither normality of an
arbitrary field-adjoined ring nor rational entry alone is being invoked.

The ring A is finitely generated, normal, a UFD, and has units
\(\mathbb C^*\). These are consequences of \(A[u]=\mathcal B\):
evaluation at u=0 gives \(A\simeq\mathcal B/(u)\), proving finite
generation. An element of K integral over A is integral over the normal
ring \(\mathcal B\), hence belongs to \(\mathcal B\cap K=A\).
Factorization and units descend by comparing u-degrees in A[u], giving
the remaining assertions. No independent rationality of A is assumed.

## 2. Constant original speed already recognizes the kernel

If \(a\in\mathbb C^*\), the actual coordinate \(X/a\) is a slice
for E. The slice theorem gives \(\mathcal B=A[X]\), so
\[
A\simeq\mathcal B/(X)\simeq\mathbb C[Y,Z,W].
\tag{3}
\]
The last isomorphism uses that X is one of the original polynomial
coordinates. It would not follow merely from an arbitrary slice of a
restriction or from a field-valued parameter.

For the rest of the note assume \(a\notin\mathbb C\).

## 3. Normality makes the original intersection large enough

Set
\[
T=A\cap\mathcal F.
\tag{4}
\]
At this stage no finite generation or polynomial description of T is
assumed. The nonconstant element \(a\in T\) is transcendental over
\(\mathbb C\). Since \(\mathcal F\) is a finitely generated field
of transcendence degree one, \(\mathcal F/\mathbb C(a)\) is finite.

Let \(f\in\mathcal F\). Take a monic algebraic equation
\[
f^n+c_1f^{n-1}+\cdots+c_n=0,\qquad c_i\in\mathbb C(a).
\]
Choose \(0\ne d(a)\in\mathbb C[a]\) such that all \(d(a)c_i\)
belong to \(\mathbb C[a]\). Then \(d(a)f\) satisfies the monic
equation with coefficients \(d(a)^ic_i\in\mathbb C[a]\).
It is therefore integral over A. Since \(f\in K\) and A is normal,
\(d(a)f\in A\cap\mathcal F=T\). Also \(d(a)\in T\setminus\{0\}\).
Consequently
\[
\operatorname{Frac}T=\mathcal F.
\tag{5}
\]
This argument uses the actual nonconstant regular element a. A curve
field sitting abstractly in K would not itself give (5) by the same proof.

## 4. Clearing coefficients gives a second actual LND

On the polynomial ring \(\mathcal F[s,\sigma]\), let
\(\partial_s\) fix \(\mathcal F,\sigma\). It is not initially
asserted to preserve A.

Choose finitely many algebra generators \(a_1,\ldots,a_r\) of A.
By (1), each \(\partial_s a_i\) belongs to \(\mathcal F[A]\), so
can be written as a finite sum of elements of A multiplied by coefficients
in \(\mathcal F\). Equation (5) permits a single common denominator
\(b\in T\setminus\{0\}\) for all these finitely many coefficients.
Thus
\[
D_2:=b\partial_s\quad\hbox{satisfies}\quad D_2(A)\subseteq A.
\tag{6}
\]
This is an actual derivation of A, not just a rational vector field.
Because \(b\in\mathcal F\), it is killed by \(\partial_s\).
For every \(f\in A\subset\mathcal F[s,\sigma]\),
\[
D_2^j(f)=b^j\partial_s^j(f)=0\quad\hbox{for all sufficiently large }j.
\tag{7}
\]
Hence \(D_2\in\operatorname{LND}_{\mathbb C}(A)\). It is nonzero:
its extension to K sends s to b, and a derivation vanishing on A would
vanish on its fraction field.

The retained derivation N extends on this same polynomial ring as
\(N=\rho\partial_\sigma\). Both \(b,\rho\) belong to
\(\mathcal F^*\), so
\[
[N,D_2]=0,
\qquad N,D_2\text{ are linearly independent over }K,
\qquad K^{N,D_2}=\mathcal F.
\tag{8}
\]
Independence follows by applying a putative relation to s and sigma.
The constant-field equality follows first by taking the rational
sigma-constants, then the rational s-constants, in characteristic zero.
Intersecting with A gives
\[
\ker_A N\cap\ker_A D_2=T.
\tag{9}
\]
No division of a derivation by a noninvariant polynomial occurs here.
The coefficient used in (6) is invariant under both polynomial partials.

## 5. The precise published reduction and its residual fibers

Apply [Derksen, van den Essen and Maubach, Theorem2.1 and
Proposition3.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0806.2038) to the original UFD A
of transcendence degree three, its units \(\mathbb C^*\), and the pair
in (8). The exact result gives
\[
T=\mathbb C[\alpha],\qquad
\mathcal F=\mathbb C(\alpha),\qquad
\alpha-\lambda\text{ is prime in A for every }\lambda\in\mathbb C.
\tag{10}
\]
There are only finitely many values of lambda at which the induced
derivations are dependent over \(A/(\alpha-\lambda)\). At every
other value that quotient is a polynomial two-ring. If there are no
dependent values, the same theorem yields
\[
A=\mathbb C[\alpha]^{[2]}.
\tag{11}
\]
All its UFD, unit, commutation and independence hypotheses were proved
above. The source was freshly read at the exact theorem and proposition;
the conclusion is a cited known theorem, not a new classification result.

This leaves a definite boundary: the construction has not shown
independence on every special fiber. In particular the denominator b in
(6) can introduce dependent fibers. Saturating the invariant-coefficient
span can improve the pair, but does not in general remove all bad fibers;
the complete retained
[saturation audit](saturated-rank-two-pass.md) supplies its exact scope
and a non-polynomial-ring control, not a counterexample satisfying the
present stable-affine hypotheses.

One cannot instead apply the three-variable commuting-derivations
coordinate theorem directly to A, since \(A\simeq\mathbb C^{[3]}\)
is the missing conclusion. Nor does extending N and D2 to kill the
original slice u solve this: together with E this gives three commuting
derivations on the four-variable polynomial ring, which is not the
two-derivation three-variable case of
[Maubach's original theorem](https://www.math.ru.nl/~maubach/Papers/The_Commuting_Dervations.pdf).
Its dimension convention was checked against that primary paper.

## 6. Bounded conclusion

In the reciprocal-conic sector after318, either the original speed is a
nonzero complex constant and (3) recognizes A, or the original ring has
the exact curve intersection (10) and two commuting generically independent
LNDs constructed above. Recognition in the latter case still requires
removing the finite dependent fibers or proving a different actual
coordinate criterion.

The generic equality \(\mathcal F[A]=\mathcal F[s,\sigma]\) is not
silently replaced by the polynomial-entry hypothesis266 in three actual
ambient coordinates. Normality is used only for the concrete denominator
clearing in Section3. The claimed descent is the actual derivation (6),
not the descent of s,sigma or alpha to a polynomial coordinate.
