# Excluding the zero quadric remainder by the full scalar and actual return

Working exclusion: under the complete actual264 moving-slope chart
k=s+f, n=nu*s+n0 with nu' nonzero, the q=0 boundary of441 cannot occur.
The full scalar first forces nu' to be a complex constant and puts
the remaining coefficient xi on a fixed conic. A nonzero conic level
contradicts the strong original field return. The zero level contradicts
the original slice and the exact coefficient-adjoined polynomial ring.

The scalar does NOT become identically zero after its coefficient
constraints. Its nonzero value is retained below. No normalization
theorem, ambient coordinate promotion or recognition of another
degree profile is used.

## 3. Exact mixed compatibility gives a fixed conic

## 6. The nonzero conic level contradicts the field return

Suppose eta is nonzero. Then eta-K^2/12 is not a square in F(K).
Indeed its two distinct roots in K are complex constants, and each
has odd order one as a zero of this rational function. Thus (20)
is an irreducible quadratic equation for xi over F(K).

On the exact field in(29) there is a nontrivial involution
\[
U\longmapsto U,\qquad K\longmapsto K,\qquad
\xi\longmapsto-\xi,\qquad
P\longmapsto P+\frac{2\xi}{U},
\qquad F\ \hbox{fixed}.
\tag{30}
\]
It is well defined: the quadratic involution on F(K,xi) extends
over the independent variables U,P by the displayed rational shift.
Applying it twice fixes P as well. It is nontrivial since xi is
nonzero. No regularity at U=0 is needed for a field automorphism.

All four generators in(27),(28) are fixed. For the only quadratic
calculation,
\[
-U(P+2\xi/U)^2+2\xi(P+2\xi/U)+q_cK
=-UP^2-2\xi P+q_cK.
\]
The identities for Y1 and Z2 are immediate. It follows that the
involution fixes Frac(R) pointwise, contradicting (29), where it
negates xi. This excludes eta nonzero using the ORIGINAL strong
return, not just whole-line generation over L.

## 7. The zero conic level contradicts the original slice

Suppose eta=0. Since K is nonzero, (20) gives
\[
\xi=\omega K,\qquad
\omega\in\mathbb C^*,\qquad \omega^2=-1/12,\qquad L=F(K).
\tag{31}
\]
Introduce the polynomial ring
\[
\mathcal N=F[U,K,P].
\]
These are three algebraically independent variables over F by
Section4. Equations(27),(28),(31) give R contained in N.

The original speed a0=EX=EU lies in B, hence in R. It also lies
in L by the selected-derivation input(2). Therefore
\[
a_0\in\mathcal R\cap L\subseteq
F[U,K,P]\cap F(K)=F[K].
\tag{32}
\]
The intersection identity follows by the independence of U,P over
F(K). E kills F,K,P, so on N it acts as a0(K)*partial_U.
This is an explicit action, not an appeal to extension of a locally
nilpotent derivation to a normalization.

The ORIGINAL slice u belongs to B, hence to R and N. Its equation
Eu=1 now gives
\[
a_0(K)\,u_U=1\quad\hbox{in }F[U,K,P].
\]
Thus a0 is a unit of this polynomial ring, which proves a0 in F*.
This does not claim a0 is a complex unit in the original ring.

Since E preserves R and a0 inverse is now in F, the actual identities
\[
E(Y_1)=a_0P,\qquad E(Z_2)=a_0K
\tag{33}
\]
force P,K into R. Together with U this gives R=N.

Finally specialize U=K=0 in N, leaving the polynomial ring F[P].
Under(28),(31), each of U,Y1,Z2,W2 specializes to zero. Equation(27)
therefore makes the image of R equal to F, whereas R=N would make
that image F[P]. This contradiction excludes eta=0.
