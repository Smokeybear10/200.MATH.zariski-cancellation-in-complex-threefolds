# Derivative order against the full coordinate-initial kernel

**Status: bounded working proof of necessary inequalities for an actual
all-positive sliced frame.**

The derivative-multiplicity argument can be used without assuming that
the full initial kernel has height one. The replacement for the
multiplicity of a chosen equation is the least total order of a source
partial derivative that escapes the **full** initial prime. This gives
an exact differentiated E-degree and a necessary numerical inequality.
A polynomial translation control shows that its exact-cost inequality
can be an equality when the initial kernel has height three.

There is no assertion of a degree cutoff, coordinate completion,
degree-lowering automorphism, universal entry, cancellation, or novelty.
In particular, the control below is not a globally minimizing frame.

## 1. Actual ring and the retained all-ring shifts

Assume
\[
\begin{gathered}
B=\mathbb C[X_1,X_2,X_3,X_4],\qquad
E\in\operatorname{LND}_{\mathbb C}(B),\qquad Eu=1,\\
A=\ker E,\qquad B=A[u],\qquad
d_i=\deg_E X_i\ge1,\qquad S=\sum_{i=1}^4d_i,\\
X_i=\sum_{j=0}^{d_i}c_{i,j}u^j,\qquad
c_i=c_{i,d_i}\in A\setminus\{0\}.
\end{gathered}
\tag{1}
\]
The four displayed generators are actual polynomial coordinates.
The supplied slice need not be a coordinate. E-degree is degree in the
unique presentation \(A[u]\); the degree of zero is \(-\infty\).

Let \(T_i=\partial_{X_i}\) be the actual coordinate partials. We retain
the actual all-positive shift theorem of
[158, Sections 2, 3 and 6](arbitrary-positive-weight-initial-bound-pass158-working.md).
In precisely (1), it supplies exact nonnegative integer shifts \(k_i\)
with
\[
\begin{gathered}
k_i=\max\{\deg_E T_i(a):a\in A, T_i(a)\ne0\},\\
0\le k_i\le S-d_i-1,\qquad \deg_E T_i(u)\le k_i,\\
\deg_E T_i(f)\le\deg_E f+k_i
\quad(f\in B,\ T_i(f)\ne0).
\end{gathered}
\tag{2}
\]
The maximum exists: \(A\simeq B/(u)\) is finitely generated, and the
horizontal action is nonzero. The proof in 158 uses the actual
constant-in-\(u\) volume, cofactors, and a leading locally nilpotent
derivation; it does not require a polynomial presentation of \(A\).
We use this proved all-ring statement, not a bound on only four
generator images.

Introduce formal source variables and an independent indeterminate
\(\eta\):
\[
\begin{gathered}
\mathscr P=\mathbb C[\mathsf X_1,\mathsf X_2,\mathsf X_3,\mathsf X_4],
\qquad \operatorname{wt}\mathsf X_i=d_i,\\
\theta:\mathscr P\longrightarrow A[\eta],\qquad
\mathsf X_i\longmapsto c_i\eta^{d_i},\qquad I=\ker\theta,\\
r_{\mathrm{init}}=\operatorname{trdeg}_{\mathbb C}
\operatorname{im}\theta.
\end{gathered}
\tag{3}
\]
Here \(I\) is the full homogeneous prime of the algebra generated by
the four coordinate initials. It is not replaced by an arbitrarily
chosen relation or irreducible factor. That generated algebra is not
being identified with the full associated graded ring \(A[\eta]\).
The same retained theorem gives
\[
1\le r_{\mathrm{init}}\le3,
\qquad
r_{\mathrm{init}}\le2\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
k_i\le S-d_i-2\quad\hbox{for every }i.
\tag{4}
\]
In fact, the maximal value \(k_i=S-d_i-1\) is equivalent to algebraic
independence of the other three coordinate initials. Thus the
strengthening in (4) applies to every pivot at initial rank at most
two, without making \(I\) principal.

## 2. The full-kernel derivative order

Let \(0\ne P\in\mathscr P\) have positive weighted degree \(M\), and
write \(Q=P_M\) for its nonzero top weighted homogeneous part.
For a multi-index \(\alpha\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^4\), put
\[
|\alpha|=\sum_i\alpha_i,\qquad
\delta(\alpha)=\sum_i d_i\alpha_i,\qquad
\partial^\alpha=\prod_i\partial_{\mathsf X_i}^{\alpha_i},
\qquad T^\alpha=\prod_iT_i^{\alpha_i}.
\tag{5}
\]
The source and actual partials commute in their respective polynomial
rings. Define
\[
\ell_I(Q)=\min\{\,|\alpha|:\partial^\alpha Q\notin I\,\}.
\tag{6}
\]
This minimum always exists. Take a monomial of maximal ordinary total
degree in \(Q\) and differentiate by its full exponent vector. All
monomials of smaller total degree vanish, and a different monomial of
the same degree cannot survive unless its exponent vector dominates
the selected vector, which would make them equal. The result is a
nonzero complex constant. Such a constant does not lie in \(I\).
If \(Q\in I\), then \(\ell_I(Q)\ge1\).

For **every** \(\alpha\) with \(\partial^\alpha Q\notin I\), not
only those attaining (6), one has
\[
\boxed{\quad
\deg_E\bigl(T^\alpha P(X_1,X_2,X_3,X_4)\bigr)
=M-\delta(\alpha).
\quad}
\tag{7}
\]
Indeed, \(\partial^\alpha Q\) is weighted homogeneous of weight
\(M-\delta(\alpha)\), and its image under the full initial map is
nonzero. Its actual substitution therefore has exactly that E-degree.
Every lower weighted component of \(P\), differentiated by the same
multi-index, either vanishes or has strictly smaller weight. Actual
substitution cannot increase that weight. Hence it cannot cancel the
displayed nonzero leading term. In particular, the right side of (7)
is nonnegative and the final actual derivative is nonzero.

Write \(f=P(X_1,X_2,X_3,X_4)\). Iterating (2), all intermediate
derivatives in a chosen ordering being nonzero when the final one is
nonzero, yields
\[
\deg_E f\ge M-\sum_i(d_i+k_i)\alpha_i
\qquad(\partial^\alpha Q\notin I).
\tag{8}
\]
This is an inequality about actual coordinate derivatives of an actual
polynomial. No symbolic-power characterization of (6), formal
derivative on a quotient, or chosen-factor multiplicity is needed.

## 3. Consequence for an invariant polynomial

Take \(f\in A\setminus\mathbb C\), and let \(P\) be its unique source
polynomial in the actual coordinates. Positivity of every \(d_i\)
implies \(M>0\). Since \(\deg_E f=0\), its top \(Q\) belongs to \(I\).
Put \(\ell=\ell_I(Q)\ge1\), and choose any minimizing multi-index
\(\alpha\) in (6). Equations (2), (4), (7) and (8) give
\[
\boxed{\quad
M\le\sum_i(d_i+k_i)\alpha_i\le(S-1)\ell.
\quad}
\tag{9}
\]
If the generated initial algebra has transcendence degree at most two,
the more precise conclusion is
\[
\boxed{\quad
M\le\sum_i(d_i+k_i)\alpha_i\le(S-2)\ell
\qquad(r_{\mathrm{init}}\le2).
\quad}
\tag{10}
\]
The first bound is the exact-cost bound for the chosen derivative.
Different minimizing multi-indices may give different costs. One may
take the least cost among them, or use (8) for another escaping
derivative, but no such choice changes the proof or supplies a
coordinate-completion statement.

If \(I=(H)\) is principal, \(H\) is irreducible, and
\(Q=H^eG\) with \(H\nmid G\), then \(\ell_I(Q)=e\).
Fewer than \(e\) derivatives leave a factor \(H\) in every product-rule
term. For any variable on which \(H\) depends, characteristic zero and
irreducibility give \(H_{\mathsf X_j}\notin(H)\), and
\[
\partial_{\mathsf X_j}^{e}Q
\equiv e!\,G(H_{\mathsf X_j})^e\not\equiv0\pmod H.
\tag{11}
\]
Thus this definition includes the principal multiplicity mechanism.
The **strict generator-weight bound** in 158 additionally uses a
single generator and growth of three algebraically independent
invariants. It is not obtained merely by replacing \(e\) by (6), and
it is not asserted here for a selected generator in higher height.

## 4. A complete height-three polynomial control

Let
\[
B=\mathbb C[u,b,c,d],\qquad E=\partial_u,
\qquad A=\mathbb C[b,c,d],
\tag{12}
\]
and give it the following actual polynomial coordinates:
\[
X_1=b+u^2,\qquad
X_2=c+X_1^2,\qquad
X_3=d+X_2^2,\qquad
X_4=u+X_3^2.
\tag{13}
\]
Their polynomial inverse is
\[
u=X_4-X_3^2,\qquad
b=X_1-(X_4-X_3^2)^2,\qquad
c=X_2-X_1^2,\qquad
d=X_3-X_2^2.
\tag{14}
\]
Substituting (13) in (14) gives \(u,b,c,d\), and substituting (14)
in (13) returns all four \(X_i\). In particular these are coordinates
of a polynomial ring, not only generators of a fraction field.
Their exact positive degrees and initial map are
\[
(d_1,d_2,d_3,d_4)=(2,4,8,16),\qquad S=30,\qquad
\theta(\mathsf X_i)=\eta^{d_i}.
\tag{15}
\]
The full initial ideal is
\[
I=(F,G,H),\qquad
F=\mathsf X_2-\mathsf X_1^2,\quad
G=\mathsf X_3-\mathsf X_2^2,\quad
H=\mathsf X_4-\mathsf X_3^2.
\tag{16}
\]
These relations are weighted homogeneous. Their quotient is
\(\mathbb C[\mathsf X_1]\), whose map
\(\mathsf X_1\mapsto\eta^2\) is injective. This proves that (16)
is the full kernel, a height-three prime with initial rank one.

Differentiating the complete inverse (14) gives the actual partials:

| Partial | \(T_i(b)\) | \(T_i(c)\) | \(T_i(d)\) | \(T_i(u)\) | \(k_i\) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| \(T_1\) | \(1\) | \(-2X_1\) | \(0\) | \(0\) | \(2\) |
| \(T_2\) | \(0\) | \(1\) | \(-2X_2\) | \(0\) | \(4\) |
| \(T_3\) | \(4uX_3\) | \(0\) | \(1\) | \(-2X_3\) | \(9\) |
| \(T_4\) | \(-2u\) | \(0\) | \(0\) | \(1\) | \(1\) |

Since \(b,c,d\) generate \(A\), the derivation rule bounds all
horizontal images by the largest nonzero degree in the corresponding
row. Each listed bound is attained. The four \(u\)-images have
degrees \(-\infty,-\infty,8,0\), respectively, so the all-ring
shifts are exactly
\[
(k_1,k_2,k_3,k_4)=(2,4,9,1),\qquad
(d_i+k_i)_{i=1}^4=(4,8,17,17).
\tag{17}
\]
These values obey the stronger rank-one bound in (4).

Now use the nonconstant invariant \(f=cd\). Its unique source
polynomial is
\[
P=Q=FG,\qquad M=12.
\tag{18}
\]
Every first source derivative of \(Q\) lies in \((F,G)\subset I\),
whereas
\[
\alpha=(1,1,0,0),\qquad
\partial^\alpha Q=4\mathsf X_1\mathsf X_2,\qquad
\theta(\partial^\alpha Q)=4\eta^6\ne0.
\tag{19}
\]
Consequently \(\ell_I(Q)=2\), and the entire actual derivative is
\[
T_1T_2(cd)=4X_1X_2,\qquad
\deg_E T_1T_2(cd)=6=12-(2+4).
\tag{20}
\]
The exact-cost inequality is attained:
\[
M=12=(d_1+k_1)+(d_2+k_2).
\tag{21}
\]
There cannot be an escaping derivative with smaller exact cost for
this invariant, by (8). Thus optimizing the exact derivative cost
does not remove this equality.

This disproves a proposed universal **strict exact-cost inequality**
under the present all-positive hypotheses. It does not disprove a
strict version of either coarser bound involving \(S\): those bounds
are loose in this example. Nor does it disprove an extra assertion
specifically about globally degree-minimizing frames. The control
has the explicit alternative frame \((u,b,c,d)\).

It also exhibits why one selected factor is insufficient. Although
\(G\notin(F)\), the derivative
\(\partial_{\mathsf X_2}(FG)=G-2\mathsf X_2F\) still lies in the
full initial ideal. Its actual value is \(d-2X_2c\), of E-degree four,
not the putative value \(12-d_2=8\). The full-kernel condition in (7)
excludes exactly this invalid inference.

## 5. Scope of the repair

The result is (7)--(10), with the complete sharpness control above.
It neither bounds the four positive degrees nor constructs a
polynomial coordinate from a relation. In higher height there can be
multiple independent relation directions. Least derivative order
does not by itself imply that all invariant tops are scalar powers
of one polynomial, which was the growth obstruction in the principal
case.

A globally minimizing sorted degree tuple forbids an **actual**
coordinate change that lowers it. The existence of a polynomial with
lower E-degree, including the supplied slice, does not prove that it
can replace a member of that frame. Nothing in the derivative-order
inequality closes that coordinate-completion gap. The examples here
diagnose specific inferences; they do not disprove a theorem about
true minimizing frames or establish any arbitrary-entry theorem.

The only retained proof input is the all-positive actual shift and
initial-rank theorem158, used with its exact hypotheses. Its full
source was read through EOF for this note. No saved checker was
executed, imported, or compiled. The control was independently
checked by fresh inline polynomial differentiation; the complete
inverse and derivative calculations needed for the proof are printed
above. No earlier source or canonical record is changed.
