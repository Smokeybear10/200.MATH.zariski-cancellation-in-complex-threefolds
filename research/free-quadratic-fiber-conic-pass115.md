# Every fixed-point-free quadratic fiber has an actual local conic

In the supplied actual nonconical quadratic frame, a closed fiber with
no fixed point has either one translating line or two translating lines.
The three-line possibility is excluded. At its local invariant base the
first two derivative-image ideals agree, and the entire actual algebra
is generated by its degree-two module. A one-line fiber has a local
slice; a two-line fiber has a split reduced conic fiber.

This proves a local statement under the retained frame hypotheses. It
does not supply that frame for an arbitrary sliced action, patch these
presentations globally, or prove complex-threefold cancellation.

## 1. Actual inputs and the general conic criterion

Fix the closed base point and write R=S_m, with maximal ideal n and
fraction field K. Write A for the actual base localization A_R. The
retained inputs are
\[
\begin{gathered}
S=\mathbb C[s_1,s_2],\quad A\text{ a smooth complex domain},\quad
R\longrightarrow A\text{ faithfully flat},\quad\ker N=R,\quad A_K=K[v],\\
A=R[A_1,A_2,c],\quad A_i\in F_2,\quad c\in F_4,\quad
F_j=\ker N^{j+1}\simeq R^{j+1},\quad I_j=N^jF_j,\\
I_1=PR\ne0,\qquad
\operatorname{ord}_{\mathfrak p}I_j
 =\lceil j/2\rceil\operatorname{ord}_{\mathfrak p}P,
\qquad A_{\mathfrak p}=R_{\mathfrak p}[(F_2)_{\mathfrak p}]
\quad(\operatorname{ht}\mathfrak p=1),\\
\overline A=A/\mathfrak nA=\prod_{i=1}^k\mathbb C[t_i],
\qquad\overline N|_i=\delta_i\partial_{t_i},\quad
\delta_i\in\mathbb C^*,\qquad1\le k\le3.
\end{gathered}
\tag{1}
\]
The first line means that A is smooth over the complex numbers and that
R to A is faithfully flat; it does not say that the quotient morphism
is smooth at every other fiber. The degree modules come from102, and
the all-height-one statements from the actual degree-one slice and
quadratic constant-divisor models107. Their actual good-generator
noncollapse hypothesis is retained. The last line follows from the
whole-fiber results95--97: components are disjoint entire affine lines,
and a multiplicity-one component carries a nonzero translation.
A component of larger multiplicity would be fixed, so none occurs here.
The fiber is reduced because it is Cohen--Macaulay with no embedded
primes and has generic multiplicity one on every component.[^inputs]

Let W_j denote the image of F_j in the whole fiber. The following
criterion applies without a fixed-point-free hypothesis:
\[
\boxed{\quad
\dim_{\mathbb C}W_1=2\ \Longrightarrow\ I_2=I_1
\ \Longrightarrow\
A=R[U,r]\simeq R[\mathsf U,\mathsf r]/
(\mathsf r^2-2P\mathsf U+\eta),
\quad NU=r,\quad Nr=P.
\quad}
\tag{2}
\]
Here U and r are actual elements, and eta=2PU-r squared belongs to R.
For the first implication, extend 1 to a basis 1,r0 of F1. Its two
independent whole residues are independent in F2/nF2, so F1 is a
direct summand of the free rank-three F2. Thus I2=F2/F1 is principal.
Its height-one valuations equal those of the principal I1, proving
I2=I1 in the local UFD R.

For the second implication choose U with N squared U=P and put r=NU.
Then F1=R{1,r} and F2=R{1,r,U}. The monic conic in (2) injects into A:
its source is R-torsion-free and becomes K[r] generically. It is free
over R with basis U^j,rU^j for j at least zero. At every height-one
prime this actual subalgebra equals A by (1). An arbitrary element of A
has a finite, unique expansion over K in the same basis. Every scalar
coefficient lies in every R_p, hence in their intersection R. This
proves the entire algebra equality, not only generic equality.
This is the finite-coefficient intersection argument of111, now stated
without a special closed-fiber presentation.[^conic]

If P is a unit, a local slice gives A=R[r]. If P belongs to n, reduction
of (2) is C[U,r]/(r squared+eta_bar). In the fixed-point-free case it
must be reduced, so eta_bar is nonzero and this is two disjoint lines.
In particular (2) cannot give three translating components.

## 2. The derivative-square constraint on moving components

For any U in F2 put p_U=N squared U and eta_U=2p_U U-(NU) squared.
Both belong to the exact kernel R. Suppose k is at least two. If
p_U has nonzero closed value, U restricts to a quadratic polynomial
on every translating line. The actual all-component projection bound
would give 2k at most deg_N(U), which is at most two. Therefore
\[
\begin{gathered}
p_U\in\mathfrak n,\qquad
(\overline{NU})^2=-\overline{\eta_U}\in\mathbb C\mathbf1,\\
\overline{NU}\ne0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\overline{NU}|_i\in\{d,-d\}\text{ for every }i,
\quad d\in\mathbb C^*,\qquad
U|_i\text{ is an affine coordinate for every }i.
\end{gathered}
\tag{3}
\]
The projection bound applies to any actual function nonconstant on
every component, not only to original good combinations. Localized U
are allowed: clearing a base denominator outside m does not affect
these degree or residue claims. The algebra C[t_i] is reduced, so the
square equation has exactly the indicated constant solutions. These
are consequences of an invariant conic, not a lifted slice assumption.
The arbitrary-function projection inequality and its semilocal flatness
proof are recorded in [weighted97](quadratic-weighted-rank-defect-pass97.md),
Section 1, with the exact local construction in total-multiplicity92.

For k=3 a nonzero derivative in (3) would make U admissible with
three degree-one residues, again impossible for degree at most two.
Thus Nbar kills all of W2 in the three-component case.

Whenever Nbar kills W2, both actual good generators are constant on
each component. Actual algebra generation then makes the specified c
an affine coordinate on each line. The pairs of constant good values
are distinct: if two pairs agreed, every polynomial in A1,A2,c would
have identical restrictions on those two copies of C[c], contrary to
generation of their product. A generic constant good combination G
therefore separates all k components. Consequently
\[
\overline A=D[c],\quad D=\mathbb C^k,\quad
\mathbb C\{1,G\}\subseteq W_2\subseteq D\ (k\ge2),
\qquad\overline{Nc}\in D^*.
\tag{4}
\]
The last nonzero residue will allow contraction through the one-element
pure module R Nc. No equality of the k translation speeds is needed.

## 3. Three translating components are impossible

Suppose k=3. Equation (4) leaves dim W2 equal to two or three.
The algebraic exclusions107 and110 transfer to D=C cubed as follows;
their old mixed-fiber hypotheses are not simply dropped.

If dim W2=3, choose a basis 1,z,w of F2 whose residues form a basis of
D. The conic construction and the divisor valuations in (1) give
\[
\begin{gathered}
F_1=R\{1,r\},\quad r=az+bw,\quad Nr=P,\quad
Nz=\epsilon br+B_1,\quad Nw=-\epsilon ar+B_2,\quad\epsilon\in R^*,\\
J=(a,b)\text{ is proper maximal-primary},\qquad
Q=\epsilon r^2-2B_2z+2B_1w\in R,\qquad
B_1,B_2,Q\in J^2.
\end{gathered}
\tag{5}
\]
Indeed F2/F1 embeds in R under N squared, so the row (a,b) is
primitive. Initially the nonzero coefficient epsilon lies in R; its
valuations are zero at every height-one prime by (1), making it a unit.
Nbar kills the independent three residues. The resulting zero matrix
modulo n forces a squared and b squared into n, so J is proper. Finally
the entire residue-independent F2 is pure in flat A. Contracting its
conic through J squared gives the last three coefficients in J squared.

The regular-sequence divisions of full-Artin107, Sections 2--4, now
produce actual quartics U4,V4 and a cubic T with
N cubed T=3epsilon squared P squared. Its exact two-row determinant
gives F4=R{1,z,w,U4,V4}. The three defining relations for these
quartics have raw fiber S0[U4,V4], where S0 is spanned by 1,z,w and
maps onto their actual three-dimensional algebra D. Thus S0=D here
too. The proof that the three-relation presentation has no base
torsion uses its actual height-one presentations and expected
codimension, followed by Cohen--Macaulayness and absence of embedded
primes. Each of these inputs remains valid under (1) and (5).
It follows that the actual quartic algebra has fiber D[U4,V4] of
dimension two. But A=R[F4] by (1), whereas (4) has dimension one.
This is the contradiction. The detailed proof uses only dim D=3 and
the independent basis, not a nilpotent element of D.[^full]

If dim W2=2, choose G with three distinct constant values as in (4),
so W2=C{1,G} and 1,G,G squared have independent whole residues.
Write F2=R{1,G,H} with H in nA. Criterion (2) makes W1=C, and
I2 cannot equal I1, since either would give a conic with at most two
reduced components. The normalized conic therefore has the proper
primitive row and coefficients
\[
\begin{gathered}
r=aG+bH,\quad NG=\epsilon br+h,\quad NH=-\epsilon ar+B_2,
\quad P=ah+bB_2,\quad J=(a,b)\text{ proper maximal-primary},\\
h\in\mathfrak n,\quad H\in\mathfrak nA,\quad
Q=\epsilon r^2-2B_2G+2hH\in R,\\
a^2,B_2,Q\in\mathfrak n(h)+\mathfrak n(ab)+\mathfrak n^2(b^2),
\qquad h\ne0,
\quad\gcd(h,b)=1.
\end{gathered}
\tag{6}
\]
To obtain the sharp membership, rearrange the conic as
Q+2B2G-epsilon a squared G squared
=2hH+2epsilon abGH+epsilon b squared H squared.
Contract through the pure piece R{1,G,G squared}; H lies in nA.
Any common prime factor of h,b would then divide a, so h is nonzero
and coprime to b. This reconstructs precisely the needed sharp108
gate for the present reduced fiber.

Proper-image110, Sections 2--4, divides this actual conic using the
regular sequence (h,b), producing W in F4 with
Delta(1,G,G squared,GH,W)=-epsilon fourth b/(8P fourth).
The divisor determinants (1) imply bF4 is contained in the span of
those five elements. Its explicit derivative lies in
((h)+n(a)+n squared(b))A. Since c belongs to F4 and Nc has nonzero
residue by (4), one-element purity contracts this containment to
b in (h)+n(a). Modulo h, substituting b=av with v in the maximal
ideal into (6) forces h to divide a squared. Any common prime of
h,a would divide b, contrary to gcd(a,b)=1. This is impossible for
the nonzero nonunit h. The only use of the whole fiber in this
divided-quartic argument is the independent three-term piece and
nonzero residue of Nc, both established here.[^proper]

Both dimensions are impossible, excluding the three-line fiber.

## 4. A nonconstant quadratic on two components gives a conic

Suppose k=2 and some U in F2 has nonzero residue derivative.
Equation (3) makes U a coordinate on both lines, with derivative
values d or minus d, where d is nonzero. The projection bound gives
generic degree exactly two. In particular p=N squared U is nonzero
in R, even though it vanishes at the closed point: a generic
degree-one U would have a nonzero invariant derivative, whose
nonzero closed value would give a slice and a single component.

Let q1,q2 be the component primes, and put
T=(R[U])_(nR[U]) and O'=(A minus (q1 union q2))^(-1)A.
The actual local projection argument92 makes O' flat over T, with
generic rank two and residue dimension two. The equality-in-flat-rank
lemma93 therefore makes O' finite free of rank two. Its residue
algebra is C(U) times C(U). The entire base change A tensor_(R[U]) T
also equals O' by the integral-localization argument94, although
only finite freeness of O' is needed for the trace below.

With v=NU and eta=2pU-v squared, the generic degree-two field
extension K(v)/K(U) has minimal polynomial V squared-2pU+eta.
Thus multiplication by v on the finite free T-module O' has trace
zero. Trace commutes with reduction, giving
\[
\operatorname{Tr}_{K(v)/K(U)}v=0,
\qquad\overline v_1+\overline v_2=0,
\qquad(\overline v_1,\overline v_2)=(d,-d)
\text{ up to ordering},\qquad \dim W_1=2.
\tag{7}
\]
The generic trace is zero in T because T is a domain. On the
special product algebra it is the sum of the two scalar values.
This is an actual finite-module trace, not a formal-root or
same-sign exclusion inferred from a dimension count. Criterion (2)
now gives I2=I1 and the entire actual local conic.

## 5. The supported topology at one or two free components

Choose the product-complement curve Gamma of repaired96, enlarging
the finite exceptional set to include the base point. Its complete
supported lattice gives, with r_base normalized base branches and
q_src normalized source points above them,
\[
\begin{gathered}
k=q_{\rm src}-r_{\rm base}+1,\qquad
Tv_i=\mathbf1,\qquad v_i\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{q_{\rm src}},\\
\text{each cover degree }N_j\in\{1,2\},\qquad
T(e_{ja})=e_{ja}^{\rm ram}e_j,
\qquad\sum_a e_{ja}^{\rm ram}=N_j.
\end{gathered}
\tag{8}
\]
Here the coefficients on the right are the entire component
multiplicities, all one. A degree-two branch with a sole source
point would have ramification index two, making its row equation
2(v_i)_a=1 impossible. Consequently every degree-two branch has
two source points, both of index one. Degree-one branches have one
source point. Thus the number of degree-two normalized base branches
is exactly k-1. This uses the actual component lattice, not an
assumed connectedness theorem for free actions.[^support]

Every divisor of P has constant-field degree two by (1), and every
such divisor belongs to Gamma. For k=1 there is no such divisor
through m. Hence P is a unit and A has a slice, settling the
one-line case. If m lies in the product complement this conclusion
is already immediate; the argument also handles artificially
added degree-one exceptional branches.

For k=2 there is exactly one degree-two normalized base branch.
The factors of P therefore consist of a single irreducible base
prime p, and that curve has only one normalized branch at m.
Consequently
\[
P=u p^e\quad(u\in R^*,\ e\ge1),\qquad
\widehat R/(p)\text{ is a domain}.
\tag{9}
\]
For the last assertion, normalize the local curve R/(p). Its unique
branch makes the finite normalization a local DVR with residue C.
The original curve injects into that finite module, and completion
is exact for finite modules. Its completion therefore injects into
the DVR completion C[[t]], proving (9). This is the base-curve part
of the113 argument. The normalized constant cover here has two
points and splits after completion. No claim that p remains prime
in A tensor_R Rhat, or any scalar-completion descent, is made.

## 6. Two components cannot kill every quadratic derivative

It remains to suppose k=2 and Nbar kills W2. Equation (4) permits
a constant shift and rescaling of the actual G so that
\[
\overline A=D[c],\quad D=\mathbb C[G]/(G^2-G),\quad
W_2=\mathbb C\{1,G\},\quad \overline{Nc}\in D^*,\quad
F_2=R\{1,G,H\},\quad H\in\mathfrak nA.
\tag{10}
\]
Criterion (2) implies W1=C and I2 is not I1 in this case: its
conic conclusion would contain a degree-two reduced parameter,
contrary to Nbar killing W2. Normalize the same conic data as in
(6), with proper maximal-primary J=(a,b), epsilon a unit, and
h,B2 in n. The conic and purity of 1,G also give Q,B2 in n squared,
since r and H lie in nA. This preliminary order statement is
independent of the quadratic relation for G.

Now G squared-G, rather than G squared, lies in nA. The exact
conic extraction gives
\[
\begin{gathered}
\mathfrak K=\mathfrak n(h)+\mathfrak n(a^2)
                  +\mathfrak n(ab)+\mathfrak n^2(b^2),\\
\widetilde B_2=B_2-\varepsilon a^2/2,\qquad
Q,\widetilde B_2\in\mathfrak K,\\
\widetilde h=h+\varepsilon ab/2,\qquad
P=a\widetilde h+b\widetilde B_2,\qquad
b^2\in(\widetilde h)+aJ.
\end{gathered}
\tag{11}
\]
Indeed subtract epsilon a squared G from the conic and write
G squared=G+(G squared-G). The remaining four products have the
same ideal orders as in product112, so purity of 1,G proves the
first extraction. To check the final membership without assuming
a new determinant theorem, use the actual five elements
M_b=R{1,G,H,G squared,GH}. Their generic determinant is the same
epsilon fourth b squared/(16P fourth) as in112, independently of
their residues. Comparison with (1) and the adjugate give
b squared F4 contained in M_b. Its four nonconstant derivatives
belong to ((h)+aJ+n(b squared))A: B2 belongs to this ideal because
it differs from Btilde2 by epsilon a squared/2. Contract
b squared Nc through R Nc and absorb n(b squared). This gives
b squared in (h)+aJ, which equals (htilde)+aJ.

Changing h to htilde leaves K unchanged, since their difference
belongs to (ab). Also htilde belongs to n and cannot be zero:
otherwise b squared would belong to aJ, contradicting coprimality.
Writing b squared=s htilde+a squared u+abv and absorbing the
n squared(b squared) term in K gives
Btilde2=htilde eta+a(az+bt), with eta,z,t in n.

These are exactly the coefficients of universal-factorization114.
Pass to the faithfully flat completed base; the parameter ideal
J remains maximal-primary and (9) keeps P a power of one prime.
That theorem produces two nonzero nonunit factors of P whose joint
ideal contains J squared, contradicting its sole prime support.
No actual conic generator is obtained by descending its formal
auxiliary root. Instead the contrary actual alternative (10) has
been excluded. This settles the last two-line case.[^universal]

## 7. Conclusion and proof boundaries

Every free closed fiber therefore has I2=I1. Criterion (2) supplies
the actual generators and, by its union or unique generic expansion,
the exact alternating filtration
\[
\boxed{\begin{gathered}
A=R[U,r],\qquad r^2-2PU+\eta=0,\qquad
F_j=\bigoplus_{2i\le j}RU^i
       \ \oplus\!\bigoplus_{2i+1\le j}RrU^i,\\
k=1:\ P\in R^*,\ A=R[r];\qquad
k=2:\ P\in\mathfrak n,\ \eta\in R^*,\qquad
\overline A\simeq\mathbb C[U]\times\mathbb C[U].
\end{gathered}}
\tag{12}
\]
For the filtration, the monic-basis elements have pairwise distinct
generic degrees as listed in (12); their generic
leading coefficients are nonzero. Thus a finite R-linear expansion
lies in Fj exactly when it uses the listed terms. In the slice case
one may choose U=r squared/(2P), eta=0, and the alternating list is
just a unit rescaling of the ordinary powers of r.

The main new moving-fiber gates are the derivative-square restriction,
the finite trace that rules out equal derivative signs, and the
idempotent correction (11). The actual quartic exclusions are reused
only after reconstructing their required algebraic inputs. The source
topology uses repaired96 and the full supported lattice97, with
degree-one exceptional components allowed throughout.

These conclusions can be combined with the fixed-fiber result114, but
this note does not perform global module gluing or polynomial
recognition. It asserts neither that every free Ga action has a slice
nor that an arbitrary sliced E admits the supplied original frame.
No new primary source or numerical method was used. Saved dependencies
were read in full where their proof interfaces are used; peer outline
checks and final saved-file reviews are recorded separately. No file
through114 was changed.
