# Quadratic graph denominators and special-fiber relations

The generic bad-pair theorem gives an explicit affine-modification
presentation with a bounded denominator. All coefficients of its
quadratic graph have denominators dividing the square of the actual
bad-projection determinant. Away from that determinant the original
slice quotient is already a polynomial line over the invariant plane.

At a genuine coefficient-pole divisor, the actual bad coordinates
must satisfy a nonconstant relation of degree at most two in the
generic divisor fiber. If one reduced speed is nonzero, the entire
relation ideal is generated by an irreducible polynomial in the
reduced transverse invariant, again of degree at most two. This
does not show that such relations are absent.

## 1. Original setup

Use exactly the hypotheses and notation of
[the generic bad-pair proof](generic-bad-pair-pass44.md).
Thus \(C\simeq\mathbb C^{[2]}\) is the already known common invariant
ring, \(L=\operatorname{Frac}C\), and
\[
B\otimes_C L=L[x_3,x_4],\qquad
x_i=\mathcal Q_i(x_3,x_4)\quad(i=1,2),
\tag{1}
\]
where each \(\mathcal Q_i\) has bad-coordinate degree at most two
and coefficients in \(L\). The nonzero polynomial
\(\Delta\in C\) gives the global commuting LNDs
\[
K_3=-\Delta\partial_{x_4},\qquad
K_4=\Delta\partial_{x_3}
\tag{2}
\]
on the generic ring, and these are polynomial fields on the
original \(B\). Also
\[
E=f\partial_{x_3}+g\partial_{x_4},\qquad
f,g\in C,\qquad E(s_*)=1
\tag{3}
\]
for the original polynomial slice.

## 2. Every graph coefficient has denominator dividing \(\Delta^2\)

Apply each ordered pair of the fields in (2) to \(x_i\).
They kill \(\Delta\), so these values are, with the indicated
possible signs, precisely
\[
\Delta^2
\frac{\partial^2\mathcal Q_i}{\partial x_j\partial x_k},
\qquad j,k\in\{3,4\}.
\tag{4}
\]
Every value is in \(B\). The second derivatives are constants
with respect to the bad variables, so (4) also lies in \(L\).
The equality \(B\cap L=C\) therefore puts all of (4) in \(C\).
Since the base contains \(\mathbb Q\), every quadratic coefficient
of \(\Delta^2\mathcal Q_i\) belongs to \(C\).

Next \(\Delta K_j(x_i)\in B\) is, up to sign, a first bad
derivative of \(\Delta^2\mathcal Q_i\). Subtract the already
known linear polynomial coming from its quadratic part.
The remainder is both in \(B\) and in \(L\), so belongs to \(C\).
This proves the same statement for the linear coefficients.

Finally subtract the quadratic and linear parts from
\(\Delta^2x_i\). The constant remainder again belongs to
\(B\cap L=C\). Hence
\[
P_i:=\Delta^2\mathcal Q_i\in C[x_3,x_4],
\qquad \deg_{x_3,x_4}P_i\le2,\quad i=1,2.
\tag{5}
\]
This is a fixed denominator bound, not just the existence of
some unspecified common denominator.

Let \(R_0=C[x_3,x_4]\). Original generation now gives the exact
subring presentation
\[
\boxed{B=R_0[P_1/\Delta^2,P_2/\Delta^2].}
\tag{6}
\]
Equivalently it is the affine modification
\(R_0[I/\Delta^2]\) with
\(I=(\Delta^2,P_1,P_2)\).
No radical is taken. A presentation by equations is
\[
B\simeq
R_0[Y_1,Y_2]\Big/
\bigl((\Delta^2Y_1-P_1,\Delta^2Y_2-P_2):\Delta^\infty\bigr).
\tag{7}
\]
The saturation is justified by the actual embedding in
\(L[x_3,x_4]\) and the isomorphism after inverting \(\Delta\).
It is not omitted or assumed unnecessary. Nor is \(I\) asserted
to equal the possibly larger contracted ideal
\(\Delta^2B\cap R_0\).

In particular
\[
B_\Delta=C_\Delta[x_3,x_4].
\tag{8}
\]

## 3. The original slice gives the localized quotient exactly

On (8), all values of \(E\) belong to the ideal
\((f,g)C_\Delta[x_3,x_4]\). Since \(E(s_*)=1\), contraction
to the coefficient ring gives
\((f,g)C_\Delta=C_\Delta\).
Choose \(\alpha,\beta\in C_\Delta\) with
\(\alpha f+\beta g=1\), and set
\[
\sigma=\alpha x_3+\beta x_4,\qquad
j=gx_3-fx_4.
\tag{9}
\]
These are actual coordinates over \(C_\Delta\), with inverse
\[
x_3=f\sigma+\beta j,\qquad
x_4=g\sigma-\alpha j.
\tag{10}
\]
Here \(E\sigma=1\) and \(Ej=0\), so
\[
\boxed{A_\Delta=C_\Delta[j].}
\tag{11}
\]
A compatible second localized direction is explicit:
\[
W=\alpha K_3+\beta K_4,\qquad
W\sigma=0,\quad Wj=\Delta,\qquad [E,W]=0.
\tag{12}
\]
The coefficients \(\alpha,\beta\) need not lie in \(C\).
Equation (12) is a localized coordinate statement, not a global
slice for a primitive determinant field.

## 4. A genuine pole forces a low-degree special-fiber relation

Let \(\pi\) be an irreducible element of \(C\), and suppose some
coefficient of some \(\mathcal Q_i\) has a pole at \(\pi\).
By (5), \(\pi\mid\Delta\). Work over the discrete valuation ring
\(C_{(\pi)}\), with residue field
\(\kappa=\operatorname{Frac}(C/\pi C)\).

Let \(n\ge1\) be the smallest integer for which
\[
R=\pi^n\mathcal Q_i
\in C_{(\pi)}[x_3,x_4].
\]
At least one coefficient of \(R\) is a unit of \(C_{(\pi)}\).
Since \(R=\pi^n x_i\) in the localized original ring, reduction
gives a nonzero formal polynomial
\[
0\ne\overline R\in\kappa[X_3,X_4],\qquad
\deg\overline R\le2,\qquad
\overline R(\overline x_3,\overline x_4)=0
\quad\hbox{in }B\otimes_C\kappa.
\tag{13}
\]
Faithful flatness makes this fiber nonzero; a nonzero constant
cannot vanish in it. Therefore (13) is a genuinely nonconstant
relation, of degree one or two.

The generic divisor fiber is a domain in this setting.
Indeed \(C\) is factorially closed in the UFD \(B\), being a
common kernel of LNDs. An irreducible \(\pi\) of \(C\) remains
irreducible, hence prime, in \(B\): a factorization in \(B\)
would have both factors in \(C\). Localizing the prime quotient
at the nonzero elements of \(C/\pi C\) preserves a domain.
This establishes the domain premise used next.

## 5. Nonzero reduced speed determines the entire relation ideal

Suppose at least one of \(\overline f,\overline g\) is nonzero
in \(\kappa\). The derivation on the bad-coordinate polynomial
ring is the nonzero translation
\[
\overline E_0=\overline f\partial_{X_3}
+\overline g\partial_{X_4}.
\]
Its map to the actual divisor fiber has prime kernel
\[
J=\ker\bigl(\kappa[X_3,X_4]\longrightarrow
B\otimes_C\kappa\bigr).
\tag{14}
\]
At a genuine pole, \(J\ne0\) by (13).
Its height cannot be two: the quotient would be a finitely
generated algebraic domain over \(\kappa\), hence a finite
algebraic field extension, on which every \(\kappa\)-derivation
vanishes in characteristic zero. But the descended derivation
has a nonzero constant value on one bad coordinate.
Thus \(J\) has height one.

The UFD \(\kappa[X_3,X_4]\) gives \(J=(R_0)\) for an
irreducible nonconstant \(R_0\).
It divides (13), so \(\deg R_0\le2\).
The ideal is \(\overline E_0\)-stable because \(E\) acts on
the actual fiber. Consequently \(R_0\) divides
\(\overline E_0(R_0)\).
The LND divisibility lemma forces that derivative to be zero.
Writing
\(\overline j=\overline gX_3-\overline fX_4\), one obtains
\[
\boxed{J=(r(\overline j)),\qquad
r\in\kappa[T]\text{ irreducible},\quad 1\le\deg r\le2.}
\tag{15}
\]
Over \(\kappa\), a linear coordinate complementary to
\(\overline j\) then identifies the bad-image ring with
\((\kappa[T]/(r))[z]\).

This statement concerns the actual bad-image subring.
It does not identify the whole divisor fiber or its full
invariant field with that subring. In particular, it does
not bound every residue-cover degree of an intrinsic action
without an additional generation proof.
If both reduced speeds vanish, the height-one and univariate
conclusion are not asserted.

## 6. An exact sufficient denominator-removal condition

If, for every prime divisor \(\pi\) of \(\Delta\), the six
images
\[
1,\ x_3,\ x_4,\ x_3^2,\ x_3x_4,\ x_4^2
\tag{16}
\]
are linearly independent over
\(\operatorname{Frac}(C/\pi C)\) in the actual generic divisor
fiber, then (13) excludes every coefficient pole.
Since \(C\) is a UFD and (5) already excludes poles away from
\(\Delta\), all graph coefficients belong to \(C\).
It follows that
\[
B=C[x_3,x_4].
\]
The argument of Section 3 now works globally:
\((f,g)C=C\) and \(A=C[j]\simeq\mathbb C^{[3]}\).

Condition (16) is sufficient, not automatic. The primitive
determinant pair, faithful flatness and the generic graph do
not by themselves establish it. The remaining task is to
exclude or handle the actual linear and quadratic relations
at the possible pole divisors, retaining their multiplicities
and the full modification ideal in (6)--(7).

## Verification and scope

All steps above are exact consequences of the already audited
generic graph, the polynomial determinant actions, original
generation, original slice and inherited faithful-flat affine-plane
quotient. The affine-modification presentation is proved directly
as an equality of subrings; no external lifting theorem is invoked.

This adds no numerical test methods. The coefficient extraction,
prime-ideal argument and divisor quantifiers are written proofs,
not conclusions inferred from a finite list of examples.
The original full-profile and unrestricted cancellation gaps
remain. No novelty, external expert review or formal proof
claim is made.
