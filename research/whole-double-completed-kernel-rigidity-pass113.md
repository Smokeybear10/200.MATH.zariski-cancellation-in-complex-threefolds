# Completed invariant rigidity in the proper whole-double alternative

The actual completed invariant kernel equals the completed base, including
when the first normal action is zero. Evaluation on a transverse surface
makes a larger kernel a finite quadratic extension. Such an extension would
supply formal invariant normal coordinates and force a common nonunit factor
in every derivative value. Actual primitivity and flat completion exclude
that factor.

Together with the separate ramified scalar contraction113, this proves
descent of every finite-degree module to the completed base. It does not
construct a hidden quadratic parameter, exclude the proper whole-double
alternative, or recognize the unknown threefold.

## 1. Actual input and the complete chart

Retain the actual proper-image whole-double setting of
[product-index112](whole-double-product-index-pass112.md) and the entire
uniform chart of [conormal112](whole-double-global-conormal-chart-pass112.md).
Write
\[
\begin{gathered}
R=S_{\mathfrak m},\qquad S=\ker N=\mathbb C[s_1,s_2],\qquad
\widehat R=\mathbb C[[x,f]],\qquad F_j=\ker N^{j+1},\\
A'=A\otimes_R\widehat R,\qquad
\mathcal A=\varprojlim A/\mathfrak m^nA
          =\mathbb C[c][[x,G]],\\
A/\mathfrak mA=\mathbb C[c,G]/(G^2),\qquad
\bar N=\lambda G\partial_c,\quad\lambda\in\mathbb C^*,\\
f=A(c)x+BG^2+O_{\rm wt}(3),\qquad
\operatorname{wt}x=2,\quad\operatorname{wt}G=1,
\quad\operatorname{wt}\mathbb C[c]=0,\\
N_* =\gamma x\partial_G+\lambda G\partial_c,\qquad
\gamma\in\mathbb C,\quad B\in\mathbb C^*.
\end{gathered}
\tag{1}
\]
Here x and f are actual invariant base parameters. The scalar gamma may
vanish. The complete coefficient ring is C[c], not C[[c-c0]]. The two
normal-adic and weighted topologies are cofinal. The formal derivation is
continuous, kills x and f, and raises weight by at least one.

The ring mathcal A is a regular Noetherian domain. This also follows from
its displayed formal power-series presentation over the regular ring C[c].
Its ideal (x,G) lies in its Jacobson radical; at a maximal point of the
c-line the local completion is C[[x,G,c-c0]], a regular local ring.
In particular mathcal A is integrally closed. None of this makes N locally
nilpotent on every formal element.

The actual image ideal I_N=(N(a):a in A) has no height-one prime divisor.
This is the primitive canonical-field assertion retained in
[fixed-scheme92, Sections 1--3](nonconical-fixed-scheme-structure-pass92.md),
not a claim deduced from the formal chart. The regular factorial actual
ring A is still being used before recognition.

## 2. Evaluation is injective on completed invariants

Fix c0 in C. Ordinary coefficient evaluation gives
\[
\operatorname{ev}_{c_0}:\mathcal A\longrightarrow
B_0:=\mathbb C[[x,G]],\qquad
f\longmapsto f_0:=f(x,G,c_0).
\tag{2}
\]
It is a homomorphism of Rhat-algebras for the indicated map into B0.
Put K=ker(N on mathcal A). Suppose z in K has zero evaluation. Since Nx=0,
the formal derivation is NG partial_G+Nc partial_c in the chart (1).
Evaluating Nz=0 first gives
(Nc)|c0 times (partial_c z)|c0=0: the G derivative of the identically
zero evaluated series vanishes. The series (Nc)|c0 is nonzero, with
leading term lambda G. Since B0 is a domain, (partial_c z)|c0=0.

Now differentiate Nz=0 repeatedly with respect to c. If the first j
c-derivatives of z evaluate to zero, every term in the j-th differentiated
equation vanishes on evaluation except (Nc)|c0 times the (j+1)-st
derivative of z. That derivative also evaluates to zero. Induction gives
all c-Taylor derivatives zero. Each coefficient of x^i G^j in z is a
polynomial in c; in characteristic zero it is zero if all its derivatives
at c0 vanish. Hence z=0, proving that evaluation is injective on K.

This argument does not interchange an R-linear kernel with completion.
It uses the actual nonzero nilpotent first derivative Nc on the whole
double. It also does not require Nc to be a unit.

## 3. The transverse ring has rank two over the completed base

Modulo x, the series f0 is G squared times a unit. Therefore
B0/(x,f0) has C-basis 1,G. The (x,f0)-adic and (x,G)-adic topologies
are equivalent. Lifting this basis degree by degree and summing in the
complete topologies shows that B0 is generated by 1,G over Rhat.
The map from Rhat is injective: the leading weighted term of a nonzero
series in x,f0 is its first homogeneous polynomial in x,A(c0)x+BG squared,
which is nonzero.

The same weighted comparison gives linear independence of 1,G over Rhat.
A nonzero alpha(x,f0) has even leading weight. A nonzero G beta(x,f0)
has odd leading weight. They cannot cancel. Thus
\[
\boxed{\qquad B_0=\widehat R\oplus\widehat R G,\qquad
\operatorname{rank}_{\widehat R} B_0=2.\qquad}
\tag{3}
\]
No Weierstrass polynomial is needed for this particular freeness proof.
It is the formal length-two transverse section expressed using the actual
uniform invariant x and the actual nilradical generator G.

By (2), K embeds into B0 as an Rhat-submodule. Since Rhat is Noetherian
and B0 is finite, K is finite over Rhat. It is a domain containing Rhat,
so its rank is one or two. It is also normal: if an element of Frac(K)
is integral over K, its monic equation makes it integral over mathcal A.
Normality of mathcal A puts it in mathcal A, and membership in Frac(K)
makes its derivative zero. Hence it belongs to K.

If the rank is one, normality of Rhat implies K=Rhat. If the rank is two,
the evaluated image of K and B0 have the same fraction field. Every
element of B0 is integral over Rhat, hence over that image of K. Normality
of K then gives
\[
\operatorname{rank}_{\widehat R}K=2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\operatorname{ev}_{c_0}(K)=B_0.
\tag{4}
\]
This is a finite integral-extension argument, not a dimension count alone.

## 4. Rank two would make the formal field divisible

Assume rank two and choose Z in K with ev_c0(Z)=G by (4). The reduced
residue of Z is a polynomial z0(c). Its whole-fiber derivative is
lambda G z0'(c), so z0 is constant. Evaluation at c0 makes that constant
zero. Therefore Z belongs to (x,G)mathcal A, and its weight-one term is
G beta(c), with beta(c0)=1.

Since NZ=0, the weight-two part is
gamma beta(c)x+lambda beta'(c)G squared. The two coefficients vanish.
Consequently beta is the constant 1 and gamma is zero. In particular
\[
\begin{gathered}
Z=G+O_{\rm wt}(2),\qquad
\mathcal A=\mathbb C[c][[x,Z]],\qquad Nx=NZ=0,\\
\boxed{\quad N=n\partial_c,\qquad n=Nc\ne0,\qquad
n\in\sqrt{\mathfrak m\mathcal A}.\quad}
\end{gathered}
\tag{5}
\]
To justify the coordinate statement, the possible weight-two linear term
in Z is alpha(c)x. Thus x,Z have an invertible triangular conormal change
from x,G over the entire C[c]. Degree-by-degree formal inversion gives
the displayed complete coordinate ring. Continuity then determines N
from its values on x,Z,c.

The element n is nonzero because its whole residue is lambda G. It is a
nonunit because its reduced residue is zero on the whole c-line. Equation
(5) says that every formal derivative value has this same nonunit factor.

## 5. Primitivity rules out the common formal factor

Choose 0 not equal to u in the actual ideal I_N. The finitely many
height-one prime factors of u do not contain I_N. Prime avoidance gives
v in I_N outside all of them. Since A is factorial, u,v form an actual
regular sequence: multiplication by v on A/(u) is injective. The two
elements need not individually be single N-values; membership in the
image ideal is what is required.

The completion A to mathcal A is flat, so this regularity survives.[^flat]
The quotient remains nonzero: I_N vanishes on the reduced fixed line and
its completed ideal is proper. But (5) writes u=n u1 and v=n v1 in
mathcal A. The class of u1 modulo u is nonzero, because u1=u w would imply
1=n w in the domain. On the other hand v u1=u v1 is zero modulo u.
This contradicts the surviving regular sequence. Rank two in (4) is
therefore impossible, and
\[
\boxed{\qquad
\ker(N:\mathcal A\to\mathcal A)=\widehat R
\quad\text{for both values }\gamma=0\text{ and }\gamma\ne0.
\qquad}
\tag{6}
\]
The argument excludes a formal height-one fixed divisor using an actual
codimension-two image ideal. It does not infer primitivity of a formal
derivation merely from its nonzero leading term.

## 6. Finite-degree descent and the exact remaining obstacle

The independent [ramified completion113, Sections 2--4](whole-double-ramified-completion-descent-pass113.md)
proves mathcal A intersect A'[1/P]=A' using the single ramified divisor
support of P. That statement already holds without a gamma assumption.
Combine it with (6). The integration argument of its Section 6 now works
in every normal-action case: for a formal finite-degree element z, first
descend Nz by induction, integrate it in Rhat[1/P][r] where Nr=P, and
use (6) to see that the difference is in Rhat[1/P]. Scalar contraction
then puts z in A'. Flat base change of the actual degree kernels gives
\[
\boxed{\begin{gathered}
\ker(N^{j+1}:\mathcal A\to\mathcal A)
       =F_j\otimes_R\widehat R\quad(j\ge0),\qquad
\mathcal A_{\rm ln}=A',\\
N^3U=0\text{ in }\mathcal A
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\bar U\in\mathbb C\{1,G\}\subseteq\mathbb C[c,G]/(G^2).
\end{gathered}}
\tag{7}
\]
The last line uses the actual proper alternative W2=C{1,G}. Thus even a
formal hidden quadratic component parameter is forbidden in that
alternative. No such parameter has been constructed here, so this is not
an exclusion of the alternative itself. An element of A' also need not
have coefficients in uncompleted R. The general cancellation target and
the arbitrary-frame entry problem remain open.

## Scope and verification

This note strengthens only the gamma restriction in Sections 5--6 of the
separate ramified-descent113 proof. Its scalar-contraction argument remains
an independent input to (7); it is not needed for the kernel equality (6).
No claim that p stays prime in mathcal A is inserted. Nor is completion
of the non-A-linear operator N asserted exact by general module theory.

Root and Geometry developed the finite transverse-kernel and grade
arguments; General independently reconstructed the outline. This file
contains the full argument so that the saved proof, rather than only its
outline, can be audited. No numerical method is introduced. No existing113
or earlier proof or checker is changed.
