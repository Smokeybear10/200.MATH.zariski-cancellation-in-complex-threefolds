## 2. Full literal rows and the zero-dot-intercept branch

## 3. The exact R basis and the first complete scalar gates

(6) on F. Let \(J=J_{R,\omega,\xi}\). Then
\[
J=-\frac{a_0^4t(t+M')}{\rho\dot R}\in L^*,
\tag{17}
\]
Here \(t(t+M')=(\partial_\omega|_t)s\) is the original
parameter Jacobian, not the fixed-R derivative of s. The scalar
may depend on R. Only coefficients of positive X or xi
powers are required to vanish.

The \(\xi^3\) coefficient first integrates to

\[
\begin{pmatrix}

\tag{18}

The coefficients of \(X\xi^2\) and \(X^2\xi\), in that

V=-2\gamma/u\in F^*,\qquad G=VR+G_0,\qquad H=H_0.
\tag{20}

The \(\xi^2\) coefficient of (18) then gives the complete

\tag{21}

This is obtained by subtracting the displayed primitive in R,

## 4. The case \(u'\ne0\)

The first gate in (22) says \(uu''=3u'^2\). Hence
\((u'/u^3)'=0\), and there is \(a\in\mathbb C^*\) with
\(u'=u^3/a\). After that gate the second entry of (22) is
\(4u'V'/u^3\); thus \(V\in\mathbb C^*\). The third entry
then forces \(M'=0\). Equation (10) also gives \(\phi'=0\).
Therefore M and phi are complex constants.

Because \(u'\ne0\), \((R,u)\) is a separating basis and
the same F is finite over \(\mathbb C(u)\). Use a subscript u
for its new coefficient derivative. Exact integration gives
\[
\lambda=\lambda_0+\frac a{2u},\qquad
\omega=\omega_0-\frac a{2u^2},\qquad
A_1=A_0+\frac{aV}{2u},
\quad \lambda_0,\omega_0,A_0\in\mathbb C.
\tag{23}
\]
For example \(\omega_u=a/u^3\),
\(\lambda_u=-a/(2u^2)\), and
\(A_{1,u}=-aV/(2u^2)\); subtracting the displayed expressions
leaves joint constants. This does not assert \(F=\mathbb C(u)\).

In these parameters (20),(21) become
\[
b=B-2R/u,\qquad
c=-\frac{2u^2B_u}{a}R-\frac{2R^2}{a}+C_0,
\qquad G=VR+G_0,\quad H=H_0.
\tag{24}
\]
Let \(\widetilde J=J_{R,u,\xi}=(a/u^3)J\in L^*\).
Reconstructing its full determinant directly from (12),(23),(24)
gives
\[
[R\xi]\widetilde J=\frac{4u}{a}(uB)_{uu}.
\tag{25}
\]
It follows by exact subtraction that
\(B=B_0-B_1/u\), with \(B_0,B_1\in\mathbb C\).
The entire remaining determinant is
\[
\begin{aligned}
\widetilde J={}&\frac{4G_{0,u}}a RX
 +\frac{4\lambda_0G_{0,u}}a R+VH_{0,u}X^2\\
&+\left(-2H_{0,u}+\frac{2G_{0,u}}u\right)X\xi\\
&+\left(-A_0H_{0,u}+\frac{2B_1}{a}G_{0,u}
       +MG_{0,u}+V\lambda_0H_{0,u}+VC_{0,u}\right)X\\
&+\left(2(\lambda_0/u+\omega_0)G_{0,u}-2C_{0,u}\right)\xi\\
&-A_0C_{0,u}+B_0G_{0,u}

\end{aligned}
\tag{26}
\]
The RX, \(X\xi\), and xi coefficients, in that order, force
\(G_{0,u}=H_{0,u}=C_{0,u}=0\). Equation (26) then vanishes
entirely, including its constant term. No constant or R-only
coefficient was independently required to vanish. This contradicts
\(\widetilde J\in L^*\).

## 5. The case \(u'=0\)

Now \(u\in\mathbb C^*\). The third entry of (22) forces
\(\gamma'=0\), so \(\gamma\in\mathbb C^*\). Equations
(7),(10) integrate by exact subtraction to
\[
\lambda=\lambda_0-u\omega/2,\quad
A_1=uM+\gamma\omega+A_0,\quad
\phi=\gamma M+\phi_0,
\qquad \lambda_0,A_0,\phi_0\in\mathbb C.
\tag{27}
\]
Here M is still arbitrary in F. The \(R\xi\) coefficient of
the full determinant (18) is
\[
\frac4u\{B''-(\lambda+u\omega)M''-uM'\}.
\tag{28}
\]
Since \((\lambda+u\omega)'=u/2\), its vanishing says
\((B-(\lambda_0+u\omega/2)M)''=0\). Thus
\[
\begin{aligned}
B&=(\lambda_0+u\omega/2)M+B_1\omega+B_0,
                         &&B_0,B_1\in\mathbb C,\\
b&=B-2R/u,& c&=-(M+2B_1/u)R+C_0,\\
G&=-2\gamma R/u+G_0,& H&=H_0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{29}
\]
All of \(G_0,H_0,C_0,M\) remain in the exact F at this point.

The complete determinant after (27),(29) has only monomials
\(X^2,X\xi,X,\xi,1\). Its first two coefficients are
\[
\begin{aligned}
[X^2]J&=\frac{2\gamma}{u}
                       \{(B_1+uM)M'-H_0'\},\\
[X\xi]J&=
 \frac{(-uA_0+2uB_1-2\gamma\lambda_0+u^2M)M'
                                -2uH_0'+2G_0'}{u}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{30}
\]
Their vanishing gives
\[
H_0'=(B_1+uM)M',\qquad
G_0'=(uA_0/2+\gamma\lambda_0+u^2M/2)M'.
\tag{31}
\]
After only these two substitutions the WHOLE determinant factors:
\[
\boxed{\quad
J=\mathcal F\left\{\xi+\frac\gamma uX
                  +\frac{A_0u+2\gamma\lambda_0+u^2M}{2u}\right\},
\qquad
\mathcal F=(uB_0+2B_1\lambda_0+2\lambda_0uM)M'-2C_0'.
\quad}
\tag{32}
\]
The xi coefficient forces \(\mathcal F=0\). The entire
determinant is therefore zero, contrary to (17). Formula (32)
retains \(M'=0\) and every zero value of
\(\lambda_0,A_0,B_0,B_1\). There is no division by any of them.
Again the R-only or constant coefficient was not set to zero by
calibration; its cancellation is part of the full identity.
