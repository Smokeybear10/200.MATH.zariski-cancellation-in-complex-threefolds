# Delta zero forces a rational coefficient field and a finite polynomial normalization

Working consequence of the complete actual degree-one Delta-zero
family401. The strong complex-field return forces L=F(g), with F
the exact constant field for its t derivative. The ring obtained by
adjoining F to the original four-ring has a finite birational
normalization F[X,g,R]. The original global slice then shows that
the normalization of F[A0] is a polynomial two-ring over F.

This is not an equality F[A0]=F[g,V]. Adjoining a coefficient
subfield to a normal ring does not automatically preserve normality.
No original-ring recognition or exclusion is claimed.

## 1. Actual input and notation

Retain every actual264 hypothesis and the complete Delta-zero
necessary family in
[401](principal-paraboloid-equal-degree-one-delta-zero-necessary-family-pass401-working.md).
Thus B=C[X,Y,Z,W]=A0[u], E=partial_u, a0=EX is nonzero, and
N=[u^4]partial_Z restricted to A0 is the selected nonzero derivation.
The full initial kernel, exact time profile, both actual returns and
calibrated scalar remain imposed. In particular,
\[
L=\operatorname{Frac}\ker_{A_0}N,\qquad
K=\operatorname{Frac}A_0=L(P)=\mathbb C(y,z,w),\qquad
O=L[P],\quad P=\sigma,
\tag{1}
\]
with k=s, n_ss nonzero, deg sigma=deg theta=1 and lambda=n_s.

Use401's exact coefficient basis t,xi, where xi=n_s is nonzero.
Dots and primes are respectively the commuting t and xi partials
on L. Put F=ker_L(dot). Then xi belongs to F, t is transcendental
over F, and the coefficient data satisfy
\[
\begin{gathered}
A,B_0,\Lambda\in F,\qquad B_0''=(A'')^2/8,\qquad
g\in L^*,\quad\dot g\ne0,\\
s=-2\xi t^2-A't-B_0',\quad
\mu=-4\xi t-A',\quad a=n_t=2\xi^2t+A,\\
n=-\xi^2t^2+(A-\xi A')t+B_0-\xi B_0',\\
\ell=2\xi^3t^2+\xi^2A't+\Lambda,\quad
r=g/2-\xi^2t-A/2,\\
h=-g(t+A''/4)+4\xi^2t^2+2\xi A't
                  +\Lambda'-\xi^2(A'')^2/8,\\
y=\xi P+r,\qquad\theta=\mu P+h.
\end{gathered}
\tag{2}
\]
The name B0 here is the coefficient called B in401, not the
original four-ring B. No element of F is presumed rational in xi.
All these field elements are E-invariant because they lie in K.

The original nonzero leader a0 lies in L. Define
\[
p=P-\xi t-A'/2,\qquad R=p+tX.
\tag{3}
\]
The first change is affine over L on the intrinsic line; the second
is a parameter change over L[X]. Neither is asserted to be an
original coordinate transformation.

## 2. The strong return eliminates an algebraic coefficient extension

Set the following elements of F:
\[
\begin{aligned}
C_1&=(\xi A'-A)/2,\\
C_2&=B_0-\xi B_0'+\Lambda'
                  -\xi^2(A'')^2/8-(A')^2/4,\\
C_3&=\Lambda-\xi\Lambda'+\xi^3(A'')^2/8.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]
Substitution of (2),(3) into the complete intercept formulas
z=2ty+P^2+theta+n and w=ell-2Py-aP-xi*theta gives
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
y&=\xi p+g/2+C_1,\\
z&=p^2-A''g/4+C_2,\\
w&=-2\xi p^2-(g+\xi A')p+C_3
                          +g(\xi A''/4-A'/2).
\end{aligned}}
\tag{5}
\]
All explicit t dependence cancels. In particular these three
elements belong to F[g,p]. Since F(g) is a subfield of L, the
strong return in (1) gives inclusions in both directions:
\[
L(p)=\mathbb C(y,z,w)\ \subseteq\ F(g,p)\ \subseteq\ L(p).
\tag{6}
\]
Hence equality holds. Intersect with L to obtain
\[
\boxed{L=F(g).}
\tag{7}
\]
Here is the field-intersection justification. If an element l of L
is f(p)/h(p) with f,h in F(g)[p] and h nonzero, then p is
transcendental over L. Comparing coefficients in l*h(p)=f(p)
shows l belongs to F(g). Thus F(g,p) intersect L equals F(g).
This uses the actual complex-field return, not just O=L[P].

Also g is transcendental over F: if it were algebraic, differentiating
its separable minimal polynomial over F would give dot g=0. This
contradicts (2). Therefore (7) is a rational one-variable extension,
and t is a nonconstant rational function of g over F. It does not
assert L=F(t) or F=C(xi).

## 3. All actual coordinates lie in a polynomial three-ring over F

Let
\[
B_F=F[\mathcal B],\qquad A_F=F[A_0],\qquad
T=F[X,g,R].
\tag{8}
\]
These are subrings of the original fraction field, not newly chosen
ambient coordinate rings. The notation F[B] means the subring
generated by the given field F and ring B.

The three elements X,g,R are algebraically independent over F.
Indeed g is transcendental over F; P, and hence p, is transcendental
over L=F(g). Finally X is transcendental over K, since E X=a0
is nonzero and X=a0*u+b0 for some b0 in A0. Replacing p by
R=p+tX preserves the function field and this independence.

Using (2),(3), the FULL literal coordinate formulas become
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
Y={}&(\xi+X)R+g/2+(A'/2)(\xi+X)-A/2,\\
Z={}&R^2-A''g/4+C_2-B_0'X,\\
W={}&-2\xi R^2-[g+A'(\xi+X)]R+\Lambda+(X-\xi)\Lambda'\\
&+\xi^2(\xi-X)(A'')^2/8-(A')^2X/2-B_0'X^2\\
&+g[(\xi-X)A''/4-A'/2].
\end{aligned}}
\tag{9}
\]
In particular B_F is a subring of T, despite the possible poles
of t as a rational function of g. The moving parameter R is
essential to this cancellation. Merely (5) would not prove (9).

The first equation of (9) gives
\[
g=2Y-2(\xi+X)R-A'(\xi+X)+A.
\tag{10}
\]
Substituting into the second produces a monic equation for R:
\[
\begin{aligned}
0={}&R^2+\frac{A''}{2}(\xi+X)R+C_2-B_0'X
       -\frac{A''}{2}Y\\
&+\frac{A''A'}4(\xi+X)-\frac{A''A}4-Z.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]
Its coefficients belong to B_F, even when A''=0. Equation (10)
then gives
\[
\boxed{T=B_F[R]\text{ is finite over }B_F,
\quad T=B_F\cdot1+B_F\cdot R.}
\tag{12}
\]
The displayed module generators need not be linearly independent.

Their fraction fields coincide. In fact F is already a subfield
of K, so Frac B_F=Frac B=K(u)=K(X). By (7),(3), this is
F(g,p,X)=F(g,R,X)=Frac T. Thus (12) is birational. Since T is a
polynomial ring over a field, it is integrally closed. Every element
of the common fraction field integral over B_F is also integral
over T and hence lies in T. Conversely every element of T is
integral over B_F by (12). Therefore
\[
\boxed{T\text{ is the integral closure of }B_F.}
\tag{13}
\]
This argument has not asserted that B_F is normal.

## 4. The original global slice forces a coprime polynomial pair

The coefficient field F is E-invariant, as are g,t,p. Equation (3)
therefore gives, on the common fraction field,
\[
E=a_0\partial_X+(a_0t)\partial_R,
\tag{14}
\]
where these partials fix g and F. At this point its two coefficients
are known only to be in L=F(g). They are in fact polynomials in g.

First EX=a0 belongs to B_F, hence to T. The intersection
F(g) intersect F[X,g,R] is F[g], by coefficient comparison. Thus
\[
d_0:=a_0\in F[g]\setminus\{0\}.
\tag{15}
\]
Next differentiate the first literal formula of (9) by E:
\[
EY=d_0(R+A'/2)+(a_0t)(\xi+X).
\tag{16}
\]
Its left side belongs to B_F and hence T. The coefficient of X in
this identity is a0*t, so
\[
d_1:=a_0t\in F[g],\qquad t=d_1/d_0.
\tag{17}
\]
No normality extension theorem for derivations is needed: (15)--(17)
show directly that E restricts to the polynomial triangular derivation
d0*partial_X+d1*partial_R on T.

The ORIGINAL slice u belongs to B_F, hence to T, and Eu=1. Thus
\[
1=d_0\partial_Xu+d_1\partial_Ru\in(d_0,d_1)T.
\tag{18}
\]
Evaluate the two polynomial derivatives at X=R=0 to see that
1 belongs to the ideal (d0,d1) of F[g]. Consequently
\[
\boxed{\gcd(d_0,d_1)=1\text{ in }F[g].}
\tag{19}
\]
This is not an assertion that d0 alone is a unit or that t is
polynomial. Nonconstant denominators are still allowed in (17).

## 5. Finite polynomial normalization of the invariant base extension

Choose q0,q1 in F[g] with q0*d0+q1*d1=1 and define
\[
U=q_0X+q_1R,\qquad V=d_0R-d_1X=a_0p.
\tag{20}
\]
This is an invertible linear change over F[g]: its determinant is
one, and its inverse is X=d0*U-q1*V, R=d1*U+q0*V. Hence
\[
T=F[g,U,V],\qquad EU=1,\quad EV=0,\quad E=\partial_U.
\tag{21}
\]
In characteristic zero, its kernel is exactly F[g,V]. Since u also
has E derivative one, u-U belongs to that kernel. Therefore
\[
\boxed{T=F[g,V,u],\qquad\ker_T E=F[g,V].}
\tag{22}
\]

On the original subring, B_F=A_F[u] because B=A0[u] and F is a
subfield of K. It is finite in T by (12). Taking the quotient by
the same slice u in both rings gives a finite extension
\[
A_F\ \subseteq\ F[g,V].
\tag{23}
\]
The injection is justified by (22): an element of A_F is constant
in u, so cannot belong to u*T unless it is zero. Both fraction
fields are K=F(g,p)=F(g,V), since a0=d0 is nonzero. The larger
ring in (23) is polynomial and integrally closed. As in Section3,
it follows that
\[
\boxed{F[g,V]\text{ is the finite integral closure of }F[A_0]
\text{ in }K.}
\tag{24}
\]
The original A0 is not identified with this polynomial ring, nor is
the generally larger ring F[A0] proved equal to its integral closure.

## 6. Scope, proof gap and evidence

This consequence uses the strong complex-field return and original
global slice, beyond the coefficient equations of401. It replaces
an unspecified finite extension L/C(t,xi) by L=F(g), and gives a
finite explicit normalization after adjoining F. It also restricts
t to a ratio of two coprime polynomials arising from the actual E.
All conclusions remain conditional on the full actual entry and
the Delta-zero necessary family. The Delta-nonzero equal-degree
case, higher equal degrees and arbitrary-chart entry remain open.

One must not infer that F[A0] is normal just because A0 is normal:
F is a subfield of its fraction field being adjoined, not a declared
localization by elements of A0. No such localization description
has been proved here. Even a future proof of equality in (24) would
still require descent from F to recognize the original complex ring.
This note makes no exclusion or unrestricted cancellation claim.
