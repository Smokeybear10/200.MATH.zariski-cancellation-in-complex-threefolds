# Exclusion of the constant-midpoint quadratic-affine branch

Working exclusion: under the complete actual hypotheses and normalized
transverse frame of539, the branch
\(\nu_0=0,\ C_0\ne0\) is impossible. The proof uses the entire scalar
at actual X=0. After returning to an intrinsic parameter in the
ORIGINAL coefficient field, its quadratic coefficient is nonzero in
both cases \(h_s=0\) and \(h_s\ne0\).

No square root adjoined in539 is promoted to the original fraction
field. The lower potentials remain arbitrary subject to their actual
identities, and no lower scalar coefficient is replaced by a constant.
The other branch of539 is not addressed here. No wider recognition,
general chart-entry, cancellation, or novelty claim is made.

## 3. Integration of the two h_s cases

First suppose \(h_s=0\). Then \(h_t=0\) makes \(h\in\mathbb C^*\),
and \(m-hs\) has both partials zero. Thus
\[
m=hs+\mu,\quad \mu\in\mathbb C,\qquad
d_s=0,\qquad
r=-\lambda_0/2-hd^2/4,\qquad \mathscr C=\alpha\ne0.
\tag{9}
\]
The last nonzero statement follows from the branch assumption.
Moreover \(r_t=-hdd_t/2\ne0\) by (5).

Now suppose \(h_s\ne0\), meaning nonzero as an element of the exact
field, not nonvanishing at every geometric place. Set
\[
\mu=m-\frac{3h^2}{h_s},\qquad M=m-\mu=\frac{3h^2}{h_s}.
\tag{10}
\]
All these operations take place in L. Direct differentiation using
(6) gives
\[
\mu_s=-5h+\frac{3h^2h_{ss}}{h_s^2}=0,\qquad \mu_t=0.
\]
Thus \(\mu\in\mathbb C\) and \(M\ne0\). The element
\[
\chi=h/M^3
\tag{11}
\]
has t derivative zero and logarithmic s derivative
\(h_s/h-3h/M=0\). Hence \(\chi\in\mathbb C^*\) and
\(h=\chi M^3\).

It is convenient temporarily to write \(\delta=dM\in L'\).
Equation (5) gives \(\delta_s=0\), because
\(h_s/(3h)=h/M\). Also \(\delta_t=Md_t\ne0\).
Crucially only its square is needed below:
\[
\delta^2=d^2M^2\in L^*,\qquad
r=-\lambda_0/2+\frac{\mu\chi\delta^2}{4},\qquad
\mathscr C=\alpha+\frac{\chi\delta^2}{2}.
\tag{12}
\]
Indeed \(R_1=\mu\chi M^2/4\), proving (12) directly from (8).

If \(\mu=0\), then r is complex. This contradicts the strong
complex-field return in (1): \(\mathbb C(r,z_{\rm int},w)\) would
have transcendence degree at most two, whereas \(L(v)\) has
transcendence degree three. Therefore
\[
\mu\ne0,\qquad r_t=\mu\chi\delta\delta_t/2\ne0
\quad\text{in the case }h_s\ne0.
\tag{13}
\]
This also handles the possible zero of R1 in this case: it would
make \(\mu=0\) and is excluded by the stated field return, not by an
unlicensed division. The constant \(\alpha\) may be zero in (12).
No conclusion that \(\delta\in L\) has been used.

## 4. An original-field parameter and the exact scalar factor

Return now to the original field and define
\[
\xi=\theta-e=D(v-v_0)\in L[v],\qquad
a=1/d^2=A_\sigma/D^2\in L^*.
\tag{14}
\]
Then \(L[\xi]=L[v]\), \(N\xi=D\in L^*\), and the ENTIRE intrinsic
rows are
\[
\sigma=a\xi^2+c,\qquad\theta=\xi+e,\qquad y=r.
\tag{15}
\]
This uses an affine intrinsic reparametrization over L, not a square
root or an actual ambient-coordinate change.

In both cases of Section 3, \(r_t\ne0\). Since \(m_t=0\) and
\(m_s=h\ne0\), the coefficient pair \((r,m)\) is separating:
\[
dr\wedge dm=r_th\,dt\wedge ds\ne0.
\tag{16}
\]
Thus L is a finite separable extension of \(\mathbb C(r,m)\): the
pair is a transcendence basis of this finitely generated field.
We do not assert \(L=\mathbb C(r,m)\). Extend its two partials
uniquely to L, and then fix xi. In the rest of the proof a subscript
m denotes this NEW partial with r fixed, not the original s partial.

The moving terms in xi cause no missing Jacobian factor. The first
two rows of \(\partial(r,m,\xi)/\partial(t,s,v)\) have zero v entry,
while \(\xi_v=D\). Therefore
\[
\det\frac{\partial(r,m,\xi)}{\partial(t,s,v)}=r_thD\ne0,
\qquad
J_{r,m,\xi}(X)=\frac{-a_0^4}{r_thD}\in L^*.
\tag{17}
\]
The calibrated scalar is independent of xi and X. The right side
may depend on r and m; it is not being set to a complex constant.
Neither r nor m is asserted to be an actual ambient coordinate.

## 6. Both cases contradict the quadratic scalar coefficient

If \(h_s=0\), equation (9) expresses
\[
a=-\frac{h}{4(r+\lambda_0/2)},\qquad
\gamma=-\alpha a.
\tag{21}
\]
The denominator is nonzero by (9) and \(d\ne0\).
Both quantities in (21) are functions of r with complex coefficients,
so their m partials in Section 4 vanish. Equation (20) therefore
has left side \(2\gamma\ne0\), a contradiction.

If \(h_s\ne0\), equations (12),(13) instead give
\[
\delta^2=\frac{4(r+\lambda_0/2)}{\mu\chi},\qquad
a=\frac{M^2}{\delta^2},\qquad
\gamma=-M^2\left(\frac{\alpha}{\delta^2}+\frac\chi2\right),
\qquad M=m-\mu.
\tag{22}
\]
These identities are all in L. With r fixed, delta squared and the
parenthetical factor have m derivative zero, whereas \(M_m=1\).
Consequently
\[
\gamma_m=2\gamma/M,\qquad
2\gamma-m\gamma_m=-\frac{2\mu\gamma}{M}\ne0.
\tag{23}
\]
Here \(\mu\ne0\) was proved using the strong return, \(M\ne0\) was
proved in (10), and \(\gamma\ne0\) is the branch condition in (18).
No division by \(\alpha\), c, e, G, or w0 occurs. This contradicts
(20) and completes the exclusion of \(\nu_0=0,\ C_0\ne0\).
