# The quadratic conic's fixed surface and coefficient restrictions

The global conic now has an exact fixed-surface recognition criterion
and stronger restrictions on its actual original-coordinate
coefficients. Neither result proves that the fixed surface is a plane.
An initially promising zero-column coordinate argument was checked
against the earlier generic-generation theorem and found to concern
a case already excluded in the remaining quadratic branch. It is
retained as a valid conditional, not counted as a newly solved sector.

The full working question remains the unconfirmed intended
complex-threefold version of Luo's email: does
\(A[u]\simeq\mathbb C^{[4]}\) force \(A\simeq\mathbb C^{[3]}\)?
The general cancellation problem is not solved. The supplied special
frame, the relative cubic branch, and entry into a suitable frame
for arbitrary sliced actions remain distinct obligations.

## 1. What the fixed-surface criterion actually establishes

Under the complete supplied quadratic-frame hypotheses,
[theorem115](quadratic-global-conic-frontier-pass115.md) gives

\[
\begin{gathered}
S=\mathbb C[s,t],\qquad
A=S[U,r]/(r^2-2PU+\eta),\qquad 0\ne P\in S,\quad \eta\in S,\\
NU=r,\quad Nr=P,\quad E=\partial_u,\quad B=A[u]\simeq\mathbb C^{[4]}.
\end{gathered}
\tag{1}
\]

The [fixed-surface proof116](quadratic-fixed-surface-recognition-pass116.md)
first shows gcd(P,eta)=1 using the actual prime-divisor property
and minimal first derivative image PS. This is not comaximality:
P and eta may still have common closed zeros. The polynomial
F=2PU-eta is irreducible in S[U]. Hence r is prime and
D=Spec(S[U]/(F)) is a smooth irreducible affine surface.

The sign involution fixes S[U,u] and sends r to -r. Its invariant
ring is polynomial, and its fixed scheme is D times the u-line.
The following conditions are equivalent in this particular conic:

\[
\begin{aligned}
D\simeq\mathbb A^2
&\ \Longleftrightarrow\ F\text{ is a coordinate of }S[U]\\
&\ \Longleftrightarrow\ F\text{ is a coordinate of }S[U,u]\\
&\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \sigma\text{ is polynomially linearizable on }B\\
&\ \Longleftrightarrow\ r\text{ is a coordinate of }B.
\end{aligned}
\qquad
\text{Each implies }A=\mathbb C[r]^{[2]}.
\tag{2}
\]

Two published inputs are kept explicit. If r is a coordinate,
D[u] is affine three-space and plane cancellation identifies D.
If D is a plane, the Sathaye--Russell theorem applies because F
is linear in U, and makes F a coordinate before adjoining u.
Substituting F=r squared then recognizes A. This is not an appeal
to the general affine-plane embedding conjecture. The precise
primary statements and read scope are in the linked proof and
[source audit116](quadratic-involution-recognition-source-gate-pass116.md).

The source audit also checks why a double cover alone does not
finish the argument. Jelonek's unrestricted degree-two result
gives the deck involution, already explicit here. Its unconditional
global standardization requires a coordinate branch hyperplane;
its general local result is holomorphic; its global polynomial
statement assumes involution linearization. Smoothness of D does
not meet the coordinate-hyperplane hypothesis.

## 2. Actual polynomial generation forces more than a nonzero odd row

Write the actual four original coordinates as

\[
\begin{gathered}
x_{\rm tr}=bu+dU+er+f,\qquad b,d,e,f\in S^3,\\
z=\frac q2u^2+k_4U^2+k_3rU+k_2U+k_1r+k_0,
\qquad q\in\mathbb C^*,\quad k_i\in S.
\end{gathered}
\tag{3}
\]

These are genuine polynomial generators of B, not a proposed
tuple with a nonzero Jacobian. The
[odd-row proof116](quadratic-involution-odd-row-pass116.md)
uses their differences under sigma to obtain
(e1,e2,e3,k3 U+k1)=S[U,u]. For I=(e1,e2,e3), the exact condition
is (I,k1)=S and k3 in the radical of I. It need not give k3 in I.

Replacing sigma by its conjugate under the actual flow
exp(alpha r E) gives, for every alpha in S, the unit row
(e-alpha b,k3 U+k1-q alpha v), where v=u+alpha r. Thus a good
odd column cannot be a nonzero scalar multiple of b at any closed
base point. The proof includes points where the involution has no
fixed point; a tangent-space argument on the branch would not
cover those points.

The [intrinsic-shift proof116](quadratic-intrinsic-shift-odd-row-pass116.md)
adds the independent actual flow exp(aN). Its new conic parameters
are U'=U+a r+a squared P/2 and r'=r+aP, with polynomial inverse.
After this shift and the slice translation, the exact row is

\[
\begin{gathered}
(e-a d-\alpha b,\ (k_3-2a k_4)U'+h_a-q\alpha v)
                         S[U',v]=S[U',v],\\
h_a=k_1-a k_2+\frac32a^2P k_3-a^3P k_4,
\qquad a,\alpha\in S.
\end{gathered}
\tag{4}
\]

Reduction modulo the ideal generated by the first three entries
forces alpha and k3-2a k4 into its radical, and h_a to be a unit
modulo that ideal. These are scheme-sensitive restrictions, not
just generic rank calculations.

At a closed point, if e belongs to the span of b,d and b,d are
independent, it must be a multiple a d, with no b component. Then
k3-2a k4 vanishes and h_a is nonzero there. The rows alone do not
exclude d being a nonzero multiple of b while e is independent of
b. That possible pattern must not be discarded by a faulty linear
algebra inference.

## 3. Whole fibers and the actual exceptional locus

There is a stronger exclusion when P vanishes at the base point.
If d=delta b with delta nonzero, original generation on the whole
fiber first recovers w=u+delta U and r from the good coordinates.
On each reduced component, the remaining z-polynomial would then
have to generate C[w][U] over C[w]. Its linear U-coefficient contains
-q delta w, so it cannot be a unit; a nonzero quadratic U-term is
also impossible for a polynomial generator. This excludes the
pattern on both the double-line and two-line fibers. The complete
argument in the intrinsic-shift proof checks generation before
passing to the reduction.

Let Delta=det[b,d,e] and Omega=det[b,b_s,b_t]. The retained
generation A localized at Omega by its two relative good
generators also implies that a closed point with P=0 and Omega
nonzero has Delta nonzero. A dependent pair would generate either
a polynomial line or a finite-dimensional ring, neither of which
is the whole double-line or disconnected two-line fiber.
Equivalently Omega belongs to the radical of (P,Delta). This is
a restriction on determinant degeneration, not a claim that Delta
is a unit or that all exceptional fibers disappear.

## 4. Correction to the initial zero-column interpretation

If e=0, the involution fixes three actual original coordinates and
is triangular in the fourth. Its difference recovers r with an
explicit triangular inverse, and (2) would identify A. Likewise
an intrinsic shift would handle e in S d. These implications are
algebraically valid.

But the exact relative-degree-two branch already has Delta nonzero
generically. The complete generic-generation proof90 gives
K[y1,y2]=K[r]. If d,e are dependent with d nonzero, both generators
lie in K[V] for a quadratic V in r, a proper subring. If d=0,
the relative plane relation is linear, a case recognized in91.
Together with the volume identity L dot d=L dot e=0 and
L dot b=Omega nonzero, this forces Delta nonzero.

Thus e=0 and e in S d are already excluded in the exact remaining
quadratic branch. They are not newly solved nonempty sectors.
This scope correction replaces the initial interpretation stated
during the investigation. The genuinely useful current results are
the equivalent fixed-surface criteria and the stronger closed-point
coefficient restrictions, including the whole-fiber exclusion.

A separate proposed quadratic velocity shear was also checked
against the ledger. Its sufficient condition is already recorded
in [shear81](rank-one-acceleration-shear-boundary-pass81.md), so it
is not counted as a new result or a new general reduction.
