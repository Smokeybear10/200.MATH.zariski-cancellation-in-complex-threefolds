# A primitive nonsliced Euler-induced derivation in an actual translation frame

## 1. Statement and scope

There is a polynomial coordinate frame on the translation ring
\[
 B=\mathbf C[a,b,c,u],\qquad E=\partial_u,\qquad
 A=\ker_B E=\mathbf C[a,b,c],
\tag{1}
\]
whose four coordinate \(E\)-degrees are positive and whose weighted
Euler derivation has positive exact \(E\)-filtration shift. The
induced derivation on \(A\) is
\[
 N=a\partial_b+b\partial_c.
\tag{2}
\]
It is already primitive: its polynomial components have greatest
common divisor one. Nevertheless it has a common-zero line and
has no slice. Thus removing a common polynomial factor from an
Euler-induced derivation does not, by itself, guarantee a slice.

This is a complete actual polynomial frame, not a prescribed
leading vector field or an embedding of one orbit. Its invariant
ring is the known polynomial ring in (1), and the displayed frame
has explicit lower-degree replacements. No assertion about
globally minimizing frames, coordinate-entry termination, or
cancellation follows from this control. No novelty claim is made.

The positive-shift mechanism in source576 supplies the context;
the frame, exact shift, and induced derivation are computed
directly below, so this control does not require applying a
classification theorem.

## 2. The full polynomial coordinate frame and inverse

Define
\[
 \begin{split}
 X&=u,\\
 Y&=a+u^2,\\
 Z&=b+au+u^4,\\
 W&=c+bu+\frac12au^2+u^6.
 \end{split}
\tag{3}
\]
The inverse can be read successively as polynomial expressions:
\[
 \begin{split}
 u&=X,\\
 a&=Y-X^2,\\
 b&=Z-(Y-X^2)X-X^4,\\
 c&=W-\bigl[Z-(Y-X^2)X-X^4\bigr]X
           -\frac12(Y-X^2)X^2-X^6.
 \end{split}
\tag{4}
\]
For completeness the last two expressions expand to
\[
 b=Z-XY+X^3-X^4,\qquad
 c=W-XZ+\frac12X^2Y-\frac12X^4+X^5-X^6.
\tag{5}
\]
Substituting (3) in (4) first recovers \(u\), then \(a\), then
\(b\), and finally \(c\). Conversely, substituting (4) in (3)
recovers \(X,Y,Z,W\) in that order. These are identities on the
entire affine fourfold, without any localization. In the ordered
old variables \((a,b,c,u)\), the Jacobian of (3) is \(-1\).
In particular
\[
 B=\mathbf C[X,Y,Z,W].
\tag{6}
\]

The whole polynomials in (3) have exact \(u\)-degrees
\[
 (d_X,d_Y,d_Z,d_W)=(1,2,4,6),
\tag{7}
\]
all with leading coefficient one. These are degrees for the
given translation derivation \(E\); they are not total degrees
in \(a,b,c,u\).

## 3. Entire transported Euler derivation and exact shift

Use the weighted Euler derivation determined by (7):
\[
 D=X\partial_X+2Y\partial_Y+4Z\partial_Z+6W\partial_W.
\tag{8}
\]
Differentiating the complete inverse (4), or equivalently solving
the four Euler eigen-equations in (3), gives
\[
 D(u)=u,\qquad D(a)=2a,\qquad
 D(b)=4b+au,\qquad D(c)=6c+bu.
\tag{9}
\]
For example, after the first three equations have been obtained,
the full equation \(DW=6W\) reads
\[
 \begin{split}
 6W
 &=D(c)+(4b+au)u+bu
       +\frac12(2a)u^2+au^2+6u^6\\
 &=D(c)+5bu+3au^2+6u^6.
 \end{split}
\tag{10}
\]
Comparison with \(6W=6c+6bu+3au^2+6u^6\) gives the last
equation of (9). In particular the apparent degree-two terms
cancel in the whole computation; they have not been discarded
as lower-order data.

On the entire old polynomial ring, (9) is the identity
\[
 D=D_0+uN,\qquad
 D_0=u\partial_u+2a\partial_a+4b\partial_b+6c\partial_c,
 \qquad N=a\partial_b+b\partial_c.
\tag{11}
\]
For every \(f\in A[u]\), \(D_0f\) has \(u\)-degree at most
\(\deg_u f\), while \(uNf\) has degree at most
\(\deg_u f+1\). Thus the \(E\)-filtration shift of \(D\) is at
most one on the whole ring. It is exactly one because
\(b\in A\) has degree zero and \(D(b)=4b+au\) has degree one.
Hence
\[
 \delta=\deg_E D=1,\qquad
 \operatorname{gr}_E B=A[\eta],\qquad
 \overline D=\eta N,\qquad \overline D(\eta)=0.
\tag{12}
\]
This uses the full associated graded ring, not only the algebra
generated by the four coordinate initials. No change of the
given slice or extension of the coefficient field is involved.

## 4. Primitivity, local nilpotence, and failure of a slice

The derivation in (2) satisfies
\[
 N(a)=0,\qquad N(b)=a,\qquad N(c)=b.
\tag{13}
\]
It is nonzero and locally nilpotent. For instance, give \(b,c\)
weights one and two, respectively, and \(a\) weight zero. Every
nonzero image under \(N\) has strictly smaller weight, so every
polynomial is killed by a sufficiently high power of \(N\).

In the unique factorization domain \(A=\mathbf C[a,b,c]\), the
nonzero components \(a,b\) have greatest common divisor one.
Consequently, if \(N=gN_1\) for a polynomial derivation \(N_1\)
of \(A\) and \(g\in A\), then \(g\) divides both \(a\) and
\(b\), and is a nonzero constant. This also rules out any
nonconstant invariant factor that could be removed in a
primitive normalization.

For every \(f\in A\), the entire image is
\[
 N(f)=a\frac{\partial f}{\partial b}
             +b\frac{\partial f}{\partial c}\in(a,b).
\tag{14}
\]
The ideal \((a,b)\) is proper, so \(N(f)=1\) is impossible.
Thus \(N\) has no slice. Its common-zero locus is exactly the
line \(a=b=0\), with \(c\) arbitrary. Primitivity rules out
a fixed divisor, but does not rule out this codimension-two
zero locus. A nonzero constant rescaling of
\(N\) retains both the zero locus and the failure of a slice.

This is a stronger control than the raw
\(72a\partial_c\) example in source576, whose common factor
can be divided out to obtain a sliced derivation. Here the
obstruction remains after every polynomial common-factor
normalization.

## 5. Known polynomial kernel and nonminimal-frame boundary

The original ambient derivation remains \(E=\partial_u\) with
the global slice \(u=X\). Its kernel is already
\(\mathbf C[a,b,c]\), and the inverse (4) gives the invariant
ambient coordinates explicitly. Thus the failure of a slice
for \(N\) says nothing against polynomiality of \(\ker E\).

The frame (3) is not degree-minimal. The old coordinates have
degrees \((0,0,0,1)\). Even among all-positive frames, the actual
polynomial coordinates
\[
 (u,a+u,b+u,c+u)
\tag{15}
\]
have degree tuple \((1,1,1,1)\). Their inverse subtracts the
first coordinate from each of the last three. Composing that
inverse or forward map with (3)--(4) gives actual polynomial
coordinate changes, not rational replacements. In particular
the displayed control cannot justify a claim about frames
minimizing a degree sum or sorted degree tuple.

The exact conclusion of this control is that a positive-shift
weighted Euler derivation of an actual sliced polynomial ring
can induce a primitive nonsliced LND on its invariant ring.
The following realization statement is restricted to already
homogeneous LNDs on a known polynomial ring. Neither part is
a termination argument or a new entry criterion.

## 6. Realizing a homogeneous LND on a known polynomial ring

Let \(A=\mathbf C[a_1,\ldots,a_s]\) be a given polynomial ring,
and suppose a nonzero LND \(N\) is homogeneous for positive
integer weights \(v_i\). More precisely, for
\(D_v=\sum_i v_i a_i\partial_{a_i}\), suppose
\[
 [D_v,N]=e_0N,\qquad e_0\in\mathbf Z.
\tag{16}
\]
Here \([P,Q]=PQ-QP\). Homogeneity is a hypothesis; it is not
asserted that every LND has such a positive-weight frame.

Let \(\nu_i=\deg_N a_i\), with \(\nu_i=0\) when \(N(a_i)=0\).
Choose an integer \(K\ge2\) sufficiently large that
\[
 w_i=Kv_i>\nu_i\quad\hbox{for every }i,
 \qquad e=Ke_0.
\tag{17}
\]
In particular \(1+e\ne0\): the integer \(Ke_0\) cannot equal
\(-1\) for \(K\ge2\). Extend \(N\) to \(A[u]\) by \(N(u)=0\)
and put
\[
 D_0=\sum_i w_i a_i\partial_{a_i}+u\partial_u,
 \qquad H=\frac{uN}{1+e},\qquad \phi=\exp(-H).
\tag{18}
\]
Since \(N(u)=0\), the derivation \(H\) is locally nilpotent.
Thus both \(\phi\) and \(\phi^{-1}=\exp(H)\) are polynomial
automorphisms of the whole ring, fixing \(u\). Their finite
exponential formulas are valid over \(\mathbf C[u]\).

The commutators are
\[
 [D_0,H]=uN,\qquad [H,uN]=0.
\tag{19}
\]
Conjugating therefore gives the exact identity
\[
 D:=\phi D_0\phi^{-1}=D_0+uN.
\tag{20}
\]
For example, this follows by differentiating
\(\exp(-zH)D_0\exp(zH)\) in the auxiliary scalar \(z\):
its derivative is the constant operator \(uN\), because the
second commutator in (19) vanishes. The calculation is finite
on each polynomial, so it does not require convergence or a
formal coordinate substitution.

Define actual coordinates
\[
 X_i=\phi(a_i)+u^{w_i}\quad(1\le i\le s),
 \qquad X_{s+1}=u.
\tag{21}
\]
This is the polynomial automorphism \(\phi\) followed by
elementary shears fixing \(u\). An explicit entire inverse is
obtained by setting \(U=X_{s+1}\), \(Y_i=X_i-U^{w_i}\), and
then
\[
 a_i=\left(\exp\!\left(\frac{U N}{1+e}\right)a_i\right)
                \bigg|_{a_j=Y_j},\qquad u=U.
\tag{22}
\]
The exponential in (22) is first computed as a polynomial in
the old \(a_j\) with parameter \(U\), and those variables are
then replaced by the displayed \(Y_j\). This specifies a
polynomial expression without assuming the inverse coordinates
in advance. Both inverse orders follow from the inverse
exponentials and the inverse shears.

The polynomial \(\phi(a_i)\) has \(u\)-degree \(\nu_i\), so
(17) makes the exact \(E=\partial_u\) degree of \(X_i\)
equal to \(w_i\), with leader one. The last coordinate has
degree one. Since \(D\phi(a_i)=w_i\phi(a_i)\) and \(Du=u\),
\[
 D(X_i)=w_iX_i,\qquad D(X_{s+1})=X_{s+1}.
\tag{23}
\]
Consequently (20) is precisely the weighted Euler derivation
of this actual frame for its actual \(E\)-degrees. It has
exact filtration shift one: (20) gives the all-ring upper
bound, and \(N(a_i)\ne0\) for some \(i\) gives equality on
that \(a_i\). Its induced derivation is \(\eta N\).

The specific control (3) is recovered from weights
\((v_a,v_b,v_c)=(1,2,3)\), \(e_0=-1\), and \(K=2\).
Then \(1+e=-1\), so \(\phi=\exp(uN)\), and (21) gives
\(Y,Z,W,X\) in that order. The elementary realization
therefore preserves the primitive nonsliced derivation itself,
not just a multiple of it.

This construction starts with polynomial \(A\) and a supplied
homogeneous \(N\). It does not establish polynomiality of the
kernel of an arbitrary sliced derivation, a positive grading
for an arbitrary invariant ring, or homogeneity of an arbitrary
LND. It provides no degree-minimum or cancellation claim.

## 7. Verification and status

Fresh unsaved symbolic algebra reconstructed both complete
inverse compositions in (3)--(4), the Jacobian, all four exact
degrees, and the entire transported derivation (9). Each Euler
eigen-equation \(D(X_i)=d_iX_i\) was checked separately. The
all-ring shift, primitivity, common-zero locus, and slice
obstruction are proved above without relying on a saved
checker. Source576 was read completely before this note was
written. The general homogeneous realization follows from the
full inverse and exact commutators (19). Additional fresh
polynomial controls verify both inverse orders and all Euler
eigen-equations for scaled homogeneous degrees zero and two,
alongside the degree-minus-two control (3).

No saved checker was executed, imported, or compiled, and no
canonical file was edited. Author verification does not stand
in for an independent saved-source audit. This is a bounded
control for the primitive-normalization boundary, with no
novelty or general cancellation claim.
