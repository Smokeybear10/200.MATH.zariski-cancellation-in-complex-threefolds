# Primitive quotient freeness is independent of slice adjustment

Changing the supplied slice does not change the primitive intrinsic
direction produced by a fixed commuting action, or its fixed-locus ideal.
That ideal has a finite ambient description: divide the six coefficients
of the wedge of the two fields by their common factor. The remaining
unit-ideal question is not settled by primitivity.

This is an elementary refinement of the existing
[pass44 wedge criterion](heisenberg-common-base-pass44.md), together with
a bounded primary-source applicability check. It does not prove that the
one-good-partial construction always passes that criterion.

## 1. Actual setting and canonical primitive direction

Let
\[
B=\mathbb C[x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4]=A[s],\qquad
E(s)=1,\qquad A=\ker E,
\]
\[
0\ne Q\in\operatorname{LND}_{\mathbb C}(B),\qquad
[E,Q]=0,\qquad d:=Q|_A\ne0.
\tag{1}
\]
The last condition is equivalent to fraction-field independence of
E and Q. The intended application has E nice and
Q=[T,E] for an actual good coordinate partial T. Those additional
hypotheses are not needed for the algebra below and are not discarded
when stating the open implication.

The ring A is a finitely generated UFD with units \(\mathbb C^*\):
finite generation follows from the actual slice presentation, and
factoriality from factorial closure in B. Choose algebra generators
\(a_1,\ldots,a_r\) of A and let h be a gcd of the nonzero \(d(a_i)\).
Their ideal equals the ideal generated by all d(A), so h is independent
of the chosen generators up to a complex unit. Write
\[
d=hD,\qquad D(A)\subseteq A,\qquad
\gcd(D(a_1),\ldots,D(a_r))=1.
\tag{2}
\]

Here D really is an LND. Every prime factor \(\pi\) of h divides
d(A), hence divides \(d(\pi)\). For an LND on a characteristic-zero
domain, \(\pi\mid d(\pi)\) implies \(d(\pi)=0\): otherwise the
additive d-degree of the product \(\pi(d(\pi)/\pi)\) is at least
the d-degree of \(\pi\), contradicting
\(\deg_d d(\pi)=\deg_d\pi-1\).
Thus \(d(h)=0\), and
\(D^n(a)=h^{-n}d^n(a)\) in the fraction field. This proves local
nilpotence and equality of the two kernels.

For every other E-slice \(s'=s+b\), with \(b\in A\), the adjustment
\[
Q_{s'}=Q-Q(s')E,\qquad
Q_{s'}(s')=0,\qquad Q_{s'}|_A=d
\tag{3}
\]
restricts to precisely the same d. Its local nilpotence follows by
extending d coefficientwise on \(A[s']\), as proved in the
[adjustment note](good-partial-slice-adjustment-scope-pass75.md).
Consequently h, the primitive intrinsic direction D up to a constant,
and the ideal \(\mathfrak f=(D(A))A\) do not depend on s'.

## 2. Finite ambient test and exact meaning of saturation

Let
\[
J(E,Q)=
\big(E(x_i)Q(x_j)-E(x_j)Q(x_i):i<j\big)B.
\tag{4}
\]
The coordinate-independent alternating derivation \(E\wedge Q\)
has the same image ideal. In \(B=A[s]\), evaluation on \((s,a)\)
gives d(a), while expansion of arbitrary inputs gives only elements
of \((d(A))B\). Therefore
\[
J(E,Q)=(d(A))B=h\mathfrak f B,\qquad
h^{-1}J(E,Q)=\mathfrak f B.
\tag{5}
\]
In particular h is a gcd in B of the six displayed minors. To see
that no extra common factor appears, a polynomial of positive s-degree
cannot divide a nonzero element of A, and a prime of A remains prime
in A[s]. The primitive generators in (2) therefore still have gcd
one in B.

The notation \(h^{-1}J\) means the integral ideal generated by the
six quotients, equivalently the colon ideal \((J:h)\) here. It is
not an assertion about the possibly larger infinite saturation
\((J:h^\infty)\). A residual component of \(\mathfrak f\) may still
lie inside \(V(h)\).

Faithful flatness of \(A\subset A[s]\) gives
\[
h^{-1}J(E,Q)=B
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\mathfrak f=A
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
D\text{ has no fixed point on }\operatorname{Spec}A.
\tag{6}
\]
For a single additive group over \(\mathbb C\), absence of fixed
points is equivalent to freeness. Primitivity only says that
\(\mathfrak f\) lies in no height-one prime. It does not make this
ideal the unit ideal.

## 3. Slice and group-action distinctions

An actual global slice of \(Q_{s'}\) exists if and only if d has a
global slice on A. Indeed, writing a purported slice as a polynomial
in s', its constant coefficient is a d-slice. Conversely a d-slice
is already a \(Q_{s'}\)-slice. Likewise the fixed-locus ideal of
\(Q_{s'}\) is \((d(A))B\), so freeness of \(Q_{s'}\) is equivalent
to freeness of d.

If d already has a slice, or is fixed-point-free, (2) forces h to
be a unit. In this literal preservation sense primitive division
preserves either property. The unsupported inference is that a
slice or freeness of the original Q supplies either property for d.
With \(q=Q(s)\in A\), the distinction is
\[
(Q(B))B=(d(A),q)B,\qquad
J(E,Q)=(d(A))B.
\tag{7}
\]
The first ideal may be the unit ideal while the second is proper.

For the commuting pair, pointwise independence of E and Q is
equivalent to freeness of the full \(\mathbb G_a^2\)-action. The
stabilizer is an algebraic subgroup of a vector group in
characteristic zero, so trivial infinitesimal stabilizer is
equivalent to trivial stabilizer. Generic independence merely
says the wedge ideal is nonzero; individual slices say nothing
about every pointwise linear combination.

The [actual one-good-partial example](nice-common-slice-obstruction-pass75.md)
has both actions globally sliced but their joint rank drops on
a=1 and a=-1. There d=(1-a^2) times an actual coordinate partial,
and the primitive ideal in (6) is the unit ideal. Thus it is a
positive primitive repair, not a counterexample to the desired
saturation statement.

The already retained primitive fixed-line control in
[pass44, Section 3](heisenberg-common-base-pass44.md) has
\(\mathfrak f=(x,y)\). It disproves an inference from primitivity,
the cylinder and a commuting action alone. It does not supply
the additional equation Q=[T,E] for the specified nice E and an
actual good partial T. No new example is counted here.

## 4. Exact primary-source gate and the missing implication

Kaliman--Saveliev Corollary 2.8 applies to a smooth contractible
affine complex threefold with a nontrivial additive action.
Its free-action conclusion, together with its domination
hypothesis, recognizes the threefold as affine three-space.
Here the cylinder supplies the required domination by an affine
three-plane, as proved in the
[intrinsic quotient note](intrinsic-quotient-surface-pass.md).
Thus (6) implies \(A\simeq\mathbb C^{[3]}\). This recognition
criterion was already established in pass44; it is not a new
consequence of the present source search.[^ks]

A superficially relevant group-action theorem does not bypass
(6). Derksen--van den Essen--Finston--Maubach Theorem 2 concerns
an n-dimensional unipotent group acting on a factorial variety
of dimension n+1 with only constant units. Its part (1) uses
one trivial stabilizer for a generic-fiber conclusion; part (2)
requires freeness everywhere for an equivariant product.
The pair here has quotient dimension two, not one. The abstract's
compressed stronger wording is not substituted for the actual
two-part theorem. Neither the dimension gate nor the everywhere-free
gate is supplied by individual slices.[^unipotent]

The resulting bounded target is exact: prove the unit-ideal
condition in (6) from the actual nice sliced E and derived good Q,
or construct a control meeting those same additional hypotheses
with a proper primitive ideal. Changing the E-slice cannot change
this target. The checked primary statements do not settle it;
no exhaustive nonexistence-of-a-theorem claim is made.
