# Polynomial coefficient return in the remaining reciprocal conic case

Working result: in the genuine-conic branch of306, all potential
coefficients needed for the original map are polynomial in s over an exact
curve field. The remaining coefficient extension is generated by the
nonvanishing evaluated surface factor:

\[
L=\mathcal F(s,\eta),\qquad
[\mathcal F:\mathbb C(t)]<\infty,\qquad
\eta=(A-B)(\sigma,\theta)\in L^*.
\tag{1}
\]

This is an exact field return and a bounded polynomial presentation, not
recognition. In particular it does not prove that eta is polynomial or

attempt to extend312's conductor method to this branch.

There is no assumption that L is a rational function field.

## 2. Bounds for k, n and the selected affine coefficients

Since m belongs to \(\mathcal F\), the first integration gives

\[
k=ms+f,\qquad f\in\mathcal F,\qquad \deg_s k\le1.
\tag{5}
\]

The V-squared factor coefficient from306 gives

\[
n_{ss}=\frac{p^2}{2}-\frac{m^2q^2}{8p^2}\in\mathcal F.
\tag{6}
\]

Two applications of the same integration rule imply
\(n\in\mathcal F[s]\), \(\deg_s n\le2\). Differentiation in t
preserves these s-degree bounds. The affine slopes, already determined
by the cubic factor coefficients, are

\[
b_P=\frac{k_{tt}}4-\frac{m k_{ttt}}{12p}\in\mathcal F[s],
\quad \deg_s b_P\le1,\qquad
b_V=\frac p2-\frac{m q}{4p}\in\mathcal F.
\tag{7}
\]

In particular
\(p-2b_V=mq/(2p)\in\mathcal F^*\). Define coefficient functions

\[
\mu=k_{tt}-\frac{k_t p+2n_s}{m},\qquad
\nu=n_t-\frac{k_t n_s}{m}.
\tag{8}
\]

Then \(\mu\in\mathcal F[s]\) has degree at most one and
\(\nu\in\mathcal F[s]\) degree at most two. The coefficient of U in
the monic surface is

\[
A_1=A+B=-\frac{2p}{m}P^2+pV+\mu P+\nu.
\tag{9}
\]

We now determine the degree of b0 from a lower coefficient rather than
assuming its regularity. Let A0 be the constant-U coefficient of H-h0.
The literal formula296 gives

\[
[P^2]A_0=C_2:=\frac{n_{tt}}2-\frac{p n_t+n_s k_{tt}/2}{m}.
\tag{10}
\]

On the other hand, the P-squared coefficient of \((A_1-B)B\) is
\(-2p b_0/m+(\mu-b_P)b_P\). Since p is nonzero, equality gives

\[
b_0=\frac{m}{2p}\bigl((\mu-b_P)b_P-C_2\bigr)
\in\mathcal F[s],\qquad \deg_s b_0\le2.
\tag{11}
\]

## 3. The linear V coefficient bounds the second potential

The entire linear V coefficient in A0 is
\(-n_s^2/m-\ell_s\). The corresponding coefficient of
\((A_1-B)B\) is \((p-2b_V)b_0+\nu b_V\). Hence

\[
\ell_s=-\frac{n_s^2}{m}-(p-2b_V)b_0-\nu b_V
\in\mathcal F[s],\qquad \deg_s\ell_s\le2.
\tag{12}
\]

Exact integration proves

\[
\ell\in\mathcal F[s],\qquad \deg_s\ell\le3.
\tag{13}

Neither the constant term h0 of the surface nor its derivative was
discarded: h0 does not enter either coefficient used in (10),(12).
