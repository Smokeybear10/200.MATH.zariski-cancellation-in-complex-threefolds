# The actual split chart cannot have constant projected y

**Status: working exclusion under the full actual split-chart hypotheses.**

In the actual split-cubic chart175, the projected polynomial \(y\)
does not belong to the selected coefficient field \(L\). A single
determinant computation forces an actual commuting locally nilpotent
derivation. Its kernel is a polynomial three-ring. Wang's
two-coordinate classification then contradicts the original global
slice through the nondegenerate critical point of a split quadratic
form with an arbitrary correction in \(X,Y\).

The proof uses neither the whole-plane completion175 nor its quartic
degree bound or cone lemma. The determinant handles constant, linear
and quadratic projected \(w\) together. It does not require a
rationality or integral descent claim for the coefficient field.

## 1. Actual chart and a whole-line parameter

Retain the full actual hypotheses of
[the split chart175](split-cubic-generic-chart-pass175-working.md):
\[
\begin{gathered}
B=\mathbb C[X,Y,Z,W]=A[u],\quad E=\partial_u,\quad A=\ker E,\\
(\deg_E X,\deg_E Y,\deg_E Z,\deg_E W)=(1,1,2,2),\quad
\ker\theta_0=(\mathsf X\mathsf Z+\mathsf Y\mathsf W),\\
a=EX\ne0,\quad N=[u^3](\partial_Z|_A)\ne0,\quad
L=\operatorname{Frac}(\ker_A N),\quad A_L=L[v],\quad Nv=1 .
\end{gathered}
\tag{1}
\]
Here \(\theta_0\) is the map from the weighted polynomial ring to
the algebra generated by the four leading forms. Its displayed
kernel is the full initial kernel. The selected original
\(\partial_Z\) fixes \(X,Y,W\). Set
\(t=EY/a\) and \(\rho=(E^2W/2)/a^2\).
These ratios are algebraically independent, \(a\in L^*\), and
\(L/\mathbb C(t,\rho)\) is finite separable. The old coefficient
partials extend to \(L[v]\) fixing \(v\).

The actual chart and its calibrated determinant are
\[
\begin{gathered}
Y=y+tX,\qquad
Z=z+\sigma X-t\rho X^2,\qquad
W=w+\vartheta X+\rho X^2,\\
\sigma=-t\vartheta-\rho y+k,\qquad
z+tw=k_t^{\rm old}y+(k_\rho^{\rm old}-y)\vartheta+n,\\
y,z,w,\sigma,\vartheta\in L[v],\quad k,n\in L,\quad
L[y,z,w]=L[v],\\
\det\frac{\partial(Y,Z,W)}{\partial(t,\rho,v)}
=-a^3\in L^* .
\end{gathered}
\tag{2}
\]
The determinant is taken at fixed \(X\). Also \(E=a\partial_X\)
on the chart, fixing \(L[v]\).

Assume, for contradiction, that \(y\in L\). Then
\[
y_\rho^{\rm old}\ne0,\qquad
t,y\text{ are algebraically independent},\qquad
[L:\mathbb C(t,y)]<\infty .
\tag{3}
\]
Indeed, if the first derivative vanished, the first row of the
determinant in (2) would be \((X+y_t^{\rm old},0,0)\).
Its monic positive-degree factor in \(X\) cannot divide the
nonzero scalar determinant. If \(y\) were algebraic over
\(\mathbb C(t)\), the old rho derivative would kill it by
separability, giving the same contradiction. Finally \(L\)
is finitely generated, so the displayed transcendence basis
makes the last extension finite and separable.

By the separate denominator-free
[zero-R exclusion175](split-zero-R-exclusion-pass175-working.md),
\(R=k_\rho^{\rm old}-y\ne0\). In the present branch \(R\in L^*\).
The first potential of chart175 gives
\[
Rw+f(y,\vartheta)=\ell,\qquad
\deg f(U,V)\le2,\qquad \ell\in L .
\tag{4}
\]
Thus \(w\in L[\vartheta]\) has degree at most two, and (2)
puts \(z\) in this same ring. Conversely \(\vartheta\in L[v]\).
The whole-line equality in (2) therefore gives
\(L[\vartheta]=L[v]\): \(\vartheta\) is an affine parameter
with nonzero slope. This conclusion uses only the first potential.

## 2. Change of coefficient partials and the exact determinant

Use the commuting extensions of \(\partial_t,\partial_y\) to
the finite separable field \(L/\mathbb C(t,y)\), and extend
them to \(L[\vartheta,X]\) fixing \(\vartheta,X\).
Their joint constant field is exactly \(\mathbb C\).
For clarity, a joint constant algebraic over \(\mathbb C(t,y)\)
has a monic minimal polynomial. Differentiating it gives a
lower-degree relation, so every coefficient has both derivatives
zero. These coefficients lie in \(\mathbb C\), and algebraic
closedness of \(\mathbb C\) proves the assertion.

The Jacobian of the parameter change is
\[
\det\frac{\partial(t,y,\vartheta)}{\partial(t,\rho,v)}
=y_\rho^{\rm old}\vartheta_v\in L^* .
\tag{5}
\]
Consequently the new determinant
\(\mathcal J(X)=\det\partial(Y,Z,W)/\partial(t,y,\vartheta)\)
is still a nonzero element of \(L\), independent of \(X\) and
\(\vartheta\). It need not be a global unit in an original ring.

Write
\[
\begin{gathered}
w=\alpha\vartheta^2+\beta\vartheta+g,\qquad
\alpha,\beta,g\in L,\\
r=k_\rho^{\rm old},\quad R=r-y,\quad
A_0=k_t^{\rm old}y+n,\quad K_0=k-\rho y,\\
Z+tW=R\vartheta+A_0+K_0X .
\end{gathered}
\tag{6}
\]
From now on subscripts \(t,y\) are the new coefficient partials.
In particular derivatives of \(r,A_0,K_0,\rho\) in the next
display are taken in the exact field \(L\), not in the old chart.

Adding \(t\) times the \(W\)-row to the \(Z\)-row in the
Jacobian gives the literal matrix
\[
\begin{pmatrix}
X&1&0\\
R_t\vartheta+A_{0,t}-w+(K_{0,t}-\vartheta)X-\rho X^2&
R_y\vartheta+A_{0,y}+K_{0,y}X&R\\
w_t+\rho_tX^2&w_y+\rho_yX^2&w_\vartheta+X
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{7}
\]
The middle row includes the term \(-W\,dt\) from
\(dZ+t\,dW=d(Z+tW)-W\,dt\).
Expanding (7), with no preceding coefficient simplification,
yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal J(X)={}&(2\alpha\vartheta+\beta+X)
\bigl\{\alpha\vartheta^2+(\beta-R_t)\vartheta+g-A_{0,t}\\
&\qquad+X[(R_y+1)\vartheta+A_{0,y}-K_{0,t}]
       +X^2(K_{0,y}+\rho)\bigr\}\\
&+R[w_t-Xw_y+\rho_tX^2-\rho_yX^3].
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]
All identities here are in the polynomial ring \(L[\vartheta,X]\).

## 3. The determinant forces constant affine coefficients

Since \(\mathcal J\in L^*\), the coefficient of
\(\vartheta^3\) at \(X=0\) in (8) is zero. It is \(2\alpha^2\),
so \(\alpha=0\). Now take successively the coefficients of
\(X^2\vartheta\), \(X\vartheta\), and \(\vartheta\) at \(X=0\).
They give
\[
R_y=-1,\qquad
\beta-R_t-R\beta_y=0,\qquad
\beta^2-\beta R_t+R\beta_t=0 .
\tag{9}
\]
The second equation and \(R\ne0\) turn the third into
\(\beta_t+\beta\beta_y=0\).

Put \(\mathcal K=\ker_L\partial_y\). It is stable under
\(\partial_t\). The first equation gives \(r=R+y\in\mathcal K\).
The second says \(\partial_y(R\beta)=-r_t\).
Subtracting the explicit primitive \(r_tR\) gives
\[
R\beta=r_tR+d,\qquad
\beta=r_t+\frac dR,\qquad d\in\mathcal K .
\tag{10}
\]
This subtraction does not presume \(\mathcal K=\mathbb C(t)\)
or the existence of arbitrary antiderivatives in \(\mathcal K\).
Substitution in the remaining equation gives exactly
\[
0=\beta_t+\beta\beta_y
=r_{tt}+\frac{d_t}{R}+\frac{d^2}{R^3},\qquad
r_{tt}R^3+d_tR^2+d^2=0 .
\tag{11}
\]
The element \(R\) is transcendental over \(\mathcal K\):
otherwise differentiating a separable algebraic equation over
\(\mathcal K\) would give \(R_y=0\), contrary to (9).
Coefficient comparison in (11) therefore gives \(d=0\) and
\(r_{tt}=0\). The exact joint constant field from Section 2
then proves
\[
r=\mu t+\nu,\quad R=\mu t+\nu-y,\quad
\beta=\mu,\quad w=\mu\vartheta+g,\qquad \mu,\nu\in\mathbb C .
\tag{12}
\]
The possibility \(\mu=0\), including \(w\in L\), is retained.

## 4. An actual commuting derivation and its polynomial kernel

Take \(T_0=\partial_\vartheta\) on the chart, fixing \(L,X\).
No factor \(R\) multiplies this derivative. Equations (2),
(6), and (12) give
\[
T_0X=T_0Y=0,\quad
T_0Z=R-t\mu-tX=\nu-Y,\quad
T_0W=X+\mu .
\tag{13}
\]
Thus it is the actual polynomial derivation
\[
T_0=(\nu-Y)\partial_Z+(X+\mu)\partial_W .
\tag{14}
\]
It is locally nilpotent because it fixes its coefficients in
\(\mathbb C[X,Y]\). It is not asserted to be a coordinate
partial: both coefficients vanish on the plane
\(X=-\mu,\ Y=\nu\). On the chart \(E=a\partial_X\) with
\(a\in L\), so \([E,T_0]=0\), and this equality holds on \(B\).

Set \(p=X+\mu\), \(q=\nu-Y\), and
\[
F=pZ-qW=(X+\mu)Z+(Y-\nu)W,\qquad
\ker_B T_0=\mathbb C[p,q,F]=\mathbb C[X,Y,F].
\tag{15}
\]
Here is the kernel proof. The three elements \(p,q,F\) are
algebraically independent. After inverting \(p\), use
\(Z=(F+qW)/p\); then \(T_0=p\partial_W\), and its kernel is
\(\mathbb C[p,q,F]_p\). Moreover
\(\mathbb C[p,q,F]\cap pB=p\mathbb C[p,q,F]\):
modulo \(p\), the map
\(\mathbb C[q,F]\longrightarrow\mathbb C[q,Z,W]\),
\(F\longmapsto-qW\), is injective by coefficient comparison.
Reducing a least possible denominator power of \(p\) proves
the unlocalized equality (15).

Commutation makes \(E\) restrict to a nonzero LND \(D\) on the
actual polynomial three-ring \(C=\mathbb C[X,Y,F]\).
One has \(DX=a\ne0\) and \(D^2X=D^2Y=0\).
Divide the coefficient gcd \(\delta\) to write \(D=\delta D_0\).
This gcd is invariant: if \(D\delta=\delta D_0\delta\ne0\),
additivity of LND degree on products contradicts its decrease
by one under differentiation. Therefore
\[
D\delta=D_0\delta=0,\qquad
D^jh=\delta^jD_0^jh,\qquad
\ker D_0=\ker D,\qquad D_0^2X=D_0^2Y=0 .
\tag{16}
\]
In particular \(D_0\) is a nonzero irreducible LND of \(C\).
No slice for \(D\) or \(D_0\) has been assumed.

## 5. Wang's alternatives and the critical-point contradiction

Apply Wang's theorem as reproduced in Dasgupta--Gupta
Theorem 4.1 to \(C=\mathbb C[X,Y,F]\) and \(D_0\).
In the first alternative a nonzero invariant complex linear
form in \(X,Y\) makes \(t=EY/EX\) complex, contrary to (2).
The second alternative supplies \(\xi\in\ker D_0\) such that
\(C=\mathbb C[\xi,X,Y]\). Necessarily
\[
\xi=cF+Q(X,Y)
=c(XZ+YW)+c\mu Z-c\nu W+Q(X,Y),\quad
c\in\mathbb C^*,\quad Q\in\mathbb C[X,Y],\quad E\xi=0 .
\tag{17}
\]
Indeed the polynomial presentations by \(F\) and \(\xi\)
over \(\mathbb C[X,Y]\) are inverse one-variable polynomial
maps. Their degrees multiply to one, and their leading
coefficients are units of that base ring. This argument does
not assert that \(F\) or \(\xi\) is an ambient coordinate.

For completeness, any polynomial
\(c(XZ+YW)+\alpha_0Z+\beta_0W+Q(X,Y)\), \(c\ne0\),
has a unique critical point:
\[
X_*=-\alpha_0/c,\quad Y_*=-\beta_0/c,\quad
Z_*=-Q_X(X_*,Y_*)/c,\quad W_*=-Q_Y(X_*,Y_*)/c .
\tag{18}
\]
In the order \(X,Y,Z,W\), its Hessian at this point is
\[
\begin{pmatrix}Q''(X_*,Y_*)&cI_2\\cI_2&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad \det=c^4\ne0 .
\tag{19}
\]
Differentiate \(E\xi=0\) and evaluate at the critical point.
Since the gradient of \(\xi\) vanishes there, the Hessian
annihilates the vector of coefficients of \(E\). Invertibility
makes all those coefficients zero there, contradicting \(Eu=1\).
This is the arbitrary-\(Q\) critical lemma158; no degree bound
on \(Q\) is required.

The assumed constant projection is therefore impossible:
\[
y\notin L\quad\text{under the full actual split-chart hypotheses}.
\tag{20}
\]
