# Universal frame search: exact finite levels and the missing descent

This note separates the unrestricted coordinate-frame question from the
conditional recognition theorems. Its new implementation-facing reduction
is an exact coefficient ideal for **all inverse pairs of bounded degree**,
not only a supplied family or elementary ansatz. It proves neither a
successful degree bound nor termination. The original complex threefold
cancellation problem is not solved here.

## 1. What the previous minima do and do not establish

Let
\[
B=\mathbb C[x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4]=A[s],\qquad E(s)=1,
\qquad A=\ker E,
\tag{1}
\]
where \(E\) is a nonzero polynomial LND. For a coordinate frame \(F\),
write \(T_i^F\) for its actual coordinate partials and
\[
\lambda_i(F)=\max\{j:\operatorname{ad}_E^j(T_i^F)\ne0\},
\qquad \Lambda(F)=\sum_i\lambda_i(F).
\tag{2}
\]
These adjoint heights are finite because conjugation by the polynomial
flow of \(E\) acts polynomially on each polynomial derivation. They are
not the shifted slice-filtration statistic \(H=\sum(q_i+1)\).

The well-ordering argument supplies minima, not a method for lowering a
given frame. In particular, \(\min_F\Lambda(F)=0\) is equivalent to
\(A\simeq\mathbb C^{[3]}\): an adapted frame proves the forward search
has a zero-height target, while four coordinate partials commuting with
\(E\) force its coefficients in that frame to be constants. The nonzero
constant vector field is straightened by a linear change.

The [original minimization note](coordinate-frame-minimization-pass.md)
likewise proves \(H_{\min}=3\) exactly when \(A\) is polynomial. This is
an equivalence, not an independent universal estimate. Its historical
thresholds are not used as the current frontier. Passes 41 and 42 have
closed more supplied low-height profiles, but have not bounded the
minimum of an arbitrary sliced action.

The word "unrestricted" in
[pass 32](unrestricted-minimum-pass32.md) refers instead to all horizontal
actions in its specified canonical model, fixing the specified base
coordinate. That note already demonstrates an unsuccessful global
minimum for that different objective. It neither supplies nor disproves
a universal frame-height descent.

The [pass-39 coefficient criterion](coordinate-reduction-pass39.md)
requires a supplied polynomial inverse family. Its bounded elementary
ansatz and its all-degree wrong-shear example do not quantify over every
polynomial automorphism. A rereading of these notes found no earlier
all-inverse-pairs construction of the finite levels below. No novelty
claim is made for this standard polynomial-equation construction.

## 2. A finite algebraic test for every inverse pair of a fixed degree

Fix \(d\ge1\). Introduce independent coefficient parameters for
\[
F_i(x)=\sum_{|\alpha|\le d}a_{i,\alpha}x^\alpha,
\qquad
G_i(y)=\sum_{|\alpha|\le d}b_{i,\alpha}y^\alpha,
\quad i=1,\ldots,4.
\tag{3}
\]
There are \(8\binom{d+4}{4}\) parameters. Let \(\mathcal I_d^{\rm inv}\)
be the ideal generated by every coefficient of both sets of identities
\[
G(F(x))-x=0,\qquad F(G(y))-y=0.
\tag{4}
\]
Both polynomial degrees in the displayed spatial variables are at most
\(d^2\), and each coefficient equation has parameter degree at most
\(d+1\). No unit-Jacobian implication or tameness assertion is used.

Over the parameter polynomial ring define the following polynomial
derivation, even before imposing (4):
\[
\mathcal T_i=
\sum_{j=1}^4
\left(\frac{\partial G_j}{\partial y_i}\right)(F(x))\partial_{x_j}.
\tag{5}
\]
After any specialization satisfying (4), the chain rule shows
\(\mathcal T_i(F_k)=\delta_{ik}\). Thus these are exactly the partials
of the specialized actual frame, not merely commuting candidate fields.

Let \(e\) be the maximum total degree of the coefficients of \(E\),
and put \(e_+=\max(e-1,0)\). The coefficients of (5) have spatial degree
at most \(d(d-1)\) and parameter degree at most \(d\). A bracket with
the fixed field \(E\) raises spatial degree by at most \(e_+\) and does
not raise parameter degree. Consequently every coefficient of
\(\operatorname{ad}_E^{r+1}\mathcal T_i\) has spatial degree at most
\[
L(d,r)=d(d-1)+(r+1)e_+,
\tag{6}
\]
and parameter degree at most \(d\). This bound is deliberately safe
also for constant \(E\), where differentiation can only lower degree.

For a prescribed vector \(\mathbf r=(r_1,\ldots,r_4)\) of nonnegative
integers, adjoin to \(\mathcal I_d^{\rm inv}\) all spatial coefficients
of \(\operatorname{ad}_E^{r_i+1}\mathcal T_i(x_j)\), for every \(i,j\).
Call the resulting finite ideal \(\mathcal I_{d,\mathbf r}\).

**Finite-level criterion.** There is an actual polynomial coordinate
frame with forward and inverse degree at most \(d\) and
\(\lambda_i(F)\le r_i\) for every \(i\) if and only if
\[
1\notin\mathcal I_{d,\mathbf r}.
\tag{7}
\]

Indeed, an actual inverse pair of these degrees supplies a zero of all
the equations. Conversely, a common zero supplies the genuine inverse
maps (4), and (5) identifies their partials, so the bracket equations
give precisely the required bounds. Existence of a common complex zero
is equivalent to properness by the Hilbert Nullstellensatz.[^null]

If the coefficients of \(E\) lie in a specified number field, the ideal
can be computed there. A Gröbner-basis test for membership of 1 gives an
exact decision for existence over \(\mathbb C\). Properness already
gives a solution over a finite algebraic extension of that coefficient
field. This is not an algorithm using undecidable zero tests on arbitrary
black-box complex constants. No practicality or small complexity is
asserted for the number of parameters in (3).

A rejection can be certified by a finite identity
\(1=\sum_j H_j P_j\), where the \(P_j\) are the displayed coefficient
equations. A positive algebraic specialization can be checked by both
inverse identities and the actual brackets. These are finite-degree
certificates, not certificates against all larger degrees.

For fixed \(L\), existence of a frame of \(\Lambda\le L\) in this
degree class is the finite disjunction of (7) over
\(\sum_i r_i\le L\). This supplies an exact finite minimization of
\(\Lambda\) at that level. It does not conflate \(\Lambda\) with \(H\).

## 3. An exact positive semidecision for the original target

There is an even simpler coefficient ideal for a fixed invariant
coordinate. Add to \(\mathcal I_d^{\rm inv}\) all coefficients of
\[
E(F_1)=0
\tag{8}
\]
and call the result \(\mathcal J_d\). The extra equations have spatial
degree at most \(e+d-1\) and parameter degree one. They ask for an
actual ambient coordinate, not a merely nonconstant invariant.

Under the sliced hypothesis (1),
\[
A\simeq\mathbb C^{[3]}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\mathcal J_d\text{ is proper for at least one finite }d.
\tag{9}
\]
For the nontrivial direction, a solution makes \(F_1\) an invariant
ambient coordinate. The original slice makes \(A\) regular, so the
regular-kernel theorem over that coordinate applies. For the converse,
an adapted frame \((a_1,a_2,a_3,s)\), with \(A=\mathbb C[a_1,a_2,a_3]\),
has a polynomial inverse of some finite degree.[^bgl]

The same equivalence holds using \(\mathcal I_{d,\mathbf0}\). Exhausting
\(d=1,2,\ldots\) is therefore a positive semidecision on exact inputs:
it terminates with a certificate whenever the kernel is polynomial.
Every automorphism and its inverse have finite degree, including any
non-tame possibility, so no class of actual coordinate changes is
omitted by this exhaustion. There is no proved reason here that it must
terminate for every sliced \(E\).

The logical gap cannot be removed by well-ordering: it is the
nonemptiness of at least one of the finite levels, not the existence of
a least integer once a level is nonempty. An unrestricted decreasing
invariant would need a proved move from every remaining configuration,
with an actual polynomial inverse and a strict decrease. Neither
well-ordering nor the coefficient equations provide that move.

## 4. Why tame reduction and finite jets do not supply termination

The September 4, 2026 primary survey by Lamy explicitly states that
whether the tame subgroup equals the full automorphism group remains
open in dimension at least four. Its reduction theorem in Section 2.4
is for tame three-variable automorphisms. Its four-variable discussion
is for the subgroup preserving a fixed nondegenerate quadratic form,
not arbitrary sliced actions or their frames. I directly read the
introductory scope, Theorem 2.16 with its setup, and Section 3.1.
These results do not replace (4) by an unrestricted sequence of
elementary reductions, and their coordinate-degree function is not
the adjoint-height function (2).[^lamy]

Augmentation approximation is a distinct topology. The approximation
discussion of Kanel-Belov, Yu and Elishev invokes tame approximation to
arbitrary finite augmentation order in characteristic zero. Its
augmentation subgroup consists of maps equal to the identity modulo a
power of the variable ideal. I read Definition 2.2, the Section 3.1
discussion, and the relevant approximation passage in Section 3.3,
not the original full proof of Anick's approximation theorem. The
following direct control does not depend on that theorem.[^approx]

For \(E=\partial_s\) on \(\mathbb C[a,s,b,c]\), consider the actual
inverse pair
\[
F_N=(a+s^N,s,b,c),\qquad
G_N=(a-s^N,s,b,c),\qquad N\ge2.
\tag{10}
\]
Both agree with the identity modulo \((a,s,b,c)^N\). The frame
partials are
\[
T_1^{F_N}=\partial_a,\qquad
T_2^{F_N}=\partial_s-Ns^{N-1}\partial_a,\qquad
T_3^{F_N}=\partial_b,\quad T_4^{F_N}=\partial_c.
\]
For \(1\le j\le N-1\),
\[
\operatorname{ad}_E^jT_2^{F_N}
=-\frac{N!}{(N-1-j)!}s^{N-1-j}\partial_a,
\quad
\operatorname{ad}_E^N T_2^{F_N}=0.
\tag{11}
\]
Thus the exact adjoint profile is \((0,N-1,0,0)\), while the limiting
identity frame has profile zero. Arbitrarily accurate finite jets
therefore do not preserve a fixed low-height target. A density statement
alone cannot guarantee an exact height-reducing approximant. This does
not exclude a more carefully constrained approximation theorem; it
identifies the additional condition such a theorem would have to prove.

## 5. Verification and remaining obligation

The finite-level algebra and the all-\(N\) control above are proved
directly. This note does not implement the very large inverse-pair
search and adds no unittest count. The original source dependencies
for fixed-coordinate recognition are unchanged. The separate new
pass-43 global-corrector argument is not an assumption in this note.

The strongest remaining universal obligation is still a proved
operation or geometric theorem producing an actual recognized frame
for arbitrary (1). Merely asserting that such a frame exists restates
the target by (9). The bounded ideals instead make each specified
degree-level claim independently falsifiable and certifiable. A
complete novelty audit and external mathematical review have not
occurred, and no public novelty claim is made.
