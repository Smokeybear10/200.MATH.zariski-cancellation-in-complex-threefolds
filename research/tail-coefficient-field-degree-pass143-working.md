# Missing tail coefficients have an exact degree cost

Let \(L_0\) be the field generated by the two speed ratios and all
peeling coefficients, including the evaluated first negative coefficient.
The actual projected source field implies a sharper degree statement:
the minimal plane equation over \(L_0\) has degree
\([L:L_0]\deg g\), where \(g\) is the selected equation over the full
coefficient field \(L\). It divides the first tail.

Thus any coefficient extension not detected by the tail data consumes
at least that many copies of the selected degree. This is a restriction
on possible extra factors, not a proof that they cannot occur.

## 1. Exact fields and source inputs

Use the actual dense chart139 and its signed tails, with
\[
\begin{gathered}
K=\mathbb C(\tau,\rho)\subset L,\qquad [L:K]<\infty,\qquad
F=L(v)=\mathbb C(y,z,w_0),\\
y,z\in L[v],\qquad L[y,z]=L[v],\qquad
\ker\bigl(L[U,V]\longrightarrow L[v]\bigr)=(g),\\
w_0=\Omega(y,z),\qquad
\Omega=\sum_{j=0}^{m}H_j(f_j;U,V),\\
\Phi=-\sum_{j=0}^{m}H_{j+1}(f_j;U,V)-f_{-1}\ne0,
\qquad \Phi(y,z)=0 .
\end{gathered}
\tag{1}
\]
Here \(H_j\) uses the commuting extensions of the two coefficient
partials, and \(f_0,\ldots,f_m,f_{-1}\in L\).
The source triple \(y,z,w_0\) is algebraically independent over
\(\mathbb C\). These are actual chart inputs, not consequences
assumed for every formal component.[^1]

Set
\[
L_0=K(f_0,\ldots,f_m,f_{-1}),\qquad
n=[L:L_0],\qquad d=\deg g,\qquad D=\deg\Phi .
\tag{2}
\]
Every derivation of \(K\) extends uniquely to each intermediate
finite separable field. Consequently \(L_0\) is preserved by both
coefficient partials: restricting the unique extensions on \(L\)
gives the extensions on \(L_0\). In particular
\(\Omega,\Phi\in L_0[U,V]\).

The source presentation supplies the important equality
\[
F=L_0(y,z).
\tag{3}
\]
Indeed \(L_0\subset F\) gives one inclusion. Conversely
\(w_0=\Omega(y,z)\in L_0(y,z)\), and
\(F=\mathbb C(y,z,w_0)\) gives the other.
Omitting this actual source-field hypothesis would invalidate the
degree argument below.

Let \(g_0\in L_0[U,V]\) generate the kernel of evaluation in \(F\).
This is a height-one prime: its image has fraction field (3),
which has transcendence degree one over \(L_0\).
The polynomial ring is a UFD, so \(g_0\) is irreducible. Since
\(\Phi\) is a nonzero element of this kernel, \(g_0\mid\Phi\).

## 2. Generic projection computes the degree exactly

Choose \(\lambda\in\mathbb C\) outside a finite exceptional set and
put \(\ell=y+\lambda z\). The choice can ensure simultaneously
that the top homogeneous parts of \(g_0\) and \(g\) do not vanish
at \((-\lambda,1)\), and that
\(\deg_v\ell=\max(\deg_v y,\deg_v z)>0\).
Each restriction excludes only finitely many values because its
polynomial condition is nonzero, and \(\mathbb C\) is infinite.

The polynomial \(g_0(\ell-\lambda V,V)\), with \(\ell\) regarded
as an independent variable, is irreducible in \(L_0[\ell,V]\)
by an invertible linear substitution. It has \(V\)-degree
\(\deg g_0\) by the chosen leading homogeneous coefficient.
Gauss's lemma preserves irreducibility over \(L_0(\ell)\).
Since \(F=L_0(\ell,z)\), it follows that
\([F:L_0(\ell)]=\deg g_0\).
The same argument for \(g\) gives
\([F:L(\ell)]=d\).

The transcendental element \(\ell\) does not change the degree
of the finite coefficient extension. A basis of \(L/L_0\)
remains linearly independent over \(L_0(\ell)\): clearing
denominators and comparing polynomial coefficients in \(\ell\)
reduces any proposed dependence to one over \(L_0\).
It also spans. The field tower therefore gives
\[
\boxed{\quad
\deg g_0=[F:L_0(\ell)]
       =[F:L(\ell)]\,[L(\ell):L_0(\ell)]
       =nd .
\quad}
\tag{4}
\]

For comparison with the polynomial parametrization, if
\(\ell=q(v)\) has positive degree, then
\([L(v):L(q(v))]=\deg q\). One proof is that
\(q(T)-S\) is irreducible in \(L[T,S]\), since its quotient is
the domain \(L[T]\), and is primitive as a polynomial in \(T\)
over \(L[S]\). Gauss's lemma then gives its degree as the
minimal polynomial over \(L(S)\).
Thus also \(d=\max(\deg_v y,\deg_v z)\).

## 3. The norm interpretation retains all coefficient embeddings

Normalize \(g\) by setting its leading coefficient for a fixed
monomial order equal to one. Its coefficients generate all of
\(L\) over \(L_0\).

To prove this, let \(L_g\) be that coefficient-generated subfield.
Then \(L_0\subset L_g\subset L\subset F\), and (3) implies
\(F=L_g(y,z)\). The polynomial \(g\) is irreducible over \(L_g\)
because it is irreducible even over \(L\); consequently it is
also the evaluation-kernel generator over \(L_g\).
The projection and tower argument of Section 2, now for
\(L/L_g\), gives \(d=[L:L_g]d\). Since \(d>0\), this forces
\(L_g=L\).

Let \(\iota\) range over the \(n\) distinct \(L_0\)-embeddings
of \(L\) into an algebraic closure of \(L_0\). Coefficientwise
application defines
\[
N_g(U,V)=\prod_{\iota:L\hookrightarrow\overline{L_0}}
                 \iota(g)(U,V)\in L_0[U,V],
\qquad N_g\sim g_0,\qquad \deg N_g=nd .
\tag{5}
\]
The product belongs to \(L_0[U,V]\) because the normal closure
permutes its factors. Each factor is absolutely irreducible:
the quotient by \(g\) over \(L\) is \(L[v]\), which remains
a domain after extending the coefficient field to an algebraic
closure. The factors are pairwise distinct. Association would
be equality after the chosen
normalization, and equality of their coefficients would make the
embeddings agree on all of \(L\).

The polynomial \(N_g\) evaluates to zero at \((y,z)\): in an
algebraic closure of \(F\), the product includes the identity
embedding factor \(g(y,z)=0\). Hence \(g_0\mid N_g\), and equal
degrees from (4) prove association in (5).
If \(L/L_0\) is not normal, not every conjugate factor need have
coefficients in \(L\). The norm statement is over an algebraic
closure and does not count all \(n\) factors as \(L\)-rational
components.

## 4. Consequences and the unresolved implication

Since \(g_0\mid\Phi\), equations (2)--(4) give
\[
\boxed{\quad [L:L_0]\,\deg g\le\deg\Phi=\mu+1.\quad}
\qquad
\deg\Phi=\deg g\ \Longrightarrow\ L=L_0 .
\tag{6}
\]
The equality with \(\mu+1\) uses the actual fixed-good shear
minimum141.[^2] No such minimum is asserted for arbitrary formal
data.

More generally \(D<2d\) forces \(L=L_0\). If \(D=nd\), then
\(\Phi\sim g_0\) already over \(L_0\); if also \(n=1\), it is
primitive over \(L\). The conclusions \(L=L_0\) and
\(\Phi\sim g\) are not equivalent in general by this argument.
Even with no missing coefficient extension, an additional factor
not dictated by the norm could remain.

The actual sharp family140 has \(D=d\), so it necessarily
recovers the full coefficient field from these tail data.
The actual cover family140 has \(D=d=1\), despite
\([L:K]\) being arbitrarily large. Its coefficient \(f_{-1}\)
recovers the extra primitive element, consistent with \(n=1\).
Thus this bound concerns the missing extension \(L/L_0\), not
the possibly much larger extension \(L/K\).[^3]

The uniform noninvertible formal family141 also illustrates the
algebraic degree mechanism when its explicitly verified source
field equality is retained: \(L_0=\mathbb C(F(k),\rho)\),
\(L=\mathbb C(k,\rho)\), \(d=1\), and
\(g_0=F(V)-F(k)\) has degree \(\deg F=[L:L_0]\).
That family is not an actual polynomial-coordinate frame and
is not evidence that this missing extension occurs in an
actual example.[^4]

In particular, take the formal polynomial \(P(T)=T^3+T\).
Then \(K=L_0=\mathbb C(k^3+k,\rho)\), \(L=\mathbb C(k,\rho)\),
and
\[
P(V)-P(k)=(V-k)(V^2+kV+k^2+1),\qquad
\operatorname{disc}_V(V^2+kV+k^2+1)=-3k^2-4 .
\tag{7}
\]
The discriminant has simple roots as a polynomial in \(k\),
so it is not a square in \(\mathbb C(\rho)(k)=L\): a square
rational function has even valuation at every irreducible factor.
The residual quadratic is therefore irreducible over \(L\).
Its component field is \(L'(U)\), where \(L'/L\) is the quadratic
constant extension obtained by adjoining a root of that quadratic.

Nevertheless \(\operatorname{Aut}_K(L)\) is trivial. If such
an automorphism sends \(k\) to a rational function \(R(k)\)
over \(\mathbb C(\rho)\), then \(P(R(k))=P(k)\). A finite pole
of \(R\) would remain a pole of its cubic composition, so
\(R\) is polynomial. Degrees force \(R=Ak+B\); comparing the
cubic, quadratic and linear coefficients gives \(A^3=1\),
\(B=0\), and \(A=1\).
Thus a trivial stabilizer alone does not exclude every formal
component: this residual component enlarges the constant field.
The example still fails the actual inverse premise.

The actual first-tail primitivity problem remains open here.
So do global polynomial descent and arbitrary coordinate entry.
The norm and degree arguments are elementary consequences of
the stated fields, not a novelty claim or a cancellation proof.
