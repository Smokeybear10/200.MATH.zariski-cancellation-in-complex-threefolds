# The quartic algebra still has a surface fiber

At a reduced actual second-image obstruction with zero first jet,
the algebra generated by the two quartic corrections has an exact
three-equation presentation. Its closed fiber is the product of
a finite nonempty scheme of length at most three with an affine
plane. It therefore remains strictly smaller than the actual
intrinsic ring.

The parallel fourth-image calculation identifies this algebra with
the algebra generated by the entire module \(F_4\). Thus in this
precise zero-first-jet sector, every generating set of the actual
ring requires intrinsic degree at least five. This is not an
existence assertion for an obstruction point, a statement about
rank-one first jets, or a degree-five generation theorem.

## 1. Exact zero-first-jet assumptions

Retain the actual setup of
[the quartic correction theorem](triple-point-quartic-corrections-pass51.md):
\[
R=C_{\mathfrak m},\qquad \mathfrak n=\mathfrak mR=(a,b),\qquad
\mathcal A=A\otimes_C R,\qquad L=\operatorname{Frac}R.
\tag{1}
\]
The parameters \(a,b\) generate the regular local surface ring's
maximal ideal, the actual \(\mathcal A\) is a faithfully flat
domain, and its closed fiber is nonempty and one-dimensional.
Assume
\[
JR=\mathfrak n,\qquad B_1,B_2\in\mathfrak n^2.
\tag{2}
\]
The second condition is the zero-first-jet premise. It holds in
the ordinary-triple sector established in pass 51, but is not
automatic at every reduced obstruction point.

The actual degree-two module and conic data are
\[
F_2=R\oplus Rz\oplus Rw,\quad r=az+bw,\quad
Dr=p,\quad \epsilon\in R^*,
\]
\[
Dz=\epsilon br+B_1,\qquad
Dw=-\epsilon ar+B_2,\qquad p=aB_1+bB_2,
\]
\[
\epsilon r^2-2B_2z+2B_1w-Q=0.
\tag{3}
\]
Faithful-flat contraction gives \(Q\in\mathfrak n^2\).
Choose a coherent quadratic decomposition
\[
B_1=a^2c+ab d+b^2e,\quad
B_2=a^2f+ab g+b^2h,\quad
Q=a^2q_1+abq_2+b^2q_3
\tag{4}
\]
with all displayed coefficients in \(R=\ker D\).

Define polynomials in \(R[Z,W]\)
\[
\begin{split}
Q_{aa}&=\epsilon Z^2-2fZ+2cW-q_1,\\
Q_{ab}&=2\epsilon ZW-2gZ+2dW-q_2,\\
Q_{bb}&=\epsilon W^2-2hZ+2eW-q_3.
\end{split}
\tag{5}
\]
The conic is precisely
\[
a^2Q_{aa}(z,w)+abQ_{ab}(z,w)+b^2Q_{bb}(z,w)=0.
\tag{6}
\]

## 2. Actual quartics and elimination of the cubic generator

The two actual regular-sequence divisions from pass 51 give
\[
U=T_a=\frac{Q_{bb}(z,w)}a\in\mathcal A,\qquad
V=T_b=\frac{Q_{aa}(z,w)}b\in\mathcal A.
\tag{7}
\]
The fractions in (7) specify already proved polynomial elements;
their existence is not inferred from a presentation alone.
Their derivatives are
\[
D^4U=6\epsilon^3ap^2,\qquad
D^4V=6\epsilon^3bp^2.
\tag{8}
\]
Substituting (7) into (6), and cancelling \(ab\ne0\) in the
actual domain, gives the third exact equation
\[
aV+bU+Q_{ab}(z,w)=0.
\tag{9}
\]

Choose the cubic generator \(t\) with coherent old coefficients
\[
c_{\rm old}=ac+bd,\quad d_{\rm old}=be,\quad
e_{\rm old}=af+bg,\quad f_{\rm old}=bh,
\]
\[
q_{\rm old}=aq_1+bq_2,\qquad h_{\rm old}=bq_3.
\]
Its actual equations give
\[
bU=-t-\epsilon zw,\qquad
aV=t-\epsilon zw+2gz-2dw+q_2,
\]
and therefore
\[
bU-aV=-2t-2gz+2dw-q_2.
\tag{10}
\]
In particular \(t\) lies in the \(R\)-linear span of
\(1,z,w,U,V\), not merely in the algebra they generate.
A different choice of cubic generator differs from this one
by \(F_2\), after the common normalization of \(D^3t\).

For completeness, (10) also reconstructs the parallel result
in [the fourth-image note](fourth-image-boundary-pass52.md).
The previously proved alternatives were
\(I_4=p^2\mathfrak n\) or \(p^2R\). In the latter case choose
\(Y\in F_4\) with \(D^4Y=6\epsilon^3p^2\). Then
\[
U-aY,\ V-bY\in F_3,\qquad
bU-aV\in\mathfrak nF_3,
\]
contradicting the unit coefficient \(-2\) of \(t\) in (10),
since \(F_3=F_2\oplus Rt\). Hence
\[
I_4=p^2\mathfrak n,\qquad
F_4=F_2\oplus RU\oplus RV.
\tag{11}
\]
For the sum, match the fourth derivative of any element of
\(F_4\) using (8), then use (10) to remove the cubic remainder.
For directness, an \(F_2\)-valued relation \(AU+BV\) gives
\(aA+bB=0\), so \(A=bk,\ B=-ak\). Applying \(D^3\) to
(10) forces \(k=0\).

This fourth-image and basis argument was found in the parallel
investigation and independently reconstructed here. It identifies
the algebra to be studied as
\[
K_4=R[F_3,U,V]=R[z,w,U,V]=R[F_4].
\tag{12}
\]

## 3. The exact quartic presentation

In the polynomial ring
\[
S=R[Z,W,\mathsf U,\mathsf V]
\]
put
\[
J_4=
\bigl(
 b\mathsf V-Q_{aa},\
 a\mathsf V+b\mathsf U+Q_{ab},\
 a\mathsf U-Q_{bb}
\bigr).
\tag{13}
\]
Equations (7), (9) give a natural surjection
\(S/J_4\to K_4\subset\mathcal A\).
We now prove it is injective.

### The actual generic and divisor localizations

After inverting \(a\), the third equation solves
\(\mathsf U=Q_{bb}/a\), and the middle equation solves
\[
\mathsf V=-\frac{bQ_{bb}+aQ_{ab}}{a^2}.
\]
The first remaining equation is a nonzero scalar multiple of
the conic (6). Interchanging \(a,b\) gives the same conclusion
after inverting \(b\).

At each height-one prime of \(R\), one of \(a,b\) is a unit.
The full quadratic divisor theorem therefore gives
\[
(S/J_4)_{(\pi)}=K_{4,(\pi)}=\mathcal A_{(\pi)}
\quad\text{for every height-one base prime},
\]
\[
(S/J_4)_L=L[r].
\tag{14}
\]
These are equalities of the actual embedded algebras. In particular
the divisor localizations are domains in which the base prime
remains nonzero. The generic ideal in the four-variable
polynomial ring over \(L\) has height three.

### The raw closed fiber is already two-dimensional

Let
\[
S_0=\mathbb C[Z,W]/
  (\overline Q_{aa},\overline Q_{ab},\overline Q_{bb}).
\tag{15}
\]
The three leading quadratic terms are
\(\bar\epsilon Z^2,2\bar\epsilon ZW,\bar\epsilon W^2\).
Thus every quadratic monomial reduces to the span of \(1,Z,W\).
Induction on monomial degree gives
\[
\dim_{\mathbb C}S_0\le3.
\]
This algebra is nonzero: the actual residues \(\bar z,\bar w\)
satisfy its three equations by (7), (9), and
\(\mathcal A/\mathfrak n\mathcal A\ne0\).
Consequently
\[
1\le\dim_{\mathbb C}S_0\le3,\qquad
S/(J_4+\mathfrak nS)=S_0[\mathsf U,\mathsf V].
\tag{16}
\]
Every prime containing \(J_4+\mathfrak nS\) has height at
least four in \(S\): the base parameters have height two,
and the finite nonempty equations in \(Z,W\) add two more.
This conclusion does not discard nilpotents in \(S_0\).

### No vertical minimal or embedded component survives

Every minimal prime of the three-generated ideal \(J_4\)
has height at most three. Equation (16) therefore excludes
contraction to \(\mathfrak n\). Contraction to a height-one
base prime is excluded by (14): after that localization a
minimal prime is the unique generic prime of the stated domain
and cannot contain the base prime.

All minimal primes thus contract to zero. The generic domain
in (14) gives exactly one such prime \(P\), of height three.
In each regular local ring along \(V(J_4)\), its three
equations form a regular sequence, since they cut out
codimension three. Hence \(S/J_4\) is Cohen--Macaulay there
and has no embedded associated primes. These standard inputs
are [Stacks 02JN](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/02JN),
its [proof input 00N6](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00N6),
and [Stacks 0BUS](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0BUS);
their statements and proofs were directly read.

It follows that \(P\) is the only associated prime.
Every nonzero element of \(R\) is outside \(P\), so multiplication
by it is injective on \(S/J_4\). Therefore
\[
S/J_4\hookrightarrow(S/J_4)_L=L[r].
\tag{17}
\]
The natural map into \(\mathcal A\) is generically injective,
so (17) makes it injective globally. We have proved the exact
presentation and saturation statement
\[
\boxed{
K_4=
R[Z,W,\mathsf U,\mathsf V]/J_4,\qquad
J_4=J_4:(R\setminus\{0\})^\infty.
}
\tag{18}
\]
No flatness of \(K_4\) has been presumed. In particular this is
not the operation of removing a vertical component by declaration;
the three actual equations have no such component.

## 4. The full \(F_4\) algebra cannot be the actual ring

Reduce the proved presentation (18):
\[
\boxed{
K_4/\mathfrak nK_4
=S_0[\mathsf U,\mathsf V],\qquad
1\le\dim_{\mathbb C}S_0\le3.
}
\tag{19}
\]
It is the product of the finite scheme \(\operatorname{Spec}S_0\)
with \(\mathbb A^2\). Equivalently its coordinate ring is finite
free of rank \(\dim_{\mathbb C}S_0\) over
\(\mathbb C[\mathsf U,\mathsf V]\).
This describes the full scheme, including any nilpotent
thickening, and its dimension is two.

The actual fiber \(\mathcal A/\mathfrak n\mathcal A\) has
dimension one. Thus, with all hypotheses (1)--(2) retained,
\[
\boxed{R[F_4]=K_4\ne\mathcal A.}
\tag{20}
\]
Every generating set of \(\mathcal A\) over \(R\) needs an
element of intrinsic \(D\)-degree at least five. The actual
fiber map has nonzero kernel:
\[
\mathfrak n\mathcal A\cap K_4
\supsetneq\mathfrak nK_4.
\tag{21}
\]
Relations created by still higher actual elements remain necessary.

The finite algebra \(S_0\) in (19) is the entire three-equation
quotient (15). Its image in the actual fiber can be smaller.
In particular (19) does not identify the actual fiber with a
union of planes, or bound that fiber's connected components.

## 5. Scope of the obstruction

The zero-first-jet condition in (2) is essential to this proof:
it supplies the coherent quadratic decomposition, the actual
quartics, and the finite algebra \(S_0\). A reduced obstruction
with rank-one first jet is not covered. Second-image obstruction
points lying at ordinary triples of the full plinth are covered:
pass 51 proved both reducedness of \(J\) and vanishing of the
first jets at those obstruction points.

Equations (18)--(20) show that adjoining these quartics does
not close the actual algebra even when their fourth image and
full module are now known exactly. They neither construct an
actual obstruction point nor rule it out by themselves.
No degree-five generation theorem, unrestricted cancellation
claim, or numerical verification of the universal commutative
algebra is made. Every file from pass 51 and earlier remains frozen.

The standalone
[quartic_fiber_gate_checks_pass52.py](quartic_fiber_gate_checks_pass52.py)
contains two exact methods. They verify both generic divisor
eliminations and the conic numerator, then independently compute
saturation at \(a\) and \(b\) in two bounded coefficient models.
The models have respectively a reduced length-one and a nonreduced
length-three algebra \(S_0\); they are not asserted to realize
an unresolved actual frame.

The first complete run passed two methods in 0.093 seconds,
exit zero, with no failed assertions. Only unittest and SymPy
are imported. These finite identities do not verify the universal
complete-intersection argument, the full fourth-image theorem,
or the actual fiber-dimension contradiction.
