# The remaining square class is confined to embedded critical points

In the actual rank-one quadratic sliced four-variable setting, the
acceleration squared belongs to the good-speed ideal away from a finite
set of embedded points of its three-variable specialization. Each
possible exceptional point has zero full Jacobian matrix for the three
specialized good speeds and analytic spread three. In particular, no
embedded maximal associated primes implies square membership.

This is a conditional improvement of the proved cubic bound. The
original action is not shown to exclude those embedded critical points,
and no coordinate reduction or kernel recognition is claimed.

## 1. Exact inherited setup

Use actual coordinates and the supplied slice, without an adjoint-frame
assumption:
\[
B=\mathbb C[x_1,x_2,x_3,z],\qquad E\in\operatorname{LND}(B),
\qquad E(s)=1,\qquad E^2x_i=0,\quad E^2z=q,\quad E(q)=0.
\tag{1}
\]
Put a_i=E(x_i), I=(a_1,a_2,a_3)B and R=C[x_1,x_2,x_3].
The zero and unit ideals give q=0 and immediate membership,
respectively. In the remaining case use
\[
J=I\cap R=(f_1,f_2,f_3)R,\qquad f_i=a_i(x,0),\qquad
I=J[z],\qquad q_0=q(x,0),\qquad q-q_0\in I.
\tag{2}
\]
The exact ideal extension is the pass82
[divisorial result](rank-one-acceleration-divisorial-ideal-pass82.md).
The pass83 [return-map proof](rank-one-return-map-integral-closure-pass83.md)
gives q_0 integral over J and q_0 cubed in J. Its combination with the
[socle characterization](rank-one-cubic-acceleration-socle-pass83.md)
excludes isolated points of V(J). These statements retain the entire
scheme, including embedded associated primes. In particular, a maximal
associated prime of R/J, if any, is embedded, not a minimal prime.

Consider the cyclic submodule
\[
M=R\,\overline{q_0^2}\ \subseteq R/J,
\qquad M\simeq R/(J:q_0^2).
\tag{3}
\]
By (2), M=0 is equivalent to q squared belonging to I.

## 2. The precise local Briancon--Skoda applications

The source is Lipman--Sathaye,
[*Jacobian ideals and a theorem of Briancon--Skoda*](https://www.math.purdue.edu/~jlipman/papers-older/%5B1981%5D%20Jacobian%20ideals%20and%20a%20theorem%20of%20Briancon-Skoda.pdf),
Theorem 1 on printed p.199, together with the minimal-reduction
discussion on pp.203--204. The first theorem applies in a normal
Noetherian domain with an auxiliary ideal U whose associated graded
ring is regular. For an ideal K=U+(h_1,...,h_(d+1)), it gives
overline(K^(d+lambda)) contained in K^lambda, for d>=0 and lambda>=1.
The square applications use d=1 and lambda=1; the supplementary
linear-rank refinement also uses d=0 or d=2.

First let p contain J with height at most two. Set D=R_p and K=JD.
The residue field of D is infinite, and D is a regular local ring of
dimension at most two. Its ideal K admits a reduction K' generated by
at most two elements. To specify this gate, the source's p.203
discussion provides a reduction with analytic-spread-many generators
over an infinite residue field. The analytic spread is at most dim D.
For this nonzero ideal in a domain, the usual geometric bound follows
from the blowup: the closed fiber lies in the effective Cartier
divisor defined by the invertible ideal, in a blowup of dimension
dim D. Its dimension is thus at most dim D minus one; analytic spread
is one plus that fiber dimension.

Being a reduction means K is integral over K'. Hence q_0 is integral
over K' as well. Apply Theorem 1 with auxiliary ideal zero and retain
a zero second generator if necessary. The regularity hypothesis is
then simply regularity of D. The square of an ideal-integral element
is integral over the square ideal, by the elementary Rees-ring power
property used in the return-map proof. This gives
\[
q_0^2\in\overline{(K')^2}\subseteq K'\subseteq K=JR_{\mathfrak p}
\qquad(\operatorname{ht}\mathfrak p\le2).
\tag{4}
\]
The same proof applies at a maximal ideal m whenever the analytic
spread of JR_m is at most two. This is a local spread condition,
not something inferred merely from dim V(J)>0.

There is a separate direct application at a complex maximal ideal m
when some f_i has a nonzero linear term there. Permute the three
generators so that t=f_1 has order one in D=R_m. Then t is part of a
regular system of parameters, D/(t) is regular, and
gr_(t) D is the polynomial ring (D/(t))[T], hence regular. Apply
Theorem 1 directly with auxiliary ideal U=(t), the remaining two
generators f_2,f_3, d=1 and lambda=1. It gives
\[
q_0^2\in\overline{(JD)^2}\subseteq JD
\quad\text{if }\operatorname{Jac}(f_1,f_2,f_3)(\mathfrak m)\ne0.
\tag{5}
\]
Here a nonzero matrix means at least one entry is nonzero, not a
nonzero determinant. Since the f_i generate J and vanish at m, this
matrix tests exactly whether J has a nonzero linear part in m/m
squared. No independence of all three differentials is assumed.

More generally, at a closed speed zero with linear rank
r=rank Jac(f)(m), constant linear combinations of the three generators
give r independent regular parameters t_1,...,t_r. Taking the auxiliary
ideal U=(t_1,...,t_r) and retaining the other 3-r generators applies
Theorem 1 with d=2-r. For r=0,1,2 it gives q_0^(3-r) in JR_m.
Thus rank at least two gives first-power membership locally. Rank
three would make J m-primary, contradicting the already proved
absence of isolated speed zeros, and no negative d is used.
For rank two there is also a direct check: the remaining generator
in the one-dimensional regular local domain D/(t_1,t_2) must be zero,
since otherwise J would be m-primary. Hence J=(t_1,t_2) is locally
radical, and the inherited radical membership alone gives q_0 in J.

The source's subsequent analytic-spread reformulations motivate these
two applications, but the proof above uses Theorem 1 only with the
nonnegative exponent parameters d=0,1,2. No possibly negative expression
for a spread correction is substituted into a power of an ideal.

## 3. Finite embedded critical support and the conditional conclusion

Equation (4) makes M vanish at every nonmaximal prime of R. A finite
module has support equal to the union of the closed sets of its
associated primes, and Ass(M) is contained in Ass(R/J), since M is a
submodule. Thus every associated prime of M is maximal. There are
finitely many, and Supp(M)=Ass(M) is a finite set of closed points.

At any such point, (5) forces the full specialized Jacobian to be
zero. The reduction argument following (4) forces analytic spread
three. Finally, absence of isolated V(J) components makes every such
maximal associated prime embedded. Consequently
\[
\begin{gathered}
\operatorname{Supp}M\subseteq
\left\{\mathfrak m\in\operatorname{Ass}(R/J):
\begin{array}{l}
\operatorname{ht}\mathfrak m=3,\quad \mathfrak m\text{ embedded},\\
\ell(JR_{\mathfrak m})=3,\quad
\operatorname{Jac}(f)(\mathfrak m)=0
\end{array}\right\},\\[3pt]
\operatorname{Ass}(R/J)\text{ has no embedded maximal primes}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad q^2\in I.
\end{gathered}
\tag{6}
\]
In particular, an unmixed J satisfies the conditional conclusion,
because the earlier isolated-point exclusion prevents maximal minimal
primes. More generally, it suffices that every embedded maximal
associated prime have a nonzero linear part of J, or have analytic
spread at most two. No equality with the displayed exceptional set is
asserted: a point satisfying those necessary conditions need not
support a nonzero square class.

The ambient square-defect module is the polynomial extension of M
in the original coordinate z. Thus finite support in (6) refers to
the three-variable good-coordinate scheme. Its possible support in
the full four-variable speed scheme consists of the corresponding
z-lines, not isolated ambient points.

## 4. What this does and does not settle

This places the remaining square-membership question at embedded
critical points, after the separate pass83 proof already excluded
isolated components. These notions are not interchangeable.
A three-generated ideal in a regular three-variable ring need not
be unmixed or perfect merely because its zero set is positive
dimensional. No actual-flow proof of that additional condition is
supplied here, and no action with a nonzero square class is constructed.

The primary Theorem 1, its deduction from Theorem 1-prime on p.199,
and the complete analytic-spread/minimal-reduction discussion on
pp.203--204 were visually read for this application. The core
Lipman--Sathaye Theorem 2 proofs in the later sections, and the original
minimal-reduction reference cited on p.203, were not freshly audited.
This is an explicit published-theorem application with its inherited
gates, not a new proof of Briancon--Skoda.
