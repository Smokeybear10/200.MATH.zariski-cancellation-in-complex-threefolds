# A pole of the slice difference is fixed by the original last partial

The actual two-relative-generator frame imposes a further restriction
on possible poles of its slice difference. Every pole comes from an
irreducible divisor of the invariant plane, and that divisor is fixed
by the original coordinate derivative \(\partial_z\).

The key input beyond normality and the generator pencil is the
original four-coordinate reconstruction. On the relevant whole
divisor fiber, its free parameter derivative becomes the original
\(\partial_z\). This is not an assumption that the smaller
three-ring maps surjectively to the kernel.

## 1. Actual frame and generic polynomial membership

Use the two-generator construction of
[conical-normal89](constant-acceleration-conical-normal-pass89.md).
The argument below only needs its resulting actual identities:
\[
\begin{gathered}
S=\mathbb C[s_1,s_2]=\ker(W|A),\qquad
B=\mathbb C[x_1,x_2,x_3,z]=A[u],\qquad Wu=0,\quad Eu=1,\\
x_{\rm tr}=b\sigma+VY+U_0c_0,\qquad
b,V,U_0\in S^3,\quad [b,V,U_0]\in\operatorname{GL}_3(S),
\quad c_0\in S,\\
A=S[Y,c],\qquad R=S[Y],\qquad h=u-\sigma\in A\cap\operatorname{Frac}(R),
\qquad z=c+\frac q2(u^2-h^2),\quad q\in\mathbb C^*.
\end{gathered}
\tag{1}
\]
Here \(E\) and \(W\) are the actual commuting LNDs from the
invariant-plane construction. In particular \(S\) is their common
kernel on \(B\), and \(S\to A\to B\) are faithfully flat.
Assume \(Y\) is transcendental over \(K_0=\operatorname{Frac}(S)\).
The complementary case already gives \(Y\in S\) and \(A=S[c]\).
Thus \(R\) in the current case is a normal polynomial three-ring.

A local slice of \(W|A\) gives \(A_{K_0}=K_0[v]\).
The actual element \(Y\) is a nonconstant polynomial in \(v\).
Its leading coefficient belongs to the field \(K_0^*\), so its
defining expression gives a monic equation for \(v\) over \(K_0[Y]\).
Hence \(K_0[v]\) is integral over \(K_0[Y]\).
Since \(h\) lies both in \(K_0[v]\) and in \(K_0(Y)\), normality
of \(K_0[Y]\) proves
\[
h\in K_0[Y].
\quad\text{Consequently every height-one pole of \(h\) on \(R\)
is \(pR\) for an irreducible \(p\in S\).}
\tag{2}
\]
For the second assertion, clear the finitely many coefficient
denominators of this polynomial in \(Y\). A denominator lies in
\(S\); its irreducible factors remain prime in \(S[Y]\).
This is not a claim that \(A\) is integral over \(R\) globally.

The [universal-pencil proof88](universal-generator-pencil-pass88.md)
also gives the whole tensor identity
\[
h\otimes1-1\otimes h
\text{ is nilpotent in }A\otimes_R A.
\tag{3}
\]
We use this before reduction, so that it survives the particular
base change below. Agreement only at selected closed points would
not suffice.

## 2. The whole divisor fiber is a polynomial line over a finite field

Suppose \(pR\) is a pole in (2). Put \(D=S_{(p)}\) and
\(k=\operatorname{Frac}(S/(p))\), the residue field of this DVR.
The coefficient valuation description of (2) gives
\[
p^n h=f(Y),\qquad n\ge1,\qquad f\in D[Y],\qquad
\overline f\ne0\text{ in }k[Y].
\tag{4}
\]
Here \(n\) removes the full negative coefficient valuation.
Since \(h\in A\), reduction in \(A_D/pA_D\) shows that
\(\overline Y\) is algebraic over \(k\).

The required whole fiber is a domain. Indeed the common kernel
\(S=\ker E\cap\ker W\) is factorially closed in the UFD \(B\):
apply the factorial-closure property of an LND kernel to both
derivations. A nonconstant irreducible \(p\in S\) therefore remains
irreducible, hence prime, in \(B\). Its contraction and the
presentation \(B=A[u]\) make \(pA\) prime as well. The localization
does not kill that quotient; for example faithful-flat contraction
gives \(pA\cap S=pS\).

The surjection \(S[Y,C]\to A\), sending \(C\) to \(c\), has a
height-one prime kernel: its source has transcendence degree four
and its target has transcendence degree three. Since the source
is a UFD, its kernel is generated by an irreducible polynomial \(F\).
After localization and then reduction modulo \(p\), we have
\[
A_D=D[Y,C]/(F),\qquad
A_D/pA_D=k[Y,C]/(\overline F)=k'[c],
\qquad k'=k[\overline Y]\text{ a finite field extension of }k.
\tag{5}
\]
To justify the last equality without a reduced-fiber assumption,
the quotient is already a nonzero domain by the preceding paragraph.
Its defining polynomial \(\overline F\) is neither zero nor a unit:
zero would leave \(\overline Y\) transcendental, contrary to (4),
and a unit would make the fiber empty. The prime principal ideal
\((\overline F)\) contains the nonzero polynomial \(\overline f(Y)\).
Thus \(\overline F\) is, up to a nonzero scalar, an irreducible
polynomial in \(Y\) alone. The variable \(c\) remains free.
The extension \(k'/k\) is separable in characteristic zero, but
it need not be trivial.

## 3. Tensor nilpotence makes the slice difference constant on that line

The map \(R_D=D[Y]\to k'\) just described sends \(p\) to zero
and \(Y\) to its residue class. From (5),
\(A_D\otimes_{R_D}k'=k'[c]\).
Base change of (3) now places the difference of the two images
of \(h\) in the polynomial domain \(k'[c_1,c_2]\).
A nilpotent element of that domain is zero. A polynomial in one
variable whose values in the two independent variables agree is
constant. Consequently
\[
\overline h\in k',\qquad
B_D/pB_D=k'[c,u],\qquad
\partial_c(\overline{x_i})=0\ (i\le3),\qquad
\partial_c(\overline z)=1.
\tag{6}
\]
For the last two assertions, specialize (1).
Every coefficient from \(S\), the element \(\overline Y\), and
\(\overline h\) belong to \(k'\). Thus
\(\overline\sigma=u-\overline h\), the three reconstructed good
coordinates are independent of \(c\), and
\(\overline z=c+q(u^2-\overline h^2)/2\).
This uses the exact actual reconstruction, not an arbitrary
polynomial presentation \(A=S[Y,c]\).

Apply \(\partial_c\) to the image of any polynomial in the four
original coordinates. By (6), its result is the image of its
ordinary original \(z\)-derivative. In particular the image of
the polynomial \(p\) is zero, so the image of \(\partial_zp\)
is zero. We obtain \(\partial_zp\in pB_D\). The prime ideal \(pB\)
is disjoint from the denominators \(S\setminus(p)\), hence contracts
back from this localization. Finally the actual coordinate
derivative is locally nilpotent, and self-divisibility forces
\[
\boxed{\quad
\operatorname{ord}_{pR}(h)<0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\partial_zp\in pB
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\partial_zp=0.
\quad}
\tag{7}
\]
Equivalently the last implication follows by comparing the
ordinary \(z\)-degree of a nonzero derivative with that of its
nonzero polynomial multiple. Thus every possible pole is a
nonconstant polynomial fixed by both \(E\) and the original
\(\partial_z\), and lies in the actual good-coordinate subring.

The same fiber argument works whenever \(\overline Y\) is algebraic
over \(k\), whether or not that algebraicity was obtained from a
pole. It does not assume that \(\overline Y\) is a base-field
scalar, that every divisor fiber is geometrically integral, or
that \(R\to A\) is faithfully flat.

## 4. Scope and provenance

Equation (7) narrows possible divisor collapse. It does not alone
prove that the common kernel of \(E\) and \(\partial_z\) is trivial,
that \(p\) is an actual coordinate, or that \(h\in R\).
Those require an additional argument using the speed geometry.
The rejected normal-ring pencil controls do not have this exact
highest-plane and original-coordinate reconstruction.

General derived the whole divisor-fiber and original-partial
argument, and Root independently reconstructed its outline and
requested the saved proof. The generic polynomial argument and
the tensor nilpotence input are separate from Root's valuation
criterion for simple poles of \(c\). This proof uses the retained
actual invariant-plane and pencil theorems; it adds no new primary
classification theorem or numerical method. Pass88 and earlier
files remain unchanged.
