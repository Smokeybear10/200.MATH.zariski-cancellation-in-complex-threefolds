Working exclusion: under the complete actual264 moving-slope chart and
the full normalized equations of441, the following cases cannot occur:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
f'''=6b &( \deg p,\deg x,\deg q)=(3,4,5),(4,5,6)\\
f'''\ne6b &( \deg p,\deg x,\deg q)=(3,4,5),(3,5,7).
\end{array}
\tag{1}
\]
The proof uses the entire genuine fourth equation, whole polynomial-line
generation and its evaluated gradient-minor UNIT. It is not a consequence

## 2. The primitive tangent lemma gives an exact degree

We repeat the elementary argument of
[454, Section3](principal-paraboloid-cubic-profile-parity-minor-exclusion-pass454-working.md)
for general degrees, without its quadratic normalization of p.
In this section a dot denotes ordinary differentiation in the
polynomial parameter v, with every element of L held fixed.
It is NOT a coefficient-field t- or s-derivative.

Whole generation O=L[x,p,q] gives a polynomial expression for v
in x,p,q. Differentiating that expression yields elements a1,a2,a3
of O such that
\[
a_1\dot x+a_2\dot p+a_3\dot q=1.
\tag{8}
\]
The tangent vector is therefore primitive and nonzero.

Let Cvec be the evaluated cross product of the formal gradients
of Hcal and Phi, in the variable order (x,p,q). Differentiating(7)
shows that both gradients annihilate the tangent vector. Their
cross product is nonzero by the minor-unit condition. Linear algebra
over Frac(O) gives Cvec=psi*(dot x,dot p,dot q) for some psi in
Frac(O). Equation(8) then gives
psi=a1*Cvec_1+a2*Cvec_2+a3*Cvec_3 in O.
The ideal generated by the components of Cvec is (psi), again by(8).
Consequently
\[
\psi\in O^*=L^*,\qquad
\mathbf C=\psi(\dot x,\dot p,\dot q).
\tag{9}
\]

The particular evaluated minor
\[
W=(2x\Phi_q-bp\Phi_x)_{\rm ev}
\tag{10}
\]
is MINUS the second cross-product component. Put a=deg_v p>0.
Characteristic zero and(9) give the exact, not merely upper, degree
\[
\boxed{W=-\psi\dot p,\qquad \deg_v W=a-1.}
\tag{11}
\]
This implication would not follow from nonvanishing of the minors
alone. It uses their unit ideal and whole polynomial-ring generation.

The complete support(4) gives
Phi_x=R+bq and Phi_q=bx+L0+2gamma*q. Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
2x\Phi_q-bp\Phi_x
&=2bx^2+2xL_0(p)+4\gamma xq-bpR(p)-b^2pq\\
&=3bx^2+2xL_0(p)+4\gamma xq-bpR(p)-bd-b\mathcal H.
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
\]
On the curve this is the exact identity
\[
\boxed{W=3bx^2+2xL_0(p)+4\gamma xq-bpR(p)-bd.}
\tag{13}
\]
The entire polynomial F0 drops out because both formal derivatives
in(10) kill it, not because its lower coefficients were discarded.

## 3. Coefficient vanishings licensed directly by the full support

Write c=deg_v x and e=deg_v q. For the four cases under consideration
all three generators are nonconstant. Their quadric leading terms
give 2c=a+e. The high degrees in the full level(4) are
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrr}
\text{term}&p^4&p^2q&q^2&p^2x&p^3&xq\\ \hline
\text{degree}&4a&2a+e&2e&2a+c&3a&c+e .
\end{array}
\tag{14}
\]
Other terms have degree at most max(a+c,2a,a+e). A coefficient is
removed only after its term is uniquely highest.

For (a,c,e)=(3,4,5), the delta*p^4 term has degree12, uniquely
highest, so delta=0. The epsilon*p^2*q term then has degree11,
uniquely highest, so epsilon=0. This holds in both regimes.

For the resonant (4,5,6) case, alpha=0. The degrees16 and14
of delta*p^4 and epsilon*p^2*q are successively uniquely highest.
Thus delta=epsilon=0 here as well. These are exactly the relevant
licenses in
[442, Section5](principal-paraboloid-moving-slope-resonance-degree-profiles-pass442-working.md);
no generic nonresonant coefficient argument is imported.

For the nonresonant (3,5,7) case, gamma*q^2 has degree14,
uniquely highest, so gamma=0. Next epsilon*p^2*q has degree13,
uniquely highest, so epsilon=0. The p^4 and xq terms may both
have degree12. No vanishing of delta is used.

## 4. The two resonant cases

Suppose alpha=0 and (a,c,e) is either (3,4,5) or (4,5,6).
Section3 gives epsilon=0. Consequently(13) becomes
\[
W=3bx^2+2c_{11}px+2c_{01}x
 +4\gamma xq-bu_1p^2-bd.
\tag{15}
\]
In both cases a<c<e. If gamma is nonzero, the xq term has
degree c+e, strictly greater than 2c, a+c, c and 2a.
It is uniquely highest with a nonzero leading coefficient.
This contradicts deg W=a-1.

Therefore gamma would have to be zero. But then 3b*x^2 is
uniquely highest, since 2c>a+c, c and 2a. Its leading coefficient
is nonzero because b is nonzero and the characteristic is zero.
Again deg W=2c>a-1, a contradiction.

For explicit comparison, the successive impossible degrees are
9 then8 for (3,4,5), and11 then10 for (4,5,6). This reasoning
retains the gamma=0 boundary directly; it does not divide by gamma,
b', ell_ss or any lower coefficient.

## 5. The nonresonant (3,5,7) case

Here alpha is nonzero and Section3 gives gamma=epsilon=0.
The full minor is
\[
W=3bx^2+2c_{11}px+2c_{01}x
 -b\alpha p^3-bu_1p^2-bd.
\tag{16}
\]
Its six displayed terms have respective degrees at most
10,8,5,9,6,0, with the first exactly10 and nonzero.
Thus deg W=10, contradicting(11), which requires degree2.
No coefficient of F0, including its potentially nonzero quartic
coefficient, enters this contradiction.

## 6. The nonresonant (3,4,5) case

This case needs the leading level identity, because two terms
of W could initially cancel. Section3 gives delta=epsilon=0.
In(4), the only terms of degree10 are alpha*p^2*x and gamma*q^2.
Since alpha is nonzero, the level identity first forces gamma
nonzero and gives their leading cancellation.

Put
\[
P_*=\operatorname{lc}_v p,\qquad
X_*=\operatorname{lc}_v x,\qquad
Q_*=\operatorname{lc}_v q.
\]
All three are in L*. The top parts of Hcal and Phi say
\[
X_*^2+bP_*Q_*=0,\qquad
\alpha P_*^2X_*+\gamma Q_*^2=0.
\tag{17}
\]
Eliminating Q* using nonzero b and P* gives
\[
Q_*=-\frac{X_*^2}{bP_*},\qquad
\gamma X_*^3=-\alpha b^2P_*^4.
\tag{18}
\]

The two potentially top terms of W are 4gamma*xq and
-b*alpha*p^3, both of degree9. All remaining terms in(13)
have degree at most8. Their combined degree9 coefficient is
\[
\begin{aligned}
4\gamma X_*Q_*-b\alpha P_*^3
&=-\frac{4\gamma X_*^3}{bP_*}-b\alpha P_*^3\\
&=3\alpha bP_*^3\ne0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{19}
\]
Thus deg W=9, whereas(11) requires degree2. The required
cancellation in the fourth level makes cancellation in this
minor impossible.
