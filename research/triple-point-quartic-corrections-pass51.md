# Ordinary triples force a reduced obstruction and actual quartic corrections

At an ordinary triple point of the full primitive plinth, an actual
second-image obstruction ideal must be the reduced maximal ideal.
Faithful flatness then supplies two elements of intrinsic degree four,
whose fourth derivatives generate \(p^2\mathfrak n\). The full fourth
image ideal is consequently either \(p^2\mathfrak n\) or \(p^2R\).

This does not exclude an ordinary triple point or prove generation by
degree-four elements. The divisions below take place in the actual
flat ring. They are not justified in an arbitrary conic subalgebra.

## 1. Actual hypotheses and invariant changes of basis

Retain the actual degree-two frame and primitive intrinsic derivation
from [the multi-tangent note](multitangent-plinth-boundary-pass50.md).
At a closed second-image obstruction point write
\[
R=C_{\mathfrak m},\qquad \mathfrak n=\mathfrak mR,
\qquad \mathcal A=A\otimes_C R,\qquad L=\operatorname{Frac}R.
\tag{1}
\]
Here \(C\simeq\mathbb C^{[2]}\), the original ring satisfies
\(A[s_*]\simeq\mathbb C^{[4]}\), and \(\mathcal A\) is a
faithfully flat domain over the regular local surface ring \(R\).
Put \(F_i=\ker D^{i+1}\) and \(I_i=D^i(F_i)\subset R\).
The inherited bundle and conic data are
\[
F_2=R\oplus Rz\oplus Rw,\quad r=az+bw,\quad JR=(a,b),
\quad \gcd(a,b)=1,
\tag{2}
\]
\[
Dz=\epsilon br+B_1,\qquad Dw=-\epsilon ar+B_2,
\qquad Dr=p=aB_1+bB_2,
\tag{3}
\]
\[
\epsilon r^2-2B_2z+2B_1w-Q=0,\qquad \epsilon\in R^*.
\tag{4}
\]
All of \(a,b,B_1,B_2,Q\) lie in \(\mathfrak n\). The ideal
\(JR\) is a proper \(\mathfrak n\)-primary complete intersection.
The element \(r\) has been translated by an invariant so that it
vanishes on the whole scheme-theoretic closed fiber.

The [actual normalized-cover trace input](nodal-plinth-obstruction-pass49.md)
says, on each normalized branch through this point,
\[
\operatorname{ord}(B_1)\geq\operatorname{ord}(b)+1
\tag{5}
\]
when \(b\) is not identically zero on its component. If it is
identically zero, then \(B_1\) is too. There is a companion
statement for \(B_2,a\). This uses the actual minimal polynomial,
prime exceptional pullback and reached normalized cover points.
No analogous assertion is assumed for a weak conic model.

The conic form is preserved by an invariant basis change. In column
notation put \(x=(z,w)^t\), \(\ell=(a,b)\),
\(B=(B_1,B_2)^t\), and
\(S=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\).
For \(M\in\mathrm{GL}_2(R)\), set
\[
x'=Mx,\quad \ell'=\ell M^{-1},\quad B'=MB,
\quad \epsilon'=\epsilon\det M,\quad Q'=Q\det M.
\tag{6}
\]
The identity \(MSM^t=(\det M)S\) proves (3) in the new
basis, while \(r=\ell'x'\) is unchanged. Multiplying (4) by
\(\det M\) gives its transformed version. In particular the
condition \(B_1,B_2\in\mathfrak n^2\) is preserved. Any two
minimal generator pairs of \(JR\) are related by such a basis
change: lift their change of basis modulo \(\mathfrak nJR\),
then use that its determinant is a unit in the local ring.

## 2. An ordinary triple forces \(JR=\mathfrak n\)

Assume the full element \(p\), not merely its reduced support,
has an ordinary triple point: its initial homogeneous form has
degree three and is a product of three distinct linear forms.
The actual multi-tangent theorem gives
\[
B_1,B_2\in\mathfrak n^2,\qquad
p\in\mathfrak n^2JR.
\tag{7}
\]
Thus \(JR\) contains an element \(a\) of order one. Choose a
complementary regular parameter \(b\). The ring \(R/(a)\) is a
DVR, so the nonzero proper image of \(JR\) is generated by
\(b^n\) for some \(n\geq1\). Lifting its unit coefficient and
subtracting a multiple of \(a\) gives
\[
JR=(a,b^n).
\tag{8}
\]
This is an equality in \(R\), not a completed-ring replacement.
Use (6) to arrange the actual conic in this generator pair.
Then its equations use \(r=az+b^nw\) and
\(p=aB_1+b^nB_2\).

Suppose \(n\geq2\). Write subscripts for homogeneous initial
parts in the regular parameters \(a,b\). Since both \(B_i\)
have order at least two, the ordinary cubic initial form is
\[
p_3=a_1(B_1)_2.
\tag{9}
\]
Squarefreeness means that \((B_1)_2\) is the product of the
other two tangent forms, neither proportional to \(a_1\).
On the smooth branch tangent to \(a=0\), the parameter \(b\)
has order one and \((B_1)_2\) has a nonzero \(b^2\) coefficient.
Consequently
\[
\operatorname{ord}(B_1)=2,\qquad
\operatorname{ord}(b^n)=n.
\tag{10}
\]
The branch can belong to an irreducible component having other
branches; (5) applies at this particular normalization point.
The function \(b\) is not identically zero on that component,
as it is a parameter on the chosen branch. Equation (5) now
requires \(2\geq n+1\), a contradiction. Therefore
\[
\boxed{\quad p\text{ ordinary triple and }JR\ne R
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad JR=\mathfrak n.\quad}
\tag{11}
\]
The only smooth-branch fact used here is the local meaning of an
ordinary multiple point: its distinct tangent factors give distinct
smooth normalization branches. No surjectivity of a conic model's
normalization is substituted for the actual input (5).

## 3. Two actual quartic divisions

For this section it suffices to assume \(JR=\mathfrak n=(a,b)\)
and \(B_1,B_2\in\mathfrak n^2\). This also covers any reduced
obstruction with at least two distinct tangent directions, without
requiring an ordinary triple. From (4), \(r\in\mathfrak n\mathcal A\)
and faithful-flat contraction give \(Q\in\mathfrak n^2\).

Modulo \(a\), each of \(B_1,B_2,Q\) is divisible by \(b^2\).
Choose invariant coefficients in \(R\) with
\[
B_i=aC_i+b^2\beta_i,\qquad Q=aQ_1+b^2q.
\tag{12}
\]
Define actual elements
\[
G_a=\epsilon a z^2+2\epsilon bzw-2C_2z+2C_1w-Q_1,
\qquad
H_a=\epsilon w^2-2\beta_2z+2\beta_1w-q.
\tag{13}
\]
Expanding (4) gives \(aG_a+b^2H_a=0\). Flatness preserves
the regular sequence \(a,b^2\): reduction modulo \(a\) and
cancellation of \(b^2\) force \(H_a\in a\mathcal A\).
Hence there is an actual element \(T_a\in\mathcal A\) with
\[
H_a=aT_a,\qquad G_a=-b^2T_a.
\tag{14}
\]
In particular this assertion includes removal of the denominator
\(a\) inside the given algebra, not only in its fraction field.

The derivative identities \(D^2z=\epsilon bp\),
\(D^2w=-\epsilon ap\), \(D^3z=D^3w=0\) give
\[
D^4H_a=6\epsilon(D^2w)^2=6\epsilon^3a^2p^2,
\quad D^5H_a=0.
\]
Since \(a\) is invariant and nonzero,
\[
\boxed{T_a\in F_4,\qquad D^4T_a=6\epsilon^3ap^2.}
\tag{15}
\]

For the other parameter choose
\(B_i=a^2\gamma_i+bE_i\) and \(Q=a^2q'+bQ_2\).
Expanding (4) now gives
\[
a^2H_b+bG_b=0,
\]
\[
H_b=\epsilon z^2-2\gamma_2z+2\gamma_1w-q',\qquad
G_b=2\epsilon azw+\epsilon bw^2-2E_2z+2E_1w-Q_2.
\tag{16}
\]
The regular sequence \(b,a^2\) supplies \(H_b=bT_b\) with
\(T_b\in\mathcal A\), and the same calculation gives
\[
\boxed{T_b\in F_4,\qquad D^4T_b=6\epsilon^3bp^2.}
\tag{17}
\]
Both elements have degree exactly four, since their displayed
fourth derivatives are nonzero.

## 4. The exact remaining fourth-image alternative

The full quadratic divisor presentations give
\(I_4R_{(\pi)}=p^2R_{(\pi)}\) for every height-one base prime.
For example, after invariant shifts and unit rescaling the local
basis through degree four is \(1,T,Y,TY,Y^2\), with
\(DT=p\), \(DY=2T\), \(pY=T^2-\eta\). Its last fourth
derivative is \(D^4(Y^2)=24p^2\); all earlier basis elements
have zero fourth derivative. At primes where \(p\) is a unit,
the ordinary polynomial-line basis gives the same ideal.

Every element of \(I_4\subset R\) is therefore divisible by
\(p^2\) at every height-one prime. UFD contraction gives
\(I_4\subset p^2R\). Equations (15), (17) give the reverse
lower bound
\[
p^2\mathfrak n\subset I_4\subset p^2R.
\tag{18}
\]
The ideal \(p^{-2}I_4\subset R\) contains the maximal ideal,
so there are exactly two possibilities:
\[
\boxed{\qquad I_4=p^2\mathfrak n\quad\text{or}\quad I_4=p^2R.\qquad}
\tag{19}
\]
The calculations do not choose between them.

The [full cubic non-generation theorem](cubic-generation-obstruction-pass51.md)
separately proves that \(R[F_3]\ne\mathcal A\) at every
reduced obstruction. Thus (15), (17) supply actual higher-degree
elements outside the module \(F_3\). Their nonmembership in the
algebra \(R[F_3]\) has not been proved here. No assertion says
they generate the whole algebra or eliminate its closed-fiber defect.

## 5. Why actual flatness is essential

In the weaker ordinary-triple conic from pass 50,
\(B_1=-a^2\), \(B_2=b^2\), \(Q=-ab+a^3\), \(\epsilon=1\).
The two proposed divisions would read
\[
T_a=(w^2-2z)/a,\qquad T_b=(z^2-2w-a)/b.
\tag{20}
\]
They do not belong to that weak ring merely because the conic
relation holds. Modulo \(a\), its relation is \(b^2(w^2-2z)\);
the class \(w^2-2z\) is nonzero. Modulo \(b\), the relation is
\(a^2(z^2-2w-a)\), and the second numerator is nonzero there.
These failures are precisely where regular-sequence cancellation
cannot be applied. The actual argument (14), (16) is stronger
than saturating away a chosen vertical component without proof.

The original polynomial-cylinder, quadratic-divisor and normalized
cover hypotheses are retained throughout Sections 1–4. No actual
ordinary-triple realization is constructed, no unrestricted
cancellation or historical novelty claim is made, and all files
from passes 50 and earlier remain unchanged.

## Finite verification boundary

The standalone [three-method checker](triple_point_quartic_checks_pass51.py)
independently checks both conic expansions and all fourth/fifth
derivatives with full formal quadratic coefficients, the local
divisor basis and invariant basis-change identity, and the failure
of both divisions in the inherited weak triple conic. The first
complete run passed three methods in 0.200 seconds, exit zero,
with no failed assertions. Only unittest and SymPy are imported.
No actual unresolved-frame example, universal flatness, normalized
cover theorem or fourth-degree generation claim is tested.
