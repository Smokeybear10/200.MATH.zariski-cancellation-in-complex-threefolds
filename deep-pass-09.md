# Central arrangements and separated polynomial slices

The general complex three-dimensional cancellation problem remains
unresolved in this investigation:
\[
X\times\mathbb A^1_{\mathbb C}\cong\mathbb A^4_{\mathbb C}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
X\cong\mathbb A^3_{\mathbb C}.
\]
Two further families now have complete written arguments, with separate
proof reconstructions. Neither family is known here to contain every
slice arising from the cancellation problem.

The first family has a coefficient consisting of any finite collection
of concurrent lines, with arbitrary positive multiplicities. The second
is a separated polynomial sum whose three-variable remainder is binomial
in one variable. Their proofs use existing theorems, credited below.
No novelty, formal verification, or human expert review is claimed.

## 1. Precise conclusions

All rings and varieties in this report are over \(\mathbb C\).
A stable coordinate is a polynomial that belongs to a polynomial
coordinate system after finitely many independent variables are added.
An original slice satisfies \(D(F)=1\) for a locally nilpotent
derivation of the original four-variable polynomial ring.

| Presentation | Hypothesis | Conclusion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| \(F=(\prod_i\ell_i^{m_i})u+H(x,y,z)\), distinct concurrent lines \(\ell_i\), \(m_i\ge1\) | Original LND slice | \(F\) is a coordinate; the kernel is \(\mathbb C^{[3]}\) |
| \(F=h(s)+f(x,y)z^q+g(x,y)\), \(\deg h\ge2\), \(f\ne0\), \(q\ge1\) | Stable coordinate | \(f(x,y)z^q+g(x,y)\) and \(F\) are coordinates |
| \(F=xu^n+G(x,y,z)\), \(n\ge2\) | Stable coordinate | \(G(0,y,z)\) is a plane coordinate; the cyclic quotient polynomial \(xw+G\) is a coordinate |

The third row is a necessary condition, not a completed coordinate
classification. An explicit smooth quartic below shows why that
condition alone is insufficient.

## 2. Central-arrangement coefficients

Take
\[
P=\prod_{i=0}^{d-1}\ell_i^{m_i},\qquad F=Pu+H(x,y,z),
\]
where the \(\ell_i\) are distinct nonproportional homogeneous linear
forms in \(x,y\), \(d\ge2\), and all \(m_i\ge1\). Translation handles
a common intersection point other than the origin. There is no bound
on the number of lines, their multiplicities, or the degree of \(H\).

The [complete proof](research/central-arrangement-slice-pass.md)
extends the preceding two-line argument. Its essential topological
step is not a dimension count. It computes the image of an integral
residue map.

Assume first that \(F\) is stable. A fiber \(X_\lambda\) is smooth
and contractible. Away from \(P=0\), one solves uniquely for \(u\).
Choose \(\ell_0=x\), \(\ell_j=y-\alpha_jx\). The open part is
\[
U\cong\mathbb C^*\times
\bigl(\mathbb C\setminus\{\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_{d-1}\}\bigr)
\times\mathbb A^1.
\]
The closed part is \(C\times\mathbb A^1\), where \(C\) is the
union of the plane curves cut out by \(H=\lambda\) on the coefficient
planes. At a common generic regular value, compactly supported
cohomology forces
\[
H_c^1(C;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z^{d-1},
\qquad H_c^2(C;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z^d.
\tag{1}
\]
In particular every plane contributes one connected smooth curve.

### Nonconstant restriction to the common axis

If \(h(z)=H(0,0,z)\) has degree \(e>0\), the curves meet at
the same \(e\) points. The normalization sequence contributes
\(\mathbb Z^{e(d-1)}\) to \(H_c^1(C)\). Equation (1) forces
\(e=1\) and makes every curve an affine line.

Plane-line rectification, together with the linear restriction on the
common axis, gives the exact form
\[
H=az+B(x,y)+Q(x,y)R(x,y,z),\qquad
a\ne0,\quad Q=\prod_i\ell_i.
\tag{2}
\]
Conversely (2) makes \(F\) a coordinate. Put \(N=\max_i m_i\).
Since \(Q^N\) is divisible by \(P\), a finite inverse modulo \(P\)
is obtained from
\[
I_0(T)=\frac{T-B}{a},\qquad
I_{j+1}(T)=\frac{T-B-QR(x,y,I_j(T))}{a}.
\]
After \(N-1\) updates, both inverse congruences hold. The companion
and inverse coordinates are
\[
V=\frac{z-I(F)}P,\qquad
z=I(T)+PV,\qquad
u=\frac{T-H(x,y,I(T)+PV)}P.
\tag{3}
\]
The numerators are divisible by \(P\); the full proof checks both
compositions. This branch proves coordinatehood already from stability.

### Constant restriction to the common axis

If \(h=b\), the generic curves are disjoint and avoid the common
axis. The integral Gysin map is an isomorphism
\[
H^2(U;\mathbb Z)\longrightarrow
\bigoplus_i H^1(C_i;\mathbb Z).
\tag{4}
\]
In an explicit integral basis, its columns have the form
\((\eta_0,0,\ldots,-\eta_j,\ldots,0)\), where \(\eta_i\) is
the circle class pulled back by the radial coordinate on \(C_i\).
Surjectivity forces each individual group \(H^1(C_i)\) to be
generated by \(\eta_i\). Thus exactly one curve is an affine line,
and the others are punctured lines whose radial maps have degree
\(1\) or \(-1\).

After choosing the affine-line component as \(x=0\), this forces
\[
H(0,y,z)=ay+b,\qquad
H(x,\alpha_jx,z)=c_jx^{k_j}z+B_j(x),
\]
with \(a,c_j\ne0\), \(k_j\ge1\), and \(B_j(0)=b\).
These restrictions come from the existing \(H\); arbitrary independent
jets are not being assumed compatible at a multiple intersection.

After inverting \(x\), the powers of the other line ideals are
comaximal. Finite inverses on each component glue by the Chinese
remainder theorem, giving a polynomial-plane fiber over every nonzero
base value and over \(\mathbb C(x)\). At \(x=0\), the equation
is linear in \(y\). The prior residual-plane theorem therefore
makes every \(F\)-fiber an affine three-space.[^1]

For an original slice, the decomposition \(T=(\ker D)[F]\)
identifies the kernel with any one of these fibers. Hence the kernel
is polynomial and \(F\) is a coordinate. No assumption that \(D\)
fixes \(x\) or \(y\) is used.

This last step is why the result is stated for original slices.
An arbitrary stable coordinate does not supply the same decomposition
of the original ring. The proof does not settle the stronger embedding
question in that constant-axis branch.

## 3. Separated sums with a binomial remainder

Let
\[
F=h(s)+G(x,y,z),\qquad G=f(x,y)z^q+g(x,y),
\]
where \(\deg h=N\ge2\), \(f\ne0\), and \(q\ge1\). The
[full written proof](research/separable-sum-pass.md) establishes
that stability of \(F\), coordinatehood of \(F\), and coordinatehood
of \(G\) are equivalent.

Here is the key deduction. A general fiber of \(h\) is \(N\)
points. The global sum theorem expresses a general \(F\)-fiber
as their join with a general \(G\)-fiber \(Y\).[^2] Consequently
\[
\widetilde H_{j+1}(F^{-1}(\lambda);\mathbb Z)
\cong
\widetilde H_j(Y;\mathbb Z)^{\oplus(N-1)}
\]
at general values. Stability makes the left side zero, so \(Y\)
is integrally acyclic, including connectedness and the absence of
torsion. Smoothness of \(F\), evaluated at a zero of \(h'\),
makes \(G\) critical-point-free.

The binomial-surface theorem then rectifies the smooth irreducible
surface \(Y\) in \(\mathbb A^3\).[^3] Its reduced defining
equation is therefore a coordinate up to scaling and translation.
Thus \(G\) is a coordinate, and adding \(h(s)\) is a triangular
change of coordinates.

The integral argument matters. Contractibility of a suspension alone
does not justify claiming that the unsuspended space is contractible.
Only integral acyclicity is deduced, and it is exactly enough for
the cited surface theorem.

The degree condition on \(h\) also matters:
\[
F=s+z^2+xy
\]
is a coordinate although \(G=z^2+xy\) has a critical point.
The proof treats constant \(f\) or \(g\) separately and records
the stronger simultaneous-pair criterion available for \(q\ge2\).
It does not extend that pair criterion to \(q=1\).

## 4. The pure nonconstant-leading-coefficient branch

For
\[
F=xu^n+G(x,y,z),\qquad n\ge2,
\]
stability forces \(g(y,z)=G(0,y,z)\) to be a plane coordinate.
The [detailed argument](research/linear-leading-quadratic-pass.md)
uses the finite cyclic quotient, not an assumed relative coordinate
completion.

First, \(g\) has no critical point: at a hypothetical one, choose
an \(n\)-th root \(u\) canceling \(G_x\). All partial derivatives
of \(F\) would vanish. Next, the quotient of \(F^{-1}(\lambda)\)
by \(u\mapsto\zeta u\) is
\[
Y_\lambda=\{xw+G=\lambda\}.
\]
Rational cohomology of a finite quotient is the invariant part of
the cohomology upstairs, so \(Y_\lambda\) is rationally acyclic.[^4]
Its open part is \(\mathbb C^*\times\mathbb A^2\); its closed
part is \(g^{-1}(\lambda)\times\mathbb A^1\). The closed-open
sequence forces every \(g\)-fiber to be an affine line.

Choose a plane companion \(t\) to \(v=g\). Then \(G=v+xR\).
With \(W=w+R\), the quotient polynomial becomes
\[
J=xw+G=v+xW.
\]
This is explicitly a coordinate, with inverse
\(v=J-xW\), \(w=W-R(x,J-xW,t)\).

The original fiber is still a cyclic cover of that affine three-space.
Rectifying the quotient does not rectify the branch surface or the cover.
A sufficient additional condition would be that \(G\) is a coordinate
over \(\mathbb C[x]\), but no such condition has been proved in
the general pure branch.

For each prime dividing \(n\), Smith theory gives mod-\(p\)
acyclicity of the fixed surface \(G^{-1}(\lambda)\).[^5]
This does not automatically supply integral acyclicity or algebraic
coordinatehood. The distinction from the integral join argument in
Section 3 remains essential.

## 5. Explicit obstructions to the proposed shortcuts

### An original slice need not be a residual variable

The quartic
\[
F=xu^2+yu+x+z^4
\]
is an actual coordinate, with LND slice
\(D=\partial_x-u\partial_y\). Put \(w=y+xu\). Then
\[
F=x+uw+z^4,\qquad
x=F-uw-z^4,\qquad y=w-u(F-uw-z^4).
\]
Nevertheless every specialization
\[
F_\gamma=\gamma+u(y+\gamma u)+z^4
\]
has a critical point at \(u=y=z=0\). Therefore none is a
coordinate in the specialized three-variable ring.

This disproves deriving the needed residual-\(x\) hypothesis from an
absolute slice. It is not a cancellation counterexample. Its \(yu\)
term is not divisible by \(x\), so it also does not disprove the
smaller pure-power implication.

### A coordinate quotient and a smooth map are insufficient

The genuine quartic
\[
F=xu^2+y+x^2y^2
\]
has no critical points and has \(G(0,y)=y\). Its cyclic quotient
is therefore polynomial by the preceding construction. Yet at level
zero the involution-fixed set is
\[
\{y(1+x^2y)=0\}\times\mathbb A^1_z,
\]
the disjoint union of \(\mathbb A^2\) and
\(\mathbb C^*\times\mathbb A^1\). It is disconnected, violating
the mod-2 fixed-set condition for a stable coordinate. Thus the
necessary quotient condition is visibly not sufficient.

### Square completion can increase the degree

For \(F=xu^2+Bu+C\), completion of the square is a polynomial
coordinate change only when \(x\mid B\). Writing \(B=xb\)
gives
\[
F=x(u+b/2)^2+C-xb^2/4.
\]
Even if \(F\) has degree four, the remainder can have degree five.
For example \(F=xu^2+xy^2u+z+x^4\) is a coordinate via \(z\),
but the completed remainder is \(z+x^4-xy^4/4\). When \(B=y\),
the corresponding substitution has a pole at \(x=0\) and is only
localized, not a polynomial automorphism.

## 6. An integral constraint on the remaining fibers

The Smith condition in the pure-power branch has a precise consequence:
every \(G\)-fiber has \(H_2=0\), and its \(H_1\) is a finite
group of order coprime to \(n\). Thus all fibers are rationally
acyclic, while torsion at other primes remains possible.

The [additional proof](research/all-fiber-torsion-constraint.md)
constrains how that torsion could vary. Let \(B\) be a nonempty
finite set containing the bifurcation values, let \(s=|B|\), let
\(T=H^2(G^{-1}(\lambda);\mathbb Z)\) at a general value, and
let \(T_b=H^2(G^{-1}(b);\mathbb Z)\). With \(\mathcal T\)
the monodromy local system, Gysin and Leray give
\[
T^{\pi_1(\mathbb C\setminus B)}=0,\qquad
H^1(\mathbb C\setminus B;\mathcal T)
\cong\bigoplus_{b\in B}T_b,\qquad
\prod_b|T_b|=|T|^{s-1}.
\]
In particular, trivial integral monodromy, or constant torsion orders
across all fibers, would force integral acyclicity. Neither extra
condition has been proved here.

There is no general shortcut through generic-fiber torsion-freeness.
Dimca and Némethi give a quartic with general-fiber first homology
\(\mathbb Z/3\) and nontrivial monodromy.[^6] Their example
does not satisfy our all-fiber hypotheses. It shows why the missing
torsion argument cannot simply be omitted. The
[bounded literature audit](research/all-fiber-acyclicity-audit.md)
records this exact distinction and the extra hypotheses in available
positive theorems.

## 7. Reproducibility and remaining proof obligations

The three new independent checkers import no research solver:

~~~text
/opt/anaconda3/bin/python verify_central_arrangements.py
/opt/anaconda3/bin/python verify_quadratic_obstructions.py
python3 verify_torsion_monodromy.py
~~~

The arrangement checks cover two-sided polynomial inverses, arbitrary
multiplicity examples, localized Chinese remainders, integral residue
matrices and failures of rank-only reasoning. The quadratic checks cover
the displayed slice and inverse identities, residual critical points,
degree growth, the quotient, separated sums and negative controls.
The preceding thirteen suites have also been rerun successfully.

The total is 208 passing test methods across sixteen suites: 14
arrangement checks, 13 quadratic checks, six finite-group checks,
and the preceding 175 checks.
The localized checker explicitly rejects denominators with poles away
from \(x=0\); merely using the larger field \(\mathbb Q(x)\)
would not certify the required Laurent scope.

These exact computations verify identities and transcription, not
general algebraic topology. The written proofs depend on the cited
theorems and their explicitly checked hypotheses. Separate model
reconstructions appear in
[the arrangement audit](research/central-arrangement-independent-audit.md)
and [the separated-sum audit](research/separable-sum-independent-audit.md).
They are not human peer review or Lean proofs.

The unresolved obligations are substantive. No argument reduces an
arbitrary original slice to a concurrent-line coefficient, a binomial
separated sum, or degree at most three. The genuine quadratic branch
with nonconstant leading coefficient remains incomplete. The bounded
[literature audit](research/quadratic-slice-literature.md) identifies
the exact extra relative hypotheses in the available prior theorems;
failure to find a stronger theorem is not proof of its absence.

Accordingly, these are additional restricted results and decisive
counterexamples to proposed shortcuts. They are not completion of the
general conjecture, a public novelty claim, or a submission-ready
resolution.
