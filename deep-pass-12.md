# A double-commutator criterion for affine cancellation

The height-eight case is settled by the argument below. For a locally
nilpotent derivation \(E\) of \(\mathbb C[x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4]\), assume that
\[
[E,[E,\partial_{x_i}]]=0\qquad (i=1,2,3,4).
\tag{1}
\]
Then \(E\) fixes an ambient polynomial coordinate. If \(E\ne0\) and its
kernel is regular, that kernel is a polynomial ring in three variables.
In particular this conclusion holds when \(E\) has a slice.

This is a restricted recognition theorem, with a complete written
deduction from precisely stated published results. It does not resolve
the unrestricted complex threefold cancellation conjecture. No novelty,
human expert review, or proof-assistant verification is claimed.

## 1. The cancellation question and the precise improvement

The working question is
\[
X\times_{\mathbb C}\mathbb A^1\simeq\mathbb A^4
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad X\simeq\mathbb A^3.
\tag{2}
\]
It is the dimensionally corrected complex-threefold interpretation of
the supplied conjecture. That interpretation has not been confirmed by
the professor.

The variety \(X\) is affine because its zero-section is closed in the
affine cylinder. Writing \(K=\mathcal O(X)\), the premise identifies
\(B=K[s]\) with \(\mathbb C^{[4]}\). The derivation \(E=\partial_s\)
is locally nilpotent: every polynomial is killed by some iterate.
It has a slice, meaning \(E(s)=1\), and \(\ker E=K\).
Conversely, the characteristic-zero slice theorem gives this cylinder
presentation for a locally nilpotent derivation with a slice.

An ambient coordinate is a polynomial that can be completed to a
polynomial coordinate system of the entire ring \(B\). It is stronger
than an abstract generator of a smaller invariant ring. This distinction
is essential: the regular-kernel theorem applies only after the ambient
coordinate has been established.[^1]

For an ambient coordinate frame \(x=(x_1,\ldots,x_4)\), let
\(T_i=\partial_{x_i}\). Define its slice-filtration degree and height by
\[
q_i=\max_{f\ne0,\ T_if\ne0}
 \bigl(\deg_s(T_if)-\deg_s f\bigr),
\qquad H(x)=\sum_{i=1}^4(q_i+1).
\tag{3}
\]
These degrees are finite integers at least \(-1\).
The [preceding filtration argument](research/height-seven-audit.md)
proves that \(H\le7\) suffices, together with the stronger criterion
that one degree zero and one degree one suffice regardless of the
other degrees.

The new conclusion is
\[
H(x)\le8\ \Longrightarrow\ K\simeq\mathbb C^{[3]}.
\qquad
K\not\simeq\mathbb C^{[3]}\ \Longrightarrow\ H_{\min}(E)\ge9.
\tag{4}
\]
Here the minimum ranges over all ambient polynomial coordinate frames.
There is no proved universal upper bound on that minimum, or
height-decreasing coordinate operation. Equation (4) is not a finite
reduction of (2).

## 2. Published inputs and a three-variable lemma

Only the following external recognition results are needed.

1. A nonzero \(\mathbb C[p]\)-linear locally nilpotent derivation on
   \(\mathbb C[p]^{[3]}\) with regular kernel has kernel
   \(\mathbb C[p]^{[2]}\). This is Bhatwadekar, Gupta and Lokhande,
   Theorem 3.5.[^1]
2. Two polynomially independent commuting locally nilpotent derivations
   of \(\mathbb C^{[3]}\) have common kernel generated by an ambient
   coordinate. This is Maubach, Theorem 3.6.[^2]
3. Rentschler's plane theorem gives an invariant plane coordinate for
   every nonzero locally nilpotent derivation of \(\mathbb C^{[2]}\).
   The precise statement is recalled in Dasgupta and Gaifullin,
   Theorem 2.7.[^3]
4. Two quasi-translation theorems of de Bondt, stated where they are
   applied below, concern Jacobian rank one and homogeneous Jacobian
   rank two. The latter is attributed to Gordan and Noether.[^4]

For completeness, the needed three-variable bridge is reconstructed
here rather than treated as an additional source assertion.

**Lemma.** If \(D\) is locally nilpotent on \(R=\mathbb C[u,v,w]\) and
\([D,[D,\partial_u]]=0\), then \(D\) fixes a coordinate of \(R\).

**Proof.** The zero case is immediate. Put \(P=\partial_u\) and
\(W=[D,P]\). On \(R[t]\), conjugation gives
\[
\exp(tD)P\exp(-tD)=P+tW.
\tag{5}
\]
This is locally nilpotent. For each \(f\in R\), a sufficiently high
iterate \((P+tW)^Nf\) vanishes. Its coefficient of \(t^N\) is \(W^Nf\);
therefore \(W\) is locally nilpotent. The assumption says \([D,W]=0\).

If \(D,W\) are independent over \(R\), apply the commuting-pair
theorem.[^2] Otherwise \(W=hD\) over the rational function field,
including \(W=0\). Write the coefficient vector of \(D\) as \(b\).
Since \(W=-\partial_u b\), the nonzero coefficient ratios of \(b\)
belong to \(\mathbb C(v,w)\).

Let \(g\) be the coefficient gcd and \(D_0=D/g\). The relation
\(D(g)=gD_0(g)\) and local nilpotence force \(D(g)=0\): a nonzero
polynomial cannot have a nonzero derivative divisible by itself under
a locally nilpotent derivation. Thus \(D_0\) is locally nilpotent, since
\(D^Nf=g^ND_0^Nf\), and has the same kernel.

The coefficient vector of \(D_0\) is primitive in
\(\mathbb C[v,w][u]\). Clearing the ratio denominators gives a
proportional primitive vector in \(\mathbb C[v,w]^3\). At every
irreducible prime, some entry of that vector has valuation zero.
Polynomiality and primitivity therefore force the proportionality
factor to be a unit, a nonzero constant. The coefficients of \(D_0\)
are consequently independent of \(u\).

Now \(D_0\) preserves \(\mathbb C[v,w]\). If its restriction is zero,
\(v\) is invariant. Otherwise Rentschler gives an invariant coordinate
of that plane.[^3] Either is an ambient coordinate of \(R\), and is
also killed by \(D\). \(\square\)

The valuation argument does not assert a multivariable Bezout identity
from coefficient gcd one. The explicit denominator reconstruction is
also in the [single-partial audit](research/height-seven-audit.md).

## 3. Differential identities in four variables

Assume (1), and write
\[
b=(E(x_1),\ldots,E(x_4))^{\mathsf T},
\qquad J=(\partial_jb_i)_{i,j}.
\]
The coefficient vector of \([E,\partial_j]\) is minus column \(j\)
of \(J\). Taking its bracket with \(E\) gives
\[
E(J)=J^2,\qquad E^r(J)=r!J^{r+1}.
\tag{6}
\]
Local nilpotence kills all finitely many entries of \(J\) after
sufficiently many iterates. Hence \(J\) is nilpotent over the rational
function field. As a four-by-four matrix it satisfies \(J^4=0\).

Put \(h_r=E^r(x)\). The identities needed throughout the proof are
\[
h_r=(r-1)!J^{r-1}b,\qquad
\operatorname{Jac}h_r=r!J^r\qquad(r\ge1).
\tag{7}
\]
For the first, apply \(E\), using \(E(J)=J^2\) and \(E(b)=Jb\).
For the second, use the vector chain-rule identity
\[
\operatorname{Jac}(Ev)
 =E(\operatorname{Jac}v)+(\operatorname{Jac}v)J.
\]
Together with (6), it advances \(r!J^r\) to \((r+1)!J^{r+1}\).
Thus all factorials in (7) are fixed by induction, not an analogy with
the scalar case.

In particular \(h_4\) is constant and \(h_5=0\). The flow has
parameter degree at most four, and
\[
\operatorname{Jac}(\exp(tE)x)
 =I+tJ+t^2J^2+t^3J^3=(I-tJ)^{-1}.
\tag{8}
\]
This is a bound on degree in \(t\), not on the ordinary polynomial
degrees of the coefficients \(b_i(x)\).

If \(E=0\), the theorem is immediate. Otherwise let \(m\) be maximal
with \(h_m\ne0\), and set \(h=h_m\). Equations (7) imply
\[
E(h)=0,\qquad Jh=0,\qquad J\operatorname{Jac}h=0.
\tag{9}
\]
For the last identity, differentiate \(h_{m+1}=0\) to obtain
\(J^{m+1}=0\). In addition
\((\operatorname{Jac}h)h=0\). Consequently \(h\cdot\partial_x\)
is a quasi-translation derivation, and commutes with \(E\).

A quasi-translation is the special polynomial flow \(x\mapsto x+th(x)\).
The condition \((\operatorname{Jac}h)h=0\) characterizes it.[^4]
It should not be confused with every polynomial additive-group flow.

## 4. Constant and rank-one highest iterates

The highest iterate provides an exhaustive small case split.

| Highest iterate | Consequence of (7) | Remaining argument |
| --- | --- | --- |
| \(m=4\) | \(\operatorname{Jac}h=0\) | Constant direction |
| \(m=3\) | \(\operatorname{rank}J^3\le1\) | Constant or rank-one image |
| \(m=2\) | \(J^3=0\), \(\operatorname{rank}J^2\le1\) | Constant or rank-one image |
| \(m=1\) | \(Jb=0,\ J^2=0,\ \operatorname{rank}J\le2\) | Constant, rank one, or Section 5 |

The rank statements follow from the possible Jordan blocks of a
nilpotent four-by-four matrix over the rational function field.
In the \(m=2\) row, the additional identity \(J^3=0\) is necessary.

### Constant direction

If \(h=c\in\mathbb C^4\setminus\{0\}\), then \(Jc=0\) by (9).
The constant translation field \(c\cdot\partial_x\) commutes with \(E\).
Choose linear coordinates in which it is \(\partial_4\). Then \(E\)
preserves \(R=\mathbb C[x_1,x_2,x_3]\).

Every new coordinate partial is a constant linear combination of the
old ones, so (1) survives this coordinate change and restriction to
\(R\). Section 2 gives an invariant coordinate of \(R\), hence of \(B\).
If the restriction of \(E\) is zero, the conclusion is immediate.

This improves an explicitly incomplete observation in the preceding
report. A nonzero \(E^4(x_i)\) gives more than another slice: the full
constant vector \(E^4(x)\) gives a commuting translation direction.

### Rank-one image

Suppose \(\operatorname{rank}\operatorname{Jac}h=1\).
De Bondt's Theorem 5.5 allows linear coordinates in which
\[
h=(0,\ldots,0,h_{s+1},\ldots,h_4),\qquad
h_i\in\mathbb C[x_1,\ldots,x_s],
\tag{10}
\]
where \(s\ge1\) and the remaining components are linearly independent
over \(\mathbb C\). No homogeneity assumption is needed here.[^4]
The dimension \(\ell=4-s\) of the image's linear span is one, two,
or three.

If \(\ell=1\), write \(h=g c\) with \(c\) a nonzero constant vector.
Then \(Jh=0\) implies \(Jc=0\), and the constant-direction argument
applies.

If \(\ell=3\), then \(s=1\). Some \(h_i\in\mathbb C[x_1]\) is
nonconstant because its Jacobian has rank one. From \(E(h_i)=0\),
\[
h_i'(x_1)E(x_1)=0
\]
forces \(E(x_1)=0\). This gives an invariant linear coordinate directly.

If \(\ell=2\), write
\(h=(0,0,h_3(x_1,x_2),h_4(x_1,x_2))\).
There is a \(j\in\{1,2\}\) with
\[
h_3\partial_jh_4-h_4\partial_jh_3\ne0.
\tag{11}
\]
Otherwise all partial derivatives of \(h_3/h_4\) vanish, making that
ratio constant and contradicting linear independence. The vectors
\(h,\partial_jh\) span the \(e_3,e_4\) coordinate plane over the
rational function field. Equation (9) says \(J\) kills both, so columns
three and four of \(J\) vanish. Hence \(E\) preserves
\(\mathbb C[x_1,x_2]\), and the plane theorem supplies an invariant
ambient coordinate.

This closes every \(m\ge2\) branch and every rank-at-most-one branch
when \(m=1\). No polynomial Luroth parameter or global quotient
section is required.

## 5. The remaining rank-two case

The remaining case is
\[
Jb=0,\qquad J^2=0,\qquad\operatorname{rank}J=2.
\tag{12}
\]
Both image and kernel of \(J\) have dimension two over
\(F=\mathbb C(x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4)\). Thus
\(\operatorname{im}_FJ=\ker_FJ\). Since \(Jb=0\),
\[
b\in\operatorname{im}_FJ.
\tag{13}
\]
The use of rational linear algebra here is legitimate. No polynomial
solution to \(b=Jv\) is asserted.

Let \(d=\max_i\deg b_i\), introduce one variable \(t\), and homogenize:
\[
\widetilde b(x,t)=\bigl(t^d b(x/t),0\bigr).
\tag{14}
\]
This is a homogeneous polynomial vector in five variables. On \(t\ne0\),
putting \(z=x/t\) gives the exact block Jacobian
\[
\operatorname{Jac}_{x,t}\widetilde b
=t^{d-1}
\begin{pmatrix}
J(z)&d\,b(z)-J(z)z\\
0&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{15}
\]
The last column belongs to the span of the first four by (13).
Those four have rank two. Therefore the homogenized Jacobian has
rank exactly two, not three. Also
\[
(\operatorname{Jac}\widetilde b)\widetilde b
 =\bigl(t^{2d-1}J(z)b(z),0\bigr)=0.
\tag{16}
\]
Polynomiality extends this identity across \(t=0\). Thus
\(\widetilde b\) defines a homogeneous quasi-translation.

De Bondt's Theorem 5.3 states that a homogeneous quasi-translation
of Jacobian rank two in dimension \(n\) has coefficient-image linear
span of dimension at most \(n-2\). Its statement requires no
coefficient-gcd hypothesis.[^4] Applying it in dimension five gives
an image span of dimension at most three.

Specialization \(t=1\) recovers \((b(x),0)\). Hence the linear span
of the original four-component image has dimension at most three.
There is \(0\ne\lambda\in\mathbb C^4\) with
\[
\lambda\cdot b=0.
\tag{17}
\]
Therefore \(p=\lambda\cdot x\) is an invariant linear coordinate of
the original four-variable ring. The theorem from the opening is proved.

The dimension count matters. The fifth zero component explains only
one linear relation in five variables. The bound of three forces
at least one additional relation, which genuinely involves the
original four components.

## 6. A separate geometric verification of rank preservation

There is an independent way to verify the key homogenization step.
Let \(S=\overline{b(\mathbb A^4)}\), an irreducible surface, and let
\(I(S)\) be its ideal of polynomial relations. For \(P\in I(S)\),
the chain rule gives \((\nabla P)(b)J=0\). Equation (13) then yields
\[
(\nabla P)(b)b=0.
\tag{18}
\]
In other words the target Euler derivation
\(\mathcal E=\sum y_i\partial_{y_i}\) preserves \(I(S)\).

For \(P=\sum_{j\in M}P_j\) decomposed into its finitely many nonzero
homogeneous parts, the polynomials
\(P,\mathcal EP,\ldots,\mathcal E^{|M|-1}P\) all lie in \(I(S)\).
The distinct degrees \(j\) give an invertible Vandermonde matrix.
Every \(P_j\) lies in \(I(S)\). Hence \(I(S)\) is homogeneous and
\(S\) is a cone.

For \(t\ne0\), equation (14) maps into \(S\times\{0\}\). Every
polynomial equation defining that closed set then vanishes identically
after substitution, including at \(t=0\). Conversely \(t=1\) gives
a dense image in \(S\times\{0\}\). Thus
\[
\overline{\widetilde b(\mathbb A^5)}=S\times\{0\}.
\tag{19}
\]
Its dimension is two, verifying the same rank as (15).

This proof concerns the image closure. It does not say that the
coefficient map attains the origin. The [separate homogenization
audit](research/height-eight-homogenization-audit.md) reconstructs
both (15) and (19).

## 7. Return to the kernel and the height bound

If \(E\ne0\) has regular kernel \(K\), the invariant ambient coordinate
\(p\) identifies \(B=\mathbb C[p]^{[3]}\) and makes \(E\)
\(\mathbb C[p]\)-linear. The regular-kernel theorem now gives
\(K=\mathbb C[p]^{[2]}\).[^1]

For a slice derivation, regularity is already available: \(K\) is
Noetherian and regularity descends from the faithfully flat polynomial
extension \(K[s]\cong\mathbb C^{[4]}\). This does not assume
cancellation.

To obtain (4), recall the filtration details. Degree \(-1\) means
\(T_i=fE\); the coordinate slice of \(T_i\) forces \(f\) to be a
nonzero constant, so its kernel is already \(\mathbb C^{[3]}\).
For \(q_i\ge0\), local nilpotence of the leading homogeneous
derivation improves the naive vertical bound to
\[
\deg_sT_i(s)\le q_i,\qquad
T_i(K)\subseteq K+Ks+\cdots+Ks^{q_i}.
\tag{20}
\]
Indeed a nonzero highest vertical term would send \(s\) to a
multiple of itself, impossible for an LND.

Two degree-zero partials leave an \(E\)-stable coordinate plane.
One degree zero and one degree one leave an \(E\)-stable
three-variable coordinate ring to which Section 2 applies.
If none of these cases occurs and \(H\le8\), every degree is at
least one. Their sum is at most four, so they are all one.
Equation (20), checked on \(K\) and \(s\), then gives
\([E,[E,T_i]]=0\) for all four partials. The theorem above finishes
this last case.

The next unclosed degree pattern is \((1,1,1,2)\) at height nine.
A remaining frame containing a degree zero must instead have
height at least ten. These statements constrain a hypothetical
nonpolynomial factor; they do not produce one.

One subcase at the next height also closes. If \(E^2(x_i)=0\)
for all four generators and three coordinate partials satisfy (1),
then \(Jb=0\) and \(E(J)=-J^2\). The three good columns force
\(J^2=w e_4^{\mathsf T}\). Thus \(0=J^2b=w\,b_4\).
If \(w=0\), the theorem above applies; otherwise \(b_4=0\) gives
an invariant linear coordinate. The
[mixed-commutator note](research/height-nine-first-pass.md) and its
[independent audit](research/height-nine-first-audit.md) record this
subcase and the remaining defect. They do not raise the universal
height bound to nine.

## 8. Exact controls and excluded shortcuts

The examples below distinguish the theorem from nearby false
inferences. Their algebra is checked by the standalone
[height-eight script](research/height_eight_algebra_checks.py).

### Fourth flow degree genuinely occurs

On \(\mathbb C[a,b,c,d]\), set
\[
E=\partial_a+a\partial_b+(b+a^2/2)\partial_c
 +(c+ab+a^3/6)\partial_d.
\]
It satisfies all four identities (1) and \(E^4(d)=6\).
The resulting constant commuting direction is \(6\partial_d\).
Thus replacing the theorem by an assumed quadratic or cubic flow
would discard a real positive case.

### Ordinary degrees and group ranks are not bounded as needed

For every \(N\ge1\),
\[
E_N=a^N\partial_c+(a^Nb-1)\partial_d
\]
satisfies (12). It has slice \(s=bc-d\) and invariant
\(u=(a^Nb-1)c-a^Nd\), with exact inverse
\[
c=a^Ns-u,\qquad d=(a^Nb-1)s-bu.
\]
Hence its kernel is \(\mathbb C[a,b,u]\), and ordinary coefficient
degrees are unbounded.

For \(N=1\), the frame \((a-c,b-d,c,d)\) has all \(q_i=1\).
Nevertheless \(E\) and its four first commutators have generic rank
only two, with intrinsic leading rank one. Therefore the all-one
pattern does not imply a transitive additive-group action.
The coordinate map \((a,b)\mapsto(a,ab-1)\) also misses the origin
of its plane-cone closure.

### Nice derivations and square-zero matrices are different conditions

Put \(f=a+bd-c^2\) and
\[
Q=(-d+2cf-bf^2,\ 1,\ f,\ f^2).
\]
The derivation \(Q\cdot\partial\) has a slice \(b\), and kills every
component of \(Q\), but \((\operatorname{Jac}Q)^2\ne0\).
It is a primitive version of de Bondt's Example 4.3.[^4]
Thus the statement that a derivation kills its generator images
does not imply (1).

Conversely
\[
H=(0,a^2,a^3,3ba^2-2ca)
\]
has square-zero Jacobian, but
\((\operatorname{Jac}H)H=(0,0,0,a^4)\ne0\) and fails (6).
This is the ordinary-versus-strong-nilpotence control discussed
by de Bondt.[^5]

Even for quasi-translations with square-zero Jacobian,
homogenization need not preserve rank: \(b=(1,x_3,0,0)\)
has rank one, whereas \((t,x_3,0,0,0)\) has rank two.
The half-dimensional rank hypothesis in (12) is substantive.

The [literature scope note](research/height-eight-literature-pass.md)
also records why results assuming ordinary quadratic degree,
or strong nilpotency under independent-point substitutions,
cannot be invoked solely from (8).[^5][^6]

## 9. Verification and remaining proof obligation

The subsequent [mixed-commutator proof](deep-pass-13.md) closes the
next pattern \((1,1,1,2)\) under the slice hypothesis and strengthens
the sufficient height bound to nine. The nonslice theorem and
historical verification counts in this report remain unchanged.

The new independent suite has seven test methods. The full regression
run passes 260 exact test methods across twenty suites, with commands,
observed counts, exit codes, and test times in
[verification-pass-12.json](verification-pass-12.json). The new suite
can be reproduced with

    /opt/anaconda3/bin/python research/height_eight_algebra_checks.py

It uses SymPy and the standard library, with no research-module imports.
It checks the Riccati identities, iterated-Jacobian factorials,
flow composition, explicit coordinate inverses, filtration degrees,
rank preservation, and the negative controls. These finite examples
do not constitute formal verification of the general theorem or its
published inputs.

The [algebra proof](research/height-eight-algebra-pass.md) and
[homogenization reconstruction](research/height-eight-homogenization-audit.md)
record the two principal derivations. The
[full proof audit](research/pass12-full-proof-audit.md) checks the
highest-iterate case split and each recognition step, finding no gap.
The [orbit analysis](research/height-eight-orbit-pass.md) remains useful for its exact
degeneration controls, but its low-rank obstruction is superseded
by Sections 3 through 5. No full-orbit-rank assumption is used.
Separate model reconstructions are not human peer review.

To solve (2), a further global argument must place an arbitrary
slice action in a proved class, or otherwise recognize its kernel.
For example, a theorem giving a suitable coordinate frame with
\(H\le8\) would suffice. No such theorem is established here.
Advancing one height at a time, without a global bound or descent
argument, is not completion of cancellation.

The restricted theorem is ready for mathematical criticism as a
source-dependent written argument. Its originality is undetermined.
The [statement-level search](research/height-eight-statement-literature-addendum.md)
did not locate an identical formulation within its bounded coverage;
that negative search result does not establish priority.
Publication-level credibility still requires expert scrutiny and an
appropriate literature audit, not a large test count.
