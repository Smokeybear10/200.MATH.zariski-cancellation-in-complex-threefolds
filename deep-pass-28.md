# Finite symmetry criteria, a canonical top-coefficient failure, and complete invariant fibers

Private research checkpoint, September 13, 2026. Working interpretation:
complex threefold cancellation. Professor Luo has not yet confirmed either
the corrected dimension or the field.

## 1. Result and remaining gap

The unrestricted conjecture is not solved. This pass establishes four
precise statements and closes two proposed shortcuts.

First, an actual finite-dimensional Lie algebra of locally nilpotent
derivations, containing the cylinder translation and having no nonconstant
common rational invariant, is sufficient for cancellation. A finite
solvable Lie algebra generated by locally nilpotent derivations also
suffices under the same transitivity condition. This uses published
integration and triangularization theorems. Existence of such an algebra
for every cylinder remains unproved, and is equivalent to the target
conclusion in this setup.

Second, a fixed-coordinate Lie-word test recognizes linear
triangularizability in finitely many steps. A smaller published strong
Jacobian test already recognizes the same property. Finite Lie closure
alone does not imply linear triangularizability: an explicit coordinate
LND generates a five-dimensional perfect Lie algebra with the ambient
translations.

Third, the primitive highest-coefficient method fails even inside an
actual canonical homotopy with nonunit full plinth. A different horizontal
action succeeds on the very same model. This refutes automatic success
for an arbitrary horizontal choice, not existence of a successful choice.

Fourth, every closed fiber of every nonconstant invariant of the fixed conductor
surface is classified as an actual affine scheme. Some invariants have
a nonplanar fiber and cannot belong to a generating triple; other explicit
invariants have planar fibers for every value. Fiberwise planarity cannot
by itself decide the remaining global presentation question.

Detailed proofs and their independent AI reconstructions are preserved
separately. These are working mathematical proofs, not human expert review,
Lean verification, a comprehensive novelty audit, or a solution of the
general conjecture.

## 2. The exact finite-symmetry recognition criterion

Write the assumed polynomial cylinder as

\[
 B=A[s]\simeq\mathbb C[x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4],
 \qquad \delta=\partial_s,\qquad A=\ker\delta .
 \tag{1}
\]

Suppose there is a finite-dimensional complex Lie subalgebra
\(\mathfrak g\subseteq\operatorname{Der}_{\mathbb C}(B)\) such that
\(\delta\in\mathfrak g\), every element of \(\mathfrak g\) is locally
nilpotent, and

\[
 \operatorname{Frac}(B)^{\mathfrak g}=\mathbb C.
 \tag{2}
\]

Then \(A\simeq\mathbb C^{[3]}\).

The full proof is in
[the finite-Lie recognition note](research/finite-lie-recognition-pass28.md).
An ordered PBW spanning argument first makes the orbit of each polynomial
under the enveloping algebra finite-dimensional. Applying this to a finite
set of ring generators supplies a faithful finite-dimensional stable
representation. Engel's theorem and finite exponential/BCH formulas
integrate the action to a unipotent algebraic group.[^1]

The invariant-field condition gives a dense orbit; a unipotent orbit in an
affine variety is closed, so this orbit is the whole variety.[^2]
The published transitive-unipotent triangularization theorem puts the
group, hence \(\delta\), in polynomial triangular coordinates.[^3]
The action of \(\delta\) is proper because its known slice identifies its
action relation with the closed fiber product

\[
 \mathbb G_a\times\operatorname{Spec}B
 \simeq
 \operatorname{Spec}B\times_{\operatorname{Spec}A}\operatorname{Spec}B
 \hookrightarrow
 \operatorname{Spec}B\times\operatorname{Spec}B .
 \tag{3}
\]

The proper triangular \(\mathbb G_a\)-action theorem in dimension four
then gives the polynomial kernel.[^4]

It is not necessary for the one-parameter group of \(\delta\) to be normal
in the larger unipotent group. The coordinate-tower proof uses appropriate
right actions arising from normalizers of stabilizers, not an unjustified
left quotient by this particular subgroup.

If a finite solvable Lie algebra is generated by LNDs, the cited
integration theorem and solvable case imply that all its elements are
LNDs.[^3] This gives the stated solvable version. Merely saying the
generators are LNDs, without the finite solvable hypothesis, is not enough.

The existential condition does not solve cancellation by renaming it.
Indeed, if \(A\) is already polynomial, its three coordinate translations
together with \(\partial_s\) give a suitable four-dimensional abelian
algebra. Therefore, for a fixed cylinder (1),

\[
 A\simeq\mathbb C^{[3]}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \text{a finite transitive all-LND algebra containing }\delta
 \text{ exists}.
 \tag{4}
\]

The research gap is to construct that structure from the cylinder
hypothesis, not to assume it. The one-dimensional algebra
\(\mathbb C\delta\) is available on every cylinder but fails (2).

## 3. A finite fixed-frame test and its exact limitation

Let \(k\) have characteristic zero, let \(D\) be a polynomial vector field
on \(k[x_1,\ldots,x_n]\), and let its coefficients have degree at most
\(d\geq0\). In this fixed polynomial coordinate system set

\[
 \mathfrak g_D=\operatorname{Lie}_k
 \langle D,\partial_{x_1},\ldots,\partial_{x_n}\rangle,
 \qquad W=1+d+\cdots+d^{\,n-1}.
 \tag{5}
\]

The following conditions are equivalent:

1. A single linear change of coordinates makes \(D\) strictly triangular.
2. \(\mathfrak g_D\) is finite-dimensional and nilpotent.
3. Every left-normed generator word of length \(W+1\) is zero.

For the nontrivial structural implication, a nonzero central element of a
finite nilpotent algebra containing all coordinate translations must itself
be a constant vector field. Choose a linear coordinate in that direction.
All fields descend to the remaining polynomial coordinates. Induction
triangularizes the entire algebra using linear changes only.

For the word bound, assign triangular coordinates weights
\(w_1=1,\ w_i=dw_{i-1}+1\). Every generator lowers weighted degree by at
least one. A word of length \(W+1\) kills all coordinate functions.
Conversely, the Jacobi identity rewrites all Lie words in left-normed form,
so vanishing at that length forces a finite nilpotent closure.

The full proof and the cases \(d=0,1\) are in
[the translation-frame note](research/translation-frame-pass28.md).
This is a finite criterion, not a claim of efficient enumeration.

There is already a smaller published equivalent test: for the Jacobian
matrix \(JF\) of the coefficient vector \(F\), use independent variable
tuples \(y^{(1)},\ldots,y^{(n)}\) and test

\[
 JF(y^{(n)})\cdots JF(y^{(1)})=0.
 \tag{6}
\]

De Bondt's strong-nilpotence theorem gives exactly the relevant constant
linear triangularization criterion.[^5] Ordinary nilpotence of
\(JF(x)\) at one shared variable tuple is not a substitute.

An explicit warning against replacing nilpotence by finite dimension is

\[
 D=\bigl(1+4y(x+y^2)\bigr)\partial_x
       -2(x+y^2)\partial_y.
 \tag{7}
\]

Put \(u=x+y^2\) and \(s=y+u^2\). Then \(D(u)=1,D(s)=0\), and
\(y=s-u^2,\ x=u-(s-u^2)^2\) is the polynomial inverse. Thus \(D\) is
an actual coordinate LND.

Nevertheless its Lie algebra with the old coordinate translations is
exactly \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\ltimes k^2\), of dimension five and perfect.
In intermediate coordinates \(u,v=y\) it is spanned by

\[
 P_u=\partial_u,\quad P_v=\partial_v,\quad
 E=u\partial_v,\quad F=v\partial_u,\quad
 H=u\partial_u-v\partial_v.
 \tag{8}
\]

The full bracket table and generation proof are checked independently.
No nonconstant affine-linear function of \(x,y\) has constant derivative under
\(D\), so no linear triangularizing frame exists. The nonlinear inverse
does exist. Adjoining two fixed variables gives the same warning in
dimension four. This example does not disprove a broader cancellation
criterion from finite symmetry.

## 4. Failure inside the actual nonunit-plinth canonical model

Start with the polynomial threefold and LND

\[
 R=\mathbb C[x,y,z],\qquad
 D=x^2\partial_y+2y\partial_z,\qquad
 \ker D=\mathbb C[x,x^2z-y^2].
 \tag{9}
\]

Its full plinth is \(x^2\ker D\). Choose the genuine radical perturbation
\(d=x\), not divisible by \(x^2\). The canonical cylinder presentation is

\[
 B=R[\lambda,w]/((1+\lambda x)w-y)
   =\mathbb C[\lambda,x,w,z],
 \quad
 \Delta=x^2\partial_w+2(1+\lambda x)^2w\partial_z.
 \tag{10}
\]

This is an actual polynomial model, not an abstract coefficient pattern.
The vertical \(\Delta\) is primitive and has nonunit full plinth
\(x^2\ker\Delta\).

Let \(F=2xz-w^2\), \(L_0=x\partial_w+w\partial_z\), and choose the sliced
horizontal LND \(\delta=\partial_\lambda+FL_0\). A polynomial inverse
shows that coordinates fixed by \(\delta\) are

\[
 a=x,\qquad b=w-\lambda Fx,\qquad
 c=z-\lambda Fw+\tfrac12\lambda^2F^2x,
 \qquad \kappa=2ac-b^2=F.
 \tag{11}
\]

In these coordinates \(\delta=\partial_\lambda\). Expanding \(\Delta\)
in powers of \(\lambda\), the highest coefficient is

\[
 D_4=-4a^4\kappa L,\qquad L=a\partial_b+b\partial_c,
 \quad
 \ker L=\mathbb C[a,\kappa],\quad
 \operatorname{plinth}(L)=a\mathbb C[a,\kappa].
 \tag{12}
\]

Thus even the primitive part \(L\) has no slice: it vanishes on \(a=b=0\).
No regular rational multiple of \(L\) creates a slice. The exact
localization/parity proof of the full plinth and the coordinate computation
are in [the canonical-top note](research/canonical-top-pass28.md).

On the same model the different horizontal choice
\(\delta_0=\partial_\lambda\) in the original physical coordinates gives
highest coefficient \(2x^2w\partial_z\), with sliced primitive part
\(\partial_z\). The conclusion is sharply quantified: arbitrary horizontal
choices need not work, while this example does have a successful choice.

## 5. Complete fibers of the actual conductor surface

Throughout this section, a fiber means the scheme-theoretic closed fiber
over a value in \(\mathbb C\), not the generic point of the base line.

Retain the exact surface and normalization from the preceding passes:

\[
 R_{\rm surf}=\mathbb C[d,e,g,h]\subset
 B_{\rm surf}=\mathbb C[u,y,z]/((u-1)(u-i)z-y(y-1)),
 \quad d=u^4-1,\quad e=du.
 \tag{13}
\]

Here \(g,h\) and the six conductor parameters are the fixed explicit
functions from [the full surface note](research/changed-fiber-surface-pass28.md),
not arbitrary choices. The conductor is \(I=dB_{\rm surf}\), with the
three specified paired lines lying over \((1,i),(1,-i),(-1,i)\).
The invariant ring is \(T=\mathbb C+d\mathbb C[u]\).

For any nonconstant \(j\in T\), let \(c_0\) be its common value at the four
roots of \(d\), and put \(m_r=\operatorname{ord}_r(j-c_0)\).
For a conductor pair \((r,s)\), the entire corresponding factor of the
actual exceptional fiber is

\[
 \mathbb C[t]\otimes_{\mathbb C}
 D_{m_r,m_s},\qquad
 D_{m,n}=\mathbb C[X,Y]/(XY,X^m+Y^n).
 \tag{14}
\]

All off-conductor factors of every fiber are ordinary fat lines
\(\mathbb C[t,\epsilon]/(\epsilon^q)\), with \(q\) the relevant root
multiplicity. These are global decompositions of the affine fiber rings,
not just completed local models.

The proof constructs all six actual finite jet charts, computes the
paired equal-constant-term rings, and uses lifted idempotents to separate
the components. Nilpotence of the conductor on its factors makes a
sufficiently high jet quotient exact there. Finite unit-root changes
reduce the equation to (14). The idempotent step uses the stated
nilpotent-ideal lifting lemma.[^6]

The factor (14) has length \(m+n\) transverse to its line. It embeds in a
plane exactly when \(\min(m,n)=1\): then eliminate one variable to obtain
a fat line. If both orders are at least two, its closed points have
cotangent dimension three, impossible for a closed subscheme of a plane.
Products of fat lines embed globally by the Chinese remainder theorem.

Consequently every fiber of \(j\) is planar if and only if all three
paired minima are one. Writing \(j=c_0+dF(u)\), a failure occurs precisely
when the zero set of \(F\) contains both endpoints of one of the three
paired edges. Such a \(j\) cannot occur in any generating triple of
\(R_{\rm surf}\).

This excludes, for example, all nonconstant \(c_0+d^2F\). But \(j=d\)
has three double lines in its zero fiber and only planar fibers everywhere.
The choice \(j=d(u^2-1)\) similarly has three triple lines at zero and
planar fibers at every other value. Passing these tests does not produce
two global companion generators.

The earlier exclusion of every triple containing \(h\) remains valid.
The unresolved question is whether some triple replacing \(h\), possibly
containing one of these surviving invariants, generates the actual ring.
Even settling this surface presentation would not alone supply its
required ambient realization or solve threefold cancellation.
